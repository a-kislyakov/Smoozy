## 1. What binds this before anything is decided

**The transfer itself is `Server protocol and transfers`' §7 and is not designed a second time.** Staging inside the destination folder, on the same filesystem as the final name (S1, and S28's second bullet reading its own S1: *"A download's staging directory sits inside the user's own destination folder"*), a staging path synthesised from the transfer identity and never from a file's name (S4, S5), the desktop-side record durable before the first byte (S7–S9), the verified checkpointed resume (S12), the four values compared before a download resumes (S13), publication as one rename through the platform file adapter (S15, S18), the end-to-end integrity split (S21–S25), the three endings and their opposite cleanup rules (S26–S28). Its §11 hands this document the download primitives by name and says §7 entire is the single answer Rows 208 and 209 need: **the partial survives a drop and never sits at the final name.**

**The local collision judgement is `Desktop app and delivery` §7's and this document never takes it a second time.** Two judgements at two moments (C1): *can this name be written at all*, before the transfer is arranged (C3–C9); *is this name already taken*, at publication, as an exclusive create of the final name (C10–C13). Four name-check outcomes, closed set (C4). The adapter hands on an outcome and never a prompt, a rename or a decision (C19); a name this document composes is judged again like any other (C20); the count of what arrived is never the count of what was fetched (C21). The zero-byte reservation at the final name and its removal on Smoozy's next run are Row 303's (`Desktop app and delivery` C13, which is where the reservation is declared and where its departure from `Server protocol and transfers` S15 is carried), Creator-ruled acceptable 2026-08-18.

**The count and the inventory are `Job files and outputs` §7's.** One entry per expected file with the predicted path, the frame, the mapped folder and the destination or destinations aiming at it (I14); the count is the number of those entries and nothing derives a count a second way; the count is of files expected on disk, not writes asked for (I13); the one folder to watch is the job output folder, which holds nothing else (L7); a video destination is one entry marked *may grow* with no per-frame entries (I19–I21); the added write places of its 5.5 are carried as folders with no per-file entries and in no count; the inventory is a statement about the machine and never about the user's computer (N14).

**The pool and the order are `File Browser` §6's.** The per-server pool is four transfer channels, one pool serving both directions (O1); a per-direction ceiling structure sits inside it and the download-direction ceiling is Row 206's (O2); the five declared classes in one order with auto-download and manual download at the top (O4); the share rule with its 2N−1 bound (O5); the desktop-side point holds the same order and the same share across servers (O8).

**The job events are `Queue & History`'s.** A job's render-start and its ending, each journalled on the machine and carried here when that document learns it — live, or at its reconciliation step 3 — with the ending named as one of its four (*finished*, *failed*, *stopped*, *given up*, its E2); what happened while Smoozy was away, handed over once per connection from the same applied events as the catch-up notice (C1); a re-run is the same job told once, its attempts distinguishable by attempt number.

**The connection is `Servers`'.** A repaired connection is a **replacement** and what resumes on it is `Server protocol and transfers` C11 step 4's — interrupted rather than stopped, within the current application run; a connect after a disconnect is **period-opening** and nothing resumes on it; a removal takes staged download material held against the row with it or records it as an owed cleanup (S28's third bullet); the connection condition Servers supplies is what a download reads when it says it stopped because the connection went. Its standing repair — eight attempts per run, 15-second pauses measured from the end of the previous attempt, a 60-second standing pause between runs, never ending while the app is open (its N2, N6) — is the whole cadence for server-side trouble, and nothing here adds one.

**The reading formats are narrowed from is `Render Settings`'.** One reading at fixed moments — present after a pre-load, present from render start otherwise, never before (its Q4, Y7) — so an arrangement made at a submission with no read is made across everything, and the narrowing material exists from the moment the machine-side reading lands.

**Auto-download's moment is `Rendering`'s.** It is switched on at the moment of submitting; the arrangement is this document's entire.

**The stores and lifetimes are `State, identity and recovery`'s.** The local record is the only durable store on the user's side (§1.1), and its inventory already names *transfer arrangements*; §11.1's five classes list transfer arrangements as **user-held**; §3's D1 already names an auto-download arrangement's desired state as the local record's and its observed value as *the transfers that have run*; the partition unit is declared by §9.2's three ordered tests and never judged at run time; nothing reads a file's presence, name or size as state (§1.3 rule 5).

**The frozen machine evidence binds as follows and none of it is softened below.** A killed or dropped copy leaves bytes at the final name indistinguishable from a whole file, and an interrupted overwrite left a 40,000,000-byte hybrid — *"Neither version, plausible size, no marker"* (Fact 18). The process-watching route to finished-file detection returns *"held by nobody"* across a user boundary from 18 MB to 1.6 GB — the same answer it gives for a finished file — with two related tools returning exit 0 and no rows (Fact 20). Size stability is a trap: a frozen transfer sat at 17,233,920 bytes for 11.1 seconds while unfinished (Fact 20). *"a file of size zero is indistinguishable between 'still being written', 'the disk filled' and 'the render wrote nothing'"* (Fact 28). Seventeen names written on the rental came back as fourteen files on the Mac, with every fetch reporting success (Fact 29 / `Job files and outputs` N14). A directory deleted underneath an open descriptor reports success at write, `fsync` and `close` (Fact 23). Free space on a genuinely full volume read 1,068 KB, never zero (Fact 23). Partial listing output is delivered before a connection dies, separable from a genuinely short listing only by status (Fact 7). ## 2. The arrangement and its record (Rows 212 and 217)

### 2.1 What an arrangement is

**R1. An auto-download arrangement is one durable record in the local record, held against the local job record, and `State, identity and recovery` §1.1's inventory already names *transfer arrangements*.** It is created when auto-download is switched on — at submission (`Rendering`'s moment) or afterwards from the queue entry (`Queue & History`'s toggle) — and it holds:

- the **job identity** and the **server row** the job belongs to;
- the **destination**: the folder chosen or accepted when the arrangement was made, fixed for the life of the job (the spec's own rule — nothing can be asked of a user who is not there), and moved only by the user switching the arrangement off and on again, which resets it and asks once more (R11);
- the **narrowing**: a set of chosen formats, or the declared value *everything*, which is no filter rather than a frozen list ([6.2](#62-narrowing-applied-late-row-213s-second-clause));
- the **on/off state**, which is the arrangement's durable desired state under `State, identity and recovery` §3 D1;
- the **per-file reckoning** of [2.2](#22-the-per-file-reckoning-row-212), each entry carrying **the destination in force when its own act was taken** — so a *fetched* entry's landing path stays resolvable after a switch-on has reset the destination, which is what R6's first kind needs to match anything at all once a job's outputs are split between two folders.

**R2. The arrangement is an intention and its own acts, never a copy of the machine's queue.** Nothing in it is an observed machine value except the per-fetch identity readings of R4, which carry their moment, their clock and the installation identity they were read under, per §3 D5 and §2.3. `Queue & History`'s rule that Smoozy keeps no lasting copy of a machine's state is kept by construction.

**R3. The arrangement's lifetime is user-held, riding the job's entry.** It is removed when the user removes the job's entry, and never by age or by a sweep. A cancelled job's provisional record removal takes the arrangement with it, which is the spec's *a cancelled job takes its arrangement with it*. A removed server ends the arrangement **permanently** — the spec's own sentence — meaning no further fetch is ever arranged; the record itself stays readable as part of the entry's account until the entry goes. **Switching the arrangement off ends the arranging of new fetches from that moment, removes nothing, and lets every transfer already arranged run to its ending** — the spec's own rule, Creator-stated 2026-07-22, because only a set number run at once, so that is a few files rather than many, and cutting them off would throw away work already half done. It is the arranging that stops and not the fetching: a transfer Smoozy stopped here would be written *stopped* under M11, its staged bytes removed at once by S26's second row, and nothing would re-arrange it, because the arrangement is off.

### 2.2 The per-file reckoning (Row 212)

**R4. The reckoning holds one entry per file the arrangement has acted on, keyed by the file's predicted path relative to the job output folder — the inventory's own key, and nothing else. The attempt is not in the key**, and the fetch decision consults the reckoning by predicted path alone: a re-rendered file arriving at a name the reckoning already holds is told under R7 and is never fetched over the user's copy. An attempt number could not be carried honestly in any case — whether two attempts touched one name is knowable only once the second one does, and nothing retro-labels an entry written when there was one attempt. Each entry is split into two halves with two standings:

- **the account of Smoozy's own act** — that a fetch was arranged (the transfer identity), and its terminal state: *fetched*, *left alone*, *cannot land*, *skipped by narrowing*, or *never coming*. This half is Smoozy's own history, durable, and never disqualified by anything the machine does;
- **the server-file identity the fetch was arranged against** — S13's four values (size, inode, modification time, change time), read when the fetch was arranged, carried with its moment and the installation identity it was read under. This half is a machine-derived value: on an installation-identity change it is disqualified under §2.3 while the account half stands.

**Which of the five terminal states re-open, by what event, and which are final.** Stated here because *terminal* alone leaves a builder to guess in either direction:

- ***fetched* — final.** R9, and nothing re-opens it.
- ***cannot land* — final.** P9 says so: the name check has already refused the name and nothing later makes it legal.
- ***left alone* — final, and it is said rather than left to be inferred.** P6's amber line names those files to the user, and the natural next act is to clear them; a user who does that is owed the sentence saying nothing more will happen, because the arrangement does not fetch over a name it has already passed over.
- ***skipped by narrowing* — re-opened to *owed*** where the narrowing is widened, or reset to everything by a switch-off-and-on (N3), with the whole recomputed as for a narrowing applied late (N7). That is what keeps the spec's *"everything written while the arrangement was off comes back"* and *"changing their mind about the narrowing costs the user nothing"*; without it the arrangement would stand permanently short by exactly the files the user has just asked to have back, and silently, since a *skipped by narrowing* unit is out and silently correct.
- ***never coming* — re-opened to *owed*** for the frames a new attempt's list names, on the render-start that re-arms the arrangement (W3, E1). A frame absent at one attempt's ending walk is the frame the next attempt renders.

**R5. The reckoning answers *what have I taken*, and only the walk answers *what is there*.** No doneness judgement of [Section 4](#4-telling-a-finished-file-from-one-still-being-written-rows-211-and-203) is ever written durably: a judgement is a derivation from readings, recomputed per walk, volatile per §1.3 rule 5. What is durable is what Smoozy did and the readings its acts were keyed to. So a fresh run re-walks, re-derives what is owed, and reads the reckoning for what is already taken — and a crash loses at most a judgement that the next walk re-derives. *(**Added 2026-10-31, the twenty-sixth notes, note 4: that the ending walk completed is written down**, with the arrangement's start it completed for. It is E1 step 2's act — something Smoozy did — not a judgement. **A fresh run that finds it for the arrangement's current start, with every unit terminal, does not walk the folder again and reads the arrangement done**; where a unit is still owed, the fresh run walks once more, as before. A switch-on gives a new start and so a new walk; a re-arm re-opens the window as W3 says, and the next ending walk writes it again. The reason is the Creator's: *"I then open Smoozy and transfers are in a slight disarray, because some jobs appear to be duplicates."* Every ended auto-download came back as *downloading* after a restart, and a first-frame check's ending walk ran again. No auditor has seen it.)*

**R6. Three kinds of file can stand at one destination name, and the reckoning names all three.**

1. **A file this arrangement fetched** — a reckoning entry whose account says *fetched*, matched by byte-equal landing path, or through the stored-name outcome of `Desktop app and delivery` C15/C19 where the receiving filesystem folds two spellings this document holds as distinct.
2. **A file it did not put there** — any entry at the destination named by no reckoning entry and no reservation record. It is left alone and said ([5.3](#53-publication-and-what-a-refusal-there-means-rows-205-212-and-208)).
3. **Smoozy's own zero-byte reservation** — a final path named by a transfer record standing in *publication pending* (`Server protocol and transfers` S15), created by the exclusive create of `Desktop app and delivery` C10 and completed or removed on Smoozy's next run by that document's C13, which is where the reservation and its removal are declared. It is never announced under the second kind's promise — announcing it there would be Smoozy telling the user it left a file behind that Smoozy itself put there. Every destination enumeration in this document **subtracts the paths those records name** before anything is classified.

This is the same shape as `File Browser` Row 197's record on the upload side: records name Smoozy's own material, and the residual class is the world's.

**And the ordering that removal has to hold is this document's own requirement, stated here because no cited clause states it.** `Desktop app and delivery` C13 has the next run complete or remove the reservation and carries no ordering of any kind relative to admitting transfers — the words *admit* and *admission* do not occur in that document's reservation clauses at all; and `Server protocol and transfers` S28's second bullet orders its own desktop-side sweep at application start, which is the right shape on different material and a different act. So it is declared here: **no transfer of this document is admitted until every publication-pending record has been completed or its reservation removed.** That is what lets P6's third bullet say the reservation cannot be met at fetch time, and it runs beside S28's start sweep rather than inside it.

**R7. A file the arrangement fetched and the machine has since re-written is a fourth telling, and it is told rather than acted on.** Where a walk shows change at a path whose account says *fetched* — a re-run's attempt re-rendering the last frame its reckoning counted as already there is the ordinary case — the four values are re-read and compared with R4's recorded ones. Where they differ, the entry is marked **newer version on the server, not fetched over the local copy**, and that is said once on the arrangement with the files named. The local copy is **not** replaced: *local files are never silently overwritten* covers a file the user may already be compositing with, whoever put it there, and the arrangement never asks the replace question at all. The newer version stays on the server and is fetchable by hand, where the manual question is asked. Presentation rides the same per-request telling surface as *left alone*; wording is Design's. [Section 16](#16-moments-that-reached-the-creator) records the escalation check.

### 2.3 What the record must not do

**R8. The reckoning never asks the already-exists question.** The fetch decision is the reckoning's (*what have I taken*), never a comparison against the destination — the spec's own words: *"each time it is answering what is here that I have not taken yet, never shall I replace this."* The destination is consulted exactly once per file, by the exclusive create at publication ([5.3](#53-publication-and-what-a-refusal-there-means-rows-205-212-and-208)), which is the adapter's act and not a comparison.

**R9. A file whose account says *fetched* is never fetched again**, whatever has become of the local copy. The user deleting what came home is their own act; *what has already come home stays* is a promise about Smoozy not taking files back, not a promise to re-supply them; the manual whole-job download is the recovery route. The one exception is R7's told-not-acted case, which fetches nothing either.

**R10. Whether the arrangement recognises files the user downloaded by hand from the same job is a product decision and is not settled here** — the spec's own parenthesis. Such files carry no reckoning entry and land in R6's second kind, warned about as the spec describes.

### 2.4 What the record survives (Row 217)

**R11. The record survives the four things the spec states across its sections, and one more, by being one durable record in the one local record:**

- **a drop and a repair** — nothing about the record is connection-held; the arrangement's in-flight transfers follow `Server protocol and transfers` C11 step 4 on a replacement ([8.5](#85-the-partial-the-resume-and-the-close-rows-208-and-209));
- **the app closing and reopening** — `State, identity and recovery` §3 D6 makes a crash and a clean close leave the same record; the arrangement's *activity* is run-held and dies with the process, the record does not *(save that the ending walk completed, which is written down from 2026-10-31 — R5's dated note; the twenty-sixth notes, note 4; no auditor has seen it)*;
- **the arrangement switched off and on again** — off flips the desired state, removes nothing, and lets every transfer already arranged run to its ending (R3); on again resets destination and narrowing (two fields rewritten, Creator-stated) and keeps the per-file reckoning, which is what makes *switching it on again catches up* answer *what is here that I have not taken yet* correctly. Two things follow from the reset and both are stated rather than left to a builder. **The narrowing reset to everything re-opens the entries it had put out** as *skipped by narrowing*, to *owed*, with the whole recomputed ([2.2](#22-the-per-file-reckoning-row-212)). **And where the destination chosen at the switch-on differs from the one the arrangement previously held, and the reckoning already carries entries whose account says *fetched*, the switch-on says so before it proceeds**: what is already downloaded stays where it is, and only what has not yet arrived comes to the new folder. That telling is the spec's own, Creator-stated 2026-08-20 — the user may move the folder part-way through, and Smoozy says what it costs at the moment it costs it, and shows both places afterwards (E1). Wording and placement are Design's;
- **a re-run writing into the same folder** — the reckoning is consulted by predicted path and ignores the attempt (R4), so R7 handles every file an attempt re-writes and nothing is fetched over the user's copy; and the new render-start re-arms the arrangement, re-opening its *never coming* entries for the frames the new attempt's list names (W3, E1);
- **the reservation record** — `Server protocol and transfers` S9/S15 records live in the same local record and survive the same close, so the removal owed to Smoozy's *next* run has a record to read. That record is S9's and is not duplicated here.

**R12. The arrangement resumes on the next connection to its server, whichever label the connection carries, and this is the declaration `Server protocol and transfers` C13 leaves to this document.** That C13: *"`File Browser` and `Download` own which of their arrangements survive a run."* The declaration: **an auto-download arrangement whose desired state is on survives every application run and resumes on any connection to its server — replacement or period-opening** — because the spec states it outright (*"auto-download resumes on the next connection — because it is an arrangement attached to a job, not a request made at a moment"*, Creator-stated) and because the spec has already answered the explicit-connect objection: the arrangement is work the user already asked for, and the connection it resumes on is one the user opened. What resumes is the **arrangement** — its walks and its fresh fetches. Its individual **transfers** follow the ordinary rules unchanged: an interrupted transfer of the current run resumes on a replacement under `Server protocol and transfers` C11 step 4; a record written by an earlier run is never resumed automatically, and is taken up only as [8.5](#85-the-partial-the-resume-and-the-close-rows-208-and-209) M13 and M14 declare. A manual request has no such declaration and ends with the run ([8.5](#85-the-partial-the-resume-and-the-close-rows-208-and-209)).

## 3. The watch (Row 210)

**W1. The watch is a periodic walk of the job output folder over the file-transfer subsystem's reading route, compared against the inventory. No event mechanism, no machine-side program and no worker kind exists for it.** **It is not a standing timer**: its pause sits inside a running sequence bounded by W3's window, which is what `Desktop app and delivery` P10b admits, and P10b's rule rather than any list of examples is what it is judged against *(said 2026-08-23, routed item 30)*. The reasons, each observed:

- **One mechanism serves both halves of the promise.** A walk answers *what is there now*, so the first walk of a connection is the catch-up reading and every later walk is the live watch. An event route cannot answer for a file that landed before it started listening, so it would need the walk anyway.
- **The subsystem route takes the protocol's own fields** — kind, size, modification time, name, batched at about 100 entries per reply, 52 round trips for 5010 entries (Fact 7) — so no text is mined and a name holding a newline cannot split a reading (`Job files and outputs` N15).
- **It needs no program on the machine.** What programs an arbitrary rental carries and what they emit is unestablished ground (`File Browser` §13 item 9, `Server protocol and transfers` §12 item 3), and the one event tool the evidence describes follows inodes — *"a watch on a file follows the inode, so a save-by-rename kills it silently, and a replaced folder gives one ignored event and then nothing"* (Fact 19).
- **Its failures separate.** A refusal, an absence and a dead machine carry distinct protocol statuses at readdir (Fact 7), and a walk cut mid-listing is separable from a complete short one by status — so **a walk that did not complete is not a reading and is discarded whole** (`Job files and outputs` C9's rule, applied to this walk).
- **The cost is the connection's.** Fact 7's subsystem listing cost 18.22 seconds of wire time for 5010 entries on a loaded box and a fraction of that for ordinary folders. **No measured figure is offered for what the machine spends, because none exists for this route: Fact 19's 0.08 seconds of processor over 55 is withdrawn from this bullet, corrected 2026-08-23.** That measurement is of a machine-side event watcher — the route the bullet above rejects — and this walk plants no program to measure. What the machine is spared is structural rather than measured: nothing is installed, nothing is run, and no worker kind is declared or wanted ([Section 12](#12-declarations)).

**W2. The cadence: a fresh walk begins after a pause of 10 seconds, or the previous walk's own duration where that was longer, measured from the end of the previous walk.** Three constraints fix the shape and the number:

- measured **from the end**, never the start, so walks never overlap and a slow walk cannot pile requests onto a struggling connection — the same load-bearing shape as `Servers` N3;
- **10 seconds is short against a frame**: the one frame cost on record is about 16.8 seconds (Fact 55), and real frames run longer, so per-frame arrival — *outputs come home as they are written* — holds at this cadence;
- **the previous-duration floor bounds the duty cycle at one half, per arrangement**, on a folder large enough that the walk itself is slow. That is arithmetic about **one** walk series, and it is not by itself a bound on this document: W3 permits one series per arrangement and several arrangements per server, while `Server protocol and transfers` §1.1 item 2 gives a connection **one** long-lived reading channel to carry listings, attribute reads and free-space readings, the user's own among them (admitted under T5). Two arrangements would exhaust a per-series half and three would oversubscribe it. **So the aggregate is bounded in this document's own cadence, which is this document's to declare: all of a server's walks take at most one half of that channel between them, each arrangement's pause stretching as their number grows — to twice their number less one, times the pause the first sentence sets** — so two arrangements walk at three times that pause, three at five times, and the channel is never held for more than half its time by this document whatever the number. **The multiplier is corrected 2026-09-05, out of the build of this section's rules half; the bound is unchanged.** It read *two arrangements walk at twice the pause, three at three times* — a multiplier of the number itself, which does not deliver the half it is illustrating: at twice the pause each arrangement holds the channel a third of the time, so two hold two thirds; at three times each holds a quarter, so three hold three quarters. **At twice the number less one the aggregate is exactly one half at every number** — two at three times, three at five, four at seven — **and at one arrangement it reduces to this bullet's own first clause**, the pause being the walk's own duration and the duty cycle one half. **The bound was kept and the illustration moved**, because the half is what this bullet reasons from — the sentence above it rules two arrangements out of a per-series half — and it is what [W3](#3-the-watch-row-210) and `Desktop app and delivery` §3.3's own paragraph cite by name, while the multiplier stood in one illustration with nothing resting on it. What order the walks and a user's own interactive listing take **inside** the channel is not supplied anywhere and is not declared here; it is that document's and is carried at [Section 15](#15-unresolved).

It is a declared constant, not a tuning left open.

**W3. Walks run only while they can mean something**: while the arrangement's desired state is on, a connection to the server stands, and the job is between **a** render-start and the completion of the ending walk of [7](#7-what-ends-an-arrangement-row-220). *(From 2026-10-31 that completion survives a restart where it is written down and every unit is terminal — R5's dated note; the twenty-sixth notes, note 4; no auditor has seen it.)* Before the first render-start there is nothing to watch. The window closes at the ending walk because the arrangement's own bookkeeping for that attempt is finished — **not because the folder can never change again, which is a thing this document does not know and `Job files and outputs` L7 does not say**: L7 says only that nothing but what a render writes ever sits inside a job output folder, which is a statement about what may be there rather than about when, and that document's own I22 says the opposite in terms — *"Running the job again replaces what was left rather than adding to it."*

**So the window is per attempt and not per job, and a new render-start for the same job re-arms a done arrangement.** It opens a fresh window and the walks resume; the reckoning's *never coming* entries re-open to *owed* for the frames the new attempt's list names ([2.2](#22-the-per-file-reckoning-row-212)); the whole is recomputed (N6); and the arrangement runs to a second ending and a second done (E1). Nothing else re-opens: what is *fetched* stays fetched and is never fetched again (R9), which is what keeps a re-run from arriving over the user's copies. One arrangement is one walk series over one folder; several arrangements on one server share the reading channel serially, inside W2's aggregate bound.

**W4. The walk is one walk of the folder and everything under it** — `Job files and outputs` C7's shape, because the redirect deepens paths by construction and a single directory's entries would miss the inventory's predicted paths. Each entry comes back as its path relative to the job output folder, its kind, its size and its modification time.

**W5. What a walk yields is a reading and only a reading.** It is period-held, carries its moment, and is never written as state (R5). Change detection is the comparison of two complete walks: an entry is **active** where it is new or where its size or modification time differs from the previous complete walk. A walk failure — a refusal, a cut, the connection going — discards that walk; the next one runs at the cadence, and the connection condition covers the dead-machine case. A refusal reading the job's own folder is reported as the truth (this document could not read the job's folder, with the separated cause where the route separates it) and the walks continue; nothing gives up.

**W6. Nothing is planted, renamed, opened or changed on the machine to make watching easier.** The spec forbids it outright; the rename lever Fact 20 found is closed off by that; and *downloading never changes anything on the machine* covers the watch as much as the fetch.

## 4. Telling a finished file from one still being written (Rows 211 and 203)

**The spec's promise is already narrowed and this section does not widen it back:** *"Auto-download is not a check run on the user's behalf and is not written as one — it fetches what looks finished, and says what it could not vouch for"* (Creator-stated, 2026-08-12). Nothing below claims the arrangement can always tell. What it can tell, it tells by two mechanisms; what it cannot, it says through the count and the machine's own printed lines, which is where the spec puts the saying.

**What is closed off, before anything is built:** opening the file (rejected by the Creator, 2026-08-12 — and a stopped TIFF opens and reports the full picture size); changing anything on the machine (the spec's own rule); keying on a size — **with exactly one exception, stated at J1 and nowhere widened: a file of zero bytes is never a finished frame. No other reading of a size decides anything in this document** (`What a job writes` says outright that nothing may, and a stopped file runs from nothing to tens of thousands of bytes with no pattern), so no plausibility floor, no minimum-bytes-for-this-format test and no has-it-stopped-growing check follows from the zero-byte rule; watching the writing process (Fact 20 — *"held by nobody"* across a user boundary from 18 MB to 1.6 GB, the same answer as for a finished file, exit 0, no rows); and size stability (17,233,920 bytes for 11.1 seconds while unfinished).

### 4.1 The ground both mechanisms stand on

**J1. Three supplied facts, none of them a property of the file itself:**

- **The inventory names every file the job is expected to leave, with its frame** (I14) — so every walk entry that matches an inventory entry has a frame.
- **The render works through its frames in a known order, and writes each frame's files as that frame finishes** — a plain range ascending, a stepped range by the step, a typed list in the order written (`What a job writes`, observed in all four versions on both machines); a node writes its files as each frame finishes, never in one lot at the end; and with placeholders on, *"the empty file for the next frame appears at the moment the previous frame's picture is complete"* (`Preparation/Blender Research/Comparison/Writing.md` question 11, finding 5). **Re-quoted 2026-08-29 at the re-gate.** This bullet read *"at the instant the previous frame's picture completes"* with the research file's name after it; that string is in no research file — it is this project's own paraphrase, carried here from the row file — and a Blender fact stated in words the research does not use is the one thing this project's Blender rule exists to prevent. The substance is unchanged and nothing that rests on it moves.
- **A file of zero bytes is never a finished frame and is never brought down** — Creator-stated, 2026-08-12, and the spec's own rule. This is the one exception the section's preamble makes to the size closure, and it is not widened anywhere.

**What both mechanisms then rest on is the ordering between one frame's writes and the next frame's first write: the first write event belonging to a later frame comes after every write of the earlier frame is complete, so a later frame's activity is evidence about an earlier frame.** **REWRITTEN 2026-09-02, out of the repaired Blender research, and for the same reason [Section 15](#15-unresolved) item 17 was rewritten on 2026-09-01.** This paragraph said *"No research answer establishes it"* and *"nothing tested for one"*, and rested that on two quotations from `Preparation/Blender Research/Across topics/While a render runs.md` under the annotation *(Both re-quoted 2026-08-29 at the re-gate … No word moved.)*. **The first of the two no longer exists in that file at all** — the sentence about a short-lived file falling between two samples is gone, repaired underneath this clause on 2026-08-30, so the annotation was true of a file that has since moved. **And the claim itself is now false:** the research answers this question outright.

**What the source says, re-quoted against the files as they stand on 2026-09-02.** *Writing 14* asks this exact question and returns a verdict: *"Every write belonging to one frame is finished before any write belonging to the next frame begins."* — settled by test in all four versions, on the Creator's Mac and on a rented Linux machine, with no window and again with a window (`Preparation/Blender Research/Comparison/Writing.md`, question 14). **Two instruments produced it, and that question insists on the split: *"Two instruments were used and they are two separate claims. Do not let one carry the other."*** The folder was watched at intervals on both machines, the write made slow on purpose and the folder read every two milliseconds, *"Sixty-three separate readings fell inside one write on the Mac and thirty-one on Linux"* — fine enough to have shown an overlap of a few milliseconds, **and that half is what settles macOS**. And every file call was traced thread by thread on the rented Linux machine alone, which *"settles Linux and says nothing whatever about macOS"* (same question; the joined answer `While a render runs` carries the same split in its own shorter words). **The neighbouring result the trace settles outright, which this section leans on elsewhere:** *"there is no rename call anywhere in it"*, and every file is written under its final name from the first byte — so no temporary name is ever mistakable for a finished frame.

**What the sampled half still cannot show, and it is the one limit this paragraph was originally written about.** *"The size a part-written file was first caught at is a property of how often the folder was looked at, not of Blender."* (`Across topics/While a render runs.md`, on *Writing 11*; this quotation is unmoved since the 2026-08-29 re-gate and re-checked 2026-09-02.) A rule about the instants between two samples is not something a sampled folder can show, and the Mac's half is sampled. **So the ordering is observed on Linux by the stronger instrument and observed on macOS by the weaker one, and the residue is macOS alone.** What breaks where it fails, and the question in the shape the research would need, are [Section 15](#15-unresolved) item 17's — which also records that the macOS residue is **permanently unresolved** rather than pending.

**J2. The order is the attempt's own ordered frame list, for every attempt, and it is read rather than guessed.** `Job files and outputs` M8 writes one attempt record before each render starts, holding **the frame list handed to the render in the order it was written** — attempt 1 included — so attempt 1 is not a special case and needs no rule of its own. **The list is read from the attempt record for every attempt, attempt 1 among them**: over the connection, from the job's record directory, a reading with its moment, consumed from `Job files and outputs`' attempt records (its 3.4) on the route that document supplies by name.

**The composition is the machine's and never the desktop's. Corrected 2026-08-22, out of the scenario replays' Pile 1 item R20**, where this clause read *"For attempt 1 the list is already on the desktop, which composed and wrote it"* — and the desktop composes no frame list at all, for any attempt. The reckoning that composes one is a step of the render worker and runs on the machine (`Job files and outputs` C1, its integers-only rule C3), it runs at `Rendering` P1 step 6 for every attempt, and M8 writes what it handed the render. What the desktop composes and sends is the manifest's **frame range** (`Job files and outputs` M1, M1a), which is not an order: the two coincide only for a first attempt whose range is plain and ascending, and the paragraph below — *A range is never the order* — is what substituting one for the other costs. So nothing here reads a desktop-side value where the machine's attempt record is the authority, and J2's own fallback covers attempt 1 exactly as it covers the rest.

**A range is never the order.** A typed list is rendered in the order it was written and not sorted (`What a job writes`), and `Render Settings` holds a list as an alternative to the first, last and step rather than as a form of them — so a list written `50, 10, 20` is that order, and reading a three-number range in its place would put the frontier at 50 while Blender wrote 50 first, judging frames 10 and 20 finished before they were rendered. Nothing re-sorts, re-derives or infers the order from a range.

**Where the ordered list cannot be read — for any attempt — mechanism one does not run for that attempt and mechanism two alone decides.** Nothing is vouched for on a guessed order.

### 4.2 Mechanism one — the live judgement, for files written while the arrangement watches

**J3. A frame is judged finished when a write event belonging to a strictly later frame in the attempt's order is observed, or when the job's ending arrives.** Concretely: walks classify active entries by their inventory frame (W5, J1); **the live frontier is the highest frame in the attempt's order with activity in the current walk**, and every frame strictly earlier than it in that order is judged finished. **It is recomputed at every walk and it advances**: a frame that is the frontier at one walk is judged finished at the next walk showing a later frame active, and the judgement is a per-walk derivation like every other (R5).

**The live frontier is mechanism one's and is not mechanism two's baseline frontier (J6), which is a different rule with a different shape.** This one reads the current walk alone and rises with the render; that one is cumulative over a connection, fires once, and never rises. Mixing them freezes the boundary at the first frame that showed activity after a connection's baseline and leaves every later frame to the ending walk — a six-hour render delivering a handful of frames live and the rest in one lot, with nothing erroring and no surface showing it.

A frame judged finished is fetchable where every one of its expected files is present and none is zero bytes — a zero-byte file in a judged-finished frame is an emptied or refused write and goes to *never coming* at the ending rather than being fetched.

**J4. What the mechanism deliberately tolerates:** with placeholders on, the next frame's placeholder is itself the completion signal for the frame before it, at sub-second latency; with placeholders off, the signal is the next frame's first real write, so a frame is confirmed about one frame-render later. **The sub-second path is the exception rather than the ordinary case, and the reason is stated so nobody plans on it.** The Placeholders setting is off out of the box (`Preparation/Blender Research/Comparison/Writing.md`, documented in all four versions), and it is the **scene's own output alone**: it does not reach a file output node, and a two-frame animation with placeholders on *"produced the node's files and the scene's files, and no placeholder for the node"* (`Preparation/Blender Research/Comparison/File output nodes.md` question 10, all four versions on both machines). So a job whose frames are written by file output nodes alone has no placeholder path at all and its signal is always the next frame's first real write. Both are inside the promise — files come home as they are written, at worst one frame behind.

**J5. The last frame of an attempt has no later frame, so its signal is the job's ending** — one of the four, from `Queue & History`, live or at catch-up. This is also the whole of what mechanism one claims: it never claims the file itself proved anything, and a file a full disk truncated at its final name passes both mechanisms and is fetched — which is the case the Creator's narrowing exists for, and the saying is the count and the machine's printed line.

### 4.3 Mechanism two — the catch-up judgement, for files finished before the arrangement was watching

**J6. On every (re)connection, and when an arrangement is switched on mid-render, the first complete walk is the baseline and judges nothing by itself.** A file already present gives no event and carries no evidence — presence is not proof (Fact 18), stability is not proof (Fact 20). What decides is one of two later signals:

- **the job's ending is on record or arrives** — by the live event, or by the catch-up handed over once per connection (`Queue & History` C1). From that moment nothing more is being written, and every expected file that is present and not zero bytes is fetchable. This is the *"picks up everything that landed while they were away"* half and the ended-job half of *"switching it on mid-render brings back what has already been written"*;
- **the job is still rendering, and activity is observed** — **the baseline frontier is the earliest frame in the attempt's order with activity observed since this connection's baseline; the first time one exists, every frame strictly earlier than it in that order is judged finished, once.** It is cumulative and one-time by design, which is what a catch-up needs and what mechanism one's live frontier (J3) must never be confused with: the since-baseline set only grows and its earliest member never rises, so this rule is right exactly once and wrong as a standing frontier. So switching on forty frames in vouches for frames 1–40 the moment frame 41's first write is seen — and for frame 41 as well where what is seen is frame 42's placeholder, which J1 puts at the instant frame 41's picture completes — arriving within about one frame's render time. After that one judgement, mechanism one's live frontier carries the rest of the render. Until a signal arrives, nothing is vouched for and nothing is fetched — waiting is the honest state and it is bounded by the frame in hand.

**A current-connection worker report can supply the saved prefix without waiting for another write. Added 2026-10-23, the Creator’s seventeenth notes, note 14; no auditor has seen it.** `Queue & History` P1a already defines `AWorkersReport.newest_frame_saved` as the completed prefix of the attempt’s own ordered frame list. The desktop may use that same report, from a queue reading taken on this connection, for a rendering job whose report’s attempt matches the attempt whose order and inventory the arrangement read. A complete walk makes the expected files of that prefix, through the named frame inclusive, fetchable where all are present and none is zero bytes (J3). This applies at the first complete walk as well as later ones: the baseline alone still proves nothing. A report from an earlier connection or attempt, an unreadable order, or a saved frame absent from that order supplies no judgement. No new queue-reading sequence is added; where no suitable report stands, J6’s two signals still decide. Walks retain W2’s cadence and its one reading channel; a report queues no fetch without a complete walk. This closes the plan’s recovery delay: J6 alone can hold already saved frames for the duration of the frame in hand even when the server already reported their saves.

**J7. The two mechanisms together keep the catch-up promise as the spec narrowed it.** Mechanism one keeps *outputs come home as they are written*; mechanism two keeps *switching it on mid-render brings back what has already been written* and *picks up everything that landed while they were away*. Neither vouches for a file it has no signal for, and what neither can vouch for is said on the job's entry through the count and the printed lines — the promise judged **kept as narrowed**, and [Section 16](#16-moments-that-reached-the-creator) records the check against the second stop.

### 4.4 The files with no frame

**J8. Files under an added write place's mapped folder, files matching no current-attempt inventory entry, and any inventory entry carrying no frame — a video destination's entry among them — are judged by the ending alone.** They have no frame and no order, so mechanism one cannot reach them; they are fetched at the ending walk, never live.

**A video destination's entry is never fetchable before the ending walk, whatever its size reads.** It matches an inventory entry, so the unmatched-file rule above does not reach it, and it carries no frame (`Job files and outputs` I20), so J3 cannot judge it — and the only fetchability test outside mechanism one is E1 step 2's *present and not zero bytes*, which it passes from the first moments: I21 has the file appearing within moments at a token size and holding that size until the file is closed. Fetched on that reading it would be published as the whole animation, recorded *fetched*, made un-refetchable by R9, and the arrangement would read complete over a one-second stub — one entry (N6) with one arrival. So it is said outright rather than left to the one test available. A bake, a cache, or an earlier attempt's differently-arranged output is still the job's output and still comes home — outside every count (I14), reported separately ([6.4](#64-the-count-and-the-whole-row-215)), and **under every arrangement, narrowed or not** ([N4](#62-narrowing-applied-late-row-213s-second-clause), corrected 2026-08-21 against Row 314; this clause read *skipped entirely by a narrowed arrangement* and contradicted two specs outright).

### 4.5 What the manual half borrows (Row 203's reading)

**J9. A manual request has no arrangement, no reckoning and usually no inventory in hand, so its vouching reading is deliberately thinner — and it is stated as what it is.** At enumeration, a file is marked **cannot be vouched for** where it lies inside the job output folder of a job whose local record shows **no recorded ending**, or **a recorded ending of *stopped*, *failed* or *given up*** — `Queue & History` E2's four endings, read off the same local job records the live-set reading `File Browser` W5 already takes, with no read of the machine. **A zero-byte file inside a job output folder is marked the same way whatever the ending is**, which is J1's one exception to this section's size closure and the reading Fact 28 leaves ambiguous three ways. Outside a job's output folder, or for a job whose recorded ending is *finished*, Smoozy has no standing to judge and makes no claim — which is exactly the spec's *"this reaches exactly as far as what Smoozy can actually judge."*

**J9a. The key is the ending's kind and not an ending's absence, and the mark is the whole folder and not E1 step 2's frontier. Corrected 2026-08-22, out of the scenario replays' Pile 1 item R14.**

- **What the old key dropped.** This clause read *no recorded ending* alone, so the moment a stop, a failure or a give-up was recorded the mark went out entirely and the by-hand route made no claim about anything in that folder — while the same torn frame under an arrangement is named by E1 step 2, which keys on the ending's kind. [The spec names both cases in one sentence](../Specs/Download.md) — *"a render still working on it, or a write that ran out of room"* — and the old key kept only the first. ***Finished* stays unmarked**, matching E1 step 2, which marks on the same three endings and not on that one. A job can end *finished* with a frame that ran out of room (`Rented-server runtime` M22: a successful exit status is never read as *everything was written*), and marking every file of every finished job would be a mark that says nothing — which is the promise the spec already narrowed to *"not that it can always tell"*, not a hole to be filled here.
- **Why the mark is wide here and narrow there, and neither is a tuning of the other.** E1 step 2 names the entries of the highest frame in the attempt's order that shows any file, and it can: the arrangement holds the attempt's frame order, the inventory and a complete walk of the whole job output folder. A manual request holds none of the three — J9's own first sentence — and M2's enumeration descends from **what the user picked**, which need not be the whole folder. A frontier computed over part of a folder names the highest frame *shown*, which is an earlier frame than the one that was in flight, so it would mark a whole file and clear the torn one. That is a false claim in the vouching direction rather than a silence, which is the one direction this section refuses everywhere. The wide mark costs a longer list at J10's pre-flight; the narrow one would cost a wrong one, and nothing here reads the machine to buy it.

**J9b. Where a recorded ending arrived carrying `Rendering` K18's per-frame reckoning, the mark is read off that reckoning instead of laid over the whole folder. Added 2026-09-02, scenario replay *The user stops a running job* finding 2.** [J9](#45-what-the-manual-half-borrows-row-203s-reading) rests its width on a manual request having *"no arrangement, no reckoning and usually no inventory in hand"*. **For a job whose ending was recorded, the reckoning is in hand and it is exact**: `Rendering` [K18](Rendering.md#37-a-job-that-ends-short-delivers-what-it-wrote-and-what-which-frames-is-row-310)'s *rendered* and *not rendered* sets — *"a frame is in rendered when every file the inventory expects for it is present and none of them is empty"* — cover the job's whole asked range, are composed once **on the machine at the ending** ([K19](Rendering.md#37-a-job-that-ends-short-delivers-what-it-wrote-and-what-which-frames-is-row-310)) and are carried to the local job record with the recorded outcome. So J9's own first sentence is not true of this case. **[J9a](#45-what-the-manual-half-borrows-row-203s-reading)'s objection is met rather than overruled**: it refuses a frontier *computed over what the user picked*, and nothing here computes one — the reckoning is a statement about the whole range, read off the local record, so the *highest frame shown is an earlier frame than the one in flight* failure cannot arise.

- **The rule.** Where the local record shows a recorded ending of *stopped*, *failed* or *given up* **and carries the statement**, a file inside that job's output folder is marked **cannot be vouched for** where the frame it belongs to is **not** in the *rendered* set, and is unmarked where that frame is in it. Frames are matched by the same attempt inventory the reckoning was composed against, and **no machine is read** — J9's own constraint holds entire, which is what makes this affordable at enumeration.
- **Files with no frame keep the whole-folder mark.** [J8](#44-the-files-with-no-frame)'s three kinds have no frame for the reckoning to speak about, so it says nothing about them and the wide mark is the only honest reading there.
- **Where no statement arrived, nothing moves.** `Rendering` [K22](Rendering.md#37-a-job-that-ends-short-delivers-what-it-wrote-and-what-which-frames-is-row-310)'s two cases — the machine's walk could not be taken, and no ending was ever learned — and **J9's *no recorded ending* key, which is untouched** — all keep J9's whole-folder mark exactly as written. **An outcome carrying no statement is never read as an empty one**, which is K22's own rule.
- **The zero-byte rule stays unconditional.** A zero-byte file inside a job output folder is marked whatever the ending is and whatever the reckoning says; it is J1's exception and this clause does not touch it.
- **What the narrowing costs, stated rather than hidden.** `Rendering` [K21](Rendering.md#37-a-job-that-ends-short-delivers-what-it-wrote-and-what-which-frames-is-row-310) rides with the set — a file short but not empty counts as present, and a write that ran out of room counts as present — **so a frame the reckoning puts in *rendered* can still hold a torn picture, and it is now unmarked where the wide rule marked it.** That is the **same standing the auto route already accepts for the identical ending** ([E1](#7-what-ends-an-arrangement-row-220) step 2 marks the in-flight frame and nothing else), and the same standing every file of a *finished* job already has under J9. **The width costs more than the narrowing does:** marking four whole frames beside one torn one teaches the user the mark means nothing on the one screen where it is meant to mean something, and reaches past the spec's *"this reaches exactly as far as what Smoozy can actually judge"* — which is the sentence J9 cites to bound itself.
- **[J10](#45-what-the-manual-half-borrows-row-203s-reading) is unchanged.** Whatever is marked is named in the request's pre-flight before any transfer starts, and nothing else about the request moves.

**J10. The saying happens first.** The marked files are named in the request's pre-flight, beside the collision question and before any transfer starts; the user's go-ahead covers the request; the files come down when they insist — the machine is theirs. Wording and presentation are Design's. **A file re-written under an uninterrupted fetch is not detected, and the limit is stated rather than covered by a safety net that is not there.** `Server protocol and transfers` S13's four values are compared when a download is arranged and before a resume, so a single uninterrupted pass has no second comparison at all; and the pass-through digest proves only that the bytes read are the bytes written — S25's own words: *"It does not prove the file on the sending disk was whole before Smoozy read it."* A frame is built up in place at its own final name, so a fetch beginning mid-write reads a prefix of one state and a suffix of another, and the digest matches. Where a resume does follow a drop, S13's comparison starts the file over rather than resuming it. Outside the live-set reading of J9 there is no further claim.

## 5. The fetch pipeline

### 5.1 What a fetch is

**P1. A fetch is one file's transfer under `Server protocol and transfers` §7, unmodified**: bound to the server row (T3), its own transfer identity (T4), staged under S4's path in the staging directory inside the destination folder, recorded under S9 before its first byte, integrity-checked under S21–S25, published under S15/S18 through the platform file adapter, ended under S26. The arrangement and the manual request add the layer above: which transfers exist, what class they carry, and what the whole means.

**P2. The fetch order within an arrangement is frame order, earliest judged-finished first**; within a manual request, the enumeration's order (the plain reading that a folder arrives roughly front to back, `File Browser` O7's shape). Admission is by class then arrival at the per-server point, under the pool, the ceiling and the share of [Section 10](#10-the-ceiling-and-the-classes-rows-206-and-207).

**P3. A failed fetch is a recorded unit outcome, and trying again is a new transfer with a new identity** (`State, identity and recovery` §9.5). For an arrangement, a unit that failed for a server-side or transfer reason returns to *owed* and is re-arranged at a later walk cycle — the arrangement is standing work, and each fresh fetch is its own action. For a manual request, a failed unit stays failed with its per-file Try again being the user's act (Design's control).

### 5.2 The name check, before any byte moves (Row 205's first half)

**P4. Every landing path is judged by the adapter's name check before the transfer is arranged** — `Desktop app and delivery` C3, per component, in the receiving system's own unit (C5), against the receiving volume. The check runs at enumeration for a manual request and at the moment a file first becomes fetchable for an arrangement. An outcome of *not representable*, *too long in this system's own unit* or *silently transformed* is **not acceptable as given** and the file is handled under [5.4](#54-the-name-that-cannot-land-row-205).

### 5.3 Publication, and what a refusal there means (Rows 205, 212 and 208)

**P5. Every publication of this document is create-intended: the taken question is the exclusive create of the final name (`Desktop app and delivery` C10), and this document never answers *replace* on its own.** The reservation the create makes is the entry publication renames over — and because it stands there, the publishing rename **always replaces and never lands on an absent name, in every case and not only in C14's** (`Desktop app and delivery` C22); what create-intended governs is what the exclusive create answers, not which operation runs underneath. A crash inside the window leaves the file the publication-pending record names, completed or removed on the next run by `Desktop app and delivery` C13, whose reservation and whose removal these are. The one deliberate replacement is a manual request's user-answered **Replace**, which publishes with the replacing rename and takes no reservation (`Desktop app and delivery` C14) — *Row 303 forbids silence, not replacement.*

**P6. Where the exclusive create fails, the name is taken, and the reckoning says by what — which is Row 212's second promise kept at the only moment it can be kept.**

- **Taken by nothing the record names** → the file is **left alone**: the transfer ends *stopped* (`Server protocol and transfers` S26's second row — the record written before the channel is closed, `Server protocol and transfers` C23), the staged material is removed at once or owed under S27, and the file is said once on the request or arrangement with the list of such files behind it — the spec's *"passes over that one file, carries on with the rest, and tells the user which files it left behind"*, and the design's one amber line with a link naming them. That S26 row is worded *"Stopped, deliberately"* while this is Smoozy stopping rather than the user, which is the wording seam `File Browser` Section 13 item 12 already records; this document takes the same route and carries it at [Section 15](#15-unresolved) rather than writing a second copy of it. The bytes crossed once and were discarded; the down direction is the fast one, and **the cost is one crossing per file the arrangement did not put there — bounded by how much of the job the user fetched by hand and by nothing else.** The alternatives are the already-exists question R8 forbids and a reservation held for the length of every transfer, so the crossing is paid.
- **Taken by this document's own fetch under another spelling** — the stored-name outcome (`Desktop app and delivery` C15/C19) or byte comparison maps the taken name to a *fetched* entry → the file is **cannot land**: the destination treats two of the machine's names as one (the seventeen-to-fourteen loss arriving at this document's door, `File Browser` N2's own routing of it), nothing is overwritten, and the file is said with that cause. On Windows the outcome says *taken* without the stored name (`Desktop app and delivery` C18) and the saying carries no name, which is that limit and not a new one.
- **Taken by a *fetched* entry of this arrangement at the byte-equal landing path** — the same spelling, whatever put the create in flight, and a re-run's second attempt re-rendering a name attempt 1 already brought home is the way it arises → **nothing is written over**: the transfer ends *stopped* with its staged material removed at once or owed under S27, the entry stays *fetched*, and it is said as R7's telling — a newer version on the server, not fetched over the local copy. Because the fetch decision consults the reckoning by predicted path and ignores the attempt (R4), R7 normally reaches this before any transfer is arranged; the bullet exists because the exclusive create is the last word and every way it can fail is owed an answer here rather than left to a builder to invent.
- **Taken by its own reservation** — cannot arise at fetch time: R6's run-start requirement has every publication-pending record completed, or its reservation removed under `Desktop app and delivery` C13, before this document admits any transfer.

*(**Built 2026-10-31, the twenty-sixth notes, note 4.** `Server protocol and transfers` §7 reads a create refused for a taken name as a local failure, so the transfer record ends *failed*, not *stopped*. **The arrangement reads the refusal itself** and writes the entry by this clause — *left alone* where the record names nothing — so the file is not owed again. Before this, a taken name was owed again at every pass and the whole file crossed each time: about 270 MB a minute on one live server. No auditor has seen it.)*

**P7. Auto-download asks no question at any of this.** The already-exists question is the manual half's ([8.3](#83-the-question-asked-once-row-202s-second-clause-and-the-collision-question)); the arrangement's answer to a taken name is always the truth-plus-carry-on above, because it runs with nobody watching and quietly overwriting is the one outcome the spec names as destroying work unseen.

### 5.4 The name that cannot land (Row 205)

**P8. A render output's name is never touched, so a name the receiving system will not take as given is refused per file, not repaired.** Substituting an artist's output name would be the silent transformation Row 303 forbids arriving by Smoozy's own hand. The file is marked **cannot land**, with which of the three refusals it was, said in the request or arrangement outcome; the rest of the folder lands (the partition unit is the file); the file stays on the server. `Desktop app and delivery` C6's rule that the adapter's writes descend rather than composing one long absolute path covers the deep-tree case, so depth is not a refusal.

**P9. A *cannot land* file will never arrive, and the bookkeeping says so rather than waiting forever**: the unit is terminal, it leaves the whole and is reported separately ([6.4](#64-the-count-and-the-whole-row-215)) — the interaction with Row 215 stated in one sentence: an arrangement never waits on a file the name check has already refused.

### 5.5 The job folder's landing name (Row 205's label half)

**P10. The job folder's landing name is Smoozy's own composition and is rendered against the receiving system's rules, by the same rule as on the machine.** The machine-side name is `Job files and outputs` N3's: the job identity's textual form plus a bounded rendering of the label, substitution rather than refusal. The landing name is composed the same way — the identity's textual form (fixed-length lowercase letters and digits, safe in every system's unit) plus a bounded rendering of the label judged by `Desktop app and delivery` C3 against the **receiving** system, with what cannot enter the name substituted and the composed name judged again like any other (`Desktop app and delivery` C20). So a label legal on Linux and unwritable as given on the destination — a trailing period on Windows is the case that will bite — costs the label's rendering and never the folder or the files, and two jobs given one label land in two folders because the identity is in the name. The full label stays authoritative on the entry, and the folder's real name is what paths show — `Queue & History` Y8's presentation rule, unchanged. This closes the Open Threads label question for the landing side; nothing parses a folder name back into anything. *(**A first-frame check lands under its range job's landing name** from 2026-10-29, since on the machine it writes into the range job's folder (`Job files and outputs` P18a): one folder on the computer for the pair, the check's files told apart by their `check_` mark. H5's set for either job is then that one folder. The twenty-first notes, note 6, Creator-stated. No auditor has seen it.)*

**P11. Inside the folder, the structure the files had on the server is the structure they have when they land** — each file's landing path is the destination plus its path relative to what was asked for, component for component, names carried as the machine's bytes and never rewritten in flight (`File Browser` N2's discipline in the other direction) — except where a component fails the name check, which is P8's per-file refusal for everything beneath it, reported on that branch's files.

## 6. Narrowing, the count and the formats (Rows 213, 215 and 298)

### 6.1 The format list, and the moment it exists (Row 213)

**N1. The list of formats offered is read off the recorded reading's destination set — every destination's format, deduplicated — and it exists at the moments `Render Settings` fixes: after a pre-load, or from render start otherwise, never before.** It is a product of the file and the version rendering it, so it is never a fixed list Smoozy carries. Where a node gathers outputs into a multi-layer file, the format that counts is that file's — `What a job writes`' rule, already inside the recorded reading.

**N2. The list reaches the desktop as a reading of the job's record directory.** Where the reading ran at pre-load, the material is already on the desktop side from that moment. Where the job was submitted unread, the machine-side reading lands when the job starts, and the desktop reads the recorded reading and the inventory from the job's record directory over the connection, at or after render-start — a reading with its moment. Until it lands, there is no list and nothing to narrow with.

### 6.2 Narrowing applied late (Row 213's second clause)

**N3. An arrangement made with no list covers everything, and *everything* is no filter rather than a frozen list** — so the list arriving later invalidates nothing. From the moment the narrowing material is on the desktop, the job's queue entry offers the narrowing (`Queue & History` owns the entry and the toggle's placement; what the control does is this document's): a narrowing the user applies takes effect on the arrangement from that moment forward. Files already fetched outside the new narrowing stay — *what has already come home stays* — and files not yet fetched outside it stop being owed. Switching off and on resets the narrowing to everything and the choice is made again, per the spec.

**N4. Narrowing is by format and reaches only the inventory's per-file entries.** A file with no format in the reading — an added write place's file, an unmatched earlier-attempt file — carries nothing for a format cut to select against. **Corrected 2026-08-21, answering Row 314: such a file is *outside the narrowing*, which means it is not filtered out by it, and not *outside the arrangement*, which is what this rule used to say.** It comes at the ending walk under every arrangement, narrowed or not (J8). Whatever a narrowing passed over — a file that **has** a format and was not one of the chosen ones — is still on the server and fetchable by hand, which is what makes that choice safe.

**N4b. Why the old reading could not stand, in one line each.** [What a job writes](../Specs/What%20a%20job%20writes.md) promises unconditionally that a bake and a cache *"are brought home with it, and are not left on a machine that is about to be destroyed"*, and [Dependency Upload & Remap](../Specs/Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md) repeats it — *"[Download] … sees what the render produced, and it comes home with the frames"*. *(Re-quoted 2026-08-29 at the re-gate. The source's words are a markdown link, `[Download](Download.md)`; this quotation wrote the link's words as plain text, which `Coverage.md`'s rule of 2026-08-23 names outright as leaving a quotation that can never match, and the whole parenthesis is now a marked elision so the two fragments each match exactly.)* **The user cannot ask for them back either**: the choice is *"by file format, and goes no finer"* and the formats offered are the ones the job will produce, while `Job files and outputs` I14 gives an added write place **no per-file entries and so no format**, so nothing a bake writes can ever appear in that list. **And this document's own reassurance is the sentence that fails** — *"for as long as the server exists"* — on the one case the ruling of 2026-08-18 was made about, a machine destroyed with the bake still on it.

**N4c. This is not a finer cut and does not touch the Creator's ruling.** *By file format, and goes no finer* fixes what the **cut** is. A file with no format is not a finer cut of the same set; it is a file the cut cannot speak about, and reading the cut's silence as exclusion was this document's inference rather than any spec's promise. **No new control, no new choice, and nothing new on screen.**

**N4d. What it costs, stated rather than absorbed.** A narrowed arrangement now also brings back whatever the render wrote into an added write place, which can be far larger than the frames the user narrowed to. **The arrangement's own reading is what makes that visible** — such files are counted separately and reported separately ([6.4](#64-the-count-and-the-whole-row-215)), never folded into the narrowed count — and the words are Design's. Nothing here lets a user decline them, because both specs say they come home.

### 6.3 Matching an arriving file (Row 298)

**N5. A file is one of the narrowed formats when its inventory entry's destination carries one of the chosen formats in the recorded reading. The match is against the inventory by predicted path, never against the file's name, and nothing is opened and nothing is changed.** A name-suffix rule is refused outright: an output's name is the artist's and carries no promise about its format, a multi-layer file's format is the file's rather than its outputs', and the inventory already holds the one authoritative mapping (I14) — deriving a second one from spelling would be a second count by another name. A walk entry matching no inventory entry has no format and falls under N4.

**N5a. One class of file under the job folder is skipped on the way home: a bake or cache Smoozy itself placed there before the render. Added 2026-08-22 on the Creator's ruling of Row 312.** [Dependency Upload & Remap](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md#67-the-uploaded-cache-and-the-redirect--a-seam-between-two-closed-rulings-ruled-row-312) M22a places a bake the user uploaded at the redirect's own job-folder target, so the render finds it; [Job files and outputs](Job%20files%20and%20outputs.md#12-the-areas-and-what-may-never-sit-under-a-job-folder) L7a carries the one exception to *nothing but what a render writes sits inside a job output folder* that this needs. **Without this rule the user would download the bake they had just uploaded**, which on a simulation cache is not a rounding error.

- **The test is the placement record and never the file, and it is a comparison against what the record says was placed. Corrected 2026-08-22, out of the third sanity sweep's verification round.** Smoozy placed it, so Smoozy knows the exact paths it wrote **and what it wrote at each of them** — [Dependency Upload & Remap](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md#67-the-uploaded-cache-and-the-redirect--a-seam-between-two-closed-rulings-ruled-row-312) M22f's placed-copy list, a field of the job's own local record, holding each copy's exact job-folder path and the four-value identity read the moment the copy was made. **A path in that list whose four values still match is the copy Smoozy placed, and is skipped. A path whose values have moved is not that copy any more — the render wrote there — and it comes home like everything else.** **The values compared against the list are read on the machine, by Smoozy's own program, the moment the attempt's render has ended, and the desktop reads that reading rather than taking its own at the ending walk; where there is no such reading, nothing is skipped.** The file-transfer surface carries only two of the four values (`Dependency Upload & Remap` Q5 route 1), so the desktop cannot take them, and nothing writes into the job's folder once the render has ended, so the reading taken then is the one the ending walk would have taken. Corrected 2026-10-02, ruled by the build runner; no auditor of this document has seen it.
- **What this bullet used to say, and why it was wrong.** It said *nothing is compared by name, by size, by time or by content*, on the ground that a bake the render overwrote is byte-different from the one placed and still sits at a path Smoozy placed. **The first half is still right and the conclusion was not.** Comparing against another **file** — by name, or by looking like a bake — would indeed get it wrong. Comparing against Smoozy's own **record** is the opposite thing: it is how every other reading in this project separates what Smoozy put somewhere from what it did not, and without it the Creator's own *both* case ([M22b](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md#67-the-uploaded-cache-and-the-redirect--a-seam-between-two-closed-rulings-ruled-row-312) — send a bake up **and** have the machine's own bake come home) lost the recompute silently: skipped on the way home, destroyed with the machine, no count moved and the job reported success.
- **Its failure direction is the safe one.** A copy whose four values are unchanged is skipped, which costs the user nothing — an identical result is not a result lost. A copy that was touched comes home, which costs at worst a re-download of something the user already has, and that is the direction N5a's last bullet already chooses where the record cannot be read at all.
- **The skip is by path and by the four values at it, and stops there.** Anything the render wrote **beside** a placed file, under the same added write place, is not a path Smoozy placed and comes home like everything else — as does anything it wrote **over** one. So a job whose render did recompute into that folder brings its results home, and a job whose render read the placed bake and wrote nothing new brings home an empty-of-new folder, which is [Job files and outputs](Job%20files%20and%20outputs.md) D21e's own *reported as empty rather than as a loss*.
- **No count moves.** A placed bake was never an inventory entry — I14 gives an added write place a folder with no per-file entries and no contribution to the count — so skipping it removes nothing from any figure, and N6's whole is untouched. **This is a rule about what is fetched, never about what is counted.**
- **It never reaches an ordinary output.** The skip is confined to paths under [Job files and outputs](Job%20files%20and%20outputs.md) 5.5 D20's four added write places, which is where L7a confines the placement in the first place. The regular output path and every file output node's folder are outside it by construction, so no frame can ever be skipped by this rule.
- **Where the record is gone, nothing is skipped.** A machine whose placement record cannot be read brings the folder home whole — the safe direction, costing the user a re-download rather than a missing file.
- **No download route brings home a copy this rule skips, or `File Browser` X12's reserved `.smoozy-staging` folder, as a job's output — not the auto-download, not *Download all*, not a job's own download and not `File Browser`'s *Download* of a job folder — while a copy the render wrote over still comes home.** Added 2026-10-02, ruled by the build runner; no auditor of this document has seen it.

### 6.4 The count and the whole (Row 215)

**N6. The whole an arrangement reads against is the number of the narrowed inventory entries — every entry where the arrangement is unnarrowed — plus the video statement.** The count is `Job files and outputs`' and is consumed, never derived a second way: entries only (I14), files expected on disk (I13), frames producing nothing counting as nothing, view layers multiplying nothing, added write places in no count. A video destination's entry counts one and the whole is stated as possibly growing (I19–I20); further split files arriving under the folder are fetched at the ending and counted as the growth the statement allowed. **Once the job has ended short of a clean finish, the whole is what the ending's walk found, since no more will come** — never the asked range's count. An entry the walk found at zero bytes was found, so it stays in the whole as N7's shortfall; an entry absent at that walk is not in it. *(Added 2026-10-19, out of the live pass on Vast for the thirteenth and fourteenth notes: Transfers read ended 100 / 450 for a job stopped at 52 frames. No auditor has seen it.)*

**N7. Units leave the whole only where the promise itself says they are not coming through this arrangement, and everything that leaves is reported separately** — `File Browser` U11's discipline, agreed with rather than re-derived:

- **left alone** (P6) — out of the whole, reported with the amber line's list;
- **cannot land** (P8) — out, reported with the refusal;
- **skipped by narrowing** (N3, applied late) — out, silently correct, the whole recomputed;
- **newer version on the server** (R7) — the original stays counted as fetched; the telling rides beside it;
- **never coming** — a narrowed entry absent at the ending walk, or present at zero bytes — **stays in the whole and is the shortfall.** That is deliberate: the short count is the one saying the spec promises for what nobody could vouch for, and moving these out would round the arrangement up to done over a hole. *(Narrowed 2026-10-19 with N6's last sentence: for a job that ended short of a clean finish, an entry absent at the ending walk is out of the whole, since the frames a stopped or failed render never reached are not a hole, and the ending already says the job did not finish. A zero-byte entry stays in, as above. No auditor has seen it.)*

**N8. The manual whole is [8.7](#87-the-reading-of-the-request-as-a-whole-row-299)'s and is counted the same way in files**; the arrangement's whole has no byte bar, because a byte total over files not yet written does not exist — the one declared divergence from U11's pair, with its reason.

## 7. What ends an arrangement (Row 220)

**E1. The arrangement outlives the job's ending on `Queue & History`'s signal, and then decides for itself that nothing more is coming — by finishing its own bookkeeping, never by a judgement about the render.** The sequence:

1. **The ending arrives** — one of the four, live or at catch-up. The module never finds out on its own.
2. **The ending walk runs**: one final complete walk of the job output folder. From it, every narrowed entry present and not zero bytes is fetchable (J6); added-place and unmatched files are fetchable **under every arrangement, narrowed or not** ([N4](#62-narrowing-applied-late-row-213s-second-clause), [J8](#44-the-files-with-no-frame)), and a video destination's entry is fetchable here and nowhere earlier (J8); every narrowed entry absent or zero-byte becomes **never coming** (N7). **Corrected 2026-09-05, out of the build of this section's rules half.** This step read *fetchable where the arrangement is unnarrowed*, which is the reading N4 and J8 both corrected on 2026-08-21 answering Row 314 and this step was the last place still carrying it: such a file is *outside the narrowing*, meaning it is not filtered out by it, and not *outside the arrangement*. **The old reading contradicted two specs outright** — N4b names them, each promising unconditionally that a bake and a cache come home — and **the user cannot ask for one back**, an added write place having no per-file entries and so no format for the cut to select on (`Job files and outputs` I14). Nothing is counted differently: such files were never in any count (I14) and are reported separately ([6.4](#64-the-count-and-the-whole-row-215), N4d). **And where the ending is *stopped*, *failed* or *given up*, the entries of the highest frame in the attempt's order that shows any file — or, where the frame boundary cannot be established, the entries active in the last complete walk — are marked *fetched, could not be vouched for* and named on the arrangement's existing telling surface.** They are fetched exactly as they are now; the machine is the user's. The reason for the mark is that the one file most likely to be torn in the whole job is the frame that was being written when the job ended badly, and it is the one file nothing else here would say anything about: the count is not short, because the file arrived; N7's categories that leave the whole do not include it; and there is no Blender saved line for a frame whose save never completed. The arrangement holds everything the mark needs — the attempt's frame order, the ending's kind and the frontier — and the saying rides the same per-request surface as *left alone* and R7's newer-version telling, adding no page and no question. Wording is Design's; [Section 16](#16-moments-that-reached-the-creator) records the check. *(**Added 2026-10-31, the twenty-sixth notes, note 4: a first-frame pair writes one folder (`Job files and outputs` P18a), and each half's ending walk takes only its own files.** The range job's leaves to the check every file its inventory does not name that carries the check's mark; **the check's leaves to the range job every file its inventory does not name that carries no mark**, since every file the check writes is marked. Before this, a check whose ending walk ran again after a restart took the range job's frames as its own. **And where the pair's other half has an entry *fetched* at the same landing path, the entry is written *fetched* and nothing is fetched**: one folder, one file, taken once. No auditor has seen it.)*
3. **The fetch queue drains** to terminal states — fetched, left alone, cannot land — under the ordinary pipeline, surviving drops and destination trouble on the way ([Section 9](#9-destination-trouble-rows-218-and-219)).
4. **The arrangement is done when every unit is terminal.** The reading then says **ended**, with arrivals against the whole — short where short, and never *complete* — which is the spec's own wording for a job that ends badly, and the correct wording for one that ended well short of a lost write too. **Where a switch-on has reset the destination part-way through, the reading names both folders**: what came home before the reset is at the folder it went to and stays there, and what came after is at the new one (R1, R11). **And done is not for ever**: a new render-start for the same job re-arms the arrangement (W3) — the *never coming* entries re-open to *owed* for the frames the new attempt's list names, the whole is recomputed, the walks resume, and this sequence runs again to a second done. Nothing else re-opens, so a re-run never arrives over a file the user already has (R9).

**E2. In the interval between the ending and done, the reading says the job has ended and files are still coming home** — the arrangement still active, its progress moving; the entry's own ending is `Queue & History`'s to show; the bar's wording for a badly-ended job is Design's marked question and is not settled here.

**E2a. An arrangement with units still owed and no run behind it reads as waiting, and neither of E2's two readings covers that. Added 2026-09-02, scenario replay *The user queues multiple jobs overnight* finding 4.** [R11](#24-what-the-record-survives-row-217) makes the arrangement's **activity** run-held while its record is durable, and [W3](#3-the-watch-row-210) runs a walk only while a connection to that server stands. Both are right and both follow from the explicit-connect model the spec states outright. **What was missing is the saying, and Smoozy going quiet is Smoozy's fault whoever caused it.** A user who switched auto-download on at submission and closed Smoozy for the night meets, in the morning, a job that ended and files that never came, with nothing between E2's *ended and still coming home* and E1 step 4's *done* to say why. [R12](#24-what-the-record-survives-row-217)'s own sentence — *"an arrangement attached to a job, not a request made at a moment"* — is what such a user would have read, and it points the other way.

- **The third reading.** Where the arrangement's desired state is on and units are still owed, and **no walk can run because no connection to that server stands**, the reading is **waiting for a connection to that server** — distinct from E2's and from done.
- **It is composed from what is already on the user's own computer**: the arrangement record (R4, R5) and the connection condition `Servers` supplies. **It needs no reading of a machine**, so nothing here joins `Desktop app and delivery` [P10d](Desktop%20app%20and%20delivery.md#33-ui-event-delivery)'s closed set — the same ground [E3a](#7-what-ends-an-arrangement-row-220) stands on.
- **Where it is said is the job's own entry**, which is where the spec puts every saying an arrangement makes; **the wording is Design's**, as the rest of this section leaves it.
- **It is not a notification and none is owed.** `Desktop app and delivery` T9's four kinds are closed, and E3a already rules the outputs being home out of them; something that has **not** happened is further out still.
- **It ends by itself and nothing watches for it.** The next connection to that server resumes the walks under R12, and the reading returns to E2's or to done. Nothing withdraws it, nothing schedules it, nothing reaches out.
- **What it does not claim.** It says nothing about what is on the machine beyond what the arrangement's own record holds, and it is not a warning against destroying the rental — no clause anywhere lets Smoozy speak for a rental it did not take (`Servers` W40, [Rented-server runtime](Rented-server%20runtime.md#12-health-readings) V13). **What it prevents is the one real loss in that morning**: a user reading the job as *finished*, concluding the frames are home, and destroying the machine they are still on.

**E3. A job that ends badly still gives up what it made** — steps 2 and 3 run identically for *failed*, *stopped* and *given up*; **the ending's kind changes nothing about what is fetched, only how much there is — and one thing about what is said**, which is the *could not be vouched for* mark of E1 step 2 on the frame that was in flight when the job ended. Where the server itself went down, what it made may be less than what it reported making, the emptied frames land as zero-byte *never coming* entries, and the short count says so. And the user is told when those outputs are home, the same as for a finished job: **the moment is the done transition of E1 step 4 and the surface is the job's own entry** — which is where the spec puts every saying an arrangement makes, *"where it says so is the job's entry, not the moment"*, and it does not change with the ending's kind. What the entry reads is the arrangement's progress reading, already supplied to `Queue & History` at [Section 14](#14-what-this-document-supplies-and-to-which-document) — arrivals against the whole, reading *ended* and never *complete* — drawn through `Desktop app and delivery` Section 16's progress presentation plumbing.

**E3a. No notification is posted for it and none is owed. ## 9. Destination trouble (Rows 218 and 219)

### 9.1 The causes, and what can be told apart (Row 218)

**D1. The outcome classes are the adapter's and are consumed, never re-derived** (`Desktop app and delivery` F1–F9): `ENOSPC` — the volume is full; `EACCES` — writing is refused; `ENOENT` — the destination is no longer there, **and the adapter names neither cause behind it**, because a removed directory and a detached volume are one value where the mount point went with the volume, and the separating test the findings once proposed is struck; **`EIO` at `write()` — the write failed at the device, and the adapter names no cause behind it either** (F4a, supplied 2026-08-23), **a class that is macOS-observed and is claimed for neither Windows nor Linux**, whose detached-volume values are unestablished ([Section 15](#15-unresolved) items 1 and 2). The write sequence's outcome is the first error any call returned (F2), and the destination is re-checked by path after closing because a deleted directory reports success at every call (F1).

**The `EIO` class, taken as supplied 2026-08-23 and not derived here.** A detached volume whose mount point went with it gives `EIO` (5) **once, at the write**, with `fsync` and `close` then returning 0 — Fact 23's own reading, carried by `Desktop app and delivery` F2 in those words. F4's classifier named no class for it, so the value reached this document as a failed write with no named cause and the gap was routed to F4 rather than filled here; **F4a declares the fourth class** — *the write failed at the device*, cause unnamed — on two grounds that are that document's: `EIO` is a different returned value from `ENOENT`, and a detached volume is the only observed cause of it while nothing read establishes it is the only possible one, so reporting *the destination is no longer there* would claim a cause the system did not give. D1 consumes it exactly as it consumes the other three, and [Section 15](#15-unresolved) item 23 is closed. **What still bounds the case:** F2 makes the first error the write sequence's outcome, so the failure is reported and never read as a clean finish; D2 claims no cause the system does not give; the unit fails and its file is not recorded *fetched*, so R9 never makes it permanent; and D4's condition key is the destination folder's own recorded path, which is available whether or not a cause is named.

**D2. The promise is kept narrowed, and the narrowing is stated rather than smoothed.** *A cause Smoozy genuinely knows* holds whole for the full volume and the refused write — each is named and each carries its remedy. For `ENOENT` the genuinely known cause is **that the destination is gone**, and Smoozy says that and says what covers both readings — make the destination exist again — **without claiming which of the two it was**, because a message naming the wrong cause is worse than one naming neither. **For `EIO` at `write()` the genuinely known cause is none**: Smoozy says the write failed at the device and names nothing behind it (F4a), because the value is not `ENOENT` and nothing establishes a detached volume as its only cause — **and no remedy is named there, because no cause is**. At `open()` four named causes become three named classes, and at `write()` the device failure names none; nothing false is ever said; [Section 16](#16-moments-that-reached-the-creator) records the first-stop check. The case with no error at all — a detached volume whose mount point survived, where the frame lands on the underlying volume with **no error anywhere**, and which is therefore no class of D1's — is not detected, not defended and not claimed: it is [Section 15](#15-unresolved)'s, carried with `Desktop app and delivery` F14.

**D3. No amount decides anything.** A full volume never reads zero — 1,068 KB on the genuinely full one — so no threshold exists here (F5's rule taken whole), and only a write attempt establishes that a refusal is over (F6).

### 9.2 Said once (Row 218's second clause)

**D4. One condition, keyed on the destination folder's own path.** A destination-unwritable condition is raised with a key carrying **the refusal's kind and the destination folder's own path in one normalised form** — so a pool of transfers failing at the same moment for the same reason resolves to **one** condition identity, and `Desktop app and delivery`'s Q1–Q5 make *said once* fall out of the key rather than being enforced. **The folder and not its volume, because only one of the classes is a property of a volume.** `ENOSPC` is; `EACCES` is a folder's permissions, `ENOENT` is a folder deleted, and the `EIO` class names no cause at all — so a volume-keyed condition would be wider than the fact for three of D1's four classes. **D5. **D6. Two ceilings in one pool, not two pools; the count the machine sees is one count at the one per-server point (T5); no second pool is opened and no number presumes the pool.
