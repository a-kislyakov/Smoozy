### 1.1 The settings half of the one recorded reading

**Q1. The recorded reading of `Job files and outputs` R4 also holds the settings payload — payload two of Fact 5 — and it is the same single read, never a second invocation.** R1's own statement is that all three payloads come out of one read; observed on 2026-08-17 in 4.5.9, 5.0.1 and 5.2.0, the settings values came back in the same sentinel-framed document as the destinations and the locations ([One box with Blender on it](One%20box%20with%20Blender%20on%20it%20—%20the%20reading,%20the%20listing,%20and%20the%20names%20that%20do%20not%20come%20back.md) §1.3). A second reading shape here would be the fault R9 exists to prevent.

**Q2. What the settings half holds, itemised, per version-correct evaluation** (each item observed in §1.3 of that document except where its own label says otherwise):

1. **Per scene — for every scene the file holds, never only the one in hand, which is the spec's *one read covers the whole file***: the frame start, end and step, and the frame count; the full size in pixels (both axes), the resolution percentage, and the size the percentage works out to; the engine as read, as the reading version's own identifier; the per-engine sample counts, all present at once; the device choice (cards or processor); the early-stop setup (whether adaptive sampling is on, its threshold and its minimum); the time limit; the layer-samples rule's value (one of `USE`, `BOUNDED`, `IGNORE` — the identifiers **observed (2026-08-19 test)**; *Settings 16* carries the rule's three answers and its default under the manual's own names); and the saved-with version.
2. **Per view layer**: the name, exactly as the file's own bytes; whether it renders at each frame of Q3's evaluated span; whether the switch is animated, and by what — keyframes, a driver, or neither — with the curve's keys (frame, value, interpolation kind) where keyframed and the driver's expression and validity where driven (**observed, 2026-08-19 test**, both versions); and the layer's own sample count with whether it overrides the scene's.
3. **Whether one scene pulls in another, by both routes** — the compositor and the video sequencer — which is R4 item 3's reading, consumed here for the warning at the spec's *A scene that pulls in another scene*.
4. **The two offered engines' identifiers in the reading Blender** — the built-in real-time engine's identifier from the engine property's own enumeration, and the registered add-on engines from the engine-class walk. **Observed, 2026-08-19 test, and the negative is the finding**: the property's enumeration lists only the built-in identifier (`BLENDER_EEVEE_NEXT` in 4.5.12, `BLENDER_EEVEE` in 5.2.0) and does not list Cycles; the class walk lists `CYCLES` and does not list the built-in; one engine class carries no identifier at all and is skipped rather than read. Neither route alone answers, so the reading takes both.
5. **The one add-on case a file announces**, per [6.2](#62-the-one-add-on-case-a-file-announces) E5.
6. **Per scene, the Simplify group — the switch, and the render half of each of the three offered settings**: `use_simplify`; `simplify_subdivision_render`; `simplify_child_particles_render`; and `texture_limit_render`, reached by the scene's Cycles-block path and present whatever the active engine. **Added 2026-08-29, per Row 472 and Fact 134, and not among the batch of 2026-08-17's observed payloads**: the properties, their ranges, their read-back with no window open and their survival of saving and of an operating-system crossing are *What a render demands 6*'s — **documented** for the names and ranges, **observed** for the texture pair and for the round trip, in all four versions on two machines — while riding this reading's invocation is this clause's own composition, the same one read as everything above. These values feed the offer's italic half (`Rendering` A1d) and Y1a's companion like every other setting the payload can override.

**Q2a. The pixel figure beside the percentage is computed on the desktop, per axis, by truncation, and it costs no read. Added 2026-08-21, per Row 366.** The page shows a size for a percentage the user has just typed, which no reading holds and which [Q5](#12-when-the-read-runs-and-what-starts-it) prices a re-read of at 3.2 to 3.8 seconds end to end; the arithmetic is established rather than invented.

- **The rule: each axis on its own — the axis's full pixel size multiplied by the percentage, divided by a hundred, the fraction discarded. Never rounded.** Observed, measured out of the written files rather than computed, in all four versions on both of the research's machines (*Settings 15*): 1920×1080 at 200 % wrote 3840×2160; at 33 %, 633×356; at 1 %, 19×10; and 4×4 at 32767 %, 1310×1310. **The two axes that discriminate are the ones whose products end .6 and .8 and still came back down** — 633.6 to 633 and 10.8 to 10 — which is what rules rounding out. The bound the rule is used with is [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 25.
- **The two numbers it reads, and nothing else.** The **full size in pixels, both axes**, from the recorded reading in force for the scene in hand (Q2 item 1); and the **percentage in force** — the store's entry where the user answered, else that same reading's own percentage ([J1](#21-two-objects-never-merged)). Nothing is asked of the machine, and typing a percentage starts no read.
- **Every whole percentage from 1 to 32767 is producible against a standing reading, and no other value ever reaches the arithmetic.** [Y1](#91-what-the-payload-is)'s box holds the store to exactly that range, and F1's refuse-in-place shape means a value the page will not take never becomes an entry — so Blender's own silent pull-back to the nearest limit and its outright refusal of a fraction (*Settings 14*, *Settings 15*: the correction prints nothing at all and leaves the exit status at 0) have nothing to bite on in a Smoozy job.
- **There are exactly two states with no figure, and the arithmetic is not run in either.** **No reading in force** — before a pre-load, and after any of [J10](#23-what-drops-the-files-half)'s three drops — so there is no full size to take a percentage of. **And a typed value the page has not taken**, where the store still holds the last answered percentage and nothing has been asked to compute from what was typed. Whether the second draws the standing figure or draws nothing is Design's; both stand, because no unaccepted value is ever computed from. **A page opened from a history entry, before any pre-load, is the first of those states, and no third source exists — stated 2026-08-22, scenario replays Pile 1 R35.** The entry supplies no substitute for the full pixel size: the kept setup carries the user's own percentage where one was typed; [Y1a](#91-what-the-payload-is)'s companion carries the file's value **per overridden setting**, which for the percentage override is the file's own percentage and never a pixel size; and `Queue & History` N11 states outright that the entry holds *no pixel size at all*. So such a page asks for a percentage with no companion figure until a pre-load lands, and Design draws that as the absence it is rather than assuming a figure will be there. Composing a base figure into the local job record at submission was weighed and not taken: it would add a field to a closed document for a figure no promise names, and the reading that fills the page in is one Pre-load away.
- **The reading's own worked-out size is not a second source.** Q2 item 1's carried figure is Blender's own answer at the file's own percentage, and it is the same number this rule gives at that percentage; the rule answers every other percentage, which is the whole of why it is needed.
- **A figure of zero on an axis is producible and is left as the figure it is.** A request working out to less than a pixel is refused by Blender with `Image too small` and nothing is written — 4×4 at 1 %, observed in all four versions on both machines (*Settings 15*). **This document supplies the figure and adds no warning of its own**: no spec grants the user one here, and what the render is given is [C7](#5-samples-the-imposition-set)'s imposition of the number they typed. What the page draws for a zero is Design's.

**Q3. The per-frame view-layer answer is evaluated by stepping frames inside the read, over a stated span, with the two extrapolation constants beside it.** The span is the union of the scene's own frame range and the switch's key frames; the read also records the switch's value before the first key and after the last, which are constants (**observed, 2026-08-19 test**: frames past the last key held the last key's value in both versions; the before-first side was not posed and is [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 8). A frame inside the span is answered by the table; a frame outside it by the extrapolation constant. A frame inside the key span but outside the evaluated table is answered from the curve data — exact where every key is constant-interpolation, and labelled as computed rather than evaluated where any key carries another kind ([Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 8). **Stepping frames inside the read evaluates a driver identically to the render** (**observed, 2026-08-19 test**, [4.1](#41-what-one-read-answers-per-frame) V2), which is why the answer is the stepped table first and the curve second. **Where the switch is driven rather than keyed there are no key frames, so the span is the scene's own frame range alone and there is no extrapolation constant either side of it**: a frame outside that span is answered *unknown until read* and labelled as such, the same honesty this clause already uses for a frame answered from the curve rather than evaluated. The same holds for a switch a stored script drives under an enabled preference (V3). The residue is [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 7. **Narrowed 2026-10-03, out of the Creator's notes on 0.1.8 (note 4): a scene is stepped over its span only where something on it can change from frame to frame** — the scene carries animation data (keys, drivers or strips); its compositing tree, or any group tree inside it, carries animation data; or one of its timeline markers names a camera. **Otherwise the values read once, at the frame the file was saved on, stand for every frame of its span**, and the table holds that one answer at each of them. The job's own scene follows the same rule. Stepping every scene made Blender rebuild each one at each frame, and a file of ten still scenes took about 22 seconds at the desk where the narrowed read takes about 2. Label: **observed in a test** — 214 files, each read by each of the four versions both ways (with no frames, and with a render's frames), gave byte-identical readings before and after the narrowing, apart from one file's per-run temporary folder name ([Sources](Sources/Render%20Settings.sources.md) B31). **Where the reading and the render can part:** a value changed at each frame by something the condition does not look at is read as still. The one route known is a script run on a frame change, where V3 lets scripts run; *expected, not tested*: with scripts off, Smoozy's default, the render agrees with the reading.

### 1.2 When the read runs, and what starts it

**Q4. Pre-load Settings is the user's own act, and the read it starts is one file-inspection operation driven from the desktop through the supervisor's command path.** R3's desktop side. It is admitted at the machine's one admission point under [Section 10](#10-the-file-inspection-worker-kind)'s declarations, exactly as `Dependency Upload & Remap` G1 admits its check reading — the bare-connection invocation the batch of 2026-08-17 observed is the mechanism's shape, not a route around the admission point, and a reading that bypassed admission would be unarbitrated beside a render (A4's argument). The render-start reading is not this operation: it runs inside the admitted render operation as a step of the render worker (`Rendering` P1 step 4) and contends with nothing. *(Withdrawn 2026-10-13: the note of 2026-10-12 that a pre-load also starts `Dependency Upload & Remap` G1b's check reading, and the reverse, was taken back the next day because the Creator deferred the request (the ninth notes, note 4). G1b is struck; the idea is parked in Open Threads.)*

**Q5. While the read runs, the page is left exactly as it was.** Nothing is blanked, nothing is provisionally filled: the user's own values stand, the previous file-half (where one stands) stands, and the reading's result lands whole or not at all (Q7). One read per (server, file) runs at a time; a second Pre-load asked for while one runs joins the running one rather than starting a second beside it. The observed cost — 3.2 to 3.8 seconds end to end, an upper bound taken on a contended container — is R16's: not a reason to skip it, and not long enough to need a second mechanism. What the user sees while it runs is Design's.

**Q6. A read that lands replaces only the file's half.** The recorded reading for that (server, file) is replaced whole — never merged, never patched — and the override store of [Section 2](#2-the-override-store) is untouched by construction, because the two are different objects and no code path writes from one into the other (J4).

**Q17. A read carries what it was started under, and a reading that lands after the events meant to drop it is discarded rather than stored.** The read is in flight for 3.2 to 3.8 seconds and nothing is locked while it runs — the version can be changed and the file can be replaced under it, and Q5 leaves the page exactly as it was — so the reading arrives at a page that may no longer be the page that asked for it. The read therefore carries **the version pick and the four-value file identity it was started under**, and on landing Q6 stores the reading only where both still stand: where the version is not the page's current pick, or where the four values are not the ones now standing for that (server, file), **the arriving reading is discarded and nothing is written** — the same act J10's three events perform, applied to an object that did not exist yet when they fired. Nothing is left half-filled by it (Q7) and the page is where it was: the store untouched, no file half standing, and Pre-load offered again. This is what makes Q12's *nothing anywhere holds a read that outlives its version* true rather than nearly true, and it closes the same hole on the noticing's side, where J10's drop lands before the reading it was meant to drop.

**Q18. The moment the header states is the desktop's clock, stamped at the act of storing the reading, and it is a field of the reading it describes. Added 2026-08-23, per Row 438.** *When it was read* is a moment for an act a Blender performed on the machine and the desktop draws, so the clock is a decision; neither standing precedent carries into it by resemblance — `Servers` H2a gave the server record a second, desktop-side moment because a *last seen* mark is a comparison against now, and `Queue & History` N16 put the submitted moment on the desktop's clock because submission happens on the desktop and the machine was never told when. Both grounds are argued here rather than inherited.

- **The clock is the desktop's, and D5 permits it in its own words** — *"the desktop's clock is used for presentation and for nothing a promise rests on"*. **Nothing rests on it:** staleness is R13's rule entire, the four values and the version and never an age (Q13); Q14's noticing is three acts and computes no interval (Q15); Q17's discard tests the version pick and the four values and not a moment; and no clause in this document or below it reads this figure to decide anything. **The presentation needs that clock rather than merely tolerating it**: drawn as an age it is arithmetic against the desktop's now, and drawn as a clock time it is read against the clock on the user's own screen — both are the subtraction D5 forbids across two clocks, and only a desktop-clock moment makes either without it.
- **It is stamped when [Q6](#12-when-the-read-runs-and-what-starts-it) stores the reading — not when Pre-load was asked for, and not at the invocation's start.** The read is in flight for 3.2 to 3.8 seconds against a page that may move under it, and a reading that is no reading (Q7) or that arrives to be discarded (Q17) never reaches the stamp. So the header states a moment only for a read whose result is the one standing, which is what *when it was read* has to mean beside values the user is reading off the same header.
- **The machine's own clock is not read for this, and no duration is ever computed across the two** (D5; `Servers` H2's rule). The render-start reading (R3) is composed on the machine, carries no desktop-side stamp, and is never shown here — the header's moment is only ever a pre-load's. **The desktop's clock is itself undefended** (`Desktop app and delivery` T19, Fact 13's negative half); what that costs here is a moment drawn wrong, never a decision taken wrong, which is the whole reason the figure is allowed on that clock.
- **It dies with the reading it rides**, so all three of [J10](#23-what-drops-the-files-half)'s drops take it. **That is what keeps the header's second claim true**: changing the version discards the reading for that (server, file) whole (Q12), so the header has no read moment left to draw and cannot state when the file was read beside a version the read was not taken with — the pair goes together rather than the moment being corrected or left standing.

### 1.3 All-or-nothing, and the three endings

**Constraint:** Row 141 — *"A read either succeeds whole or gives nothing… Nothing is left half-filled."* The read is by construction many readings — no single means reaches every place a file writes — so the whole-or-nothing boundary has to be drawn by the mechanism, not assumed.

**Q7. A reading is the complete sentinel-framed document with an empty error map, and anything else is not a reading.** The in-Blender reader composes the whole document and emits it once between the sentinels; a document whose end sentinel never arrived, and a document whose error map is not empty, are both **no reading**: nothing from either is stored, nothing from either is shown, and no partial document is ever written into a recorded reading. This is `Dependency Upload & Remap` G3's rule — a reading that did not complete is not a reading — applied to the same kind, and it is what keeps R4's *one recorded reading* an object downstream can trust: what is not written is the answer to the partial read.

**Q8. Three endings are told apart, and each from Smoozy's own frame rather than from anything Blender printed — and a read Smoozy itself ended is none of the three:**

1. **The machine could not be reached.** The channel's own ending, `Server protocol and transfers`' entire — the command never ran or never answered, and a waiter's expired deadline says the waiter gave up and nothing else (C16–C17). Nothing here re-diagnoses it.
2. **The file could not be opened by this version.** The invocation completed, ended at a failing status, **no start sentinel was ever emitted**, **and Smoozy did not end the invocation itself** — because a file the Blender cannot open ends the run before any handed-in script runs. **Observed, 2026-08-19 test, both versions**: against a corrupt file and against an absent file the invocation exited 1, printed Blender's own one-line refusal, and the reader never ran — no sentinel, no partial document. The page says which version failed from Smoozy's own record of which binary it invoked (the page's version pick — at pre-load there is no payload, the payload being composed at submission), never by mining Blender's message — which keeps `Rented-server runtime` §11.4's rule that an ending is never read from what was printed. **The direction this ending reads the missing sentinel in is established on two file shapes and no more**: what was observed is that a corrupt file and an absent path end the run before the reader, and whether any other file shape emits no start sentinel — a file Blender can begin to open that aborts before the handed-in reader is reached — is unposed, and the sources file's own *does not cover* list does not name it. What breaks if such a shape exists is **the ending's label alone**, never the safety guarantee: nothing is stored either way (Q7), submitting stays offered (Q9), and the version named is Smoozy's own record rather than a diagnosis of the file.
3. **The read ran and something in it failed.** The start sentinel is present and the document is short, or complete with a non-empty error map. It is no reading (Q7); the invocation's record is kept for diagnosis under the ordinary captured-streams rules and nothing of the document is consumed.

The first is distinguishable from the other two because it is a different document's ending arriving on a different surface; the second from the third by the start sentinel, which is Smoozy's own frame.

**The start sentinel is the reader's very first act, and the document's own opening mark is not it. Added 2026-10-02, out of the Creator's tests of 0.1.7.** The reader writes its start sentinel before it does anything else, and the opening mark only with the whole document at its end. **This settles one shape ending 2 left unposed, and in the direction it feared**: a file every version at the desk opens cleanly crashed Blender partway through the reader — `Job files and outputs` R4 item 6's walk reading a 3D viewport's stored Local View — and with the only mark written at the end, that crash met every test of ending 2, so the page told the user the Blender they picked could not open their file and to pick another. With the sentinel first, any failure inside the reader is ending 3. **A file Blender cannot open still writes nothing**, because the reader never runs: observed at the desk on 2026-10-02 against a corrupt, a cut-short and an absent file, in all four versions there, with `Job files and outputs` R18's script before the file ([Sources](Sources/Render%20Settings.sources.md) B30). What stays unposed is a file that aborts after it opens and before the reader is reached.

**A read Smoozy ended itself is not diagnosed as any of the three, and Smoozy always knows which those are** — the termination is its own act with a recorded outcome ([Section 10](#10-the-file-inspection-worker-kind) W2, `Rented-server runtime` A6), reached by a stop-and-clear, a supervisor shutdown, or the kind's own cancellation, which W1 declares available at any moment. Without the qualifier such a read matches ending 2 exactly — invocation over, failing status, no sentinel — and the page would tell the user their `.blend` cannot be opened by the Blender they picked, which is false, and point them at changing the version, which drops the file's half for a reason that was never the file's. **What the page says for it is one line: the read was stopped, nothing about the file is claimed, and it can be run again.** Nothing is stored, exactly as for the other three.

**Q9. A failed read changes nothing.** The user's store is untouched (it is a different object); the previous recorded reading, where one stands and is not stale under Q13's tests, goes on standing — a failed re-read is not evidence the file changed, and where the file did change Q14's noticing and the staleness tests drop the old reading on their own ground. The page says the file could not be read and with which version, and submitting stays offered, because the file is read anyway when the job starts — the spec's own sentence.

**Q10. The connection dropping mid-read is ending 1 or ending 3 by the same tests and needs no case of its own** — a channel that died before the command answered is 1; a document cut short is 3. Either way nothing is stored.

### 1.4 The version binding

**Constraint:** Row 142. Four things move across the version boundary and each changes the answer rather than the wording: a value can come back different from the value saved; the view-layer switch's declared default is off in 4.5 and on from 5.0; the real-time engine's identifier changes at 5.0; and the engines a file can name are whatever the reading Blender has registered, so with the Cycles add-on off a Cycles file reads back under the other engine's name and its Cycles settings cannot be read at all (*What a render demands 5*, *Reading a file 3*, *Settings 13*, *Add-ons 1*).

**Q11. Every recorded reading carries the identity of the Blender that took it — the build's path and its answered version — beside the file's four-value identity and the moment.** The reading and the version travel together, and R13 already makes the version one of the staleness tests.

**Q12. Nothing anywhere holds a read that outlives its version: the reading store keys one reading per (server, file) and Q6 replaces it whole, so there is no second copy to go stale.

### 1.5 Noticing the file changed while connected

**Constraint:** Row 143 — the spec's third read-ending event: *"The `.blend` is replaced or changed on the machine while Smoozy stays connected …"* The failure it prevents is silent — a job submitted against a file the page is no longer describing.

**Q13. This is the route the ordinary change takes, since a `.blend` is replaced through Smoozy's own upload. **Widened 2026-09-05 from *any Smoozy upload*, out of round 2 of the code audit of `File Browser`**: a copy and a same-filesystem move publish over the same path with the same Replace answer, and the three documents that describe this one act now describe it in the same words. **What the narrower wording cost here was small and is stated rather than glossed** — a stale display, which item 2's comparison and R12's fresh check at the start of every attempt both catch.
2. **Opening this page onto a (server, file) a recorded reading stands for runs Q13's comparison**: the four values are read fresh and compared against the identity the reading carries. The read is a command-channel reading — the file-transfer surface's revision 3 attribute structure carries no inode and no change time, the same ground `Dependency Upload & Remap` Q5 route 1 names, carried at `File Browser` §13 item 9 — and where that channel cannot answer on a given rental, the comparison is not run and nothing is claimed. It is a reading the user's own act causes — `State, identity and recovery` §8.4's admitted shape — not a timer and not a poll.
3. **A Pre-load replaces the file's half whole** (Q6, Q17), which needs no comparison.

Where the comparison finds the four values differ, the file's half is dropped (J10) and the page says the file changed.

**Q15. What the noticing costs, and what it is not. Rewritten 2026-08-22 with Q14.** Q14 item 2's comparison read is a use of the standing connection at a user's act: it starts no Blender, writes nothing durable, takes no operation record, and is not admitted at the arbitration point — the same footing as a listing. Nothing about the noticing is periodic, machine-side, or alive between acts, so there is no cadence to declare, no channel share to claim, and no lifetime to end. **Its known limit is carried rather than smoothed: a change landing between two of Q14's moments is not noticed until the next one, and the display is stale for that stretch** — the noticing is an aid and not a guarantee, exactly as the watch it replaced was declared to be. What guarantees the render is R12's fresh check at the start of every attempt, which catches every change the page missed, recomputes on the machine (R13), and costs the user a display that was stale, never a render against a wrong map.

**Q16. This page-side checking case sits outside `Job files and outputs` R12, and that is recorded as a seam rather than resolved by implication.** R12 bounds when the job pipeline owes a fresh identity check — exactly two moments — and excludes a reconnection, a listing, an elapsed period and a transfer touching the folder. Q13's comparison is a third checking moment, taken for the page's display alone: it touches no manifest, triggers nothing in the job pipeline, and writes nothing durable. Either R12 is read as scoped to the recorded reading's use in the job pipeline — this document's reading — or it gains a clause naming the page's display-only comparison as outside it; the runner routes it ([Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 14), and Q13–Q15 stand under either wording.

## 2. The override store

**Constraint:** Rows 144 and 145 — *"That split is the whole rule: **the user's answers survive, the file's do not.**"* The two halves are the same number on screen and different answers to the render; getting it wrong sends the file's stale number as though the user had chosen it, at success.

### 2.1 Two objects, never merged

**J1. There are exactly two objects and the display is composed from them; neither is ever written from the other.** The **override store** holds the user's answers and only the user's answers. The **recorded reading** holds the file's values and only the file's values. For each setting the page shows the store's entry where one exists (the user's, upright), else the standing reading's value (the file's, italic), else the promise-form — *whatever the file says*, not yet shown. Which face each takes is Design's; that they are two objects is this clause.

**J2. The store is volatile — process memory for the life of the application run — and it lives in the volatile class `State, identity and recovery` §1.1 already defines, this clause naming it a member of that class.** The naming is the point: §1.1's enumeration of that class's members does not list this store, while its durable table's local record lists *settings*, so a builder taking either literally would put the user's answers on disk. *Closing Smoozy takes everything* is the spec's own grant; nothing here is kept on disk, carried into another sitting, or written into the local record, so §1.1's *only durable store* sentence is satisfied rather than strained. The recorded reading on the desktop is the same class — a live reading taken from a machine — and additionally period-held, dying with the connected period by [2.3](#23-what-drops-the-files-half) J10.

**J3. The store is keyed three ways at once, per the spec**: per **server row**, per **file** — the file's path on that server, deliberately not its four-value identity, because the spec has the user's answers survive the file being replaced — and per **scene**, by name, for the settings that are a scene's own. Its entries:

- per (server, file): the **scenes ticked**, by name with J7's provenance; the **version pick override**, where the user picked a build against the automatic match; and the **group frame range or list**, as [3](#3-the-frame-range-and-the-tidy) F1's parsed integers with their display-only accepted text — **the one entry that stands only while more than one scene is ticked**, because the spec makes the range the one setting imposed across a ticked set and the count a group count, so with several scenes ticked there is no scene in hand to key it by. While it stands it **shadows** the per-scene frame-range entries of that (server, file) — they are neither read nor written while more than one scene is ticked — and it is deleted by the hand-everything-back control (J5). Unticking back to one scene leaves the remaining scene's own entry exactly as it was, since the group entry was never written into any scene's key;
- per (server, file, scene): the **frame range or list**, as F1's parsed integers with their display-only accepted text; the **sample count**; the **resolution percentage**; the **engine pick**, one of the two offered; the **device choice**, cards or processor; the **view-layer pins**, per layer name with J7's provenance, each on or off; and **Simplify** — the switch, and each of its three settings, its own entry where the user answered it at `Rendering`'s failed-job offer (**per-setting from 2026-08-29, Row 472/A1d; this read as one four-part block written by switching on, which presumed the drawn no-file-value premise the fact overturned**), which is the user's own act and so a store entry by J1's rule like any other;
- per (server, file), while **no scene is in hand** — no recorded reading stands for that (server, file), no scene is ticked, and no scene name has arrived from a kept setup, which is the state of a fresh page before any Pre-load — the **pre-read answer set**: the frame range or list as F1's parsed integers with their display-only accepted text, the **sample count**, the **resolution percentage**, the **engine pick**, and the **device choice** — and, **where the page draws `Rendering` A1b's offer, the Simplify entries of the bullet above** (added 2026-08-29, Row 472: a failed job submitted with no read keeps no scene name, so its re-submission page draws the offer in exactly this state and the answers need this home or have none). **Added 2026-08-22, scenario replays Pile 1 R3.** These, and no others, are the settings answerable with nothing read; a scene tick and a view-layer pin each need a name only a reading or a kept setup supplies (J7), so neither can exist in this state. Without this set, an answer given before its key exists has nowhere to live: the per-scene keys need a scene, the group entry stands only while more than one scene is ticked, and F5's composition would silently drop what the user typed — the job would render the file's own saved range at a successful ending while the page had shown the range they asked for, and the plan total, composed from the same absent entry, would agree with the payload and disagree with the user. The set shadows nothing, because in its state there are no per-scene entries to shadow. **When a reading first lands for that (server, file) (Q6), the set's entries become the per-scene entries of the scene the page then stands in, in one act — re-keyed, never copied** — so the answer goes on standing in the box the user typed it into, which is the spec's *the user's answers survive*; from that moment ordinary per-scene keying governs, and the set does not re-form for that (server, file) while the run holds any scene in hand. A job submitted while the set stands carries no scene name (Y10) and composes from it (F5).

**J4. Nothing ever converts one kind into the other, and this is the clause the whole page rests on.** A value the user has not touched has **no store entry** — there is no materialise-on-focus, no copying a file value in as a starting point, and no writing a reading's value anywhere the payload reads. A value the user typed that happens to equal the file's is an ordinary store entry — the store records the act of answering, not a difference against a reading. This is `State, identity and recovery` D2's discipline — no desired state is ever inferred from an observed reading — applied to the page: the store is the desired half, the reading the observed half, and the arrow between them points only through the user's own act.

**J5. Clearing is deletion, and the hand-everything-back control clears the page in hand.** The per-setting cross deletes that entry. The hand-everything-back control deletes **the entries the page is showing** — the ticked scene's own per-scene entries, or the group frame range of J3 where more than one scene is ticked, or the pre-read answer set where no scene is in hand — plus the version override, which is the one entry that is not per scene and which the spec names in that control's own sentence. **Scenes the page is not showing keep their entries**: an act performed on the page in hand acts on the key in hand (J6), and the spec's *switching scenes keeps each scene's settings* is not undone by a control the user pressed while standing somewhere else. ~~It touches neither the scenes ticked nor anything in any reading, per the spec.~~ Where a reading stands it also unticks every scene and ticks the one that reading names as saved — else its first, as J7's third act does — under that reading's provenance, and deletes that scene's own entries; it touches nothing in any reading. *(Changed 2026-10-08, the spec's own change of that day, the Creator's fifth notes, note 4; no auditor has seen it.)* There is no *cleared* marker: an entry either exists or does not.

**J6. Switching scenes, files and servers is a change of key, not a change of contents** — which is the whole of how the spec's *switching keeps them* promises are kept. Nothing is saved on leaving or restored on returning; the entries were never keyed to the visit.

### 2.2 A name-shaped answer, and what it is held against

**Constraint:** Row 145. A ticked scene and a pinned view layer are stated by name; the spec states outright that nothing checks the name still exists; the cost of a wrong name is settled and silent — *a scene name no scene bears is named and then ignored and the run reports success* (*Settings 12*); and `Rendering` L15 refuses to send anything but the exact bytes a reading of the file produced.

**J7. A name-shaped answer is held against the name's exact bytes plus a recorded provenance, and the naming act is one of exactly ~~two~~ three** *(two until 2026-10-03; the third, the read's own tick of a scene, is the paragraph after this clause)*. Either the user ticked or pinned a thing drawn from a recorded reading — the bytes are that reading's own, and the provenance records that reading's identity (file identity, version, moment) — or the answer arrived from a history entry's kept setup, where the bytes are the old job's manifest's, which were themselves a reading's bytes by L15's provenance or absent where the job carried none. **The page offers no way to type a scene or view-layer name**, so there is no ~~third~~ further naming act: every name in the store is, by provenance, bytes some reading of some file produced. Never normalised, never matched case-insensitively, never re-derived.

**The third naming act is the read's own, and it ticks one scene, only where the store holds no tick the reading can stand behind. ## 10. The file-inspection worker kind

**Constraint:** `Rented-server runtime` W1 — the kind is *one reading of a `.blend`*, declarations owned by `Render Settings` and `Job files and outputs`; W2 — no termination contract exists until an owning document states one; §16 item 6 — the kind has neither declarations nor contract, so A6's cancellation cannot fire, A3's default costs throughput, and X11's fourth thing is unanswered, leaving X1 and X9 unsound for it. `Job files and outputs` did not declare and `Dependency Upload & Remap` §9 O2 recorded the housing as a seam. **This document is the last named owner, and this section is the declaration.**

**W1. Corrected 2026-08-22 at the stage-close gate.

- **Resource class:** the processors and the machine's memory, **shared**; no card. A reading drives a headless Blender that renders nothing.
- **Priority:** **below render; above preview and media.** A reading gates something a person is waiting on — a pre-load, a submission's material, a check — where a derivative is a byproduct; and the render stays first because it is the paid work.
- **Cancellable:** **yes, at any moment.** Nothing durable is half-written at any point: the reading is consumed only whole (Q7), so ending it anywhere loses a reading and corrupts nothing.
- **Atomic or partial:** **atomic** — a reading that did not complete is not a reading, the sentinel-framed document being the unit (Q7; the same rule as `Dependency Upload & Remap` G3).

**W2. The termination contract, X11's four named things:**

1. **Which member receives the termination: the recorded external Blender process — the interrupt, aimed by the recorded execution-group pair, never anything found by name, pattern or process search** (X6, X12's shape). The same signal the render kind uses, for the same reason the stop machinery already exists for it; what an interrupt leaves half-done inside a read does not matter, because an ended read is no reading.
2. **Terminable at any moment** — no safe boundary exists or is needed.
3. **Cleanup owed: the operation record's outcome and nothing else.** A reading stages nothing, publishes nothing and writes no durable material until its document has been returned whole and consumed by its caller; the captured streams are retained under the ordinary rules.
4. **The worker outlives every member of its group.** The file-inspection worker starts the one Blender, holds both streams to their end, reaps it, and reports; it never exits before the process it started. **Declared outright, because it is the one thing X1's leader-only recording and X9's leader-gone-means-group-gone are unsound without** — with it, a gone leader really does mean an ended group, and reconciliation never writes *re-run once* over a Blender still reading.

**W3. The group is the worker and the one Blender it starts**, recorded as X1's pair at the start; the render-start reading is not a separate group — it runs inside the admitted render operation as a step of the render worker, contending with nothing (`Rendering` §7), and this kind's declarations govern the reading only where it is its own operation: a pre-load, and whatever else the runner houses under the kind.

**W4.
