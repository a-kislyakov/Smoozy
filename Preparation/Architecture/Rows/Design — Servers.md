**Was not answered anywhere, in three parts, and they are one row because they are one promise. All three are answered as of 2026-08-21 and the findings below stand as the record of why the row exists.**

**1. The moment is produced nowhere.** `Preparation/Architecture/Servers.md` Section 15 supplies `Desktop app and delivery` *"the three values and their transitions"* and nothing else about the repair; **K7** says outright *"Servers supplies that value and the transitions, and presents neither."* Its hand-over to `Download` lists the two connection labels and what resumes on a replacement — again nothing about when the next attempt falls. `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` **T13** composes the connection notification from the **condition record** alone, and §16.1 M1 has presentation read records; `Preparation/Architecture/State, identity and recovery.md` 8.2 fixes what a condition record holds — *"the **condition identity** and the **set of observed values it was raised over**"* — and **it holds no moment, no attempt count and no schedule**, while `Servers` N5 says the failure record *"says only that the connection is down"*. **A screen drawn from that can say *Reconnecting…* and cannot say when.** The only countdown anywhere in the stage is `Preparation/Architecture/Queue & History.md` B9, which is the record of one being **refused**.

**2. The value is computable, and that is not the same as arranged — and a countdown needs a clock this side is declared to have only one of.** N2 fixes the pause at 15 seconds *"measured from the **end** of the previous attempt and never from its start"*, and N6 fixes the standing pause at 60 seconds; both are the desktop's own clock, so no cross-clock arithmetic is involved and nothing forbids the countdown. What is missing is that **no document says the presenter is told which pause is running or when it started** — and N2's own rule is why that matters: an attempt carries a 20-second connect deadline, so **the moment the next attempt falls is not known until the current attempt has failed**, and a countdown started when an attempt begins would be wrong for its whole first leg. Beneath that, `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` §3.3 **P9** — *"Nothing on this side polls"* — and **P10**: *"There is exactly one timer on this side and it is the update check … **A second timer anywhere in this application is a fault in this section rather than a case it does not cover.**"* **A per-second countdown is a second timer by that sentence's plain reading**, whether or not it refreshes any observed value. Either the moment is published and P10 is narrowed to say what a drawing may tick against, or the line says something that does not count down.

**3. *Reconnecting…* is not the reading the row carries for most of a repair, so the word and the countdown are rarely both true.** N7: *"It reads **reconnecting** while a run is in flight and **disconnected** between runs."* **The standing pause — the 60-second one, the longest, and the one a countdown would most want — is a stretch in which the server reads *disconnected*,** so the design's word for it is not *Reconnecting…* at all and the sentence gives that stretch no word. K6's table and N7 also do not agree about the 15-second in-run pause: K6 gives *reconnecting* only where *"an attempt is in flight"* and *disconnected* to *"a period stands with no attempt in flight"*, while N7 reads a whole run as in flight. **The design's row sits exactly on that seam.**

**One further consequence, stated because it is what architecture produces rather than a comment on the design.** The column offers one slot, and during a repair two of its four contents are true at once: `Preparation/Architecture/Servers.md` S12 keeps the last-known queue reading alive through a drop — *"it is what lets a dropped-connection server go on showing last-known entries for the rest of the run"* — so a repairing server that was rendering owes the same column both *Rendering* with its job's name **and** *Reconnecting…* at the same moment.

**1. The moment is now produced, and the row was right that nothing produced it.** `Servers` **N10a** publishes exactly one value per server row — ***the moment the next attempt is due***, on the desktop's own clock, and nothing else: no attempt count, no run number, no schedule. **The row's own hardest observation is what fixes when it can be written**: N2 measures the pause from the end of the previous attempt and an attempt carries a 20-second connect deadline, so the moment is unknown until the current attempt has failed — **so it is written at the instant an attempt ends and reads *absent* while one is in flight**, which is a value rather than a gap. **It covers N2's 15 seconds and N6's 60 identically** and whoever draws it never learns which pause it came from. It also covers 4.2's marked-period runs, and publishes nothing at all under N8b's permanent refusal, which is the correct answer rather than a missing one.

**Where it lives is the row's other correct observation acted on.** §8.2 fixes what a condition record holds — the condition identity and the set of observed values — **and a moment is neither**, so N10a puts it beside the record rather than inside it, run-held in §11.1's class, exactly as S12's last-known queue reading is. **Nothing durable is written from it**, and P7 has nothing to bind because it is a reading and not an outcome.

**2. The timer question, and the row understated it — P10 was already false of a closed document.** `Servers` N2's 15-second pause, N6's 60-second pause and 4.2's ten-minute floor are **each a scheduled wake-up that then reaches a machine**, which is a second timer by P10's plain reading, in a document that is closed, audited and correct. **P10a therefore narrows the rule to what it was always about: a *standing* timer — one that fires with nothing under way, on a schedule of its own, and starts work.** A delay inside a sequence a declared break started is that sequence's and ends with the period. **A drawing that re-renders a value the application already holds passes P10's own test more cleanly than the update check does** — no observed value refreshed, no record withdrawn, no machine reached, nothing posted on P6's queue. **P10b states it in the form a builder applies**, and it is narrower than the old wording rather than looser. **P10c names the sleeping computer** and does not defend it.

**3. The word, and the seam under it is repaired.** **K6a**: the *disconnected* row of K6's table read *"no attempt in flight"* and glossed itself *"the stretch between one run and the next"*, **and those are not the same stretch** — the in-run pause is the first and not the second. N7 had already ruled it in plain words, so the table now says **no run of attempts in flight**, and a run is in flight for its whole length. **That is the row's K6-against-N7 disagreement closed, and it changes no presentation.**

**And the *Reconnecting…* half is answered by keeping two vocabularies apart, which is what made this look unanswerable.** **M29**: K6's three values are the **condition's**, supplied to T13's notification and to whatever draws a server's state. **The work column's four contents are Design's own** — *Rendering* with a job's name, *Idle*, *Reconnecting…*, nothing — and **not one of them is one of K6's three**. What the column reads is **whether a repair is under way at all**, which is true from the declared break until the period ends, standing pauses included. **So the column says *Reconnecting…* for the whole repair while K6 goes on flickering underneath, and the spec was changed on 2026-08-22 to say the same.** *(Corrected the same day. This ended **and neither contradicts the other**, paraphrasing M29's own ending — which held only while the spec still promised the two-state flicker. It no longer does: the Creator retired that sentence and ruled the steady label stands, so the flicker is supply nothing draws rather than a second true account.)* **M30**: where the moment is absent the line has no countdown and the word stands alone, which is the honest reading of *an attempt is running right now*.

**The further consequence is answered by supplying both and choosing neither.** **M31**: a server that was rendering when the connection went is handed *Rendering* with its job's name **and** *Reconnecting…*, each with its own moment. **Which occupies the one slot is Design's**, and `Desktop app and delivery` Section 24 now says so by name. **What is forbidden is a third reading composed from the two**, because the last-known work reading is stale by construction and Row 323's answer requires it to say so.
