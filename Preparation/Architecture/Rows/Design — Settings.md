**Row 329 — the live speed reading, and the window it is averaged over.** *Transfers.*
> "**Each cap has what is actually running beside it.** The [Whole app spec](../../Specs/Whole%20app.md) requires the reading and leaves its place to Design; here is its place, because a cap can only be judged against a real number. An empty field is no cap, which is also the default."

and, from `Preparation/Design/Transfers.md`, under *Left for later*, which carries the same reading on its own screen:
> "- The two speed caps sit in [Settings](../../Design/Settings.md), each with what is actually running beside it. This screen carries the live reading and a link there."

Kind of promise: **a drafted design sentence**, both. The value is **a settled value** in `Preparation/Design/Mockup/Smoozy.html`, which draws the reading three times — split by direction in the Transfers header, again in the title bar where it is visible from every screen, and here as *running at 12.4 MB/s*.

**One promise, two documents, one number.** Merged at the consolidation.

Earns its row by the third test in its sharpest form: **a rate is not a thing the app has**; it is a thing something has to count and publish.

Waits on: **Facts 25 and 75**, both numbered and both answered. Nothing further to get.

**Partly answered.** What is answered: `Preparation/Architecture/Server protocol and transfers.md` §8.4 **R12** — the achieved rate is *"Smoozy's own count of bytes moved over a window, taken at the same accounting point as the enforcement, per channel, and summed"*, **never** read out of a tool's output and **never** derived from a file's size and a start time. **R13** makes it period-held, so nothing durable is written from it; **R14** says the reading is real and was observed, and closes it in the Design document's own terms — *"Where that reading sits on a screen is Design's; that there is one is Row 289's second clause and this is it."* `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` §11.4 **R15** consumes it as the reading presented to the user, and §11.1's two budgets, one per direction, are what let the header split the figure the way the mockup draws it. **R8** answers the empty field: where the user has set no cap the budget is unlimited. The unit question is settled at **R11** — bytes per second inside, what unit the control shows the user being `Desktop app and delivery`'s and Design's.

**The clause that is not answered: the length of the window the rate is averaged over is fixed nowhere.** R12 says *over a window* and stops. That number is what decides whether the figure on screen is steady enough to read or twitches every time a chunk lands, **which is the whole of what a *live reading* has to be**. The emission cadence beneath it **is** bounded — R6 has the byte loop *"emit its count at least once per a fixed short period whatever the chunk size"* — so the ingredients arrive often enough; what is undecided is what is done with them before they are drawn.

**Two limits ride with the answer and are the answering documents' own, not findings.** `Desktop app and delivery` **R16** — the down reading is *"a count of bytes Smoozy accepted rather than of bytes the line carried"*, the shared budget having been exercised on uploads only, **so the number beside the download cap can sit under the cap while the line carried more**, which is the one thing this Design sentence says the reading is for. And `Server protocol and transfers` **R10** — a reading taken over a window spanning a cap change *"reads between the two rates rather than at either"*.

**Answer, 2026-08-21: the window is the trailing three seconds, at `Preparation/Architecture/Server protocol and transfers.md` [R12a](../Server%20protocol%20and%20transfers.md#84-the-achieved-rate-reading), consumed at `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` [R15a](../Desktop%20app%20and%20delivery.md#114-the-achieved-rate-reading).** The length is a decision this stage took, not a value anybody published, and R12a says so in its own words.

- **The whole computation is stated**: the bytes the accounting point counted in the last three seconds, divided by three, with **no decay, no smoothing and no average of an earlier average** — so no rate from before a cap change survives past the bound R10 sets.
- **The number sits between two bounds the document already carried, and neither was added for it.** Below, **R6**'s byte loop emits its count *"at least once per a fixed short period whatever the chunk size"* and is consulted *"many times per second per channel"*, so three seconds holds many tens of counts and no single write moves the figure. Above, **R10**'s window spanning a cap change *"reads between the two rates rather than at either"*, which makes **the window's length exactly how long the figure beside a just-changed cap is wrong.** Three seconds bounds that at three seconds, **and the same holds after a channel is admitted or ends, which R12a's upper bound now names beside the cap change.**
- **One length for every channel and both directions**, because R12 sums the per-channel counts and a sum over windows of different lengths is not a rate. **Both ends are on the one clock the accounting point runs on**, so nothing crosses two clocks under D5.
- **Two edges are stated because a builder meets them at once.** Before a full window has passed **the divisor is the elapsed time and never the window**, so a transfer's first three seconds do not read low; and the reading is published at each accounting interval **and once more as zero at the moment a direction's last channel ends** — a trailing window is never run down, because running it down would need a timer `Desktop app and delivery` **P10b** forbids.
- **R15a is the consuming half.** The window is R12a's and that document holds no second one; **the sum is taken per direction**, a channel belonging to one direction by construction and drawing from that direction's budget, which is what puts a reading beside each of the two caps and lets the header split the figure. **A published reading is held and never aged** — P10b permits a drawing to tick against a moment and a rate is not a moment — with R12a's zero the thing that stops a finished batch leaving its last rate on screen for the rest of the run. The one unit conversion is R2's, at the settings edge.
- **The three seconds is one constant in one place**, and moving it changes no shape and no promise. **The shape is observed and the length is not.** `Server protocol and transfers` **R16**'s mid-flight run vindicated the bounded trailing window against a cumulative governor — **the length that behaved was five seconds and R12a fixes three** — and R12a says so in its own words from 2026-08-23. **No achieved-rate reading has been drawn at any window length on any machine** (§12 item 14), so *steady enough to read* is still an argument from R6's sample count rather than an observation. It closes at Build by watching one transfer.
- **The two existing limits still ride.** **R16** — the down reading counts bytes Smoozy accepted, not bytes the line carried. **R10** — a reading spanning a cap change reads between the two rates, now bounded at exactly three seconds rather than unbounded.

**Partly answered, and the unanswered part is that the Design sentence is flatter than the answer.** `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` Section 13.4 answers the picture in three parts. **macOS**: *"an attachment, and it must be a file on disk — JPEG, GIF or PNG only, 10 MB maximum, with no in-memory supply."* **Windows**: *"two Microsoft pages disagree and neither acknowledges the other"*, the contradiction inherited unresolved, and *"the notification still says the job finished with no picture where it does not work."* **Linux**: Section 22 item 13 — *"The Linux picture has no capability string … on Linux the picture may be dropped silently and nothing detects it."* **T17** adds a fourth case that is not platform-specific at all: *"A finished-job notification never waits on a fetch. The four kinds themselves are answered outright: Section 13.3 composes each of the four, 13.1 and 13.2 carry the per-system preconditions and permission models, and **[Section 24](../Desktop%20app%20and%20delivery.md#24-what-this-document-does-not-own)** lists *"how the per-kind switches are offered"* as Design's.
