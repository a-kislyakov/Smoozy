**Row 340 — the pasteboard, and what becomes of it when the browser moves.** *Acting on files.*
> "**Cut, copy and paste rather than *move to* and *copy to*.** A pasteboard holds until something replaces it, so one copy can be pasted into several folders in turn. Paste carries a count of what is on it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-10)."

Kind of promise: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-10).

Earns its row by the third test. Every other act in this document is one act at one moment: name a victim, name a destination, do it. **A pasteboard is a thing that persists between acts, and nothing else in this module persists between acts.** It has to survive folder navigation by design — that is the whole point of *pasted into several folders in turn* — and once it survives navigation the question is what else it survives. `Preparation/Specs/File Browser.md` states of a move and a copy that *"Both stay within the one machine"*, and the same spec has the browser **move to another server** on its own initiative when *show outputs* sends the user there. **So a cut taken on server A can be standing when the browser is on server B, where pasting it would be the cross-machine act the spec forbids.** The same question arises at a connection drop, at a period ending, and at a cut whose source was removed by the user's own second login before the paste.

Waits on: nothing outside. Everything this needs is inside the plan.

**Not answered anywhere, as this row was filed.** `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` then used the word *paste* exactly once, at [8.1](../File%20Browser.md#81-the-three-warning-readings-row-193) **W2** — *"For a delete, a move, a rename or a paste, the readings run when the destination (or the victim) is named, before the warning box"* — which assumes a paste happens and says when its warning fires. **It never said what holds the material between the cut and the paste, how long it holds, or what it is scoped to.** Section 7 answers move, rename, copy, delete and new folder; there is no pasteboard. `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` **K7** has a clipboard and it is the wrong one — *"Cut, copy and paste act on whatever has focus, so the Edit menu's three items are one set and the focused thing decides"* — the Edit menu acting on text and data in the focused field, not a hold of server-side file identities. Also looked at: `State, identity and recovery` §11.1's five local lifetimes, which a pasteboard fits by no name (the same shape as the listing tree's own gap recorded at that document's seam); `Servers` for what a period ending drops; and `Coverage.md` for any row phrased around cut, copy or paste. **Nothing.**

**What *is* answered, and it is the smaller half.** This document also says *"**Paste has to remember which it was.**"* — that a paste after a cut and a paste after a copy offer different answers on a collision. `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` **M8** settles exactly that: *"A colliding copy offers Replace, Keep both, or Cancel; a colliding move offers Replace or Cancel"*, judged by X5's comparison at the moment of the act. **The collision half was answered; the hold itself was not.**

**Answer, 2026-08-21: `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` [7.7](../File%20Browser.md#77-the-pasteboard-row-340), M11 to M18.**

- **One pasteboard, one thing on it, and it holds the server row, the installation identity, which act it was, and the named entries as paths.** Nothing else and no reading of any of them.
- **Its lifetime is the connected period's**, on `Servers` S11's list beside the other machine-keyed readings — **not the run's, and nothing durable is written from it.** **A drop does not clear it**, because a drop does not end a period, which is what lets a cut survive the connection being repaired under the user.
- **It survives navigation and a paste does not clear it**, which is the Creator's *pasted into several folders in turn* exactly. A cut pasted twice is two moves and the second refuses at the moment of the act like any other missing source.
- **The row's sharpest finding — a cut taken on server A standing while the browser is on server B — is answered by construction rather than by a check.** **Paste is offered only where the browsing server row and identity are the pasteboard's, and nowhere else is it greyed, refused or explained: it is not offered at all.** So there is no route by which a cross-machine paste can be attempted, and the spec's *"Both stay within the one machine"* is true without anybody enforcing it.
- **A recycled machine takes it with it**, because the identity is on the pasteboard and simply does not match.
- **Nothing on it is validated while it sits there**, deliberately: **a pasteboard that quietly emptied itself would lose the user a cut for a reason nothing on the screen could show.** The entries are names, and the machine's own refusal answers at the moment of the act, which is W2's ordering already.


**Row 341 — dragging files and folders onto the list.** *Uploading.*
> "**Dragging onto the list uploads into the folder being browsed.** The list takes a dashed border and says where the files will land and how many there are. Drag-and-drop is not in the [File Browser spec](../../Specs/File%20Browser.md), and does not need to be — the spec leaves Design *how uploading is started*, and this is a how."

Kind of promise: **a drafted design sentence.**

**Earns its row by the third test twice over, and it is the sharpest case in the document.** First, **it rests on an outside fact**: whether an application is handed the paths of files *and folders* dropped onto its window is a property of each operating system and of the shell the application runs in, not a drawing decision Build can make true. Second, **it promises a value at a moment nothing has been arranged to supply one**: *"says where the files will land and how many there are"* — the count is of files, and a dropped folder's file count is not known until the folder has been walked. `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` **X1** puts that walk in the pre-flight, **after** the user has confirmed an upload: *"The local side is the enumeration of U1 — every file inside every folder the user pointed at."* The dashed border is shown before any of that.

Waits on: **a fact to get, by reading each operating system's own documentation and the chosen shell's** — whether an application on each of Mac, Windows and Linux receives the paths of files and folders dropped onto it, whether a dropped folder arrives as a folder rather than as nothing, and what is available about the drop before it is accepted. **No rental**; Smoozy does not run on the rented machine. **`Preparation/Architecture/Facts.md` holds no such fact.** Fact 96 covers which shells can host Smoozy and what their release artifacts weigh; Fact 98 covers how each platform provides a menu bar; neither reaches this. The nearest numbered neighbours — Facts 22, 23, 24 and 73 — are all the same shape, a capability of the three operating systems read from their own documentation, and **this one was never numbered.**

**Not answered anywhere, and one architecture document is resting on it.** `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md` §18 supplies menus and the picker, and **H6's eight required properties** of the shell *(seven when this row was filed; the eighth, on the window frame following the system's light-or-dark setting, was added 2026-08-21)* name the native menu bar and the native file picker at property 6 and **say nothing about receiving a drop** — so a shell chosen against that list is not chosen for this. `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` Section 10 **H4** mentions it once and **uses** it: *"dragging onto the list, which Design already carries, takes any mix everywhere."* **That clause is part of what makes Row 307's negative cost nothing**, and it is an assertion about three operating systems with no fact, no citation and no source-file entry behind it — that document's sources file is cited at H1–H3 for the picker and not here. Searching the whole of `Preparation/Architecture/` for *drag* and *drop* returns that one line and three unrelated uses.

**Scope.** This row is about whether the capability exists and what the app can know at the moment of the drop. It is not about the dashed border, its colour, or its wording, all of which are Build's.

**Answer, 2026-08-21: `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` [H3a](../File%20Browser.md#10-the-picker-row-307), on **Fact 120**, numbered and answered the same day.** **The row was right that H4 was resting on nothing; it is not the case that no fact was gettable.**

- **The capability is the shell's own and its documentation names folders outright** — *"This part of the runtime handles dragging and dropping **files and or folders** in to the window"* — with one option, off by default, that *"returns the dropped in files' absolute paths"*. **So H4's *any mix everywhere* is the shell's own statement rather than this document's assumption**, and the clause that made Row 307's negative cost nothing now has a citation.
- **The row's second limb is upheld and answered against the design.** What arrives is **paths and nothing else** — no sizes, no counts, no walk — so **nothing about what is inside a dropped folder is known at the drop**, and the count on the border is a count of **what was dropped**. X1's enumeration stays exactly where it is, after the user has confirmed; **nothing moves earlier.**
- **One hazard is named because nothing else names it**: the shell exposes a separate switch to stop dragged files opening inside the web view, and **a build that enables the drop and not that switch can open a dropped `.blend` in the interface.** Both are set.
- **What is not established** is any per-platform difference — the pages state none and this project has observed none — carried at `Desktop app and delivery` Section 22 beside that document's note on the shell's version notice.


**Row 342 — the folder that cannot be read, marked before it is opened.** *The list.*
> "**A folder the login cannot read stays in the list.** Smoozy cannot tell before it is opened whether the login can read into it — the [architecture](../File%20Browser.md) P2a rules out the per-entry probe and settles that a free one would not answer the question either — so nothing marks it in advance. Opening it is what produces the answer: the row says the login cannot read the folder, and where the machine allows one, the offer to try again with admin access is beside it. It is not silently absent: the spec's rule is to show what can be shown and say plainly what could not."

Kind of promise: **a drafted design sentence.**

Earns its row by the third test. `Preparation/Specs/File Browser.md` promises that Smoozy *"shows what it can and says plainly what it could not"* — a promise about honesty when a refusal is met. **This sentence promises something further: that the row is already greyed and already carrying its reason in a listing the user has not clicked into.** That is a per-entry fact about the login's own access, for every entry, at listing time — the exact shape the third test hunts.

Waits on: **Fact 7**, already numbered and answered — what listing a folder over the connection yields, and whether *this login may not read it* can be told from *the folder is gone* and from *the machine did not answer*. And **Fact 27**, also answered, for the elevation half.

**Partly answered, and the two halves of the sentence come apart.**

**The half that is answered, and answered well.** `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` 2.1 **P2**: *"Inside a listed folder the separation is whole and free. Opening a child folder is a readdir on the subsystem: a permission refusal, an absence and a dead machine arrive as three distinct answers with controls either side (Fact 7 §5.5). The user is told which it was in as many words, and the elevation offer of 2.2 is attached to the refusal case alone."* **P3** adds the component walk for a path the browser lands on directly. So *saying why* and *the offer beside it* are both settled — **at the moment the user opens the folder.**

**The half that is not.** **Nothing arranges for the row to be greyed before that.** **L1** states what one listing yields — *"the four fields — name, size, last-changed time, kind — come back in that one reading, batched"* — and adds *"**No reading here ever issues a stat per entry**"*. **X1** repeats the prohibition for the pre-flight walk: *"A stat per landing place is never issued."* **The per-entry probe that would let the browser know in advance is the one thing both clauses forbid**, and for a stated reason: 52 round trips rather than 5010. Nothing else supplies the knowledge another way. **So as the architecture stands, an unreadable folder lists like any other and is discovered on the click, which is not what this sentence says.**

**And a second clause of the sentence is answered with a negative.** *"with the offer to try again with admin access beside it"* is unconditional here. **P5** makes the offer conditional on the machine, from a four-outcome table: on **a container under a non-root login there is no offer at all** — Fact 27, *"that login's elevation route **demands a password Smoozy does not hold**"*, `sudo -n` demanding one and the switch to root failing authentication, and *"a password Smoozy does not have is as good as no route"* *(P5's container row said **no elevation route at all exists from that login** when this row was filed and was corrected on 2026-08-22 and again on 2026-08-23; the outcome — no offer — never moved)* — and on **a machine the kind table does not place**, no offer either, *"there is no flow to start and nothing is established about what elevation would do there."* **So there is a real case where the refusal is shown with nothing beside it.**

**Answer, 2026-08-21: `Preparation/Architecture/File Browser.md` [P2a](../File%20Browser.md#21-the-limit-and-the-walk-that-recovers-the-separation-row-181), and the architecture holds on both halves.**

- **The promise's own stated purpose is kept whole and for free, which the row did not separate out.** *"It is not silently absent"* — **a folder is listed because its parent's reading names it, and being unable to read *into* a folder is a different permission from seeing its name in the parent.** The folder always stays in the list.
- **The marking cannot be done, and the row found the reason: L1 and X1 both forbid the per-entry probe**, for a measured cost of 52 round trips against 5010.
- **And a free probe would not answer the question either**, which is the stronger half: *can **this login** read into this folder* is not answered by what the machine says about the entry — supplementary groups, access-control lists and the login's own privilege all bear on it. **A mark composed from it would be a prediction, and a wrong prediction stops a user opening a folder they could have opened.** P5 already takes the elevation readings *at the refusal* and never before one, for exactly this reason.
- **So the refusal is met on the click and told in as many words (P2), and *before it is opened* is not kept.** The drafted design sentence is parked rather than repaired here.
- **The second clause is answered with a negative, and P5 owns it**: on a container under a non-root login there is no offer at all. **A refusal shown with nothing beside it is a real state and the design has no drawing for it**, which is parked with the first.

**Re-quoted 2026-08-25, at the Design close re-sweep. The promise genuinely moved, and it moved to the opposite of what this row's title says — which is Design conforming to the architecture, not breaking it.** The document no longer marks an unreadable folder before it is opened. It states that the mark cannot be made, asserts P2a's own two grounds — that L1 and X1 rule out the per-entry probe, and that a free probe would still be a prediction — cites P2a by clause, and puts the answer on the click, which is P2. **The answer holds entirely and nothing reopens**: nothing in the new wording asks for anything P2a refuses. Everything above this note is kept as the record of what the row was filed against.

**Row 343 — **Check dependencies…** offered on every `.blend`, in a toolbar with two states.** *Acting on files.*
> "**What the toolbar offers changes with what is chosen, and only a `.blend` changes it.** Choosing one puts **Render settings…** and **Check dependencies…** at the head of the toolbar, before Upload and Download. Kind of promise: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

Earns its row by the third test. The rule is two-state — the file is a `.blend` and both controls are there, or it is not and they are gone — and it is stated as following from *can this control apply to what is selected*. **For one of the two controls the answer is not decided by the file's kind.** A dependency check works from where the file came from on the user's own computer; a `.blend` sitting on the server that Smoozy did not upload, or whose upload record the user has since cleared, has no such origin. **The kind test says the control applies; the knowledge test says it may not. That is a third state the rule as written has no room for, and it exists in the architecture by name.**

Waits on: nothing outside. It is a question about what Smoozy's own records hold.
