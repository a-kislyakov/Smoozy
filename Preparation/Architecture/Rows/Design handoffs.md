**Row 304 — the two typography rules applied by whatever renders the text.** *Typography*, in `Preparation/Design/Whole app.md`
> "**Neither can be held by remembering it.** Nobody counts dashes while designing a screen, and both rules have already been broken by the person applying them. So they are applied by whatever renders the text rather than by hand. That is Architecture's to arrange; what is settled here is that it must be arranged. **They are applied by substituting the characters themselves**, at a single point every piece of prose passes through on its way to a surface — which is what makes them hold in tooltips, button labels and menu items that no layout engine draws. **Two things narrow that, and whoever writes a screen has to know both.** The point takes each piece of text handed to it as one piece: it binds the last two words at the end of it, and does not go looking for sentence ends inside it. So several sentences handed over together get the orphan rule once, at the end of the last one; where it has to hold for each of them — a failure's message that runs to two sentences is the ordinary case — they are handed over one sentence at a time. **That one thing is left to whoever writes the screen**, deliberately: a point that hunted for sentence ends would bind the wrong two words wherever it guessed wrong. And the point is for prose alone. The server's own words, paths, file names, hosts, ports and anything the user can copy reach the screen untouched, because a non-breaking space inside a path is a path that does not exist — so *everywhere the user reads*, above, means everywhere the user reads prose. Label: **settled at Architecture** (2026-08-23)." *(Re-quoted 2026-08-23 at the Part 6.3 re-sweep: the source sentence changed when the runner's Part 6.4 clearing was repaired. The row does not reopen and does not change bucket.)*

A non-breaking space before every em dash, and the last two words of any prose bound together so no sentence ends with one word alone on a line. Both hold everywhere the user reads, tooltips and button labels included. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-10). The row exists because the Design document says outright that neither rule can be kept by hand and that arranging it is this stage's — so the answer is a property of whatever draws text, not an instruction to whoever writes a screen.

Waits on: Nothing to get.

**Answer: `Preparation/Architecture/Desktop app and delivery.md`, Sections 17.1, 17.2 and 17.3.** 17.1 puts **one application point** on the path every piece of prose takes to a surface, and makes both rules **character substitutions** — a non-breaking space before the em dash, and a non-breaking space between the last two words. 17.2 is the reason it is a substitution rather than a layout rule, and it is this row's own test: **a layout rule holds only where the thing doing the layout is under Smoozy's control**, and the two rules must hold in tooltips, button labels and menu items that no engine draws. **A character substitution travels with the text into every surface, including native ones.** 17.3 lists what it must never touch — the raw record, paths, and anything copyable — because a substituted character in a path is a path that does not resolve, **and it names the route those strings take instead**: the interface accepts prose only from the typography point and data only from a verbatim route that performs no substitution of any kind, the two being the complete set, so the boundary is kept by there being no other way in rather than by anybody remembering it.

**The technology choice is what makes it hold**, and the document says so: the three system web views are three different engines, so a rule resting on any engine's layout behaviour would hold on one and fail on the others.

**One half of the rule is narrowed, and the narrowing is recorded rather than closed by a parser.** The point binds the last two words of the string it is handed and deliberately does not parse sentences out of a paragraph, so a caller wanting the rule to hold inside a multi-sentence block hands **each sentence as its own string**. Where a caller does not, interior sentence endings go unbound — which is this row's second rule — and it is carried as unresolved rather than fixed by teaching the point to find sentence ends, because finding them is modelling and it binds the wrong pair of words wherever the model is wrong.

**Answer: `Preparation/Architecture/Server protocol and transfers.md`, Section 8.3 — and the answer is yes.** A cap reaches transfers already running, because the enforcement point is Smoozy's own byte loop and the shared budget is consulted at **each accounting interval** rather than once when a transfer starts. So a change to the cap is picked up by every transfer in flight at its next interval, and Design can be written on the assumption that the control means what it appears to mean.

**Round 1 bounded how long *at its next interval* can be, and a later run showed that bound was not the whole of it. Corrected 2026-08-23 at the Part 6.3 re-sweep.** **That is three of four conditions and not four**: `Server protocol and transfers` **R9**, corrected 2026-08-22, says in its own words that *this listed three, and the run proved the three insufficient*. The fourth is **R6b** — the window the achieved rate is measured over is itself bounded and is never the transfer's whole life — and R9 states what the first three deliver without it: three make the change **arrive**, only the fourth makes it **take effect now**. With the fourth absent and the other three in place, a cap raised mid-flight was answered by a burst to roughly double the new cap held for minutes, and a cap lowered by a fall to about a quarter of it. The interval was written as a byte quantity alone, which on a slow line is an unbounded delay; it is now bounded in both quantities — the largest write **and** a fixed short period, whichever comes first — so the delay before a changed cap takes hold is bounded by that period rather than by how long one write takes to move.

**It follows from the design Fact 75 forced rather than from a separate decision**, which is why it could be answered at all: a per-invocation cap could only ever have been set when a transfer began, so the arrangement that keeps Row 289's promise is the same arrangement that makes this yes.

**The mid-flight change has now been run, and the limit that stands is a different one.
