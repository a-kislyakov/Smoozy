### Section 1.3 — rules that bind every store

**Rule 2, shared temporary areas.** [Six machines](../Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 6. Label: **observed**. `/tmp` is wiped at every boot, and `/var/tmp` *"is the only temporary area that both survives a reboot and sits on disk, which also makes it an easy place to leave one rental's data for the next tenant"*. **Not covered:** what any other provider's image does with either area, and whether a provider sweeps `/var/tmp` on a schedule of its own.

**Rule 3, the state area's mount.** [Two more boxes](../Two%20more%20boxes%20—%20the%20volume,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20a%20day%20left%20running.md), Fact 28. Label: **observed**. A plain reading on one container returned **51 filesystems**, 48 of them memory-backed; of the three that were not, one was *"the **host's** disk, bind-mounted in to deliver the driver files"*, showing 20 GiB free the rental may not touch. Free space *"is not a reading, it is a choice of which mount to read."* **Not covered:** the filesystem count on any other image, and what a virtual machine's own list looks like. The same fact is what [8.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value) puts the location into the condition key for.

**Rule 4, first half — a full filesystem.** [Six machines](../Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 28. Label: **observed**. On a full filesystem the writability test *"**returns writable**"* and *"**creating an empty file succeeds**"*; only writing at least one byte fails. **Not covered:** a filesystem full for a reason other than the one filled here, and the same sequence on a quota rather than a full device.

**Rule 4, second half — an attached volume's ownership.** [Two more boxes](../Two%20more%20boxes%20—%20the%20volume,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20a%20day%20left%20running.md), Fact 6. Label: **observed**. as delivered, `/vol` is *"`root:root 755`"* and the non-root login was refused — `touch: cannot touch '/vol/.probe2': Permission denied` — until a setup step handed it over. **Not covered:** one provider's volume delivery on one box; nothing establishes that every provider's attached storage arrives the same way.

**Rule 5, first observation — a killed transfer.** [The rented machine](../The%20rented%20machine.md). Label: **observed**. A killed transfer leaves *"a piece of the file under the final name. No marker, no `.part`, no temporary name, nothing to say it is incomplete"*. **Not covered:** any transfer tool other than the one run, and the same interruption at a point other than the one it was killed at.

**Rule 5, second observation — a machine restarted mid-render.** [The rented machine](../The%20rented%20machine.md). Label: **observed**. Eleven whole frames, and *"Six further files sat beside them at **zero bytes**"*, so *"the output folder said seventeen and the truth was eleven"*. **Not covered:** one restart on one box; the count is that run's and not a rule about how many zero-byte files a restart leaves.

**Rule 5, third observation — Blender on a full disk.** [The rented machine](../The%20rented%20machine.md). Label: **observed**. Blender *"announces the save, exits 0, says nothing about the disk, and leaves a picture that does not end"*. **This is a Blender fact and it is not re-derived here** — the observation of record is that input document's, on one box on one day, and no Blender Research answer is assembled for it. **Not covered:** which versions were run, and whether the same holds for an output format other than the one written.

**Rule 6's motivating loss.** The kernel-level reset that left *"no frame's contents at all"*, including a frame reported saved seconds earlier, reaches this document through [Section 13](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#13-unresolved) item 6 and [Section 6](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine), which cite [Queue & History](../../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) *"citing its own sources"*. **That chain is accurate and it ends outside this stage.** The Specs sources file labels it **observed in a test** and names the joined Blender answer **Frames 13** — in all four versions on one rented Linux machine, with *"which directory entries survived at all differ[ing] between two runs of the same test"*. The entry is named and nothing further is assembled from it. **Not covered:** an actual power cut, which is Fact 44 and unposable (below).

### Section 2.1 — the rules that bind every identity

**Rule 3, an identity is never derived from what the user typed.** [Six machines](../Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 29. Label: **observed**. Two jobs given the same label are refused by a plain make-directory call *"but the make-parents form accepts it silently — so the second job overwrites the first"*. **Not covered:** any collision route other than a directory name, and label handling on a filesystem other than the one observed.

**Rule 4, an identity is never derived from a machine reading.** [The rented machine](../The%20rented%20machine.md). Label: **observed**. The rental's own name was `ubuntu` and *"Nothing about it identifies which machine was rented"*; its address and port are the provider's mapping and the machine does not know either; its rental number sits in a settings block that also *"hold[s] secrets in plain text"*. **Not covered:** one provider's image; another may set a distinctive hostname. The companion sentence — [Whole app](../../Specs/Whole%20app.md)'s *"no provider exists anywhere in the app itself"* — is a **decision** and earns no entry.

**Rule 5, never a process identifier.** [Batch A — the shape throwaway](../Batch%20A%20—%20the%20shape%20throwaway.md), Fact 68. Label: **observed**. A stop aimed by pattern *"matched **two** process ids"*, was handed a malformed argument, and the work carried on: *"So a stop must be aimed at a recorded identity, never at a pattern"*. **Not covered:** one throwaway on one box; the count of matches is that run's.

**Rule 6, never a reading of the user's computer.** [Facts](../Facts.md) Fact 11, whose observation of record is [The computer itself](../The%20computer%20itself%20—%20its%20name,%20its%20identity,%20and%20its%20clock.md). Label: **documented**, negative on all three systems — every identifier an ordinary downloaded application can read is one the operating system hands over on request, and each vendor documents that it can be changed or spoofed. The consequence is written as a constraint in [Security and licence](../Security%20and%20licence.md#121-the-local-computer-identity-is-forgeable-on-all-three-systems) §12.1 and is taken from there rather than re-decided. **Not covered:** *"Forgeability itself was never tested on any system"* — all three are documented assertions rather than demonstrations.

### Section 2.3 — the machine's installation identity

**The recycle, and it is the sharpest recovery hazard in the input documents.** [Six machines](../Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), section *Recycle*. Label: **observed**. *"Everything Smoozy installed was destroyed"*, down to the `cron` package, with the package count back to the image's; the boot hook *"was not deleted but replaced"* by an 82-byte provider default dated at the recycle moment, so *"a check for 'does my hook exist' passes while the hook does nothing Smoozy asked for"*; and *"nothing in the connection distinguishes a recycled machine from a rebooted one"* — same address, same port, back in 90 seconds against 15–20 for a reboot. *"A Smoozy that reconnects and finds its address answering has no signal that its whole installation is gone. It must check for its own state, not for reachability."* **Not covered:** one provider's recycle action on one container; no other seller's equivalent was run, and the 90-second figure is that recycle's.

**A previous tenant's leavings survive the disk.** [The rented machine](../The%20rented%20machine.md). Label: **observed**. *"The record of a boot from November 2025 was still on the disk in August 2026."* **Not covered:** how far back any other provider's images carry residue, and whether any seller wipes between tenancies.

**Fact 83.** [Facts](../Facts.md) Fact 83, observations of record [The host key](../The%20host%20key%20—%20obtained%20without%20authenticating,%20and%20whose%20it%20turns%20out%20to%20be.md) and [The last two boxes](../The%20last%20two%20boxes%20—%20the%20hook%20that%20cannot%20be%20read%20back,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20the%20unit%20that%20kills%20a%20render.md) §17. Label: **observed**. **The proxy half is answered, 2026-08-17** — the three keys offered through `ssh3.vast.ai:28452` are *"**byte-for-byte the machine's own `/etc/ssh/ssh_host_*_key.pub`**"*, so the proxy forwards rather than terminating with an identity of its own. **And the key is still not a name for what [2.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#23-the-machines-installation-identity) has to recognise:** a fresh rental on the same physical machine presented byte-identical keys after the previous one was destroyed, a container's keys are the host's, a virtual machine's are shipped in the image, and *"Whatever survives a recycle for identification purposes, it is neither the endpoint nor the host key"*. **What Fact 83 leaves open is a different question** — whether one virtual-machine template's private host key travels with the image across physical hosts — and it is entered under *No source in this project* below. **The document's §2.3 carried the answered half as though it were the open one until 2026-08-23**, when the ground was corrected; the conclusion it draws never rested on it and is unchanged. **Not covered by the answered half:** the files were read over an authenticated session through the same proxy, so a middle lying consistently on both would not show up.

### Section 4 — the event journal

**E3, confirm after the work is done.** [One box — the Telegram capture](../One%20box%20—%20the%20Telegram%20capture%20and%20the%20port%20that%20cannot%20be%20chosen.md). Label: **observed**. ### Section 5.2 — the execution-group pairing

**The mechanism.** [Facts](../Facts.md) Fact 91, **ANSWERED 2026-08-17**, observations of record [The host key](../The%20host%20key%20—%20obtained%20without%20authenticating,%20and%20whose%20it%20turns%20out%20to%20be.md) and, on the re-posing of 2026-08-18, [The last two boxes](../The%20last%20two%20boxes%20—%20the%20hook%20that%20cannot%20be%20read%20back,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20the%20unit%20that%20kills%20a%20render.md) §5. Label: **observed**. *"The group leader's identity recorded together with its start time — field 22 of `/proc/<identifier>/stat`"*, written by the kernel at fork; `/proc/self/stat` is read-only and a write was refused with `errno 22 Invalid argument` on both machine kinds. The reuse case was forced on a virtual machine: the recorded pair **(identifier 1493, start time 19186)** was killed, the next identifier handed out was set, the new unrelated process received **1493**, and validating gave **`exists=True matches=False`**. **The container's three refusals:** `pid_max` is 4194304 so natural wrap is untestable; writing the next-identifier control gives **`errno 30 Read-only file system`**; and making a namespace of one's own gives **`unshare: unshare failed: Operation not permitted`**. **Not covered, and the document carries the qualifier in place:** start time is in ticks since boot, so *"it is meaningful only within one boot"*. The container half's *mechanism* is a kernel property; only the demonstration wanted the virtual machine.

**The defunct-entry hole.** [Facts](../Facts.md) Facts 91 and 49, observation of record [The last two boxes](../The%20last%20two%20boxes%20—%20the%20hook%20that%20cannot%20be%20read%20back,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20the%20unit%20that%20kills%20a%20render.md) §9. Label: **observed**, twice on this project's own rentals. *"The supervisor never waits on the workers it starts"*, so a killed worker persists as a defunct entry whose start-time field still reads exactly what was recorded — *"the recorded pair validates for a process that no longer exists"*. Under Fact 49, immediately after a hard cut, *"with **no setup worker alive at all**, the machine reported the recorded pair as still validating"*; only the reboot, which reaped the entry, flipped it. **Not covered:** Facts.md records this as *"a property of the throwaway's reaping rather than of the pairing mechanism"*, which is exactly why [5.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#53-the-deliberate-stop) step 6 names the wait rather than changing the pair.

**The one case where validation does the work.** [Batch A — the shape throwaway](../Batch%20A%20—%20the%20shape%20throwaway.md), Fact 1. Label: **observed**. A detached program outlived the connection and the shell that started it — *"Its parent is **1**"*. **Not covered:** one detachment arrangement on one box.

**The boot identifier is a trap on a container.** [Six machines](../Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 48. Label: **observed**. Across two container restarts the host's uptime *"rose monotonically"* and the host never rebooted. **Not covered:** one container on one host; the finding is that the reading is the host's, not a figure about any particular host.

**Item 10, Fact 92 — the machine's clock across a render.** [Facts](../Facts.md) Fact 92, **ANSWERED 2026-08-18**, observation of record [The last two boxes](../The%20last%20two%20boxes%20—%20the%20hook%20that%20cannot%20be%20read%20back,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20the%20unit%20that%20kills%20a%20render.md) §13. ### `fsync(2)`, the Linux man-pages project — [1.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#13-rules-that-bind-every-store) rule 6

**Source:** `fsync(2)`, the Linux man-pages project, read **2026-08-20** at `https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/fsync.2.html`. Label: **documented**, first-party. Address, date and words all carried by the document itself. Exact words:

> "fsync() transfers ("flushes") all modified in-core data of ... the file referred to by the file descriptor fd to the disk device (or other permanent storage device) so that all changed information can be retrieved even if the system crashes or is rebooted. This includes writing through or flushing a disk cache if present."

> "Calling fsync() does not necessarily ensure that the entry in the directory containing the file has also reached disk. For that an explicit fsync() on a file descriptor for the directory is also needed."

**What it carries:** the four-part definition of *written durably* — bytes written, file flushed, rename after the flush where a name publishes the write, and the holding directory flushed too. The last clause is the one ordinarily missed and it is this page's.

**Not covered:** any non-Linux system, which is why the macOS entry below exists and why Windows is unresolved; a device that lies about completing the flush; and the cost, which the document states in place — a flush blocks until the device reports the transfer complete, so every *durably before* is a real wait.

### `fsync(2)`, macOS — [1.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#13-rules-that-bind-every-store) rule 6

**Source:** `fsync(2)`, read on the Creator's own Mac with `man 2 fsync` on **2026-08-20**, **macOS 26.5.2** — the machine identified across this project's own runs as macOS 26.5.2, build 25F84, arm64. Label: **documented**, first-party, read locally rather than at an address. Exact words:

> "while fsync() will flush all data from the host to the drive ... the drive itself may not physically write the data to the platters for quite some time and it may be written in an out-of-order sequence"

> "This is not a theoretical edge case."

> "Mac OS X provides the F_FULLFSYNC fcntl. The F_FULLFSYNC fcntl asks the drive to flush all buffered data to permanent storage."

**What it carries:** on the user's own computer a durable write is the full-flush form and on a rented machine it is the ordinary one. The difference is not cosmetic, which is the second reason the word could not be left undefined.

**Not covered:** one macOS version on one machine; a drive that does not honour the full-flush request; and Windows, which is unresolved below.

### `rename(2)` — [1.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#13-rules-that-bind-every-store) rule 6

**Source:** `rename(2)`, the Linux man-pages project, read **2026-08-20** at `https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/rename.2.html`, and re-read at that address on **2026-08-23**; page footer *"Linux man-pages 6.18            2026-02-08"*. Exact words:

**What it carries:** rule 6's closing sentence — the rename's own atomicity is guaranteed by the call and is **not** durability of what it points at. **Not covered:** atomicity across two independent files, which Fact 78 answers negatively and which [Section 13](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#13-unresolved) item 2 carries; and any non-Linux system.

### `wait(2)`, Linux — [5.3](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#53-the-deliberate-stop) step 6

**Source:** `wait(2)`, the Linux man-pages project, read **2026-08-20** and read again at the publication itself on **2026-08-23** at `https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/wait.2.html`; page footer *"Linux man-pages 6.18            2026-02-08"*. Label: **documented**, first-party. **The address was missing from the document and from everything under `Preparation/` until 2026-08-23; it was got by reading the page rather than carried across from anywhere.** **Linux is the right platform for this claim and not a convenience:** the claim is about the supervisor on a rented machine, every rented machine this project has taken is Linux, and the desktop side starts no workers this step governs. **The words on the page are the words the document already carried**, character for character. Exact words:

> "A child that terminates, but has not been waited for becomes a 'zombie'. The kernel maintains a minimal set of information about the zombie process (PID, termination status, resource usage information) in order to allow the parent to later perform a wait to obtain information about the child."

The document's second use of the page — *"a departed supervisor's unreaped children are adopted and reaped by the system's own first process, which `wait(2)` states on the same page"* — is a paraphrase. The words behind it, on the same page and read the same day, are:

> "If a parent process terminates, then its 'zombie' children (if any) are adopted by init(1), (or by the nearest 'subreaper' process as defined through the use of the prctl(2) PR_SET_CHILD_SUBREAPER operation); init(1) automatically performs a wait to remove the zombies."

**What it carries:** the instrument step 6 had been missing. Without the wait, a worker that ended on its own still validates at step 3, step 4's termination is absorbed by a defunct entry, step 5's waiters expire, and *when the group has ended* never arrives. And the supervisor's own departure is safe, because the adoption clause means [Section 6](../State,%20identity%20and%20recovery.md#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine)'s table meets a pair that correctly fails to validate.

**Not covered:** the page is Linux's and every rented machine is Linux, so nothing here reaches the desktop; and a worker that hangs rather than ending, which no wait resolves.
