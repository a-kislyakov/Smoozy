**J7. No stored sequence is trusted further than the signed statement stored beside it, and a sequence whose statement will not verify is discarded rather than obeyed. Added 2026-08-22, out of the third sanity sweep's verification round.** Each row above already retains the statement that carried its value; this rule says what that retention is for. **Before a stored sequence is used to refuse anything, the statement retained with it is verified against its own root** — the same verification that admitted it. Where the statement is **absent, malformed, or fails that verification**, the stored sequence is **unsupported**: it is discarded together with the statement, the record falls back to holding neither, and the next genuine statement is admitted on its own merits and re-establishes the floor.

**J8. Why this is a correction and not a weakening.** Each of the two refuses on a comparison against a stored number, and until this clause no route back existed from a number that read too high — an ordinary record damage, or any same-user process writing the file, and the update record refuses every genuine manifest for good and silently. **It gives an attacker nothing**, because raising a sequence still requires a statement that verifies under a root they do not hold, and lowering one was never protected in the first place: [Security and licence](Security%20and%20licence.md#9-the-local-record) §9 keeps this record unencrypted and user-owned by decision, so **anyone willing to set a value high on purpose can already delete the whole record**, which is a fresh install. An unrecoverable state therefore cost the honest user everything and the dishonest one nothing.

**J9. What it does not reach.** A damaged record that takes the statement and sequence together to a value that still verifies is forgery, not damage. Losing the whole record also loses its stored sequences; both retained records still require verified statements.

### 1.3 Rules that bind every store

1. **One writer.** The supervisor is the only writer of every durable store in [1.2](#12-on-each-rented-server) except the staging areas, which are written under its control. That serialization is the shape's, and it is what makes every rule below implementable as a rule rather than as a hope.

   **Two further stores are written by a worker under the supervisor's control, named 2026-08-22 at the Architecture seam close, routed by the runner from [Rented-server runtime](Rented-server%20runtime.md#16-unresolved) §16 item 5.** The **captured streams** are written by the render worker rather than by the supervisor — M3's own ground is that a supervisor holding the capture stops mid-render in exactly the case the capture is most needed — and the **job record directory** is written by the render worker at the moments `Job files and outputs` fixes, the attempt record before the render starts among them. **The rule's substance is unchanged**: each of these stores has exactly one writer, that writer is started and bounded by the supervisor, and no second actor writes either. What is corrected is the claim that the writer is always the supervisor itself.
2. **No store on a rented machine may sit under a shared temporary area.** Observed — `/tmp` is wiped at every boot and `/var/tmp` *"is the only temporary area that both survives a reboot and sits on disk, which also makes it an easy place to leave one rental's data for the next tenant"* ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 6).
3. **The state area's mount is recorded, and every free-space claim about state writes names that mount.** Observed — a plain reading on one container returned **51 filesystems**, 48 of them memory-backed, and of the three that were not, one was *"the **host's** disk, bind-mounted in to deliver the driver files"* showing 20 GiB free the rental may not touch ([Two more boxes](Two%20more%20boxes%20—%20the%20volume,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20a%20day%20left%20running.md), Fact 28). Free space *"is not a reading, it is a choice of which mount to read."*
4. **Placement of the state area is confirmed by a real write of at least one byte, never by a writability test and never by creating an empty file.** Observed — on a full filesystem *"the writability test **returns writable**, and **creating an empty file succeeds**"*, and only writing at least one byte fails ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 28). Observed on the volume case — an attached second filesystem is delivered *"`root:root 755`"* and refuses the non-root login until a setup step hands it over ([Two more boxes](Two%20more%20boxes%20—%20the%20volume,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20a%20day%20left%20running.md), Fact 6), so a state area may never be placed on a mount whose writability by the supervisor's own login has not been demonstrated at the moment it is needed.
5. **Nothing in this document reads a file's presence, its name or its size as state.** Every state fact comes from a record Smoozy or the supervisor wrote. Three observations force this and they are not corner cases. A killed transfer leaves *"a piece of the file under the final name. No marker, no `.part`, no temporary name, nothing to say it is incomplete"* ([The rented machine](The%20rented%20machine.md)). A machine restarted mid-render left eleven whole frames and *"Six further files sat beside them at **zero bytes**"*, so *"the output folder said seventeen and the truth was eleven"* (same). And on a full disk Blender *"announces the save, exits 0, says nothing about the disk, and leaves a picture that does not end"* (same). ~~The one place a file's presence is read deliberately is the re-run's already-there test~~ **— three places, and the correction is 2026-09-12's, routed out of step 8b's code round.** **A fourth is rule 7's store version record, added 2026-09-28.** The first is the re-run's already-there test, which `Queue & History` and `Job files and outputs` own and which is a rule about what to render, never a reading of state. **The other two are `Rented-server runtime`'s own and are named in that document's preamble**: the Telegram role's enabled-or-disabled desired state ([`Rented-server runtime`](Rented-server%20runtime.md#53-the-one-role-worker) W7) and the setup-in-progress state ([`Rented-server runtime`](Rented-server%20runtime.md#9-the-setup-in-progress-state) P1), each of which writes one of its two values by taking its record away and reads that absence back as that value. **Each holds one state with one meaning and has no second record to disagree with it**, so an absence is *disabled*, or *no setup in progress*, and nothing else; reading it as *unknown* would stop a machine that had never been written either record from reconciling at all. **Everywhere else this rule stands exactly as written**, and it is still what forces a byte count to be Smoozy's own count and a registration check to be a comparison of content.
6. **What *written durably* means, defined here once because every ordering rule in this document and in [Rented-server runtime](Rented-server%20runtime.md) rests on the word.** The word occurs throughout both, and until 2026-08-20 neither said what it meant *(the two figures this sentence carried were counts taken on 2026-08-20, had never been re-counted, and matched neither document by 2026-08-29; dropped on that date rather than restated, since the argument is what the word carries and not how often it appears)* — so a builder reading *"written durably before the act it names"* literally writes the file, reads a success back, and every one of those orderings then holds only as well as the operating system's own flushing schedule happens to hold it. **This project has already seen what that costs**: the nearest observed crash left *"no frame's contents at all"* including a frame reported saved seconds earlier ([Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 6), which is exactly what a write with nothing behind it leaves.

   **A write is durable when the following has completed, and not before.** The bytes are written; the file is flushed to the storage device; where the write is published by a name, the rename follows the flush; and the directory holding that name is flushed too. The last clause is not optional and is the one that is ordinarily missed — `fsync(2)`, the Linux man-pages project, read 2026-08-20 at `https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/fsync.2.html`: *"fsync() transfers ("flushes") all modified in-core data of ... the file referred to by the file descriptor fd to the disk device (or other permanent storage device) so that all changed information can be retrieved even if the system crashes or is rebooted. This includes writing through or flushing a disk cache if present."*, and *"Calling fsync() does not necessarily ensure that the entry in the directory containing the file has also reached disk. For that an explicit fsync() on a file descriptor for the directory is also needed."*

   **The call differs by system and the difference is not cosmetic**, which is the second reason the word could not be left undefined. On Linux — every rented machine — `fsync` reaches the device. On macOS it does not: `fsync(2)`, read on the Creator's own Mac with `man 2 fsync` on 2026-08-20, macOS 26.5.2 — *"while fsync() will flush all data from the host to the drive ... the drive itself may not physically write the data to the platters for quite some time and it may be written in an out-of-order sequence"*, *"This is not a theoretical edge case."*, and *"Mac OS X provides the F_FULLFSYNC fcntl. The F_FULLFSYNC fcntl asks the drive to flush all buffered data to permanent storage."* **So on the user's own computer a durable write is the full-flush form, and on a rented machine it is the ordinary one.** Windows is untried and takes whichever call its own documentation names for the same guarantee; that is Build's to read and this document's to require.

   **This is ordering rather than atomicity**, and the precedent is already in the project rather than invented here: [Server protocol and transfers](Server%20protocol%20and%20transfers.md) S20 draws exactly this distinction for a transfer — *"The staged bytes are made durable before publication, where the server offers it."* — and this rule is the same distinction drawn for stores. The rename's own atomicity is separately guaranteed by `rename(2)` and is not durability of what it points at.

   **What this costs, stated rather than hidden.** A flush blocks until the device reports the transfer complete, so every *durably before* in these two documents is a real wait rather than a bookkeeping note. Where a rule wants a sequence of records durable in order, that is a flush apiece. Nothing here permits batching them; a builder that finds the cost intolerable in one named place brings that place back rather than quietly weakening the word.
7. **Every store on the machine carries one version, and a newer program upgrades it step by step the first time it reads it. Added 2026-09-28, outside any round, out of step 10c of Build. No auditor of this document has seen it.** Until this rule the machine's stores had no version at all, while the local record on the user's computer has had one from the start (`Desktop app and delivery` §5.2) — and a program replaced under them read every record the old one left, in whatever shape it left it. The submission manifest has already changed shape once (the card make, 2026-09-20). **So `Desktop app and delivery` L5 and L6 are carried to the machine, with four differences.**

   - **One version for the whole state area**, held in its own record — the store version of [1.2](#12-on-each-rented-server)'s table — and never inferred from which fields a record carries (L5).
   - **Forward only, one step at a time, in order, before anything reads the stores** (L6). **Each step is safe to run again over its own partly finished result**, and the store version moves only after a step is durable, so an interrupted upgrade resumes at the recorded version. The machine has no one-step publication of many files, which is why this replaces L7's.
   - **It runs only after the placement is *verified*** (`Rented-server runtime` G15a), so no copy is kept: L8's copy exists for a rollback, and after *verified* there is none. **The previous program therefore never meets a store the newer one rewrote.**
   - **A program meeting a store version newer than its own opens none of those stores and says so** (L9), answering only the health exchange and package inspection. `Desktop app and delivery` V14 makes that unreachable by any act of Smoozy's; it is written for the case nothing plans.

   **A step changes a record's shape and never its meaning, and never removes** a job record, a hold, a history copy, a per-job note or a result not yet acknowledged — `Desktop app and delivery` L12a's rule, carried. **The package record and the installation identity are outside the store version**: package inspection reads them across every release (`Server protocol and transfers` B4), so their shape is the protocol's and changes only with it.

   **One reading keeps one shape in every release, and it is what G21 reads**: whether each operation record carries its recorded outcome, whether that outcome is acknowledged, and whether any hold record stands. A step may add to those records and never changes or moves what that reading reads. **That is what lets a desktop take G21's reading through the surface on a program it cannot talk to** (`Server protocol and transfers` P3b), including every program placed before this rule, whose shape this fixes as it stands.

   **A state area holding no store version record is at the first version.** Every program placed before this rule wrote that shape, and a program that creates the stores writes the record before its first write to any of them — so the absence has one meaning and no second record can disagree with it. **It is the fourth place rule 5 reads an absence deliberately**, on the same footing as the other three.

## 2. Identities

**Constraint:** [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) — *"Every job has an identity no other job shares … What that identity is made of is Architecture's; Specs needs only that two jobs can never collide, however quickly one follows another."* And the shape's execution-group rule: *"A worker is never discovered or stopped by executable name, command-line pattern, or process search."*

### 2.1 The rules that bind every identity

1. **Issued once, by one named issuer, and never reused.** Not after the thing it named is gone, not after a record is deleted, not after a machine is destroyed.
2. **Opaque.** It carries no meaning that anything reads. Nothing parses one, sorts by one, or infers anything from two being adjacent.
3. **Never derived from anything the user typed.** Not a label, not a server name, not a file name. Observed — two jobs given the same label are refused by a plain make-directory call *"but the make-parents form accepts it silently — so the second job overwrites the first"* ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 29). A label is carried **beside** an identity and is never load-bearing for uniqueness anywhere.
4. **Never derived from anything read off a machine.** Observed — a rental's own name was `ubuntu` and *"Nothing about it identifies which machine was rented"*; its address and port are the provider's mapping and the machine does not know either of them; its rental number sits in a settings block that also *"hold[s] secrets in plain text"* ([The rented machine](The%20rented%20machine.md)). A provider's identifier is also outside Smoozy's world entirely — [Whole app](../Specs/Whole%20app.md): *"no provider exists anywhere in the app itself."*
5. **Never a process identifier, on its own or as the whole of a runtime identity.** Observed — a stop aimed by pattern *"matched **two** process ids"*, was handed a malformed argument, and the work carried on: *"So a stop must be aimed at a recorded identity, never at a pattern"* ([Batch A](Batch%20A%20—%20the%20shape%20throwaway.md), Fact 68). A process identifier is reusable, and [5.2](#52-what-a-recorded-execution-group-must-be-paired-with) states what it must be paired with.
6. **Never derived from a reading of the user’s computer.** Every identity in §2.2 is issued by its named owner and is independent of computer names and machine readings.
7. **Never a path**, and no path is ever synthesised from an identity by anything but the one document that owns that path.

### 2.3 The machine's installation identity

**Constraint, and it is the sharpest recovery hazard in the input documents.** Observed on a container recycle: *"Everything Smoozy installed was destroyed"*, down to the `cron` package, with the package count back to what the image shipped with; the boot hook *"was not deleted but replaced"* by an 82-byte provider default dated at the recycle moment, so *"a check for 'does my hook exist' passes while the hook does nothing Smoozy asked for"*; and, decisively, *"nothing in the connection distinguishes a recycled machine from a rebooted one"* — same address, same port, back in 90 seconds against 15–20 for a reboot. *"A Smoozy that reconnects and finds its address answering has no signal that its whole installation is gone. It must check for its own state, not for reachability."* ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), *Recycle*.)

**Y8. So one value carries that check.** The desktop application issues an installation identity at hand-over, writes it into the package state area as part of the same transaction that commits the package, and records it against the server row.

**Y9. It is read on every connection, before any machine-derived state is used or trusted.** The check has two halves — the value read from Smoozy's own state area, and the supervisor answering to confirm it — so there are **four outcomes, and they are the complete set**:

- **Present and equal, confirmed.** This is the installation Smoozy placed. Every machine-derived value the local record holds against this server row is current and may be used.
- **Absent.** Nothing of Smoozy's is on this machine — never prepared, recycled, reimaged, or a different machine at a re-used address. Every machine-derived value held against this server row is discarded rather than shown, and the server reads as not prepared. `Server Setup` owns what the user is offered.
- **Present and different.** A different installation. The same discard, and a second one: no state written by another installation is ever adopted, merged, or read as Smoozy's. This is also the only defence against a previous tenant's leavings, which observation says survive the disk — *"The record of a boot from November 2025 was still on the disk in August 2026"* ([The rented machine](The%20rented%20machine.md)).
- **Present, and the supervisor did not answer.** The identity was read and the check is unconfirmed. **Nothing is discarded**: every machine-derived value held against this server row stays exactly as it is, neither used as current nor thrown away. The supervisor-unavailable condition of [8.3](#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value) is raised, [Section 7](#7-reconciliation-on-the-desktop) stops at step 1, and the absent branch is **not** taken — an unconfirmed reading is never allowed to stand in for either of the two branches that discard, and never allowed to stand in for *present and equal* either.

**Y10. Every durably remembered machine-derived value carries the installation identity it was read under.** That is one rule rather than a rule per value, and it is what makes the discard above a single act instead of a list a builder must keep in step.

**Y11. Two values in the server row are outside it, and each is outside for a different reason. Added 2026-08-22 at the Architecture seam close, routed by the runner from [Servers](Servers.md#16-unresolved) §16 item 9.**

- **The remembered host key does not carry an installation identity, and cannot.** It is read **before** any connection presenting Smoozy's credential, and it is recorded on machines where no installation exists at all (`Servers` D10). Requiring it to carry one would make Row 36's first connection unimplementable as `Security and licence` §7 already writes it. **It is not thereby unprotected**: it is the machine's own key rather than Smoozy's state about the machine, and what it is compared against is itself.
- **The remembered setup bit is not a machine-derived value at all.** It is the user's own instruction (`Servers` B8), so the rule never reached it; it sits beside machine-derived values in [11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)'s subject-held class because its lifetime is the same, not because its provenance is.

**Y12. Neither exception weakens the rule for anything that is a machine-derived value**, and every such value in the local record still carries the identity it was read under.

**Y13. What *discarded* means here, and it is not deleted.** A machine-derived value whose carried installation identity is not the one now on the machine is **disqualified**: never read, never shown, never used as authority for anything, and never counted as state Smoozy holds about that machine. It is not removed on the strength of the mismatch — nothing sweeps for it and nothing schedules its removal — and it ends with its subject under [11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)'s **subject-held** rule, the server row being that subject. So the discard stays the single act the carrying rule above makes it, and a superseded value has a stated lifetime instead of none. **The server's event cursor is one of these values**, because E2 keys it to the installation identity as well: a new installation is read from the beginning of its own journal, never from a cursor taken under a dead one.

**Y14. The check is never made against a file at a path a provider also writes**, and never against reachability. It is made against Smoozy's own state area and confirmed by the supervisor answering. That is the recycle observation applied.

**Y15. This does not rest on the machine's remembered identity, and deliberately so.** *(Ground corrected 2026-08-23: this said Fact 83 was open on whether a key recorded through a provider's SSH proxy belongs to the machine or to the proxy. That half is answered and has been since 2026-08-17. The conclusion is unchanged; the reason for it is the answered half rather than the open one.)* **Fact 83's proxy half is answered and the proxy is honest** — the keys offered through `ssh3.vast.ai:28452` are byte-for-byte the machine's own `/etc/ssh/ssh_host_*_key.pub`, so the proxy forwards rather than terminating with an identity of its own ([Facts](Facts.md) Fact 83, observed 2026-08-17). **The key is the machine's and is still not a name for what this section has to recognise.** A fresh rental on the same physical machine presented byte-identical keys after the previous rental was destroyed with everything Smoozy had put there, a container's keys are the host's rather than the image's, and a virtual machine's are shipped in the image — so *"Whatever survives a recycle for identification purposes, it is neither the endpoint nor the host key"* (same fact). **What Fact 83 still leaves open is a different question and nothing here rests on it:** whether one virtual-machine template's private host key travels with the image across physical hosts. The installation identity tells one installation from another either way.

## 3. Desired state and observed state

**Constraint:** [The shape](The%20shape.md#each-rented-server) — *"The supervisor persists intent before acting"*, and *"Reconciliation after a supervisor crash or machine reboot may resume only work whose durable desired state still permits it."* And the observed constraint behind it: *"The arrangement that makes work survive a reboot also undoes a deliberate stop"* ([Batch A](Batch%20A%20—%20the%20shape%20throwaway.md)).

**D1. Every controllable thing has exactly one durable desired state, written by exactly one writer, and separately zero or more observed readings. They are never one field.**

| Thing | Desired state, held by | Observed value, read from |
|---|---|---|
| a job's place in a machine's line | the machine's durable job record | the same record |
| an operation's continuation | the operation record's desired state ([5.1](#51-the-durable-operation-record)) | the execution group, and the recorded outcome |
| the Telegram role | durable enabled-or-disabled on the machine | the worker running or not |
| reconciliation being held back | the machine-level *setup in progress* state | — |
| a connection | the connected period on the desktop (`Servers`) | connected, reconnecting, disconnected |
| an auto-download arrangement | the local record | the transfers that have run |
| the installed package version | the active-package pointer | the supervisor's health answer |

**D2. No desired state is ever inferred from an observed reading.** A supervisor that finds a worker gone concludes nothing about what was wanted; it reads the durable desired state and acts on that. This is the whole of what stops the observed Batch A failure — a restart policy that honoured a stop and then reversed it two seconds later.

**D3. Desired state governs what is started, never what is recorded about something that has already ended.** A stop whose target had already ended when the termination would have been issued does not turn that ending into *left stopped*; the recorded outcome is what actually happened. **Where the line between *already ended* and *stopped* falls is [5.3](#53-the-deliberate-stop) step 7's one boundary — the termination's issuance — and is not restated here** *(reworded 2026-08-22 with that step, R6: this read "a stop written while the worker was already finishing", which put the boundary at the stop's write rather than at the one moment the supervisor holds a record of)*.

**D4. The desktop keeps no durable copy of an observed machine state that the machine still holds.** [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) states it and gives the reason. The exceptions are exactly three and each is named there rather than invented here: the last-known queue reading, which is **period-held** ([11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)); the history record, which is Smoozy's own note and not a copy of anything; and the remembered server state the specs name, which is **subject-held** and carries its installation identity per [2.3](#23-the-machines-installation-identity).

**D5. Every observed reading carries the moment it was taken and the clock it was taken on, and readings taken on different clocks are never subtracted from one another.**

Two constraints force this. The machine's clock is its own: observed, *"The machine is on UTC … It has no idea what time zone anyone using it is in"*, synchronised, and within about a hundredth of a second of a real time source on two checks half an hour apart, with drift over longer than half an hour untested ([The rented machine](The%20rented%20machine.md)). The user's computer's clock is not defended at all: Fact 13 came back negative on the half that matters,. So: **a duration about work on a machine is computed from that machine's own readings**, which is where a job's times and the idle stretch come from; and the desktop's clock is used for presentation and for nothing a promise rests on.

**D6. No durable state depends on the desktop application shutting down cleanly.** Every durable write happens at the moment the fact becomes true, and nothing is written on exit. A crash and a clean close therefore leave the same durable state, and there is no recovery path for one that is not the ordinary path for the other. **One exemption is named, it falls on the third sentence alone, and it is D6a's. Added 2026-08-25; the first two sentences of this clause are untouched by it.**

**D6a. The exemption, at its true width: one field, one reader, no recovery path. Added 2026-08-25, routed by the runner from `Reporting a problem` U2 to U4, on the Creator's exception of 2026-08-24 — *"Smoozy may speak up, yes."***

**What is exempted, exactly.** D6's third sentence and no part of the other two. The **run-mark** ([1.1](#11-on-the-users-computer) I2) is the one durable value on this side that differs between a crash and a clean close: a launch that finds *clean* knows the previous run ended after the user asked to quit, a launch that finds *running* knows it did not, and a launch that finds the field absent knows of no previous run at all. `Reporting a problem` §9's launch-time offer is its **one reader**.

**What is not exempted, in four parts, because the width is the whole value of this clause.**

1. **Nothing is written on exit.** The *clean* value is written when the user **asks** to quit, which is a fact becoming true; D6's second sentence stands untouched, and no handler, no exit path and no shutdown step writes durable state.
2. **No recovery path differs.** Every store, every record and every mechanism in this document behaves identically after a crash and after a clean close, and [11.2](#112-where-each-class-is-enforced)'s *the application run ending, which D6 makes identical for a crash and a clean close* is unchanged. What differs is **one prompt**.
3. **Nothing else may read the mark.** Not [Section 6](#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine)'s reconciliation and not [Section 7](#7-reconciliation-on-the-desktop)'s, not a failure record ([Section 8](#8-failure-records--a-live-condition-and-a-recorded-ending)), not a lifetime ([Section 11](#11-retention-and-cleanup--the-local-lifetimes)), not the update handover. A second reader would make a second thing depend on how a run ended, which is what D6 exists to forbid.
4. **A second such field is a fault and not a precedent.** Any further durable value whose reading differs by how the previous run ended is brought here rather than added, and this clause is written as the exception it is.

**What an absence costs, and whether the cost is bounded by the promise or by luck — the check this clause exists to record.** The mark is the first durable thing on this side whose **absence carries a meaning**, so the reading is wrong wherever the absence has a cause other than a crash. There are three such causes, and each costs one of two things and nothing beyond them.

- **The whole per-user application-data location cleared.** The mark goes and the diary goes with it, [1.1](#11-on-the-users-computer) I1's area sitting beside the local record inside that location. The launch reads **absent** and offers nothing, which is correct: there is nothing left to cut a report out of.
- **The local record removed or replaced on its own** — a fresh install, a deleted record, or [1.1](#11-on-the-users-computer)'s own *anyone willing to set a value high on purpose can already delete the whole record*. The launch reads **absent** and offers nothing, while the diary may still hold the crashed run's lines. **What is lost is the offer, not the evidence:** `Reporting a problem` §8's menu entry point takes no mark, works with nothing, and cuts its slice from the diary's own order, so the user who wants to report that crash still has a route, by the one that document makes always available.
- **A durable write of the *clean* value that failed.** The mark reads *running* where the run ended cleanly, or *clean* where it did not: **one prompt that should not have appeared, or one that should have and did not**, which is the spec's own stated bound and is carried at `Reporting a problem` U6. *(Corrected 2026-10-24 by step 11's plan pass, on `Reporting a problem` §12 item 11. The heading read ~~**A durable write of the *clean* value that failed, or `Desktop app and delivery` L8's pre-migration copy restored by L13.**~~ A restored L8 copy no longer costs a prompt: the build a rollback starts makes no offer (`Reporting a problem` U5b; `Desktop app and delivery` Z21b), exactly as U6 was corrected on 2026-08-31.)*

**And the one thing a wrong reading can never do is name the wrong run.** Every write of the field carries the identity of the run performing that write; the field is overwritten durably at every application start before anything else reads it, so a stale value cannot survive a launch; and the only restore in this project — L13's of L8's copy — restores a copy taken by the run that is still live (`Reporting a problem` U5). So a crash slice is never cut on a run other than the one the mark named. **That is what bounds this to the promise rather than to luck**: the mark holds no fact any other record derives from, so the entire cost of getting it wrong is one interruption, in one direction or the other, and never a durable state that disagrees with itself. `Reporting a problem` C5's *nothing was lost* stays a true sentence in every one of the three cases above.

## 4. The event journal and acknowledgement

**Constraint:** [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) — everything that happens to a job happens with nobody watching, and *"Smoozy learns what happened on a machine only while it is open and connected."* The shape gives the supervisor the journal.

**E1. Every event is journalled durably, with an event identity and a per-machine sequence number that only increases, before it is offered to any reader.** An event that has not reached the journal has not happened as far as any reader is concerned.

**E2. Each reader holds its own cursor, and the cursor is durable on the reader's side.** The desktop's cursor lives in the local record against the server row and the installation identity it was taken under; the Telegram worker's lives in the machine's own state area. A reader never advances another reader's cursor, and the journal holds no per-reader state beyond the registration that keeps its retention floor honest.

**E3. A cursor advances after the work the event asked for is done, never on receipt.** Observed on the one delivery mechanism this project has watched: three updates taken and deliberately not confirmed came back *"identical, same ids"* on the next ask, and went only when the ask moved past them — *"So a machine that grabs an update and dies before acting on it destroys nothing … **confirm after the work is done, never on receipt**"* ([One box](One%20box%20—%20the%20Telegram%20capture%20and%20the%20port%20that%20cannot%20be%20chosen.md)). The rule here is the general form of that, and it holds for every reader of the journal.

**E4. Delivery is therefore at least once, and every reader applies an event idempotently by its event identity.** An event applied twice leaves what one application left.

**E5. A reader detects a gap by itself, and nothing writes a marker for it.** The journal reports its own oldest retained sequence number with every read. A reader whose cursor is below that number knows it missed events, says so, and does not present the events it did receive as a complete account. Sequence numbers are contiguous per machine, so this needs no extra record and cannot be forgotten.

**E6. Retention.** The journal keeps an event until every registered cursor has passed it. Beyond that a machine-level cap applies, and **the oldest acknowledged entries are dropped first**. Where the cap would force dropping an entry no cursor has passed, it is dropped and the oldest-retained number moves, which is exactly the case E5 makes visible. **The cap's value is E6a's.**

**E6a. The cap is 32,768 entries per machine, at a 512-byte per-entry budget, and it is fixed rather than read. Added 2026-08-22, scenario replays Pile 1 item R31.** Those two figures multiply to **16 MiB**, which is the journal's share of the **64 MiB control-state reserve** `Rented-server runtime` M4a already subtracts from every stream capture. No other control-state store draws on that share, and the journal draws on nothing outside it. Three things fix the shape, and each is why a value is stated here rather than left to Build.

- **The reserve is the denominator and a free-space reading is never the numerator.** M4a derives its stream ceiling from a live reading because that ceiling only bounds *new* writes; this cap **drops entries**, and [11.4](#114-on-the-machine) forbids clearing anything on a machine on the strength of a free-space reading alone. So the cap is a constant of the server package: no reading raises it, no reading lowers it, and a mount that grows or shrinks changes nothing about what the journal keeps. What is taken from M4a's shape is only that the journal sits **inside** the reserve M4a holds back, so the cap and a stream capture can never compete for the same bytes.
- **No entry exceeds the per-entry budget.** An entry's event identity, sequence number, moment and kind are never abbreviated to fit; a kind whose fields would exceed the budget **names the record that holds the detail rather than copying it in**, which is already how the recorded reading and a render's failure reason reach a reader (`Queue & History` N9, N3). A builder that fixes a different per-entry encoding derives a different entry count from the same 16 MiB — it does not choose a count freely.
- **The cap bounds what the journal keeps and never promises the mount has those bytes.** A journal write refused for want of space is the disk-full condition and [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 1's answer, and it is not this cap firing.

**What the cap buys, stated in work rather than in hours.** A render journals its own start and ending, [Section 6](#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine) step 4's dispositions, and each derivative's production as its operation's outcome (`Queue & History` P3) — so a job costs a small multiple of one entry per finished frame. **32,768 entries therefore carries a ten-thousand-frame job whole, with room for the jobs around it.** It is stated in work and not in a length of outage on purpose: a machine renders at whatever rate the scene allows, and no figure here relates entries to hours.

**No user-facing loss is introduced by fixing the number, and that was checked against [Method](Method.md)'s third escalation before it was taken.** E6 already drops an entry no cursor has passed, and E5 already makes that gap visible; what changes is that the boundary is decided here instead of by whoever builds the journal. Where an outage does outrun the cap, the entry goes, the oldest-retained number moves, and the catch-up notice says what it cannot vouch for — `Queue & History` C4 stating a gap rather than smoothing it, which is Row 77's promise kept rather than broken.

**E7. An event carries the moment it happened, on the machine's clock, and nothing about whether it has been presented.** A reader's own cursor and that moment are together what let a connection that finds a great deal has happened produce the one catch-up notice `Queue & History` promises rather than a burst about hours-old events: what a reader has passed is its cursor, and how old what it finds is is the moment. **The journal carries no record of presentation, per event or otherwise.** E2 forbids per-reader state in the journal, and a single flag cannot serve two independent readers — an event the Telegram worker presented would read as presented to the desktop, and the desktop's catch-up account would lose it. `Desktop app and delivery` owns the presenting and Row 284; what is supplied here is the event moment and D5's clock rule.

## 5. The operation lifecycle and the four outcomes

**Constraint:** [The shape](The%20shape.md#each-rented-server) — *"Per-operation workers use the four operation outcomes: resumed, re-run once, left stopped, or given up. Role workers instead use durable enabled-or-disabled desired state."* And: *"Each command has an operation identity. Each caller waiting for a state transition has its own deadline and reports expiry without changing the operation's durable state."*

### 5.1 The durable operation record

**G1.** One per operation **that starts a worker**, written by the supervisor before any worker is started, and holding:

- the operation identity, and the subject identity it acts on — a job, a server-setup run, a transfer, a preview, or another operation, which is what a stop acts on;
- the operation's **kind**;
- the **desired state**: *should run*, *should be stopped*, or *ended*;
- the **attempt count**;
- the **recorded execution group** of the current attempt, paired per [5.2](#52-what-a-recorded-execution-group-must-be-paired-with), and the **run identifier** of the supervisor run that started it;
- the kind's own **safe-boundary marker**, whose meaning belongs to the document that defines the kind and which this document only requires to exist and to be durable;
- the **latest reconciliation disposition** and the run identifier that wrote it;
- the **recorded outcome**, written once and never rewritten.

**G2. A disposition and an outcome are two different things and they are never one field.** The shape's four names are the dispositions:

- **A reconciliation disposition** is what one supervisor run did with this record — *resumed*, *re-run once*, *left stopped* or *given up*. It is written at every run that takes a branch for the record, it replaces the one before it, and **it never closes the record**. It is written **durably before** the act it names: before the supervisor re-attaches to a resumed group, and before a second attempt is started.
- **The recorded outcome** is the operation's one terminal result and only a terminal result may set it: *left stopped*, *given up*, or the kind's own ending, which includes the ordinary successful one. Where reconciliation writes the disposition *left stopped* or *given up*, it writes the recorded outcome in the same act, because those two dispositions are terminal results as well; *resumed* and *re-run once* write no outcome.

**G3. A record with no recorded outcome is eligible for reconciliation, at this run and at every later one**, however many dispositions it has already carried. A record with a recorded outcome is closed: [Section 6](#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine) step 2 does not select it and nothing rewrites the field. **Desired state *ended* is written only in the same durable write as the recorded outcome**, so the two can never disagree and the loop can never meet a record that is *ended* and open.

**G4. A command that starts no worker gets a command record instead.** A stop, a deletion, a query, an acknowledged submission — every request [Section 12](#12-what-this-document-supplies-and-to-which-document) rule 2 makes the supervisor record an identity for but which starts nothing. It holds the **operation identity**, the **subject identity** it acts on, and the **recorded outcome**, and nothing else: no desired state, no attempt count, no execution group, no safe-boundary marker, no disposition. **A stop's subject identity is the operation identity it names** ([5.3](#53-the-deliberate-stop) step 1), **or, for a stop whose target is resolved on the machine, the operation identity the supervisor resolves it to, written into the same field at resolution. Added 2026-08-22 at the Architecture seam close, routed by the runner from [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md#18-unresolved) §18 item 16.** The field is one field with one meaning — *which operation this stop acts on* — and the only thing that moves is **when** it is filled: at submission for a caller who names the target, at resolution for a caller who cannot. **The record is written at submission either way**, with the subject identity absent and the composite's own selection rule in its place, and the supervisor's resolution completes it durably before step 2 of [5.3](#53-the-deliberate-stop). **Why this is admitted rather than refused:** Row 59's promise cannot be kept from the desktop, because the reading the desktop composes from is period-held and the job rendering when the write lands may not be the one it named — in which case the machine the user asked to empty starts a fresh render instead, which is the outcome the spec's ordering exists to prevent. **What is not admitted:** a stop with neither a named target nor a selection rule, and a subject identity rewritten after it has once been resolved. It is never put in the operation records, and [Section 6](#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine) step 2 never selects one — a record with no desired state has no branch, and a builder that gave one *should run* to fill the field would have reconciliation execute the command a second time.

### 5.2 What a recorded execution group must be paired with

**G5.** The shape already forbids finding a worker by name, pattern or process search. This adds the pairing, because a recorded identity that a later, unrelated thing can present is a pattern with extra steps.

**G6. A recorded execution group is valid only against a value recorded at the moment it was started that a later process cannot present.** A bare process identifier is not such a value: identifiers are reusable. Every stop and every re-attach validates the pair, and a pair that does not validate means the group is gone — never that the check should be relaxed.

**G7. Which mechanism supplies that value on a rented Linux machine, and whether it survives a container's reduced capability set, is established — and this sentence said otherwise until 2026-08-21, three days after the answer landed.** Fact 91, **ANSWERED 2026-08-17** and re-posed on 2026-08-18 against the real arrangement rather than a bare process, same answer: **the group leader's identity recorded together with its start time, field 22 of `/proc/<identifier>/stat`, written by the kernel at fork.** The container half is answered too, and it was not an idle doubt — the container refused the demonstration three ways, and it took a virtual machine to settle it. The reading needs nothing special, and the page says so rather than the project inferring it. Documented — `proc_pid_stat(5)`, the Linux man-pages project, read 2026-08-23 at `https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man5/proc_pid_stat.5.html`: *"Whether or not certain of these fields display valid information is governed by a ptrace access mode PTRACE_MODE_READ_FSCREDS | PTRACE_MODE_NOAUDIT check (refer to ptrace(2)). If the check denies access, then the field value is displayed as 0. The affected fields are indicated with the marking [PT]."* **Field 22 carries no such marking** — *"(22) starttime %llu — The time the process started after system boot. Before Linux 2.6, this value was expressed in jiffies. Since Linux 2.6, the value is expressed in clock ticks (divide by sysconf(_SC_CLK_TCK))."* — so it sits outside that check, and a container's reduced capability set does not reach it. **One qualifier from the fact travels with the value and may not be dropped:** the start time is measured in ticks since boot, so **it is meaningful only within one boot** — which is why the two cases below fall to the run identifier alone.

**G8. What the pair cannot do, and it is the sharper half.** **A dead worker goes on validating.** Where the supervisor does not wait on the workers it starts, a killed or self-ended worker persists as a defunct entry whose start-time field still reads exactly what was recorded, so the recorded pair validates for a process that no longer exists — observed twice on this project's own rentals (Fact 91; Fact 49, where *"with no setup worker alive at all, the machine reported the recorded pair as still validating"*). **The error runs in one direction only** — the machine can report a group live that has ended, never the reverse — so the pair stays sound as a *gone* test and is unsound as a *still running* test. **So the pair is not the instrument that learns an ending; [5.3](#53-the-deliberate-stop) step 6 names that instrument.**

**G9. Two cases need no such mechanism and are settled by the run identifier alone.** The supervisor writes a fresh run identifier at every start. A group recorded under a previous run identifier is treated as gone unless it validates. Where the whole machine restarted, or where a container was restarted or recycled, the workers died with it and the rule collapses to *gone* — **overwhelmingly likely rather than certain, and this sentence said *by construction* until 2026-09-12, when the step 8b code round found that it is not.** The residual is one case: the pair is a process identifier and a start time in ticks since boot, both of which start again after a boot, so an unrelated process on the machine that came back can present the recorded pair and validate against it. **What the reader should carry is [Rented-server runtime](Rented-server%20runtime.md#61-what-is-recorded-and-what-it-is-paired-with) X3's rule, which is stated there in terms**: a reboot, a container restart and a recycle are settled by the run identifier the supervisor writes at every start and **never** by the pair. **No separate arm is added for the residual**, because telling a reboot apart from a supervisor crash needs a reading nothing on the machine has and this stage does not invent one. Where the supervisor alone crashed and the machine stayed up, a detached worker can genuinely still be running — observed, a detached program outlived the connection and the shell that started it, *"Its parent is **1**"* ([Batch A](Batch%20A%20—%20the%20shape%20throwaway.md), Fact 1) — and that is the one case where validation does the work.

**G10. The machine's own boot identifier is not used for this, and the reason is a trap.** On a container it is the host's: observed, across two container restarts the host's uptime *"rose monotonically"* and the host never rebooted ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 48). A design keyed on it would be correct on a virtual machine and silently wrong on a container, which is the same failure the shape's two boot-return arrangements exist to prevent.

### 5.3 The deliberate stop

1. The caller submits a stop command carrying **its own** operation identity and naming the target operation's. **One carve-out, added 2026-08-22 at the Architecture seam close, routed by the runner from [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md#18-unresolved) §18 item 16: where the caller cannot name the target because only the machine knows which operation it is** — the composite stop of `Queue & History` L13, whose target is *whatever is rendering on this machine when this lands* — **the caller submits the selection rule instead of a target identity, and the supervisor resolves it against its own current state and writes the resolved operation identity into the command record's subject field before step 2.** The rule is stated by the calling document and is never invented by the supervisor; **a rule that resolves to nothing is a recorded outcome of *nothing was rendering*, not an unresolved record left open**; and the resolution happens once and is never rewritten. Every later step reads the resolved identity and cannot tell which way it was filled.
2. The supervisor writes the target's desired state to *should be stopped*, **durably, before anything else**.
3. The supervisor validates the recorded execution group per [5.2](#52-what-a-recorded-execution-group-must-be-paired-with) and confirms it still belongs to the intended operation.
4. The supervisor issues the worker-kind's own termination to the member that kind's contract names. Those contracts are per kind and are owned by the document that defines the kind; no signal contract is universal.
5. **Nothing infers completion from delivery.** Observed — the stop took effect on the program's next cycle, about 2.3 seconds against a three-second cycle, and *"A check made two seconds after sending the signal reported the program still running and would have been wrong"* ([Batch A](Batch%20A%20—%20the%20shape%20throwaway.md), Fact 2). Each waiter has its own deadline; expiry is reported and changes no durable state.
6. **The supervisor waits on every worker it starts, and the ending is taken from the wait status** — not from the pair validating, and not from the termination being delivered. When the group has ended, the supervisor records the outcome **left stopped** and journals the event. **This step named no instrument until 2026-08-21, and the omission was a hang rather than a wrong answer.** Documented — Linux `wait(2)`, the Linux man-pages project, read 2026-08-20 and again 2026-08-23 at `https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/wait.2.html`: *"A child that terminates, but has not been waited for becomes a 'zombie'. The kernel maintains a minimal set of information about the zombie process (PID, termination status, resource usage information) in order to allow the parent to later perform a wait to obtain information about the child."* **So without the wait, a worker that ended on its own still validates at step 3, step 4's termination is absorbed by a defunct entry, step 5's waiters expire, and this step's *when the group has ended* never arrives** — the operation sits open until the supervisor next restarts. `Rented-server runtime` §16 item 4 named the hole and it is closed here rather than left to Build. **[5.2](#52-what-a-recorded-execution-group-must-be-paired-with)'s pair keeps its job unchanged**, which is the safe-direction check that a group is *gone*; the wait is what says it has *ended*. **The supervisor start is not affected either way** — a departed supervisor's unreaped children are adopted and reaped by the system's own first process, which `wait(2)` states on the same page, so [Section 6](#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine)'s table meets a pair that correctly fails to validate and never re-attaches to a corpse.
7. **The boundary between an ending of the worker's own and *left stopped* is step 4's issuance of the termination, and this step is that boundary's one home — the other three statements of it cite this one** (`Rented-server runtime` X8 step 7, `Queue & History` L11, `Rendering` K5). **Where the worker ended on its own before the termination was issued** — the pair failing validation at step 3, or the group's ending already reaped by step 6's standing wait when the sequence reaches step 4 — **D3 applies and the recorded outcome is that ending.** **From the issuance onward, the ending step 6's wait returns is recorded *left stopped*.** The issuance is the only boundary the supervisor holds a record of its own about: past it, a stop that took and a worker that would have ended anyway leave the same records — an exit number names no cause (X13) and `Rendering` K11 records that the two cannot be told apart from anything Smoozy holds — so the recorded outcome past the issuance is the record of the caller's own act, with the completeness statement carried beside it saying what was actually written. *(Fixed 2026-08-22, scenario replays Pile 1 item R6: this step said between steps 2 and 6, `Rented-server runtime` X8 said the same, `Queue & History` L11 said between the request and the delivery, and `Rendering` K5 said between the steps — three windows for one boundary, and the two machine-side ones put the ordinary successful stop, a render honouring the interrupt after delivery, inside the worker's-own-ending rule, so a stopped job would read finished or failed against the spec's own marked as stopped.)* **The issuance is written durably into the render kind's own field (`Rented-server runtime` W4) in the act that issues it**, so the boundary survives a supervisor restart, and an ending a resumed worker reports (`Rented-server runtime` W12) is recorded against it as one step 6's wait returns would be. *Added 2026-10-01 with [Section 6](#6-reconciliation-on-the-machine) step 2's split first row, from step 10c's pass before Vast on Sesterce.* **The issuance is written before the termination is sent, and put back as it was where the sequence then sends nothing** (the pair gone, the termination refused, the kernel unread). A supervisor that ends between the write and the signal then leaves *issued* with nothing sent, which the user's fresh stop answers (`Rendering` K10); the other order could leave *not issued* with the termination out, and the next run would send a second of its own, which Row 18 forbids. *Added 2026-10-01, the build runner's ruling at step 10c's Sesterce-pass fix piece 2, which built it.*

### 5.4 Retry, and three things that are not one another

- **A re-run after an interruption** is the machine's own act, once, from durable state, with no user and no connection in it. The attempt count is what bounds it.
- **A re-sent command whose acknowledgement was lost** carries the **same** operation identity. The supervisor answers from the record it already holds — the recorded outcome where there is one, and *still in hand* where there is not ([Section 12](#12-what-this-document-supplies-and-to-which-document) rule 2) — and does not act again. This is not a retry of the work and it does not touch the attempt count.
- **A fresh attempt the user asked for** — submitting again, or carrying on from where a job stopped — is a **new** operation with a new identity, and where it is a render it is a new job with a new identity and a new folder. `Rendering` owns both actions.

**G11. Nothing here retries an authentication.** [Security and licence](Security%20and%20licence.md#4-reaching-a-secret-and-giving-it-to-the-thing-that-needs-it) §4 states why, on observed evidence that repeated attempts corrupt the diagnosis.

## 6. Reconciliation on the machine

**M1.** Run by the supervisor at every start, whatever caused it — a crash, a reboot, a container restart, a package commit, or an ordinary first start.

**Step 0. If the machine-level *setup in progress* state is set, reconciliation does not run and no work is started.** `Server Setup` sets and clears it through the same command path; `Rented-server runtime` owns it.

**Step 0a. If the store version is older than the program's own, reconciliation does not run until the stores are upgraded, and the upgrade waits for *verified*.** Added 2026-09-28; [1.3](#13-rules-that-bind-every-store) rule 7 and `Rented-server runtime` G15a. A start inside a package transaction — G25's activation — finds the phase record short of *verified* and reads and writes no store; the start after it, or the first request once *verified* is written, upgrades and then reconciles. No auditor of this document has seen this step.

**Step 1. Write a fresh run identifier.**

**Step 2. For each operation record with no recorded outcome, take exactly one branch.** **Command records are not in this loop at all** ([5.1](#51-the-durable-operation-record)): they carry no desired state, so no branch below applies to one, and none is ever selected here.

| Desired state | Recorded execution group | Attempt count | Disposition, and what is written |
|---|---|---|---|
| *should be stopped* | gone | — | **left stopped** — the disposition and the recorded outcome are written together; nothing is started |
| *should be stopped* | validates per [5.2](#52-what-a-recorded-execution-group-must-be-paired-with) | — | **resumed** — written before the supervisor re-attaches; no outcome is written; the record stays open and eligible, and the desired state stays *should be stopped*. **Where the record says the termination was already issued, nothing is sent** (`Rendering` K10); **where it says it was not, the termination is issued now**, as `Rented-server runtime` X8 step 4, being the stop the record asked for. The ending arrives by the worker's report (`Rented-server runtime` W12) and is recorded by [5.3](#53-the-deliberate-stop) step 7. |
| *ended* | — | — | unreachable by construction: [5.1](#51-the-durable-operation-record) writes this desired state only in the same act as the recorded outcome, so a record carrying it is closed and this loop never selects it |
| *should run* | validates per [5.2](#52-what-a-recorded-execution-group-must-be-paired-with) | — | **resumed** — the disposition is written before the supervisor re-attaches; no outcome is written and the record stays open and eligible at the next run |
| *should run* | gone | 1 | **re-run once** — the disposition and the count of 2 are written before the new attempt starts; no outcome is written and the record stays open |
| *should run* | gone | 2 | **given up** — the disposition and the recorded outcome are written together; nothing is started |

*Corrected 2026-10-01, from step 10c's pass before Vast on Sesterce: the first row closed a stop whose render `Rented-server runtime` K18 had kept alive, and a later job started beside it on the same card.* The table had one *should be stopped* row, *left stopped* whatever the group; it is now split by the group, the *gone* half unchanged. Label: **observed in a test** (the pass record, `Audits/Build/Step 10c/The pass before Vast — Sesterce.md`) for the render outliving the restart.

**Step 3. Role workers do not take this path.** The Telegram worker is the only one. The supervisor starts it while its durable state is *enabled*, restarts it for as long as it stays enabled, records and reports repeated failure rather than silently giving up, and never resurrects it after it is disabled.

**Step 4. Journal every disposition written in step 2, and every recorded outcome written with one**, so a reader learns of them under [Section 4](#4-the-event-journal-and-acknowledgement) rather than by comparing two readings. A *resumed* and a *re-run once* are journalled every time they are written, exactly like the two that close a record — an attempt a reader never heard of is the case this step exists for.

**M2. Two things this reconciliation does not do.**

**M3.** It does not read a job's output folder to decide anything about state — [1.3](#13-rules-that-bind-every-store) rule 5. What a re-run treats as already there is `Queue & History`'s and `Job files and outputs`', is a rule about what to render, and is deliberately separate from this.

**M4. One walk of a job's output folder does happen here and it decides nothing — added 2026-08-21.** Where step 2 writes a disposition that **ends** a job — *left stopped* or *given up* — [Rented-server runtime](Rented-server%20runtime.md#33-reconciliation-is-the-supervisors-and-it-runs-at-more-moments-than-a-start) S11 takes `Job files and outputs`' completeness reckoning **after** the branch above has been chosen from records alone, and writes its statement with the recorded outcome. The order is the whole of it: the walk cannot move a branch of the table, so rule 5 is kept rather than excepted. Where the walk cannot be taken, the outcome is written carrying no statement and saying so.

**M5.** And **after a boot the supervisor did not initiate, it trusts no record to be more recent than the last one it can read.** The nearest observation this project has to a power cut is a kernel-level reset, which left *"no frame's contents at all"*, including a frame Blender had reported saving seconds earlier ([Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md), citing its own sources). Fact 44 — what losing power outright leaves — is not posable on any machine this project **runs** today — **corrected 2026-08-22: this said *on any machine this project can reach*, and the blocker is what is installed rather than what exists.** A guest killed at the hypervisor would pose it and never touches the Mac's own power; the Mac has no hypervisor and no container runtime installed, and installing one is the Creator's act ([Open Threads](../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), item 1). The re-run-once rule is what bounds the cost of a record that did not reach the disk; [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 6 states what it does not cover.

**V1.** Run on every connection, in this order, before anything machine-derived is shown.

Only the confirmed branch carries on to step 2.

## 8. Failure records — a live condition and a recorded ending

**Constraint:** Row 285, quoted in full at the head of this document, and [Whole app](../Specs/Whole%20app.md)'s own reason for the withdrawal clause: *"Leaving it would have Smoozy saying the connection is down on a server that reads as connected — the app being wrong on screen about its own state, which is never past the line."*

### 8.1 Two kinds, and the kind is declared

**F1.** Every failure record carries a **kind**, written by whatever raised it, at the moment it is raised, and never inferred afterwards by anything:

- **condition** — a statement about something still live, which can stop being true;
- **ending** — a statement about something that has already happened, which cannot.

**F2.** A writer that cannot say which kind it is raising has not established what it is reporting, and that is a fault in the writer rather than a case this section covers.

**F3. An ending and the record of it are two things, and they are never one field.** The two have different lifetimes and merging them destroys one of them.

- **The subject outcome** is how the thing itself ended — a job's ending in its history record, a unit's outcome under [9.4](#94-the-outcome-record). It is written into the subject's own record under [8.6](#86-the-subject-outcome-is-durable-and-immutable), it is immutable, and **nothing the user does to a failure record touches it**.
- **The failure record of kind *ending*** is the attention mark raised against that outcome. It names the subject outcome and holds no second copy of it, so the mark and the fact are never one field. **It is not the user's to clear** and goes only with its subject ([8.8](#88-when-a-failure-record-ends)). *(Changed 2026-09-19 from a code round — the Gemini catch-up of step 10a, Area 4, upstream must-do U-1. No auditor of this document has seen it. This read *"so clearing the mark takes away the mark and leaves the fact"*.)*

**F4. Removing the subject removes both the mark and the outcome. Nothing else removes an ending's mark, and nothing removes the outcome while the subject stands.** A failed job's history entry says the job failed for as long as the entry stands, which is what [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) requires the entry to hold; and a multi-part action still has the unit outcome [9.4](#94-the-outcome-record) derives its own outcome from. *(Changed 2026-09-19 from a code round — the Gemini catch-up of step 10a, Area 4, upstream must-do U-1. No auditor of this document has seen it. This read *"Clearing under 8.8 removes the mark and never the outcome; removing the subject removes both. A user who clears a failed job's mark without deleting the history entry still has an entry that says the job failed"*. [Whole app](../Specs/Whole%20app.md) grants clearing a failure *"only where it describes something still true"*, and an ending describes something that has already happened; see [8.8](#88-when-a-failure-record-ends).)*

### 8.2 A condition record is a derivation, never a copy

**F5.** A condition record holds the **condition identity** and the **set of observed values it was raised over** — the values of that condition's kind over which the condition holds, declared by whoever raises it and taken from the column [8.3](#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value) fixes per kind. It holds no second copy of the current value and no independent notion of whether the condition still holds. A kind whose condition holds over exactly one value has a set of one; the connection condition has two, because an outage reads as *disconnected* and as *reconnecting* by turns while it lasts.

**F6. It is presented while, and only while, the condition's current observed value is one of the values it was raised over.** When the observed value moves outside that set, the record is withdrawn by that change alone. Nothing sweeps for it, nothing schedules its removal, and no code path exists that could withdraw it while the condition still holds or leave it standing after the condition has gone — which is what the per-kind column delivers rather than merely asserts.

**F7. A withdrawn record is spent.** A record whose observed value has moved outside the set it was raised over is never presented again, whatever the value does afterwards, and it is removed at the next write to its subject. Two things follow and both are the point. The value returning does not bring the record back, so a record carrying an ended condition's words and moment is never shown beside whatever is raised now — Row 285's own reason for the withdrawal, *"the reason for keeping it has been served"*, would be broken by re-presenting a served record. And condition records do not pile up on a durable subject one per outage, because nothing keeps a spent one. **Withdrawal is therefore an ending of the record and not only a change in what is shown**, and [8.8](#88-when-a-failure-record-ends) lists it beside the other two.

**F8.** That is the whole of Row 285's first clause, and it is a derivation rather than a copy precisely so that the mark and the condition cannot drift apart. There is no second record to go stale.

### 8.3 The condition identity, and who supplies each observed value

**F9. The condition identity is opaque, like every other identity in [2.2](#22-the-register-of-identities), and it is found by a condition key.** [2.1](#21-the-rules-that-bind-every-identity) binds it entire: nothing parses one, nothing reads meaning out of one, and it is never a path. What carries the meaning is the key.

**F10. A condition key is the subject the condition is about, the condition's kind, and — where the condition is about a place on a machine — the location it was read against, in one normalised form.** The last part is not decoration: Fact 28 establishes that free space *"is not a reading, it is a choice of which mount to read"*, and a disk-full condition naming *the machine* would fold two conditions that are not one into a single record. Normalising is what stops two spellings of one mount splitting one condition into two.

**F11. How the two are used.** Whatever raises a condition composes the key first and looks it up. Where the key already has a condition identity against it, that identity is used, no second record is raised, and the existing record is the one there is to withdraw. Where it does not, a fresh condition identity is issued and recorded against the key. **The key is a lookup and never an identity**: it is not in [2.2](#22-the-register-of-identities)'s register, it is never handed to another document in place of the identity, and nothing outside this section reads it. The identity is what [8.9](#89-the-repeat-key-supplied-rather-than-applied) compares two failures on and what [Section 12](#12-what-this-document-supplies-and-to-which-document) hands `Desktop app and delivery`.

| Condition kind | Observed value supplied by | Changes at | Holds over | Withdrawn on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the server connection | `Servers` — connected, reconnecting, disconnected | every connection state transition inside the connected period | *disconnected* and *reconnecting*, both of them | *connected* |
| disk full at a mount | the mount's free-space reading, and the next real write to that place | those two moments and no other | the mount's free-space reading at nothing left | a non-zero free-space reading **and** a real write to that place succeeding — both, not either |
| a place on a machine that refused a write for any other reason | the next real write to that place, and nothing else | that moment and no other | *the last write to this place was refused* | *a later write to this place succeeded* |
| the supervisor unavailable while SSH is live | `Rented-server runtime` | the supervisor answering again | the supervisor not answering | the supervisor answering |

**F12.** Any document raising a condition of its own declares it against that shape, names what supplies the observed value, and names both of the last two columns for its kind. A condition with no named supplier cannot be withdrawn and must not be raised.

**F13. Why the refusal rows are two and not one.** A write refusal is an event rather than a state, so its held-over value is the statement in the table and its withdrawal is a later success — which is a value in [8.2](#82-a-condition-record-is-a-derivation-never-a-copy)'s sense and not an inference. A fresher free-space reading says nothing whatever about a refusal that was not about space, so it is not a supplier for the second row; giving both kinds one row and one supplier pair would leave a permission refusal with no way to be withdrawn, and this section's own closing rule would then forbid raising it at all. This is the same split [8.9](#89-the-repeat-key-supplied-rather-than-applied) already makes from the other side — *a disk-full and a permission refusal on the same mount are two, because the kind differs*.

**F14. Both signals for the disk-full condition are available and both are real, and the withdrawal takes both.** Observed — deleting the large file *"returned the free space at once … and a copy into the same folder that had just refused succeeded. **Both signals are available: the reading recovers, and the write succeeds**"* ([Two more boxes](Two%20more%20boxes%20—%20the%20volume,%20the%20full%20disk,%20and%20a%20day%20left%20running.md), Fact 28). The second is the one Row 285's own example names — *the folder that could not be written to taking files again* — so a recovered reading alone does not withdraw the record. A writability test is not a signal at all, per [1.3](#13-rules-that-bind-every-store) rule 4.

### 8.4 Nothing is polled to withdraw a record

**F15.** A condition's observed value is refreshed at three moments and no others: **a transition its named supplier reports**; **the next attempt at the action that raised it**; and **a reading the user's own act already causes** — opening the file browser at that place, a queue reading on a connection, an update check already running. **No condition gets a watcher of its own, and no failure record reaches out to a machine.**

**F16. P10d's live-reading sequence is a declared origin of such readings and not a fourth moment. Added 2026-08-22, scenario replays Pile 1 item R1, routed by the runner.** `Desktop app and delivery` P10d declares one bounded sequence that reads a connected server repeatedly inside a standing connected period — started by `Servers` Q1 step 7, ended by the break declaration and by the period, its contents a closed set. Its outcomes arrive **under the three moments above rather than beside them**: its queue reading **is** this clause's own third-moment example, repeated only while the surface showing it is on screen; and where a pass's ordinary request moves the supervisor-unavailable condition's observed value, that is the second moment, with [8.3](#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value)'s named supplier reporting the transition as the first — exactly as the same request taken singly would. **The prohibition is untouched and nothing here is widened.** The sequence keys on no condition, takes no condition record as its subject, withdraws nothing of its own, and is no condition's watcher; and no reading joins its closed set because a record is standing.

**F17. What that costs, stated exactly.** The automatic withdrawal in Row 285's first clause fires when Smoozy's own knowledge of the condition changes, and not before. Where nothing has refreshed it, the record stands and the user's own clearing is the route — which is that concern's ordinary route and not something new asked of them. **Smoozy is never wrong on screen about its own state, because the record is that state**, which is the harm the clause names and the whole of it. [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 8 records the narrowing.

### 8.5 The connection condition needs a break reported without using the connection

**Constraint supplied to `Servers`:** the connection's observed value must come from a mechanism that reports a break without the caller having to use the connection first.

**F18.** Fact 57's spread is why. Observed — a machine closing politely and a provider's own stop were both **announced in the same second**, keepalive or not; the caller's own line pulled with keepalives on was **detected in 56 seconds**; with keepalives off it was **not detected at all for 21 minutes 45 seconds** ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 57). A condition record derived from a value twenty-one minutes stale is exactly the harm the clause names. Observed too — the rented machine sends nothing of its own: *"clientaliveinterval 0 … Keeping an idle connection open is the caller's job"* ([The rented machine](The%20rented%20machine.md)).

### 8.6 The subject outcome is durable and immutable

**F19.** **The subject outcome** is written into the record of the thing that ended — a job's history record, a unit's outcome under [9.4](#94-the-outcome-record) — at the moment the ending is learned, and **no observed-state change ever rewrites one, and no act of the user's against a failure record ever removes one**. That is Row 285's second clause, and it needs no mechanism beyond the immutability: a failed job stays failed because nothing anywhere is permitted to write over the field.

**F20.** The **failure record of kind *ending*** raised against that outcome is the mark of [8.1](#81-two-kinds-and-the-kind-is-declared), held apart from the outcome. It carries no copy of the outcome. The two go together when the subject itself is removed, and neither goes at any other moment. *(Changed 2026-09-19 from a code round — the Gemini catch-up of step 10a, Area 4, upstream must-do U-1. No auditor of this document has seen it. This called the mark *"the clearable half"*.)*

### 8.7 Every failure record names a durable subject

**F21.** The durable subjects are the server row, the key row, the transfer arrangement, the job record — provisional or confirmed — the history record, **the outcome record of a multi-part action** ([9.4](#94-the-outcome-record)). The outcome record is what gives a unit's failure record a durable subject by construction, including for an action with no other durable subject of its own: an ad-hoc upload from the file browser is not a transfer arrangement, and without the outcome record its failures would have nowhere to live. A surface a record is shown on may be volatile; a queue entry is a reading and does not survive the application run. **The record is kept against the durable subject; the volatile surface is a place it is shown.**

**F23. This list is closed here rather than declared per raiser, and that is deliberate.** [8.3](#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value) has the raising document declare the observed value's supplier and both withdrawal columns; the subject is the one part it does not declare. What this section promises is that **no failure record's only subject is a volatile one**, and that is a promise about the list rather than about each raiser's care. So a document raising a condition against something not named above brings it here and the list grows, rather than naming a subject of its own where nothing checks it.

**F24.** That is what keeps Row 285's third clause. Nothing about knowing what went wrong depends on the application having been open, because no failure record's only subject is a volatile one. Where a machine is destroyed before the next connection, its endings go with it and the history record says it learned none — [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s own answer, and not a hole this section leaves.

### 8.8 When a failure record ends

**F25.** Three endings, and [Whole app](../Specs/Whole%20app.md) names all three.

- **Its subject is removed** — server removed, key removed, transfer arrangement removed, history record deleted, outcome record removed ([8.7](#87-every-failure-record-names-a-durable-subject), [11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)). This is the one ending that takes the subject outcome with it as well as the mark ([8.1](#81-two-kinds-and-the-kind-is-declared)).
- **The user clears a condition record themselves**, which that concern grants for a failure that describes something still true, and which is the ordinary route [8.4](#84-nothing-is-polled-to-withdraw-a-record) leans on. The gesture itself is **Design's**, by that concern's own marker, and `Desktop app and delivery` presents it; what is fixed here is that it ends the record and nothing else — an immutable subject outcome is not the user's to clear and is not touched. **An ending record is not cleared by the user**: [Whole app](../Specs/Whole%20app.md) says a failure is *"cleared only where it describes something still true"*, and an ending describes something that has already happened. It ends only with its subject, the first bullet. *(Changed 2026-09-19 from a code round — the Gemini catch-up of step 10a, Area 4, upstream must-do U-1. No auditor of this document has seen it. This bullet read *"which that concern grants outright"* and *"it ends the record, for a condition record and an ending record alike"*, which misread the spec's grant as covering both kinds. The spec, `Preparation/Design/Whole app.md` and the application all carry no clearing of an ending.)*
- **A condition record is withdrawn** — its observed value has moved outside the set it was raised over, so it is spent and is removed at the next write to its subject ([8.2](#82-a-condition-record-is-a-derivation-never-a-copy)). This is the ending [Whole app](../Specs/Whole%20app.md) states as *"One more thing ends it: the condition the message describes plainly ending"*, and it applies to a condition record alone.

**F26.** **And at no other moment**, which rules out exactly this: no age, no cap, no sweep, no schedule, and nothing that reaches out to a machine to look ([8.4](#84-nothing-is-polled-to-withdraw-a-record)). It does not rule out the withdrawal above, which is a change in Smoozy's own knowledge and not a mechanism that goes looking. **Nor does it rule out a withdrawal that falls out of `Desktop app and delivery` P10d's live-reading sequence**, which goes looking for the queue's live promise and never for a condition, and whose passes [8.4](#84-nothing-is-polled-to-withdraw-a-record) places under the moments it already names.

**F27.** [Section 11](#11-retention-and-cleanup--the-local-lifetimes) puts the record in the **subject-held** class and carries the other two endings with it.

### 8.9 The repeat key, supplied rather than applied

**F28. Two failures share a reason when they share a condition identity** — not when they share their words, and not when they share the unit that met them. Two failures raised against the same condition key resolve to that same identity ([8.3](#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value)), which is how the identity is found; the identity is what is compared and what is supplied onward. A hundred files refused by one full mount on one machine are one condition on one subject; the same words from two servers are two conditions, because the subject differs; a disk-full and a permission refusal on the same mount are two, because the kind differs.

**F29.** Whether and how that identity is used to say one thing once is `Desktop app and delivery`'s under Row 288. **What this section supplies is the condition identity**, which is what this section's *repeat key* names; the **condition key** of [8.3](#83-the-condition-identity-and-who-supplies-each-observed-value) is a different thing, stays inside that section, and is never handed on. This section answers nothing of that row.

## 9. The atomic-or-partial boundary

**Constraint:** Row 292, quoted at the head of this document, and [Rendering](../Specs/Rendering.md)'s Row 2 — *"**A single job is all or nothing.** It either reaches the machine and joins the line, or it does not exist at all."* Both are kept. Nothing here decides which kind any particular module's action is; each feature document declares that, against the rule below.

### 9.1 The partition unit

**P1. Every action has exactly one partition unit: the smallest thing the owning spec makes a promise about the outcome of.**

- **Across partition units an action is partial.** Each unit has its own outcome; a unit that fails does not abandon the rest; the action's outcome is the list.
- **Within one partition unit an action is atomic.** It reaches its whole outcome or it leaves nothing.

**P2.** That single sentence is what a builder needs, and it is a search rather than a judgment: find the smallest promised outcome, and everything below it is atomic.

### 9.2 Three tests, applied in order, and the answer is declared rather than inferred

1. **Name the smallest thing the owning spec promises an outcome about.** That is the partition unit. Where a spec promises an outcome about the whole and about nothing smaller, the action is atomic entire — which is what makes a job all or nothing while a set of files is not.
2. **Ask whether a half-finished unit could be mistaken for a finished one.** Where it could, the unit is made atomic by staging and a single publication step, never by ordering and never by care. This is not optional and it is not a matter of degree: observed, an interrupted transfer leaves bytes at the final name with *"identical mode, permissions, extended attributes, inode flags, allocation and file-type output"* to a whole file, and an interrupted **overwrite** left *"a **40,000,000-byte hybrid** — 10,444,800 new bytes spliced onto 29,555,200 old ones. Neither version, plausible size, no marker"* ([Six machines](Six%20machines%20—%20the%20container,%20the%20webhook%20and%20the%20ready%20action.md), Fact 18). `Server protocol and transfers` owns the staging-and-publication contract and `Job files and outputs` owns publication of a job's outputs; the rule here is that no unit may be left atomic by luck.
3. **Ask whether a unit is unsafe unless another unit succeeded first.** Where it is, [9.3](#93-an-ordered-set-is-one-unit) governs the set. That is the only dependence this test asks about, because it is the only one [9.3](#93-an-ordered-set-is-one-unit) answers. **A unit that is merely useless without another, or merely later than another, is still an independent unit**: where the earlier unit fails, it is reported as **not attempted** under [9.3](#93-an-ordered-set-is-one-unit)'s recorded state, naming the unit that stopped it, and no new state and no new vocabulary is needed for it.

**P3. Each feature document declares, per action, its partition unit and which of the three tests decided it.** The declaration sits in the document. Nothing decides it at run time, because a boundary decided at run time is a boundary two callers can decide differently.

### 9.3 An ordered set is one unit

**P4.** Where the specs order two parts because the second is unsafe unless the first succeeded, **the ordered pair is one partition unit**, and where the first fails the second is **not attempted**. *Not attempted* is a recorded state of its own, distinct from *failed*, and it names the unit that stopped it. Row 292 requires the user to be told what Smoozy could not do; a unit reported as failed when it was never tried is not that.

### 9.4 The outcome record

**P5.** For every action whose partition unit is smaller than the action, there is one **outcome record**. It is a durable subject in its own right ([8.7](#87-every-failure-record-names-a-durable-subject)), it sits in the local record ([1.1](#11-on-the-users-computer)), and it is **user-held** ([11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)). It holds:

1. **The enumeration of units, durable before the first unit is attempted**, so an interruption cannot lose the fact that a unit was owed.
2. **Each unit's outcome, written durably at the moment it becomes true** — done; failed; or not attempted, naming the unit that stopped it. A unit outcome is a subject outcome under [8.1](#81-two-kinds-and-the-kind-is-declared): immutable, and not the user's to clear. The failure record raised against a failed unit hangs off this record as its durable subject; it is the mark, held apart from the unit outcome, and goes with its subject ([8.8](#88-when-a-failure-record-ends)). *(Changed 2026-09-19 from a code round — the Gemini catch-up of step 10a, Area 4, upstream must-do U-1. No auditor of this document has seen it. This called that record *"the clearable half"*.)*
3. **The action's own outcome, derived from the list of unit outcomes and never a separate durable field.** Two fields would drift, which is the same argument [8.2](#82-a-condition-record-is-a-derivation-never-a-copy) makes for a condition. It is derived from the unit outcomes alone and never from the failure records raised against them, because a failure record is the mark and holds no copy of the outcome it names ([F3](#81-two-kinds-and-the-kind-is-declared)) — the unit outcomes are the one place the fact is. *(Reason changed 2026-09-19, the leftover of the Gemini catch-up of step 10a, Area 4, upstream must-do U-1, found by that catch-up's close check. The rule is unchanged. No auditor of this document has seen it. This read *"because those the user may clear and the outcomes they may not"*, and after U-1 the user clears no ending record.)*

**P6. That ordering is what keeps the second half of Row 292.** Because each unit's outcome is durable before it is presented, an interruption between doing the part and saying so loses the saying and not the fact, and the next presentation reads the record. A design that presented first and recorded afterwards would satisfy the sentence in the ordinary case and go quiet in exactly the case the sentence exists for.

### 9.5 No unit is retried inside its action

**P7.** A failed unit is recorded and reported. Trying again is a new action with a new operation identity, per [5.4](#54-retry-and-three-things-that-are-not-one-another). An action that retried inside itself would report an outcome the user never asked for and would make the count in [9.4](#94-the-outcome-record) untrue.

### 9.6 Where a unit must be atomic and the ground for it is not established

**P8. The parts, named rather than left as *the parts that matter here*.**

- **Fact 78 is answered except in two narrow places, and neither is the two-file half.** That half is answered and negative — no single filesystem operation updates two independent files atomically — and is a settled constraint rather than an open part. The single-rename half is answered on ext4, xfs and overlayfs by real forcible kills at each commit boundary. **What is open is [9.2](#92-three-tests-applied-in-order-and-the-answer-is-declared-rather-than-inferred) test 2 on a filesystem none of those three covers, and the rollback boundary, which was never interrupted mid-flight.**
- **Fact 79 is open on the throwaway route on all three systems, and answered negatively for Windows on the documentation route.** Mac and Linux are answered on the documentation route. Windows publishes no crash guarantee for either of its two replace operations and one of them documents a failure state in which the final name is absent, so on that system the ground for test 2 is not merely unread — it is read and against. **The throwaway route is permanently unresolved on all three systems and none of the three is coming** — Windows and desktop Linux want computers this project does not have and the Creator ruled on 2026-08-31 that there will be no further rented machine for answering a question, and the Mac third goes with them because the route is one question across three systems that can never be completed (`Facts.md` Fact 79, *"not a pending item and must not be read as one"*). **So this clause governs on all three systems permanently, and not until a run.** [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 3 carries the label in full. *(Widened 2026-08-29: this read "Its throwaway half cannot be posed until a Windows computer exists", which named one blocked system where Fact 79 names two. The permanence sentence was added 2026-09-02 and corrected the same day, where it read* **so Mac is the only one that is unrun and posable** *and left the Mac third open against Fact 79's own ruling.)*

**P9. A unit that rests on either is written as atomic and the document resting on it says so**, rather than the rule being weakened to fit. [Section 13](#13-unresolved) items 2 and 3 state what breaks.

## 10. Deletion tombstones

**H1.** A tombstone exists where a later reading would otherwise undo a deletion. There are **two** kinds and the list is closed.

### 10.1 The history tombstone

**Constraint:** [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) — *"Filling in never brings back an entry the user deleted, whatever the machine still holds — the deletion rule below is the stronger one, and how that is kept is Architecture's."* And: *"**Deleting reaches both copies.** Deleting an entry takes it out of Smoozy's record, and off the machine as well when that machine is connected. When it is not connected, the entry still goes from the record, and it does not come back …"*

**H2. A history tombstone is keyed by the job identity alone**, and it holds the job identity, the moment of the deletion, and whether the machine-side copy has been removed yet.

**H3. It is keyed by the job identity and by nothing else, deliberately.** A tombstone keyed to a server row would be lost if the user removed that server and added the same machine again, and the promise is about that machine rather than about a row in a list. Job identities are never reused ([2.1](#21-the-rules-that-bind-every-identity)), so the key is sufficient on its own and the tombstone is one identity in size.

**H4. It refuses step 4 of [Section 7](#7-reconciliation-on-the-desktop) unconditionally**, whatever the machine still holds, and whether or not the machine-side copy was ever removed.

**H5. It is also the owed erase for the machine's copy.** While the copy has not been removed, the next connection to a machine holding that job identity removes it and marks the tombstone. That is the same recorded-debt pattern [Security and licence](Security%20and%20licence.md#51-the-order-the-recorded-debt-and-what-a-crash-between-the-steps-leaves) §5.1 already uses for a secret, applied a second time, and it is named as the same pattern so a builder meets one shape rather than two.

**H6. A tombstone is kept for the life of the local record** and is cleared by nothing.

### 10.2 The secret-erase mark

**H7.** Owned by [Security and licence](Security%20and%20licence.md#51-the-order-the-recorded-debt-and-what-a-crash-between-the-steps-leaves) §5.1 and cited here so that both members of the class are visible in one place. It is a tombstone in everything but name: the local record marks a subject as owing an erase, the store item is erased, and the mark is cleared only when the store reports the item gone. Its sweep runs after the first successful store operation of a run and acts on marks and on nothing else. Nothing here changes it.

### 10.3 Considered and not filed

**H8.** Named so a later reader can overrule the judgment rather than assume it was missed.

- **A cancelled or cleared job.** It leaves the queue and the provisional local record is removed. Where the machine still holds it, a later connection reads it as untracked work and offers adoption — which is the specs' own behaviour and a correct outcome, not a restoration of something deleted.
- **A server removed under *leave it be*.** Its work keeps running and a later connection to a re-added server offers adoption. Same reasoning.
- **A suppressed warning restored.** Every suppression is one row in one durable table, carrying the module and the warning identity, and the single control of Row 290 is one delete over that table. Nothing is being undone by a later reading, so nothing needs a tombstone. Row 290 is `Desktop app and delivery`'s; what is supplied here is the one table.

Reached by the scenario-replay route as item S3, not from this document; [Section 14](#14-moments-that-reached-the-creator) carries what this document had decided and why that no longer describes what is built.

**R13. What is measured, and from when.** The age of a fetched derivative is the elapsed time since **that copy was published on the user's computer**, recorded beside the copy at the fetch — one moment, on the user's computer's own clock. It is not the frame's render moment, not the job's ending and not the machine's note, because a lifetime taken from a machine reading would be arithmetic across two clocks, which D5 forbids and [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 10 shows the cost of.

**R14. The overruled argument survives as a limit, and it is carried rather than dropped.** Fact 13's negative still holds, so this is the one lifetime here that rests on a reading rather than on an act or a subject: an administrator, or a clock stepped backwards or forwards on the user's own computer, can end it early or extend it indefinitely, and nothing detects either. **The consequence is bounded on both sides and neither side reaches anything but the picture.** Early: a preview goes before its period, which is the ruling's own accepted cost arriving sooner — the job's record, its ending, its times and its figures are untouched, and the entry shows no picture. Late: the copy takes disk longer than the period, which is the unbounded state the ruling replaced. **No other record anywhere rests on a fetched derivative being present**, which is what keeps a misread clock from costing anything further.

**R15. There is no size-based class either, and that is now the Creator's ruling as well as this document's reading.** [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) settles that nothing expires and Smoozy drops nothing on its own; a cache eviction anywhere on this side would be that document's rule broken by a mechanism it never sees — and the Creator, offered a size cap as one of the three endings, declined it. **That spec sentence now carries the one exception the ruling makes**, written into the spec on 2026-08-22.

**R16. The period is thirty days, and it is a constant of the application. Ruled 2026-09-02 under the Creator's standing delegation of that date; [Section 13](#13-unresolved) item 12 carries what it costs.** The Creator ruled the age ending on 2026-08-22 and did not state the period; on 2026-09-02 they delegated every decision that could be delegated, and the figure was taken under that delegation rather than invented against a standing refusal. **The ground is the promise the preview carries and nothing else**: a preview is for glancing at a recent job, a month covers coming back to one after a break, and one picture per finished frame per job would otherwise pile up for ever, which is the state the 2026-08-22 ruling was made to end. **Nothing reads the number from anywhere** — no setting exposes it, no reading raises or lowers it, and no machine supplies it, which is the same shape [4](#4-the-event-journal-and-acknowledgement) E6a fixes for the journal cap and for the same reason: a lifetime that a reading could move is a lifetime nobody stated. [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) carries the same figure in the user's own terms under *A job's frame preview*, and nothing here may say otherwise.

**R17. User-held has a stricter consequence for the two trust records.** No ordinary control lowers or removes one: an application rollback does not lower the desktop-update record; removing a server row, repairing a package or destroying a rental does not lower the server-package record. They end only when the user removes this installation's local record itself. These are two independent anti-rollback lifetimes, not one application-wide sequence.

### 11.2 Where each class is enforced

**R18.** **user-held** and **subject-held** things are removed by the act that removes them and by no sweep. **The one exception is the age ending added on 2026-08-22, and it is an exception because no act carries it: a fetched derivative past its period is cleared by a sweep.** The sweep runs at **application start**, and again **whenever a preview fetch publishes a copy** — the moment the held set grows, and the one moment a running application is certain to reach without a timer. Nothing polls a clock on a schedule, nothing reaches a machine, and a sweep that finds nothing past its period does nothing. Where the application is never left running long enough to cross the period, the start sweep is what clears the copies.

**R19. The comparison a sweep makes, stated so it can be built. Added 2026-09-02 with the period.** Each fetched derivative carries one recorded moment — the moment that copy was published on the user's computer, on that computer's own clock ([11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)). **A sweep reads that clock once, at the start of the pass, and judges every copy in the pass against that one reading**, so no two copies in one pass are compared against different nows. **A copy is past its period when that reading minus its recorded moment is greater than thirty days, and at nothing less** — thirty days exactly is kept, which is what *kept for thirty days and cleared after that* says. **A difference that is negative — a clock now standing earlier than the moment the copy was published — is not greater than thirty days and clears nothing**, and there is no second rule for it: the copy is judged again at the next pass, which is the whole of what [11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth)'s carried limit already prices.

**R20. What a pass clears and what it may not touch.** The copy alone. The job record, its ending, its times, its figures and every other field it carries are untouched, and the entry then shows no picture, which is the ruling's own accepted cost. **A pass writes no durable state of its own** — no record of having run, no next-due moment, no count — so nothing schedules the next pass, nothing is recovered after a crash, and D6 is untouched. A copy whose removal did not complete is met again at the next pass and removed then.

**R21. The test, written here because this mechanism had none.** Record a copy's publication moment at thirty days and one second before the pass's own clock reading and run a pass: **the copy is gone and the job record is whole**. Record another at thirty days exactly before that reading: **the copy stands**. Run both at application start and again at a preview fetch's publication, which are the two moments a pass runs. Neither case needs a machine, a connection, a wait or a second application run, so the boundary is exercised directly rather than by leaving copies lying for a month.

**R22.** **period-held** things are in process memory and are dropped **at the moment the connected period ends**, which is the class's own rule above; the application run ending is one of the ways a period ends and not the only one, so a period that ends while the run carries on drops them then. **run-held** things are in process memory or in a swept area, so they end by the application run ending, which D6 makes identical for a crash and a clean close. **action-held** things end with the action, and the staged material's cleanup is `Server protocol and transfers`' under its own contract.

### 11.3 The two rulings those documents were promised

**R23. A fetched derivative is subject-held on its job record, and is not throwaway on the user's computer.** The shape calls the derivatives throwaway material and that is true of the machine-side set; it is not true of the fetched copy, and the difference is load-bearing. [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) promises that *"A finished job keeps its preview"*, so a history record shows the picture; the ordinary end of a job is that the machine is destroyed; and once the machine is gone the copy cannot be made again — observed on a destruction, *"Everything on the disk went with it … None of it was recoverable and no part of it was offered back"* ([The rented machine](The%20rented%20machine.md)). **CORRECTED 2026-08-22 by the Creator's ruling — the age ending of [11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth). This clause used to read:** ~~*"So a fetched derivative is cleared when its job record is deleted and at no other moment — never on age, never on a cap, never because a machine is still reachable."*~~ **What it says now: a fetched derivative is cleared when its job record is deleted, or when it has been held longer than thirty days ([11.1](#111-five-classes-and-the-reason-there-is-no-sixth), ruled 2026-09-02), whichever comes first — never on a cap, and never because a machine is still reachable.** `Job files and outputs` applies that class and its citation was corrected on 2026-08-22 at that document's 8.4, K1 and K2.

**R24. The reasoning above is not withdrawn, and it is what the ruling knowingly overrides.** Once the machine is gone the copy cannot be made again, so an age ending does make a finished job's preview a thing that can be lost. That was this document's ground for refusing one, it was put to the Creator as exactly that, and they ruled for the age ending anyway. **What is lost is the picture alone**: the history record, the ending, the times, the figures and every other thing the entry carries are untouched, and [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s *"A finished job keeps its preview"* is corrected in the spec on the same date rather than being quietly narrowed here.

**R25. A machine-side derivative is the other document's to clear**, and the constraint supplied is the one above's cause: the machine is the only place a derivative can be made, so a machine-side derivative may be cleared only once the fetched copy exists or the job's record is gone.

**R26. A third trigger, for the derivative nothing ever fetches. Added 2026-08-22 at the Architecture seam close, routed by the runner from the `Open Threads` item raised by `Job files and outputs`' Round 1 on 2026-08-18.** With only the two triggers above, a derivative on a machine no copy is ever fetched from can be cleared only by the job's record going, so it sits for the life of that record. **The third: a machine-side derivative may also be cleared once the machine it sits on will never be connected to again** — the server row removed, or the rental destroyed — **which is the moment the fetched copy can no longer be made at all.** It costs nothing and loses nothing: after that moment the copy is unmakeable whether or not the bytes are still there, so keeping them buys the user nothing and the job's record already says the preview was never fetched. **The two triggers above are unchanged**, and this one never fires while the machine is still reachable.

**R27. A downloaded output file is not in any class.** It is the user's file. Nothing in Smoozy counts it, caps it, ages it or removes it, and [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) says so from the other side: *"whatever the user brought down to their own computer is theirs, and untouched by any of this …"* It is stated here because a document that lists lifetimes and omits this invites a builder to invent one.

### 11.4 On the machine

**R28.** Retention of a job's note, its captured streams and its history copy belongs to `Rented-server runtime` and `Queue & History`. ## 14. Moments that reached the Creator

**None from this document, and one of the three checks below has since been overruled from elsewhere.** *(Three at the writing. Four more were checked at the tenth sitting on 2026-08-25 and all four declined; the paragraph at the end of this section carries them.)*

Three answers were checked against [Method](Method.md)'s third escalation — does this put anything in front of the user that no spec says they get — and none reached it.

**The installation-identity discard of [2.3](#23-the-machines-installation-identity).** A machine that was ready reads as not prepared after a provider recycles it, and `Server Setup`'s re-run is what the user meets. That is a fact about the rental rather than a cost Smoozy introduces: the installation genuinely is gone, observed down to the package count, and the alternative is Smoozy acting on state that no longer exists. `Server Setup` already has a user-facing route for a machine that is not ready, so nothing new is asked of anyone.

**The fetched-derivative ruling of [11.3](#113-the-two-rulings-those-documents-were-promised).** Keeping a job's small copies for the life of its history record spends the user's disk without asking. It is not a place to look, something to type, or a thing that can be lost, and it is what [Queue & History](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) already grants twice over: history keeps the preview, and nothing expires. The growth is bounded by the user's own history, which that spec put under their control on purpose. **The opposite ruling would have been the escalation** — a cache eviction would make a finished job's preview a thing that can be lost.

**OVERRULED 2026-08-22, and the paragraph above is kept as the record of what this document decided rather than as a description of what is built.** The check was made here and declined; the same cost reached the Creator by the scenario-replay route as item S3, put as the moment it is — an overnight batch leaving preview pictures on the user's own computer with no age limit and no size limit — and **they ruled for the age limit.** So the last sentence above is now the ruling: a finished job's preview is a thing that can be lost, deliberately. The two decisions in the paragraph that stand are that the growth is real and that it is the user's disk; what does not stand is the conclusion that nothing about it reached them.

**The narrowing in [8.4](#84-nothing-is-polled-to-withdraw-a-record).** A condition record that nothing refreshes stands until the user clears it. Clearing is the route that concern names first, so this asks nothing new; and the alternative considered and declined was a watcher that reaches out to a machine on a schedule, which would spend the user's connection and their rented machine's time on something no spec asks for.

**The tenth sitting's four, 2026-08-25, checked and all four declined.** **The run-mark** ([1.1](#11-on-the-users-computer) I2, [D6a](#3-desired-state-and-observed-state)): the one interruption it can produce is the spec's own Creator-granted exception, and D6a adds no reach to it — one field, one reader, one prompt, and never a thing that can be lost, since no other record derives a fact from it. **The diary** (I1): it puts nothing anywhere — no screen shows it, no setting governs it, no control clears it, and its total loss costs the contents of a future report and nothing of the user's own. **The pane widths** (I3): Design's own promise held durably, and the worst an absent value costs is the declared default, which is what a user who never dragged already meets. **The declined-update version mark** (I4): it **removes** an interruption rather than adding one — without it Z23's repeating check would draw the Linux install box again at every pass, which is the nagging the spec forbids.
