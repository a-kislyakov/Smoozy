## 1. The diary

**Constraint:** Row 451, and the spec — *"One rolling record, capped at 20 MB, oldest lines dropped as it fills."* Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-24) for the shape; the figure is the runner's.

**R1. The diary is one line-oriented plain-text record in a diary area of its own, in the per-user application-data location the platform file adapter names.** The location rule is `Desktop app and delivery` L3's, applied and not re-decided: the path comes from each system's own call and is never assembled from the login account name. The diary area sits beside the local record and never inside it.

**R2. The diary is not state, and that is the decision Row 451 waits on: it is a fifth durable non-state thing on this side, beside `Desktop app and delivery` L2's four, and not a row inside the local record.** Two grounds, one per refused alternative.

- **It is not a row in the local record because its shape is not that store's shape.** L1's store is a database with a schema version and forward-only migrations (L5–L8); the diary is a plain-text record whose oldest lines are dropped as it fills and whose report is cut out of it as text. Forcing it into the record would put a self-evicting text stream under a migration discipline built for state, and would make every diary write a database write.
- **It does not break §1.1's closing rule because that rule is about state.** *"The local record is the only durable store on this side that holds state"* — and no state fact is ever read from the diary: nothing in the application branches on anything it holds, nothing is presented from it, no promise to the user rests on its contents, and its total loss costs the contents of a future report and nothing else. It is evidentiary material, the same standing the fetched-derivative area has — durable, bounded, and not state. `State, identity and recovery` §1.3 rule 5 is honoured by construction: no file's presence, name or size in the diary area is ever read as a state fact. *(The one launch-time reading this subject needs is deliberately **not** in the diary — [Section 2](#2-the-run-identity-and-the-run-mark) U2 puts the run-mark in the local record precisely because it **is** state.)*

**What this cost the documents that own the lists, and it was routed rather than written** ([12](#12-unresolved) item 4, closed 2026-08-25): `State, identity and recovery` §1.1's durable table carries the diary a row among the non-state entries (I1), and §11.1 names it among the objects outside the five lifetime classes — **as a store and not as a fourth object of the same kind**: *"This table governs things held inside a store; it has never described a store … The diary is a store, so this table was never going to hold it … So what sits outside is three of one kind and one of another, rather than four of one."* *(Corrected 2026-08-29 at the stage-close gate repair. This cited that section's own sentence, ~~a third such object would be a reason to look again~~, as a live trigger the diary makes fall due as a fourth. That sentence was struck in the owning document on 2026-08-25 — it had already fired unnoticed — and the look it asked for reached a different outcome in both respects this note relied on: the count is not the trigger any more, two objects outside the table that share an ending is; and the diary is a store rather than a fourth of that kind.)*

**R3. The cap is 20 MB, fixed rather than read, and no free-space reading raises or lowers it.** The same rule `State, identity and recovery` **§4 E6a** fixes for the journal's own cap — a constant of the package rather than a reading — and which `Rented-server runtime` **R2** applies on the machine in as many words: *"That figure is a constant of the server package and no free-space reading raises or lowers it"*. The desktop side of it is decided here, for this store, on the same ground. The figure is the spec's own, the runner's to overrule. *(Corrected 2026-09-02 at the stage-close gate repair. This read* the same rule `State, identity and recovery` §11.4 imposes on every bounded store on this side*, and §11.4 is headed* On the machine *— its only rule, **R28**, is that nothing on a machine is cleared on the strength of a free-space reading alone, so it imposes nothing desktop-side and could not be the rule this clause inherits. The `Rented-server runtime` half of the citation was right and is kept. The same sentence is duplicated in the row handout, which is not this document's to edit and is named to the runner.)*

**R4. The diary has one writer — the diary service — and every byte it writes goes through the platform file adapter.** A4 governs with no carve-out: a module never opens the diary, never writes a line itself, and never holds a diary file handle; it hands an entry to the service ([Section 3](#3-what-goes-into-the-diary) J2's declared entries) and the service writes it. The service is a named service of `Desktop app and delivery` §21, **landed on 2026-08-25 as that document's §5.3 L14–L16** ([12](#12-unresolved) item 5, closed; this read *routed … until that row exists* until 2026-08-31) — without it, A1 would make every module's diary write a broken boundary, which is exactly `Desktop app and delivery` G2's silent-failure shape: a module writing nowhere satisfies its own spec and leaves a hole the Creator cannot tell from a thing that did not happen.

**R5. A line is made durable at the moment it is written, and eviction never rewrites a retained byte.** Two rules and both are forced.

- **Durable at the moment.** D6's second sentence applied: the diary exists to survive the crash that follows the line, so a line buffered against a later flush is a line the one crash that matters loses. Each entry is written and forced down when its fact becomes true. The cost is `State, identity and recovery` §1.3 rule 6's stated cost — a real wait per entry — and it is bounded by ~~construction: diary entries are the acts a user could name, not per-byte events, so their rate is the user's own rate~~ **the rate rules the kinds themselves carry, which is where the bound actually lives**: act lines by [3](#3-what-goes-into-the-diary) J2a's third test, one per act the user asked for; told lines by J4a, one per declared ending or reading; and the four statement kinds by their own once-rules — J6 once per condition identity or ending record, J7 once per site reached, J5 once per stated gap, J8 one per handler. **No kind is written per file, per frame, per derivative, per byte or per journal event.** *(Corrected 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair: the struck sentence was true of act lines and false of told lines, which J4 wrote with nothing narrowing which of a machine's endings and readings became one. `State, identity and recovery` E6a states the journal's cap in work — a job costs a small multiple of one entry per finished frame — so a told line per journal event is a forced device write per finished frame on the desktop, and R3's 20 MB filling at the machine's rate evicts the previous session that [5](#5-a-report-is-a-slice) S2's third slice and [8](#8-the-two-entry-points) X2 depend on.)*
- **Eviction by whole segments, never by rewriting.** Row 451's own constraint: a record trimmed by rewriting itself has a window in which a crash loses the whole of it. So the diary is kept as an ordered set of append-only segment files inside the diary area; lines are appended to the newest; when the set exceeds the cap the **oldest whole segment is deleted** and nothing already written is ever modified. The oldest lines drop first, at segment granularity; a crash at any moment loses at most the line being appended. The segment size is Build's to pick under two bounds: the sum obeys R3's cap, and every line is whole within one segment.

**R6. The diary is protected exactly as the local record is, and by the same decision.** `Security and licence` §9's argument is cited rather than remade: it is written in the per-user application-data location, created for the owning user only, protected by the operating system's own file permissions and nothing further, and **not encrypted** — an encrypting key would live in the store the secrets already live in, and on a Linux computer with nowhere to remember it would cost the user something to type, which no spec grants. It holds no secret value by construction ([Section 4](#4-what-it-never-holds-and-what-it-holds-that-is-the-users) N1–N2), which is what makes that protection sufficient.

**R7. The user never meets the diary.** No screen shows it, no setting governs it, no control clears it, and nothing counts it against anything — the spec's own words: never asked about it, never has to switch it on, does not meet it as a feature. Its lifetime is the installation's: it ends when the user removes the application-data location, and at no other moment.

**R8. Smoozy never sends the diary, and never sends anything cut from it.** [Section 10](#10-where-this-stops) B1 is the rule; this line is here so the record's own section carries it.

## 2. The run identity and the run-mark

**Constraint:** Rows 459 and 464, and the spec — *"Smoozy writes a mark at the moment the user asks to quit. If the next launch finds no mark, the run before it ended badly, and Smoozy says so and offers to report it."* The mechanism below keeps that promise in a form D6 can carry, and its one exemption is stated exactly.

**U1. Every application run has a run identity, issued by the desktop application at application start, opaque, never reused, and bound by every rule of `State, identity and recovery` §2.1.** It is random bytes: never derived from a moment, a counter another run could repeat, anything the user typed, or any reading of the computer. It is the *session* the spec's three slicing rules cut on, and it exists nowhere in this project before this clause — Row 459's own finding. **The register row landed on 2026-08-25** ([12](#12-unresolved) item 4, closed; this read *is routed* until 2026-08-31): §2.2's register carries the line — issued by the desktop application, at application start, unique across every run of this installation, held durably in the run-mark and in diary lines.

**U2. The run-mark is one field of the local record, holding a pair: a state — *running* or *clean* — and the run identity that wrote it.** It is in the local record because it **is** state — the one durable value on this side whose reading at launch takes a branch — and the local record is the only durable store on this side that holds state. **The inventory row landed on 2026-08-25** (same item, closed; this read *is routed* until 2026-08-31): L1 forbids `Desktop app and delivery` adding it, and §1.1's local-record row names the mark at **I2**, which is the home it needs.

**U3. The mark's cycle, and no step of it writes on exit.**

1. **At application start, and only once the launch has become the ordinary running application** — it holds `Desktop app and delivery` P2's instance lock — and before any feature module composes: the field is read, its value decides [Section 9](#9-after-a-crash) C1's offer, and then the field is written durably as *(running, this run's identity)*. **A launch that does not become the ordinary running application performs no part of this step: no read, no offer, no write.** *(Corrected 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair. This step read ~~At application start, before any feature module composes: the field is read, and its value decides C1's offer~~, unconditional, with nothing ordering P2's check before it — so a second ordinary launch, which P2 grants as an ordinary case, read the mark of the run that was at that moment on screen and opened C1's box about it, wrote the local record while the first process was still its sole writer, and left the mark naming a process that exited seconds later, so a genuine later crash would have cut a report holding one session line.)*
2. **At the moment the user asks to quit** — the ask, not the exit: the field is written durably as *(clean, this run's identity)*. The ask is a fact becoming true, so D6's second sentence — *every durable write happens at the moment the fact becomes true, and nothing is written on exit* — is honoured, not exempted.
3. **Nothing is written on the way out**, at any ending of any kind.

So the next launch reads: ***clean*** — the previous run ended after the user asked to quit; ***running*, with a run identity** — the previous run ended without the ask, and the identity names which run; **absent** — no previous run is known (a fresh local record), and no prompt is offered. The three readings answer Row 464's first, second and fourth open decisions at once: the mark is never *cleared*, it is overwritten at the next start, so a stale mark cannot exist; the crashed run is named by the identity the mark carries; and a fresh or replaced record reads as *no previous run* rather than as a crash.

**U4. The exemption to D6, stated exactly and no wider.** D6's third sentence — *a crash and a clean close therefore leave the same durable state, and there is no recovery path for one that is not the ordinary path for the other* — is what this mechanism breaks, and it breaks it by exactly one field with exactly one reader. The run-mark is the only durable value that differs between a crash and a clean close; the launch-time offer of [Section 9](#9-after-a-crash) is the only thing that reads it; **no recovery path differs** — every store, every record and every mechanism behaves identically on both endings, which is what makes C5's *nothing was lost* a true sentence rather than a reassurance. The Creator granted the exception on 2026-08-24 (*"Smoozy may speak up, yes."*, in the spec); the decisions-record line inside `State, identity and recovery` at D6 is Rule 7's requirement and is the runner's, per `Preparation/Groundwork/Open Threads.md` — carried as a dependency at [12](#12-unresolved) item 4.

**U5. Two running copies cannot race the mark, because two ordinary running copies do not exist.** `Desktop app and delivery` P2: one ordinary running instance per user, and a second launch raises the first. The one overlap is the update handover, and the mark rides it under the rules already written there: the probe is a launch, so its own step 1 runs — it reads the old value, **makes no offer on it (U5a)** *(corrected 2026-08-29: this said the probe offers where it reads a running mark, which on the success branch is after every ordinary successful update)*, and writes *(running, probe's identity)* as sole writer after the lock handoff; L8's pre-migration copy carries whatever the field held, so L13's restore on a failed start puts back *(running, old run's identity)* ~~, and the old process — still the running application — ends its run through step 2 as usual~~ *(corrected 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair: on Windows the old process is already gone when the restore happens — Z18 and Z19 put the relaunch in a helper that "outlives the application for the length of one relaunch" — so there is no running application left to end through step 2; and on all three systems Z21's rollback ends by **starting a build**, which U5b is what makes harmless)*. **What keeps no state in that cycle reporting a crash that did not happen is C1's trigger, not the shape of the handover**: the probe is one of the two launches C1 condition 2 names (U5a) and the rollback's relaunch is the other (U5b), and neither is a launch C1 admits.

**U5a. A launch in probe mode runs step 1's read and write and makes no offer, and it is the first of the two launches [9](#9-after-a-crash) C1's second condition names.** *(That sentence read ~~and that is the one exception to U3 step 1 and to C1's trigger~~ until 2026-08-31, when C1 was inverted and the rollback's relaunch was found to be a second such launch — U5b.)* **Added 2026-08-29 at the stage-close gate repair, where the handover was traced end to end for the first time.** Nothing writes the field on the old process's side and nothing may: `Desktop app and delivery` P2 has the old process stop writing and hand the instance lock to the probe before the probe's migration, and U3 step 3 forbids a write on the way out — no user asks to quit in a handover, so step 2 never runs. **So at the moment the probe reads, the field holds *(running, old run's identity)* and that run is alive**, and by U3 step 1's rule alone the probe would open C1's box about a run that ended unexpectedly after an ordinary, successful update in which nothing crashed. That box is the one interruption C2 admits, and this is the one place it can appear systematically rather than by accident, so it is closed here rather than priced. **The suppression is exactly as wide as the probe:** it holds for a launch the update mechanism started in probe mode and nowhere else, the probe still overwrites the field as *(running, probe's identity)*, and a crash of the probe's own run is offered at the next ordinary launch like any other. ~~**On the rollback branch nothing changes at all** — L13's restore puts back *(running, old run's identity)*, the old process is still the running application and ends through step 2 as usual — which is what makes U5's *no state in that cycle reports a crash that did not happen* true of both branches rather than of one.~~ *(Struck 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair. The rollback branch changes as much as the probe branch does: Z21 ends every rollback by starting a build, none of those launches is in probe mode, and on Windows the old process is already gone. **U5b decides that branch** and is what now makes U5's sentence true of both.)* **The launch path's own half is `Desktop app and delivery`'s and is routed** ([12](#12-unresolved) item 10): probe mode is that document's, and only it can say that a probe-mode launch carries the suppression.

**U5b. The rollback's relaunch of the previous build makes no offer either, and it is the second launch [9](#9-after-a-crash) C1's second condition names. Added 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair.** `Desktop app and delivery` **Z21** ends every rollback by starting a build: **macOS** restores the complete named bundle backup and L8's record copy, *"then starts that previous bundle"*; **Linux** reinstalls the cached verified previous package and restores L8's copy *"before starting it"*; **Windows** restores L8's copy, rewrites Z15a's shortcut to the previous known-good build and *"relaunches that build"*. **None of the three is a probe-mode launch**, and Z21b's suppression *"reaches probe-mode launches and no others"*, so none of them is covered by U5a. What each of them reads is L13's restored copy — *(running, old run's identity)*, because nothing writes *clean* in a handover (U5a) — and on C1's trigger as it stood before this repair, every failed update ended in the crash box.

**On Windows the old run really is over and its mark really is stale**: **Z18** and **Z19** put the relaunch in a helper that *"outlives the application for the length of one relaunch"*, so the old process is gone before the restore and never reaches step 2. **That ending is an update's, not a crash**, and C2 admits one interruption which this is not. So the rollback's relaunched build carries the same suppression the probe carries: it performs U3 step 1's read and write, as the ordinary running application, and makes no offer. **The launch-side half is `Desktop app and delivery`'s and is routed** ([12](#12-unresolved) item 11) — the rollback is that document's, and only it can mark the build it relaunches. Z21b's *"The rollback branch is unchanged and takes no clause of its own"* is the sentence that routing corrects.

**U6. What the mark's reading can be wrong about is bounded, stated, and accepted by the spec itself.** A durable write of step 2 that failed, or a crash after the ask but before the exit, ~~or a restored L8 copy~~ each cost **one prompt that should not have appeared or one that should have and did not** — the spec's own bound, and no durable state depends on it. *(Corrected 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair. It is neither now: U5b suppresses the offer on the rollback's relaunch, so a restored copy costs no prompt. `State, identity and recovery` **D6a** prices the same case in the same words and is owed the same correction; it is routed at [12](#12-unresolved) item 11 and is not written here.)* And the mark is Smoozy's own record, never a system reading: **Fact 130's answer is that this is the only mechanism that covers every ending** — every system-supplied route *"either misses the endings that matter most, or is off by default, or needs privileges a plain downloaded binary does not have"* (documented and observed, the input document §2; macOS observed, Windows and Linux documentation only). MetricKit, the recovery callback and the kernel log are read about there and deliberately not built on.

**U7. Every diary line carries the run identity of the run that wrote it, and each run opens with a session line.** The session line carries the run identity, the moment on the desktop clock, **Smoozy's own version and which operating system it runs on — exactly `Desktop app and delivery` E11's two values and nothing more**, which is what keeps the spec's *nothing new about the user is learned by writing them down* resting on an answered clause. Slicing by session is then a property of the record itself: a run's lines are the lines carrying its identity, and *the one before* is the run whose lines immediately precede the current run's session line in the record's own order. A previous run evicted by R5's cap is a previous run the report says it cannot include ([Section 5](#5-a-report-is-a-slice) S5).

## 3. What goes into the diary

**Constraint:** Rows 452 to 456. Every line carries the run identity (U7), a kind from the closed set below, the moment it records **and the clock that moment is on** (`State, identity and recovery` D5 applied whole), and the identities of what it concerns — job identity, group identity, local server identifier, operation identity — where there are any, with the user's own labels carried beside identities and never in place of them (§2.1 rule 3's discipline).

**J1. The kinds are closed: session, act, told, failure, assumption, gap, and last.** A line of no kind is not written, and a builder that needs an eighth kind has found a hole in this section rather than a case it does not cover.

**J2. **J2a. Whether the spec's five acts are the closed set, ruled — they are examples, and the test that admits a sixth is bounded here so that two writers applying it produce the same list. - ***Outcome*, in J2's test, means the spec's own word for one of Smoozy's completed acts** and nothing else — never `Desktop app and delivery` M10's four collection outcomes, never `State, identity and recovery` §8's ending record or the subject outcome it marks, never [8](#8-the-two-entry-points) X4's subject outcome mark.

**J3. **J4. ## 4. What it never holds, and what it holds that is the user's

**Constraint:** Rows 457 and 458, answered together because answered apart they contradict each other.

**N1. The boundary is enforced at the write, and the rule is one sentence: nothing enters the diary that may not leave the user's computer in a report.** This is `Security and licence` §13's own framing — *"Redaction here is never emitted, not filtered before display. A filter has to recognise what it is hiding"* — applied as the diary's constitution. The report cut ([Section 5](#5-a-report-is-a-slice)) therefore performs **no filtering of any kind**: it slices, trims and states, and every byte it may include was admissible the moment it was written. A rule applied at the cut instead would be a filter, and the three stored user-secret kinds have no recognisable shape for one to find.

**N2. What never enters, each exclusion on its ground.**

- **A stored secret value or any part of one** — the passphrase, any key, the bot token. Structural twice over: the adapter contract means no module ever holds a plaintext to write — `Desktop app and delivery` §9 **S6**, *"No module receives a plaintext, and no interface of this document returns one"*, built on `Security and licence` §1.4 **B30**, *"No feature module receives a stored secret as a value it may keep; a module receives either a use of the secret carried out on its behalf, or a handle it can present again"* — and `Security and licence` §3.0 rule 6 bars a stored value from every *exported record or log*, which between them name every surface the diary could be. Cited, not re-decided. *(Corrected 2026-09-02. This cited the contract as `Security and licence` **§9**, which is that document's* The local record *section and states neither rule — right section number, wrong document. Both homes are quoted above so the citation cannot slip again.)*
- **A host key or its fingerprint** — §13 rule 3, *"No host key, and no host key fingerprint, appears in any log, message or exported record."* Cited.

- **The machine's environment** — §13 rule 2 already binds *"any record Smoozy keeps or shows"*, and the diary is a record Smoozy keeps. Cited; the observed ground (a rental's settings holding a third party's secrets in plain text) travels with it.
- **The contents of the user's files** — a new prohibition on a new store, written here: no bytes and no text read out of any file of the user's — a `.blend`'s contents, a render output, anything of theirs — enter the diary. The diary holds **names of** things, never their insides.

**N3. What it holds deliberately, because that is what makes a report worth reading: file paths, folder names, scene names, machine names, usernames.** The spec's own list, Creator-stated, and the boundary that keeps it from colliding with N2 is the **acted-on rule**: a diary line names the things the recorded act was about — the path the user picked, the file the module read, the scene submitted, the server's label and login the connection used — and **never inventories things no act touched**. A line says *read a listing at this folder*; it never carries the listing. The user is the one who sends the report, so nothing on this list goes anywhere by any act but their own — which is the spec's stated consent ground and [Section 10](#10-where-this-stops)'s subject.

**N4. Two standing rules that look like they reach here do not, and the misreading is named so nobody applies the wrong one.** `Security and licence` §3.0 rule 1 — *"Never a host, a port, a username, a file path, a key comment or a fingerprint"* — is the secret store's own attribute rule and reaches no further than that store's attribute set; it is not a rule about the diary, and applying it here would delete exactly what N3 exists to keep. And §13 rule 6's *"no path beyond what the user already sees"* binds the failure **message** — which the diary holds verbatim via J6, already compliant — not the act lines, whose paths are the user's own acts' subjects and are held under N3.

**N5. The diary is neither §13's *log* nor an *exported record*: it is its own named surface, held to the exported standard at the write.** That is the ruling underneath both rows. Calling it a log would inherit four rules and lose Row 458's deliberate promise; calling it nothing would inherit none and let convenience write what it liked. Naming it, and writing it to the standard of the strictest surface a line can ever reach, keeps both rows at once — and it is the only arrangement §13's never-emitted framing permits.

## 5. A report is a slice

**Constraint:** Rows 459, 460, 461, and the spec's label on the slice rule: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-24).

**S1. A report is a plain-text file composed from the diary and from [Section 6](#6-what-a-reachable-machine-adds)'s report-time material, and nothing else.** Its head carries: the user's own sentence, verbatim; where the report was opened from a failure, that failure's naming line ([8](#8-the-two-entry-points) X5); Smoozy's version and the operating system — E11's two values again, nothing more; the moment of composition on the desktop clock, said to be that clock; and the completeness statements of S5. Every moment inside the body keeps the clock its diary line carries.

**S2. The three slices, cut on run identities and job identities, which is what U1 and U7 exist for.**

- **A failed job**: every line carrying that job identity, plus the whole of every run in which such a line occurs — *that job's own acts and the session around them*.
- **A crash**: every line carrying the run identity the run-mark named ([9](#9-after-a-crash) C3) — *the session that died*, findable after the run is gone because the identity is durable in the mark and on the lines.
- **Nothing in particular**: the current run's lines and the previous run's, the previous being read off the record's own order (U7).

**S3. The report ceiling is 5 MB, fixed rather than read, and it is this document's own constant.** The spec gives the diary 20 MB and the report no figure; *too large* is a receiving provider's limit and not Smoozy's to know, so no reading and no outside claim decides this. The figure is set inside the only number the spec gives — a quarter of the diary's cap, so a report is always strictly smaller than the record it is cut from — and it is the runner's to overrule exactly as the diary's own figure is. No free-space reading and no mail-provider belief raises or lowers it.

**S4. Where a slice exceeds the ceiling, one contiguous span is dropped from the middle, whole lines only, and the drop is stated in the file at the point of the cut.** The mechanism, exactly:

- The span grows outward from the slice's midpoint until the remainder fits — the head and tail of the slice always survive, which is where a story's beginning and its ending live.
- **Lines that state something are never dropped**: session lines, failure lines, assumption lines, gap lines and last lines are excluded from the span, and the span widens across ordinary act and told lines around them. Only where the statements alone still exceed the ceiling does the span take statements too — and then the cut statement says so.
- **At the point of the cut, a statement is written into the file**: how many lines were dropped and the span they covered, by first and last retained moments. The statement travels inside the file — the spec's one absolute, *never a quiet trim* — and S1's head repeats it.
- ***Where the least is happening* is answered by position and kind, never by reading content for meaning.** The middle of a slice is where the least is happening by the record's own shape — the diary is dense where trouble is because failures, assumptions and gaps are statement lines and statements are never dropped. Nothing models, scores or mines a line to decide its worth, which keeps the same discipline `Security and licence` §13 rule 5 imposes on a tool's output.
- **The precedent is carried at its true width.** The behaviour the spec cites — `Rented-server runtime` R2/M4a — keeps a prefix and abandons the rest, on the machine, as a stream arrives; this cut takes the middle of a complete record at composition. **What carries across is the honest statement, not the mechanism**, which is Row 460's own finding written where a builder will read it.

**S4a. What S4 cuts, said plainly; and where the diary lines are exhausted and the composed file still exceeds S3, the report-time parts are dropped whole, in a stated order, each leaving its own statement. Added 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair**, where S4 was found to cut a slice, G4 to send the same cut through the composed file, and nothing at all to say what happens when the report-time material alone will not fit.

- **The cut object.** **S4's middle-drop operates on the diary lines of the slice and on nothing else.** The report-time material of [6](#6-what-a-reachable-machine-adds) is taken whole by rule — G3's note as the record it is, G5's crash file whole or not at all — so a span falling inside one of them would breach G4's own prohibition on trimming a taken record of its statements, by the clause that states it. Where every ordinary act and told line has gone and the statement lines alone still leave the file over S3, S4's own last resort takes statements too and says so.
- **Then, and only then, the report-time parts are dropped whole, in this order: the journal tail, then the crash file, then the per-job note.** The tail goes first because it is a window of machine activity around the job and is the one part with a fixed bound (G2 — 512 entries, at most 256 KiB); the crash file next, because it is unbounded and is one ending's evidence; the note last, because it is the smallest and carries the ending and the reason a render failed.
- **Nothing is trimmed to fit and nothing goes silently.** A part is included entire or dropped entire, and each drop writes its S5 statement — *dropped for the ceiling* — naming which part. S7's umbrella covers this cut like every other.

**S5. The report says what it could not include, per part, and the states are stated rather than derived.** For each part a report of its kind is made of — the machine's words among the diary's told lines, the journal tail, the per-job note, the previous session, the crash file — the file states which is true:

- **included**;
- **absent** — the part was collected, or reachable, and there was nothing: an empty stream's outcome, a journal tail with no entries, a previous session with no lines. Where a collection outcome exists it is **carried as recorded from `Desktop app and delivery` M10's closed set of four — *collected*; *collected, and the stream was empty*; *collection incomplete*; *never established* — and never re-derived**, which is M11's rule honoured; *collection incomplete* is carried as its own statement and never collapses into *printed nothing*;
- **unreachable** — a report-time state, this document's own and the fourth Row 461 shows the closed four cannot express: at the moment the report was cut, the server could not be reached, so the report-time parts were not taken. It is a statement about the cut, laid **beside** the recorded outcomes and never overwriting one;
- **never collected** — the recorded *never established*, carried as such;
- **incomplete at the cut** — a report-time part whose read **began and did not complete**: the connection was live when the report was cut and dropped during the take, or the request expired under `Server protocol and transfers` C19. What was retained is named and the part is stated as incomplete. It is never written as *included* over a tail that is short, and never as *absent* over a note that exists — which is the quiet trim S7 forbids and the round trip through the user S6 exists to prevent. It is the shape `Desktop app and delivery` M10's *collection incomplete* already has on the machine side. **Added 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair**, where this case fitted none of the states and left a builder to invent one;
- **dropped for the ceiling** — the part was taken whole and did not fit, so S4a dropped it entire so the file could meet S3. The statement names the part and that it was dropped entire rather than trimmed. **Added 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair**;
- ~~**excluded by rule** — the crash file's standing state ([Section 6](#6-what-a-reachable-machine-adds) G5).~~ *(Struck 2026-08-31 at the round-1 audit repair. **G5 reversed on the day this list was written** — Fact 131 came back **no** and the crash file **is** taken — and this bullet was not brought along, so §5 told a builder to exclude by rule the file §6 tells them to take, and the crash file was the only part this state named. **Nothing is excluded by rule.** A crash file that does not exist is **absent** under G5's third bound; G5's *whole or not at all* is the take's rule and not an exclusion.)*

Two more statements of the same kind: where R5's cap has evicted lines a slice would have covered, the report states the moment its record begins and that older lines were dropped by the cap; and where a gap line (J5) falls inside the slice, it is a statement line and travels whole.

**S6. ## 6. What a reachable machine adds
