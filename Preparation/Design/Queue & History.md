# Queue & History

*What the queue and history look like, what a job's entry carries while it renders and after it ends, and how work Smoozy was not watching is offered back. The conventions they follow are the [whole-app Design](Whole%20app.md)'s and are not restated here.*

## Two screens, not one

**The queue and history are separate places in the rail.** They empty differently, and that difference would otherwise have to be explained on every visit: the queue is blank on a fresh launch until a server is connected, because Smoozy keeps no lasting copy of it, while history is Smoozy's own record and is there straight away. That is the [Queue & History spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s rule.

**Both are a list beside an entry.** The list on the left, the job's own detail on the right.

**Connections.** They serve the [Queue & History spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md). Jobs arrive from [Render Settings](Render%20Settings.md). *Show outputs* opens [File Browser](File%20Browser.md) on that server. Downloads appear in [Transfers](Transfers.md). What an entry says about a job's outputs is [What a job writes](../Specs/What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s.

**A group of scene-jobs reads as a group**, under its own heading, while each row still acts on its own.

**It offers two acts, not the three the spec lists.** *Cancel all 4* on the heading, and the heading itself as the grip that moves the whole group. **Stopping is not a group act**, because a machine runs one job at a time, so a group has at most one member rendering — stopping *the group* and stopping that one member are the same act, and it already sits on that member's own entry. A third control would be a second name for something the user can already do.

**Cancelling the group stops its rendering member, and the box says so.** That is the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s own order — the waiting members go first, then the running one is stopped — so the group's box is not the single job's: it names how many jobs go, says one of them is rendering and will be stopped, and carries that stop's own cost, which is the frame it is on. **Where no member of the group is rendering** — a group queued behind another job — the box says none of them has started and no frames are lost, in the single cancel box's own words, and the heading's count is unchanged.

**Reordering is dragging a row, and it only ever moves inside its own server's queue.** That is what the grouping already draws — order means nothing across servers — so a row dragged over another server's heading does not land there. The rendering row cannot be dragged and does not offer the grip: the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) says a job rendering is no longer waiting, and the [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) L9 says a reorder never touches it. A held job is not in the queue at all, so it is not there to drag.

**The screen says *queue*, never *line*.** The architecture calls it the line throughout and this document follows it when quoting; no word of it reaches a screen. Rule 9: the user already has a word for this and it is the one on the rail.

**The group's heading is the grip for the whole group.** Dragging the heading moves every waiting member as one block and keeps the order they already have among themselves; dragging one member moves that member alone. That is the spec's own rule — a scene-job is an ordinary job — and the user is not warned about pulling one out of its group, because gathering it back is what moving the group does.

**Beside dragging, a row's own menu offers *Move to the top of the queue*, and nothing else about position.** Dragging from the bottom of a long queue to the top is fiddly, and the top is the one position a user actually names. Every other position is quicker to point at than to describe.

**One line stands under the queue list and it is the only place dragging is taught.** It says a row drags up its server's queue, that a group's heading drags the whole group, and that a row's own menu holds *Move to the top of the queue*. Dragging has no other thing on screen to give it away, so this line changes what the user would do, which is the test the [whole-app Design](Whole%20app.md) sets for any line that explains rather than informs. History has no such line, because it has nothing to teach.

**A drag that lands on nothing changes nothing, and a row that could not be moved says why.** The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) L6 has the machine apply the move to what is true rather than to what Smoozy last saw, so a job that started rendering while the move was travelling is not moved. The row returns to where it was and says it is rendering now. That is the ordinary case rather than a failure, so it is a statement on the row and not the whole-app failure shape.

**A server Smoozy is not connected to is greyed and says *not updating*, with when it was last seen.** Its jobs are shown from memory. The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) is firm that an entry which was rendering when Smoozy last looked must never read as still running, because the readings that make a render look like it is still running are exactly what it carries.

**That holds only while the connection the user opened stands.** The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) C6 keeps what Smoozy last saw through a drop and while it reconnects, and drops it when the user disconnects or closes Smoozy. So a server disconnected by hand shows no jobs in the queue until it is connected again, the same as after reopening Smoozy. Its History is unchanged. *Added 2026-10-03: the paragraph above read as covering every server not connected. Design conforms to the architecture under Rule 10 (C6, with `Servers` S11a and W19's last row); the scout of the 38 promises noted it. No auditor has seen it.*

## A rendering entry

**Where no scene was named, the third field says so rather than going blank.** It reads *No scene named*, in the grey Smoozy already uses for a value it cannot confirm. A field that simply disappeared would be indistinguishable from a scene the user did not notice, and a blank between two separators reads as a fault in the drawing. The header says the field is empty; the mark above says what will actually render. Those are two different facts and neither says the other.

**It is headed by the job's name, then server, file, scene in that order.** The server first because it is the thing the user is choosing between; the file next because it is what they recognise; the scene last because it only means anything once the file is known. Where the job carries no label of its own, the name is the file. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-10). **In Queue the entry is headed by the name alone**: the server's heading, the row and the name already say the rest. History keeps the line under the name, since History is not grouped by server and that line is where a gone server is named. *Changed 2026-10-12, Creator-stated, the Creator's ninth notes (2026-10-11), note 1: "the second line is unnecessary." The sentence before stands for History; in Queue it read as the order of a line now dropped.* The paragraph above, on *No scene named*, now stands for History; in Queue a job with no scene named says so in its mark, *Submitted with no scene named.*

**It opens with a plain status line** — what it is doing, and how long it has been doing it — rather than starting in the middle of a chart. **A job that is rendering pulses**, on the status dot and on the word in its row, because a render is the one thing on screen that is happening rather than sitting there.

**Before rendering, while simulations play, the progress line reads *Simulating: frame 120 of 430*.** Both numbers are the scene’s own, including zero and negative frames. This line replaces the render figures while it stands, with no bar or estimate; the usual progress returns when rendering begins. Added 2026-11-04, the build runner’s ruling for the film’s fault 11. This sentence is the exception to the count style below.

**Counts are written as counts.** *148/240*, *812/2048 samples* — not *frame 148 of 240*. The label beside them already says which is which, and a fraction is read at a glance where a sentence is read word by word. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-10).

**Then two bars, stacked tight, each with its whole reading on one line above it.**

- **Rendering now** — the frame, the View Layer where a frame has more than one, the samples, and Blender's own estimate at the end of the same line, marked as Blender's. Under EEVEE there is no estimate and that part is simply absent.
- **Whole job** — the percentage, and nothing beside it.

**They sit together rather than as two blocks.** Two readings of one render, four pixels apart, so the eye takes them as one thing. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-11).

**The top bar is one View Layer of one frame, and the label is what makes that visible.** The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) counts both bars in samples: the top one over the piece being rendered now, which is one View Layer on one frame. On a frame with three active View Layers that bar therefore fills and empties three times while the frame number stays put — and a bar that resets for no visible reason is the worst thing on the entry. So the line above it names what it is measuring: *Frame 61 · Reflections*, or *Frame 61 · View Layer 2 of 3* where Blender does not give its name. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

**The top bar stays on the entry while a frame is in hand: between a frame starting and Blender's first sample on it, it stands empty, and it is never taken away and put back.** Found on the Creator's twelfth notes, note 7. Added 2026-10-18; no auditor has seen it.

**Where Smoozy cannot name the View Layer at all, it says the count and not the name.** Blender 4.5 puts the View Layer on its progress line and the newer three do not — the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) owns that fact — so on those the line says which of how many rather than which one. It never leaves the reset unexplained.

**Neither bar ever says *renderlet*.** It is this project's own word, invented so its documents agree, and no Blender user has heard it. On screen it is a frame and a View Layer — Blender's own words, and *layer* alone means something else there entirely.

**A bar that finishes short is right, not broken.** A renderlet with adaptive sampling on stops when the picture is clean, so it can end at 200 of 500 samples. The bar goes to its end when the work ends rather than sitting at 40% looking stalled, and nothing on screen treats the shortfall as a fault.

**The whole-job bar shows a percentage and says nothing beside it.** It used to carry a line explaining what it was counting — *two layers per frame, so the bar counts both* — and that line was withdrawn on 2026-08-13 because it can simply be false: how many View Layers are active is decided per frame, so one frame can have none and the next ten, and there is no per-frame figure to state. A percentage of the whole job needs no gloss. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

**The tile count sits on the *Rendering now* line, beside the samples, and only where Blender gives one.** A frame small enough to be one tile has no tile figure and the line simply does not carry it; a frame split into tiles reads *780/53284 tiles* alongside the sample count. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-11).

It does report them — the Creator knew it from their own use, and the evidence was already in the research, in a hand test belonging to a different topic. The spec and its sources file are fixed.

## The job's name, and its folder

**The entry's name is the whole label the user typed.** Never a shortened form, never the one inside the folder name. The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) Y8 is explicit: the label the user typed everywhere a label is shown, the folder's real name everywhere a path is shown, and no warning about the difference between them.

**The label is given on the settings page, in a field above the Render control**, which is the last thing read before submitting and the moment the user knows what they are about to make. It is optional; left empty the entry is named for the file, which is already settled above. Where the field sits on that page is [Render Settings](Render%20Settings.md)'s to draw.

**The identity is never shown as a thing of its own.** It is not labelled, not offered to copy, not put in a column, and never used on screen to tell two jobs apart — the label and the server and the times do that, and every list already shows all three. The user meets the identity in exactly one place: **inside a folder name, where a path is shown.**

**And there it is shown in full, unshortened.** The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) Y8 and [Job files and outputs](../Architecture/Job%20files%20and%20outputs.md) N3 make a job's output folder the identity plus a bounded rendering of the label, and the entry shows the recorded path verbatim rather than re-composing it from the label. **This corrects the drawing**, which showed that folder as `kitchen-final/` — a name no job will ever have. A shortened or prettied name is worse than a long one here, because the whole use of the field is matching what the entry says against what the file browser shows, and a name that does not match cannot be matched. Nothing anywhere reads the name back the other way.

**The folder sits where the file's own outputs point, so the path shown is beside the artist's own work** — `/home/ubuntu/Render/…` for a file writing to `//../Render` — and in Smoozy's own folder on the server only where the [What a job writes spec](../Specs/What%20a%20job%20writes.md) falls back. *(Corrected later on 2026-10-04: where it falls back, the path is in the server's fallback folder when one is set in the server's Edit dialog and can be used, and in Smoozy's own folder otherwise. The two sentences below stay as they are — "so the files go here" is true in either — and nothing new is said when a set folder could not be used; the path shows it. `Job files and outputs` P23.)* *(Reversed the same sitting, by the build runner's ruling under Rule 8 — the user chose that folder, so the job does not change place without a word: **where the job records that the server's fallback folder was passed over, a second line sits under the case sentence**, in the same grey and the same shape: *This server's fallback folder can't be used, so the files go to Smoozy's folder.* It stays into history like the first. `Job files and outputs` P23, `Queue & History` Y8 as widened.)* **Where it fell back, one line under the path says why**, in one of two sentences: *Your file's output folder is on another computer, so the files go here.* or *Your file's output folder can't be used, so the files go here.* It reads as a statement, like a mark, and stays into history. *Added 2026-10-04, on the Creator's ruling of that date (their note 18 on the test copy). Design conforming to Architecture under Rule 10: `Job files and outputs` 6.6 P19, `Queue & History` Y8. No auditor has seen it.*

**A waiting job whose file was not read has no folder yet**, because where it sits is worked out when the file is read at the render's start. Until then *Files on the server* and *Download all*, which goes where it goes, are not there, and one line sits where the first was: *The folder is made when the render starts.* *Added 2026-10-04, the same ruling; `Job files and outputs` 6.6 P21.*

**The path after *Writing to* is a link, drawn as one, that opens Files on that folder** — the same act as *Files on the server*. *Added 2026-10-06, Creator-stated, the third notes, note 11: "this has to be a link and look like a link."*

## The marks

**Most jobs carry none of this, and the entry shows none of it.** A job with a disturbed file, no dependency check, Simplify on and an earlier job behind it is a job in trouble four ways over, and drawing it as the ordinary case makes every entry look alarming. The plain entry is status, progress, readings, setup, output — and nothing else. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

**A mark is one line saying something that holds true of this job**, sitting between the live readings and the setup. Each is shown only when it is true, and every one of them stays on the entry into history.

**On screen the group is headed *About this job*.** *Mark* is this project's own word, invented so these documents agree with each other, and the [whole-app Design](Whole%20app.md) is firm that nothing invented for this project's own use reaches a screen. A heading reading *Marks* would have to be opened to find out what it meant. The documents keep the word; the user never sees it.

- **A file the job needs was disturbed**, naming the file. The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) is exact that the mark changes nothing about the job — it may still render perfectly — and exists so a failure hours later is not unexplained. **The verb is the act's: replaced, deleted, moved or renamed** — *A file this job needs was deleted.* Added 2026-10-02, out of step 10c's map of Files' owed acts; no auditor of this document has seen it. **A delete leaves one mark per act, never one per file.** Where one file went, the mark names that file. Where more went, it names the folder they went from and how many — *Files this job needs were deleted. 12 files were deleted from /root/job/frames/ after this job was submitted.* A single file is never named as the job's whole folder. *Added 2026-10-03, the build runner's ruling (d), under the Creator's standing delegation of 2026-08-31: the Vast pass before 0.1.9 watched one deleted frame's mark name the job's whole folder, and nothing said what a delete of many frames should mark. No auditor of this document has seen it.*
- **Dependencies were not checked before submission.** Same shape: a note of what was true at that moment, checked against nothing and re-checked never.
- **Simplify was on.** The frames will not match the file the artist saved, and nothing else on the entry would say why. It says Simplify was on and no more — what each setting was set to is part of the setup below.
- **It resumes an earlier job**, named, with a way to that entry. Without it the user is looking at two entries of one file over one range with nothing to say which came first.

**A fifth: it was interrupted and re-run, and from which frame.** The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) N1 already calls these *re-run marks* and holds what they need — the attempt count, and the frame the re-run started from — so what is left here is that they are marks and how they read. They are marks: one line, true of this job, shown only when true, kept into history. **History is where this one earns its place.** An entry reading *finished, 240/240* says nothing about the machine having gone down at frame 61 and the render having started again, and that is exactly the entry a user comes back to weeks later wondering why a render took two days. Where it happened twice the job is *abandoned* — the ending carries that — and the mark says it was interrupted twice.

**A sixth: it was submitted with no scene named.** Nothing forces a user to read the file first, and a job submitted before the read carries no scene name at all — the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) requires the entry to say so, and to say that the job renders the file's active scene. That is a statement true of the job, not a fault and not a warning, so it is a mark like the others.

**Its wording changes once the scene is known, and the mark does not go.** The file is read when the job starts, so the scene name arrives a moment later and the header can then show it. The mark stays and says which scene it turned out to be — *Submitted with no scene named. Blender rendered **Interior**, the file's active scene.* Dropping it once the name arrived would leave a history entry that cannot answer the one question worth asking about such a job: whether the user chose that scene or Blender did.

**A seventh: the server's NVIDIA driver was too old for OptiX in this job's Blender, and could not be updated.** It is on a job that was held for the driver update and let go after the update missed — the [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) N23 — and it names the driver's version and the Blender's: *The NVIDIA driver, 525.147.05, was too old for OptiX in Blender 5.1, and could not be updated.* From 5.3: *The NVIDIA driver, 575.64.03, was too old for Cycles GPU rendering in Blender 5.3, and could not be updated.* *(Added 2026-11-02, the build runner's ruling. No auditor has seen it.)* Such a job fails, and without the mark its entry would say only that the render failed. It is drawn on the failed History entry. *Added 2026-10-11, the Creator's ruling that a job needing the newer driver is held back. Design conforming to Architecture under Rule 10. No auditor has seen it.*

**Seven marks is not a crowded entry** *(six until 2026-10-11)*, because the ordinary job shows none of them. The list can grow while the plain entry stays plain, and that is what the default-plain rule above buys. **No other sentence here counts them.** The list has grown twice, and both times a sentence written for four was left saying four beside six drawn marks; wording that carries no number cannot go stale that way. This one keeps its figure because the size of the list is its whole point.

**They read as statements, never as warnings.** Not one of them is a fault, and most are things the user chose. A red mark on a job that may be perfectly fine would train the user to ignore every one of them.

## What the job writes

**Six statements, and they arrive at the start of the render rather than at submission.** Where the user pre-loaded, they read all six on the settings page and this block only repeats what is already settled. **A job that renders nothing at all is not a seventh statement.** It is the same one as a job that writes nothing, with a different reason named in it. ## The entry's controls

**Acts sit in the header; a standing arrangement sits with the reading it governs.** *Stop*, *Delete* and *Download all* are things the user does once, and they are all in the header where the entry's actions already live. *Submit again* and the carry-on are not among them: they are a group of their own in the entry's body, because each names a frame count and the carry-on carries a field and a sentence under the pair, none of which a header row can hold. Auto-download is not an act — it is a setting that keeps working after the user has looked away, and the [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) L1a is explicit that switching it on writes nothing to the machine and changes nothing about the job. So it stays in the side column beside the count it produces. That line is what keeps the header from filling with things that are not acts.

**The header is two rows: the job's name and its line, then the acts on their own row from the left edge, with *Stop* or *Cancel* last in that row.** One row held both only where the entry was wide enough, and narrower it wrapped with the second line indented and *Stop* falling to a third. *Added 2026-10-03, the Creator's note 11 on the test copy ("This is some messed up alignment"); nothing had said how the header wraps. Settled at Design by the build runner; no auditor has seen it.*

**Download all sits in the header, first among the actions.** It brings down the whole of the job's outputs at once, which is the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s own wording, and it is offered on a queue entry and a history entry alike and whatever the ending was — a job that failed, was stopped or was abandoned still wrote whatever it wrote. **It goes where *show outputs* goes**: both reach back to the machine, so an entry whose server is gone offers neither, which is the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s own pairing. It is offered whether or not auto-download is on: what happens to files already on the user's computer is [Download](../Specs/Download.md)'s, and it is the same already-there question the file browser asks, so nothing new is drawn for it.

**The auto-download switch is a switch, and the narrowing is a control beside it.** It read as a reading — a tick and a phrase, with the file kinds written underneath as a note — which is exactly what a setting the user can change at any moment must not look like. It is now a switch that moves, with the narrowing under it as the control that says which of the job's outputs the arrangement takes, and the count below both as the reading they produce. The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) L1a puts both on the entry and neither anywhere else, so this is the only place either is changed from.

**What the job reports to Telegram can be changed from its entry while it is in Queue.** Under the download switch and its narrowing, in the same column, a group headed *Telegram* holds the same control the Render Settings foot has — the five choices, and *A picture every* with its minutes where that is picked — reading the job's choice as it stands. The switch reads *Download frames as they render*, with no heading over it, and the narrowing stays under it whether it is on or off; where the window is narrow enough to set the two side by side, the switch sits level with the *Telegram* heading and the two lists, one width, on the line below, two lines each *(the Creator's twenty-fifth notes, note 1, 2026-10-31)*. Picking another saves it at once, with the control's underway shape while the server answers; there is no confirmation, because picking again undoes it. A first-frame check and its full range have one control between them. **It is offered only where it can act**: on a job waiting, waiting on the first frame or rendering, on a connected server whose Smoozy is up to date, and only where Telegram is on in Settings. On a server that is not connected the control reads the choice and is unavailable. On a server whose Smoozy is older, it reads the choice, is unavailable, and one grey line under it says *Update Smoozy on this server to change this.* **On a connected server read as not having Telegram reporting, it reads the choice, is unavailable, and one grey line under it says *Telegram reporting not installed*, so the job does not read as if it will report** ([Server Setup](Server%20Setup.md) has the action, and Render Settings' foot offers it). Where that cannot be told, nothing changes. *(Added 2026-11-03, the build runner's ruling. No auditor has seen it.)* A change the server refuses is said in the ordinary failure shape under the control, and the control goes back to what the server holds. **This placement is a recommendation awaiting the Creator's choice**: the choice sits beside auto-download because that is the other thing a user can change on a job after submitting it, and *Submitted with* stays a record of what was submitted. *(Added 2026-10-29, Creator-stated, the twenty-first notes, note 7: "The user should be able to change Telegram reporting settings on the fly from the job already in the queue." Design conforming to `Telegram` V1c under Rule 10. Drawn as `q-tg` and `q-tglist`. No auditor has seen it.)*

**Cancel sits where Stop sits.** A waiting job's entry header carries *Cancel* in the slot a rendering job's carries *Stop* — one act on the job, in the one place the entry's acts live, and no job is ever both.

**Stopping asks first, and so does cancelling. Neither word says what it costs.**

- **Stop** may throw away the frame being rendered now, and on a long frame that is however much of it had been rendered, and it was paid for. So the box says what may go and what stays, **in [Servers](Servers.md)' own words for a server removal rather than in fresh ones**: *the frame it is on may not be saved*, and *frames already rendered stay on the server*. **It never says the frame is lost.** The [architecture](../Architecture/Servers.md) W19a has the stop land in one of exactly two places and nobody able to know which before the act — mid-render the frame never arrives, and during that frame's own write the write finishes and the frame is whole — so *"the statement this document can stand behind is that the frame being rendered may not arrive, and never that it is lost"*. Under [Rule 10](../Groundwork/Rules.md) this document takes the conditional form, and the second sentence it carried — *a frame that is not finished is not saved* — is the same flat claim in other words and is gone. It is the same fact in both places and one fact gets one wording. The box adds no figure for how long that frame has been rendering: nothing Smoozy reads holds the moment the frame began — the [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) R6's closed list of readings carries no elapsed time — so the two sentences carry the whole of it.
- **Cancel** costs more than the word suggests. A cancelled job never reaches history — the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) says nothing anywhere in Smoozy afterwards shows it was submitted — so its settings go with it, exactly as deleting a history entry loses *Submit again*. It has no frames to lose and nothing left to show for it, which is why the box says the job will not appear in History and that its settings are deleted too. It says **settings** rather than *setup*, which is the word the settings page itself uses.

**Both are statements rather than a question with Yes and No**, which is the shape already settled for a server that has work on it.

**A stop Smoozy stopped waiting for says only what it knows.** The entry reads *Stop sent. Timed out waiting for the render to end.* It claims neither a stop that failed nor a render slow to honour it, because Smoozy cannot tell the two apart, and Rule 8's promise that Smoozy tells the truth about what happened is the whole of what it can offer here. The entry corrects itself when the outcome arrives, and a new stop can be sent.

**And it says so: *Press Stop again to end it.*** The sentence before it says what happened and not what to do, and on step 10c's thirteenth pass the tester read it as a stop in hand and waited while the render went on and was paid for. The second sentence says nothing of the first stop, so it claims neither a stop that failed nor a slow one. It has no *now*: the second stop meets the same wait as the first, and the one record of a second interrupt ending Blender at once is a key pressed in a terminal, never one Smoozy sent. It is said only where *Stop* is offered beside it. *Added 2026-10-01, from step 10c's thirteenth pass. Design conforming to Architecture under Rule 10: `Rendering` K10 and K11.*

**The same holds while the server is not updating.** A stop sent before the line went quiet stays said on its entry beside *Not updating* — *Stopping* while Smoozy waits, and the first sentence above once it stops waiting, without *Press Stop again* — and *Stop* comes back only when the server is updating again, since until then a stop cannot reach it. *Added 2026-09-30, from step 10c's eleventh pass, where the entry read Not updating and nothing about the stop. Design conforming to Architecture under Rule 10: `Rendering` K10.*

**Nothing else asks.** Switching auto-download, changing the narrowing, reordering and *Download all* all either cost nothing or undo by being done again. *(So does changing what the job reports to Telegram, from 2026-10-29.)*

## The two ways to render it again

**An entry that ended short offers two actions, not one, and the difference between them is the whole design problem.** *Submit again* renders the range from the start; *Resume from frame 60* renders from the frame Smoozy cannot vouch for to the end of the range. Both are **Rendering**'s, and both make a new job — see the [Rendering spec](../Specs/Rendering.md).

**They sit side by side and each names its own range.** A pair of buttons reading *Submit again* and *Resume* would leave the user working out the difference; a pair naming *240 frames* and *181 frames* does not. That is the same reasoning the settings page already uses for naming the count on its Render control.

**The carry-on names the frame it starts at, and says where that figure came from.** The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) keeps the last frame that counts as already there, and *Resume* reads the server again before it uses it — so the line says which of the two it is working from: read from the server, or the figure the entry recorded when the job ended. A remembered figure is never presented as a checked one. **The range opens at that frame and not after it** — the [architecture](../Architecture/Rendering.md) A4 and A9, and `Queue & History` N6, corrected 2026-09-02 — because the last frame that counts as already there is the one Smoozy cannot confirm finished, so it is rendered again. In the worked example that frame is 60: the carry-on starts at frame 60 and renders 181 frames. It can be moved: the [Rendering spec](../Specs/Rendering.md) settles that the user changes the starting frame rather than Smoozy backing off a frame on their behalf, so it is a field and not a fixed number.

**Only the carry-on says it leaves the frame range in two folders, and it says it on its own line under the pair.** Both actions make a new job with a folder of its own — the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md)'s rule, on the server and on the user's computer alike — so both leave a second folder behind. The difference is what those folders hold. After *Submit again* the new folder holds the whole range and the old one is simply superseded. After *Resume* neither folder holds the whole range: the first has frames 1 to 60 and the second has 60 to 240, and the user needs both. That is the fact worth a sentence, and it belongs to one of the two controls rather than to the pair.

**Simplify is offered on the settings page the re-submission opens, not on the entry.** The [Rendering spec](../Specs/Rendering.md) owns what it is and what it exposes — Max Subdivision, Child Particles and Texture Limit, Blender's own names for all three, off unless the user turns it on. What is settled here is where it sits and how it reads.

**It is a group of its own in the Render column, below the settings the job actually ran with**, and it is there only where the entry that opened the page is a failed one. Above it are the values that produced the failure; it is the one thing on the page Smoozy is adding to them, so it comes after them rather than among them.

**One line under the switch keeps it an offer.** It says what Simplify does — it cuts how much of the scene Blender loads — and then says plainly that Smoozy does not know why this job failed, apart from a full disk, which it reads off the machine, and that it offers this on every failure. Without that second half a control appearing on a failed job and nowhere else reads as a diagnosis, which is exactly what the [Rendering spec](../Specs/Rendering.md) forbids and what Smoozy usually cannot make: two of the four supported Blenders are killed by a machine running out of memory and never say so — their error stream is empty and their ordinary output simply stops at a sampling line.

**The three settings appear only once the switch is on.** Off, it is one switch and one line; a group of three controls sitting greyed under a switch nobody turned on is three more things to read on a page the user came to in order to fix something.

**_Submit again_ opens Render Settings with the frames as the user typed them**, where the entry holds that text — *1,2-4* stays *1,2-4* in the field — and with the numbers form where it does not. *Resume* starts from its own frame and fills the field with the numbers form. *(Added 2026-10-24, the eighteenth notes, note 8; `Render Settings` F1 and `Queue & History` P5b. The drawing's state is `res-againlist`. No auditor has seen it.)*

**A clean finish shows only *Submit again*.** There is nothing left to resume, so the second control is absent rather than greyed. It says *All frames rendered.*, and only a *Finished* entry says it. **Where the count says only part of what the job owed was written, it says that instead: *3 of 4 files written.*** The ending stays *Finished* and nothing else about the entry moves. Where Smoozy has no count, the line stands. *Added 2026-11-02.* **A render that ended at a successful status having written none of what the job owed is *Failed*** (`Rendering` K14b), so it is the case two paragraphs down, no frame finished, with its Output log offered like any failure's. *Added 2026-11-02.*

**An entry with no ending Smoozy ever learned shows only *Submit again* too, and says why.** There is no frame figure to start from, and the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) is firm that the action is not offered rather than offered and unable to work.

**A job that ended short having finished no frame at all shows only *Submit again* as well, and says why.** There is no frame to start from, so carrying on would be the whole range — which is what *Submit again* already is, so a second control beside it would render the same job under a different name. The [Rendering spec](../Specs/Rendering.md) names this alongside the case above as the other place there is nothing to start from. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

**So *ended short* is not on its own enough to earn the pair.** Three endings sit short of a clean finish and only one of them carries a carry-on: an entry with a frame figure behind it. The other two say why the second control is missing rather than showing it greyed, for the reason the clean finish already gives.

## Deleting an entry

**Two sentences, and the control is one word.** What goes: the record, and **Submit again** with it, since the entry is the only thing in Smoozy holding those settings. **The box says *settings* and not *setup***, which is what it said until 2026-08-23: *setup* is this app's word for a server setup, on three other screens, and one word doing two jobs is the vagueness Rule 9 exists to catch. What does not, and the [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) names both: the frames on the server, and whatever of the job was already downloaded to the user's computer. **Both are said in one sentence**, as the clear boxes say theirs: *60 frames on the server and 48 downloaded frames are not changed.* *Words changed 2026-11-06 (was *Three sentences* and *The 60 frames on the server are untouched.* / *The 48 frames already downloaded to your computer are untouched too.*): the last words read before the release. No auditor has seen it.*

**The downloaded frames were the missing half and they are the half that matters most.** A user pressing Delete is checking whether the renders go, and the renders they actually have are the ones on their own disk. It says how many where the entry knows the number. Where nothing of the job was downloaded that half is absent, because there is nothing to name.

**Where the entry does not know a number, the sentence is said without one, and never left out.** Neither count is on the entry by default: the frames on the server take a walk of the machine, and what was downloaded is `Download`'s record rather than a count the entry holds. Leaving the sentence out would leave the box silent on the one thing the user is checking. So the sentence reads *Frames on the server and downloaded frames are not changed.*, and where the server is gone its second sentence reads *Downloaded frames are not changed.* *Words changed 2026-11-06 (was *The frames on the server are untouched.* and *Anything already downloaded to your computer is untouched.*): the last words read before the release. No auditor has seen it.* A number is never guessed, and a count that is not known is never drawn as *0 frames*.

**It was a sentence, a three-item list and a fourth line**, and the list was the part nobody read — a user who has pressed Delete is checking one thing, which is whether the renders go too. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

## Clearing history

~~**Two acts beside the single delete: *Clear this server's history* and *Clear all history*.** The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) names all three.~~ **Three acts beside the single delete: *Clear this server's history*, *Clear all history* and *Clear removed servers' history*.** The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) names all four. Clearing every server at once is the Creator's addition. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-24). *(Changed 2026-10-24 with the fourth act, below.)*

**Both sit on the history list's own heading, beside the count they change.** They act on the list and not on one job, so they do not belong in the entry's header, where every act is about the one job showing. The heading already reads *kept until you clear them*; the controls that clear them are the thing that line has always been pointing at.

## Work Smoozy is not tracking

**One offer per server, not one per job**, because all of it was left behind by the same event.

**It says what declining costs**: the work still holds the server, so anything submitted afterwards waits behind it.

## Jobs that leave without an entry

**Some jobs go having never rendered, and there is no entry left to say why.** A first-frame job's waiter dropped because the first frame never came; a waiting job dropped because the Blender it was submitted with was taken off the machine. The [spec](../Specs/Queue%20&%20History.md) is firm on both: they leave no trace, exactly as saying no does, so nothing anywhere afterwards shows the job was submitted.

**So it is said where the user is, not where the job was — and it is said in the app, never as a notification.** Clicking a notification goes to the thing it is about, and a dropped job has no entry to go to, so it would be the dead end the [whole-app Design](Whole%20app.md) says no notification is. It is not one of the four kinds the [Whole app spec](../Specs/Whole%20app.md) names either, and [Settings](Settings.md) gives it no switch.

**Both cases are told the same way: one line under the Queue heading** — the job's name, then what happened to it. That holds whether the user was away or sitting in front of the app. The name is the whole point, since it is the only thing left that identifies the job. The line stays on screen until it is dismissed, and *Dismiss* asks first — all of which the section below settles. *(Changed 2026-10-12, the Creator's ninth notes, note 3. **It says the first frame was never rendered**, not that it *did not come back* — Rule 9, and the plain word says it exactly.

**It is said once.** The [architecture](../Architecture/Queue%20&%20History.md) H11 says once, and Design does not manufacture a second telling out of a job the spec has just said leaves no trace. What Design owes instead is that the one telling is hard to lose by accident, which the section below is about.

**It is a count, not a banner.** The endings Smoozy has learned are counted at the end of the Queue heading's grey line, after *9 jobs · 3 servers*: *· 3 finished · 1 failed*. Only endings above nought are shown — *finished*, *failed*, *stopped*, *abandoned* — each word in its History colour. Its hover reads *Since you last opened History.* *Changed 2026-10-12, Creator-stated, the Creator's ninth notes (2026-10-11), note 3: "pretty much useless as it is now … replace it with something a lot less invasive."*

**Pressing it opens History, where every one of those jobs is, and opening History clears it.** The count needs no link per job: each job it counts is a History entry with its ending, its times and what Blender said.

**Re-runs are said by the entry's mark only** (*The marks*, above). A re-run is not an ending, and its job is still in the queue or in History with the mark on it.

**A job dropped before it rendered is still named, in one quiet line under the Queue heading**, because it has no History entry and this is the only place it is ever named: *Kitchen — final v4 was not started — the first frame was never rendered.*, or *…was not started — its Blender 4.5 was removed.* The job's name is in bold. **No server is named on the count or on the line.** The count opens History, which names each job's server, and a dropped job's name is what identifies it.

## Keyboard shortcuts
