# What the research cannot say

Before leaning on any Blender finding in this project, this is the ceiling: how far the evidence was ever taken, and where a claim of "never" rests on runs that could not have come out any other way.

> **This joins answers that live in several topics.** Every claim below names the comparison
> questions it rests on; open those for the exact words, the exact messages, and what each
> version does. Where this document and a comparison disagree, the comparison is right.
> Where a claim is about a machine rather than about a question, it rests on the equipment
> section of `Preparation/Blender Research/Method.md`, which is where every machine is described.

*All four versions* and *every version* mean Blender 4.5 to 5.2 here, as in the comparisons; 5.3 is named wherever it is meant. Every 5.3 result is the 5.3.0 Beta, built 2026-10-16, on the Creator's Mac alone, so anything below reached on another machine was not reached for 5.3.

**This is the document most likely to be wrong in one direction.** Every time a machine was rented, a bigger scene built or an operating system reached, a ceiling here rose and nothing flagged it — and an understatement reads as caution rather than as an error. So a *never* here is worth only as much as the date beside it.

## The short answer

- **Windows has been reached, and only under two questions — on two different machines.** One test of *Frames 15* in Blender 4.5, and six tests of *Dependencies 20* — five of those in all four versions, one in 4.5 and 5.2. **Four Windows machines were rented, three were worked on, and two produced results**: one carries the whole of *Frames 15*'s Windows half and is **the only machine anywhere in this project that has rendered on Windows**; the other carries the whole of *Dependencies 20*'s and rendered nothing at all. **Neither had a graphics card of any kind.** Every other question, in every topic, is untried there rather than unreachable: a Windows machine can be rented and this project has rented four. **Every Windows machine this project has ever read reported itself a Server edition**, whatever its image was named, so what has never been reached is a **desktop** edition of Windows (*Frames 15*, *Dependencies 20*, *Writing 14*, *Builds 10*, *The machine 2*).
- **Two separate computers never shared a folder.** Every two-render result is two Blenders on one machine, and 5.3 never ran two Blenders at once at all (*Frames 14*). The one network folder ever used was mounted by a rented machine from itself, and it changed the answer (*Frames 14*). So a rented machine has mounted a network share; whether a rented container can do the same is not established. What is missing is a second computer, and a folder on network storage two of this project's machines can reach at the same moment (*Writing 14*, *Dependencies 20*).
- **Only NVIDIA cards and Apple's own graphics ever rendered.** No AMD, no Intel, no cards joined by NVLink, no mixed makes, no unequal cards (*The machine 5*, *What a render demands 7*).
- **A screen and more than one card have been on the same computer**, and a window was opened there (*Frames 13*, *Frames 14*). What has never happened is putting a **card** question to such a machine: the card-memory readout and the card-choice control were never asked at a window with more than one card behind it (*Reports 6*, *The machine 5*).
- **Blender's own window has been opened, driven and photographed on rented Linux machines**, and a render was **stopped** there — by a click on the status bar's cancel button, in all four versions on two machines, and by an Escape key, in all four versions on two machines and again in 5.1 on a third (*Frames 13*, *Frames 15*, *File output nodes 16*, *Settings 19*, *What a render demands 7*). **In 5.3 the same was done on the Creator's Mac**, with every key and click sent from inside Blender as its own simulated event: a click on the cancel button stopped a render, and Blender's own Escape stopped nothing, in 5.3 or in 5.2.2 (*Frames 15*). A render has been **begun** by a click in one place only: in 5.3, by Blender's own simulated click on a compositor node's re-render button (*The compositor 9*). No person's hand has begun or stopped a 5.3 render (*Frames 3*).
- **The scenes got very large and the pictures did not.** One question rendered **125,829,122 triangles**, where one rendering method finished and another was killed for want of the *host's* memory in all four versions (*What a render demands 7*). A separate round grew a scene until it exceeded one card’s memory, and the card filled — that scene was built out of **128 pictures of 8192 by 8192**, not out of geometry (*Reports 6*). **The two never met**: the heaviest scene ever built out of geometry did not fill a card, and the one scene that filled a card was made of images, so nothing on record says whether it is the kind of scene or the ratio between card and machine that decides which fills first. The largest picture ever written to disk is 10000 by 10000, in 5.3 on the Mac as well (*Writing 11*, *Reports 7*).
- **No run was long.** The longest on record are video renders of thirty to sixty-seven minutes (*Frames 12*). A sample count that would have taken nine to fourteen hours was begun and not finished (*Settings 18*), and a deliberately enormous render was cut off at four minutes with hours estimated as still owed (*Settings 15*).
- **Only the Creator's Mac can be reached again, and that is not the same as a question being closed.** Every rented machine is destroyed, so **no rented-machine result can be repeated on the machine that produced it**. But a fresh machine can be rented, and this project has rented seventeen Linux machines and four Windows machines, none of them for 5.3. **A silence is untried unless it names hardware nobody here has ever had** (*Reports 6*, *The machine 2*, *Frames 5*, *Images 4*).
- **Blender 5.3 was taken much less far than the four before it.** One beta build on one Mac: no Linux, no Windows, no NVIDIA card, no second graphics device, no machine without a screen, never two Blenders at once, and never a computer with nobody at it, the Creator's session being logged in throughout (*Settings 6*, *The machine 5*, *Frames 14*, *The machine 7*). None of the heavy rounds was run in it — the huge scene, the filled card, the machine's memory run out, the 65536-square picture (*What a render demands 7*, *Reports 6*, *Reports 10*, *Reports 7*). And its release notes move Linux's default drawing system to Vulkan, so what 5.3 starts on there, and does without Vulkan, is untried (*The machine 1*).
- **The Creator's Mac is not one unchanging instrument.** Its operating system and its unversioned Blender both moved while this stage ran; every 5.3 result was taken under macOS 26.6.2, with Blender 5.2.2 beside it for same-day checks. An older figure from it is a reading of the machine as it then was, not a mistake, and a difference between a 5.3 result and an older Mac reading can belong to the build, the system or the day as well as to the version (*What a render demands 1*, *Settings 18*, *Settings 19*, *The compositor 9*).

## How far each thing was taken

**Picture size.** Furthest begun: **629,126 by 353,883** — a percentage request that stored the setting's top value — cut into 53,284 tiles; no run finished, in 5.3 on the Mac either (*Settings 15*). Furthest carried to tile assembly: 65536 by 65536, divided into 1024 tiles, then a failure at the point of putting them together and nothing written — on one machine only, and never tried in 5.3 (*Reports 7*). Also begun: **16384 by 16384**, and 12288 by 12288 before it, both of which reached the top of one Linux machine's memory and went on sampling — **more pixels did not exhaust it in that setup, because the tile size caps what is held**; what did was more passes, forty light groups on an 8192 by 8192 scene, and that run was killed. All of it a **processor** render, on one machine, in 4.5 to 5.2 alone, and **whether either of the two larger sizes wrote a picture is not settled** (*Reports 10*). Largest actually written to disk: 10000 by 10000, in 5.3 on the Mac as well (*Writing 11*, *Reports 7*), and 9000 by 400 (*The compositor 6*). Heaviest single frame by bytes: about 537 megabytes, a 4096 by 4096 multi-layer OpenEXR holding two view layers, and about 268 megabytes for the same size as a plain uncompressed OpenEXR (*Frames 13*); below those, a 3000 by 3000 uncompressed OpenEXR of 144 megabytes, written repeatedly on two machines (*Writing 14*). **Never reached in Cycles:** a picture wider than one tile but no larger in area, so what crosses the tiling boundary — the width or the area — is untested. Such a picture *has* been rendered — the 9000 by 400 above, in all four versions on both machines — but under **Workbench**, which writes no tile file and prints no tile line, so it cannot show where the boundary is (*Reports 7*, *The compositor 6*, *Writing 11*). Most other work is far below all of this — 640 by 360 (*What a render demands 7*), 640 by 480 (*What a render demands 2*), 320 by 180 (*File output nodes 16*), 64 by 64 (*Dependencies 14*), eight pixels square (*Reading a file 11*).

**How heavy the scene was.** Furthest by count: **125,829,122 triangles**, rendered in all four versions, where OptiX finished and wrote its frame every time and **every CUDA run was killed by the system part-way through building its structure**, with no frame written (*What a render demands 7*). Below it, 10,485,762 triangles and 322 triangles in the same round. **In that round the limit actually met was the host machine's memory and not the card's**: the card never filled, so nothing in that question touches what happens when a card's own memory fills (*What a render demands 7*). None of that round was run in 5.3, which had no NVIDIA card (*What a render demands 7*).

**But a card has been filled, in another round, and the fall-back the manuals describe did happen.** On the four-card machine — 16 GB cards against 197 GB of system memory, the only combination this project ever had that could produce it — a scene of 128 pictures of 8192 by 8192 was grown beyond one card’s memory. The card filled to its own size, the rest came out of the machine's own memory, the picture was written, the run exited 0, and **nothing whatever was printed about it**; the cost was about ten times longer for twice the data. In all four versions, one card each (*Reports 6*, *Reports 1*, *The machine 4*). **Four cards gave that scene no more room**: every one of them filled to the same figure a single card reached, and the run took about four times longer still — **in Blender 4.5 alone**, 5.0 to 5.3 never tried that way. **So the one-to-four speed-up runs both ways and nothing on record says which way a scene will go before it is run**: about 3.6 times faster on a scene that fits on one card, about 3.8 times slower on this one (*Reports 6*, *The machine 5*). And **how far the fall-back reaches has never been tested**: the successful spill stayed well inside that machine’s memory, and no run has exercised it behind a smaller machine. In 5.3 a filled card, and everything about several cards, is untried (*Reports 6*). So a reader must not take *What a render demands 7*'s limit as a statement about the whole research. Beside those two rounds, most work in the research is a startup scene, a cube, or something near it (*Builds 6*, *Frames 5*, *What actually ran 1*).

**Sample count.** Furthest run right through: **4,096** — Cycles' own default — with adaptive sampling off, in 5.3 on the Mac as well, and EEVEE at 256 (*Settings 16*). Furthest **begun**: **16,777,216** in Cycles, silently cut down from a request of 99,999,999 and settled in to render, with nine to fourteen hours estimated as remaining in 4.5 to 5.2, and begun again in 5.3 on the Mac; and **99,999,999** in EEVEE, which is inside EEVEE's own range and was taken whole (*Settings 18*). Neither of those two is recorded as finishing. **20,000 samples on a card** were begun and stopped on purpose, in 5.3 on the Mac's own graphics too (*Frames 15*). Elsewhere 800 (*Frames 4*), 600 (*Frames 15*), 64 (*What a render demands 2*), one (*Dependencies 14*). **There is no single ceiling for "the sample count"** — each count is corrected against its own limits, so the same number is cut for one engine and taken whole by another, and nothing is said either way (*Settings 18*). **On a tiled picture the count is not one number at all**: the sample number restarts inside each tile, against a maximum that stays the scene's own and is not divided among the tiles. The furthest a render **seen** to be tiled has been taken is **16 samples**, at 2049 by 2049; the huge tiled runs were single-sample (*Reports 7*). One 64-sample Cycles run must have been tiled too — 640 by 480 against a tile of 64 — but that is arithmetic against the measured rule and not a sighting: the run kept no console output and watched no folder (*What a render demands 2*). **Never reached:** a count above Cycles' own default run right through; and a count carried out of range by a driver or a keyframe rather than by an assignment (*Settings 18*).

**Number of frames in one run.** Furthest: a list of five thousand frames, rendered in all four versions on both machines and in 5.3 on the Mac; two hundred thousand never reached Blender at all, because the shell refused the command first (*Frames 7*). Next, an Autosplit video run that put over two thousand frames into its first file before it was killed, in all four versions (*Frames 12*), and one that ran 170 frames through the two-gigabyte split, and in 5.3 on the Mac a 300-frame range taken past it and interrupted soon after (*Frames 5*). A video render carried a four-thousand-frame range in its name (*Writing 11*); a 400-frame animation filled a disk part way, in one version only (*Writing 5*). Most topics ran far shorter: one hundred (*Frames 15*), sixty (*Reading a file 2*), forty split between two Blenders (*The machine 7*), thirty (*Builds 6*), eight (*Add-ons 12*), five (*File output nodes 16*), three (*Writing 14*), two (*The compositor 2*). **Never reached:** any limit that is Blender's rather than the operating system's (*Frames 7*).

**Number and kind of graphics cards.** Furthest: four NVIDIA cards in one machine, not joined by NVLink, with forty-eight timed renders across one, two and four of them (*The machine 5*). 5.3 rendered on one graphics device only, Apple's own, alone or with the processor beside it (*The machine 5*). **Never reached, and named as unreachable:** an AMD or an Intel card, cards joined by NVLink, two cards of different makes, two cards of unequal speed, and a card near the compute-capability floor the manuals name (*The machine 5*, *What a render demands 7*, *Settings 19*). **Reached and not used:** a machine with two cards and a screen at once — a window was opened on it, but only for questions about files, never about cards (*Frames 13*, *Frames 14*, *Reports 6*). **On several rented machines the card was present and no test on it ever touched the card**, every render being a processor render (*Reports 14*, *Writing 14*, *Builds 10*).

**Operating systems.** macOS on Apple silicon, Linux on rented machines, and **Windows under two questions, on two different machines** — one test of *Frames 15* in Blender 4.5 on the machine that rendered, and six of *Dependencies 20*'s, five of them in all four versions, on a second machine that rendered nothing. Everything else in every topic is untried on Windows (*Writing 14*, *Builds 10*, *Reports 14*, *Settings 18*, *The compositor 9*, *The machine 2*). **Never reached:** a **desktop** edition of Windows — every Windows machine this project has read booted a Server edition, and the image name does not predict which (*Dependencies 20*, *Frames 15*); an Intel Mac; a Mac near the supported macOS floor; and **Wayland** — every Linux machine with a display server ran an X server, so what the launch argument that removes a window's frame changes on Wayland is untested (*The machine 1*). **One machine ran two operating system versions:** the Creator's Mac, which moved from macOS 26.5.2 to 26.6.2 during this stage, so its own results are not all from one system (*Builds 3*, *Settings 18*). **5.3 reached one operating system**, macOS 26.6.2 on that Mac. What it does on Linux is untried, and there its release notes move the default drawing system to Vulkan (*The machine 1*).

**Render engines.** Cycles, EEVEE and Workbench, in all five versions (*Settings 13*, *The compositor 9*), plus a tiny engine registered from a script, used only to see whether its own progress and reporting calls work (*Reports 7*, *Reports 2*), plus **a third-party engine built for the purpose, installed and confirmed in the engine listing** (*Add-ons 7*), and in 5.3 an engine a launch script registered (*Settings 13*). **So what a file naming a missing third-party engine does is settled**, in all five versions — 4.5 to 5.2 on two machines, 5.3 on the Mac: Blender says one line, renders the frame in EEVEE at EEVEE's own sample count instead of the file's, writes a picture, and ends at 0 (*Add-ons 7*, *Settings 13*). **A missing engine forced at launch stops the job**: with the engine named on the command line and the add-on gone, every version says the engine cannot be found and writes nothing, 5.0 to 5.3 end at a failure status, and **4.5 does not end properly** — it aborts on the Mac and never ends on Linux (*Add-ons 7*, *Settings 13*). **Never reached:** a **published** third-party engine, and whether switching an add-on on at launch happens early enough for the engine argument to see it (*Settings 13*). **The reason *Settings 13* gives for that remaining half — that installing an engine was not allowed on any machine it used — does not describe how rented machines are used elsewhere in this research**: forty-two packages were installed on one to make Blender start at all (*Builds 10*), a media reader on another (*Writing 14*), and a display server in software on a third (*Settings 19*).

**The ways a render was started, and whether a hand ever did it.** Furthest: five ways, put to the same frame in all four versions on both machines (*Reports 9*). **Also reached:** a synthetic Escape key press and a synthetic click on the status bar's cancel button both **stopped** a render inside Blender's own window on rented Linux machines — the click in all four versions on two of them, Escape in all four versions on two and in 5.1 on a third (*Frames 13*, *Frames 15*); Blender's window was also opened and worked in for other questions on rented Linux machines, though those runs drove it by its own commands rather than by clicks or keys (*File output nodes 16*, *File output nodes 17*, *Settings 19*). **In 5.3, on the Mac, keys and clicks came from a third road**: Blender's own simulated events, sent from inside it. A click on the cancel button stopped a render; Blender's own Escape did not, in 5.3 or in 5.2.2, so it is no stand-in for a key press (*Frames 15*, *Frames 3*). **Every windowed run behind the ways-of-starting work in 4.5 to 5.2 was begun by calling the command a key is bound to**, and with no window Blender's key configuration holds no items at all; the bindings were read from the window in 5.3 and in 5.2.2 alone (*Reports 9*, *Frames 10*, *Frames 3*). **A render begun by a click was reached in one place only, in 5.3**: Blender's own simulated click on the re-render button of a compositor node started a render of the scene that node names (*The compositor 9*). Blender's own simulated F12 and Ctrl-F12 reached the render command too, on files that then refused to render (*Reports 2*). The 5.3 stop tests began theirs by calling the Render Animation item's command (*Frames 15*, *Frames 10*). **A hand did start a render**, in one place only: the Creator's own F12 presses at the Mac's window, in 4.5 to 5.2 (*Reports 2*). **Input sent by a tool through macOS was refused on this Mac**, checked twice, because it needs a system security setting nobody here will change; Blender's own simulated events are a different road (*Frames 15*).

**A window has been opened and photographed on the Creator's Mac.** In 4.5 to 5.2, *Settings 17* photographed the panels it needed there in all four versions — though in another topic's runs a window started there from a terminal never got going, a timer set in it never firing and a request to quit doing nothing (*Dependencies 17*). In 5.3 a window was opened from a terminal across the topics, ran its timers, was driven by a script through Blender's own simulated pointer and keys, and photographed by Blender's own screenshot command (*Settings 17*, *Frames 15*, *What a render demands 7*). How 4.5 to 5.2 draw the Mac's own two ray-tracing controls was never photographed (*What a render demands 7*). **The tool’s input through macOS was refused** (*Frames 15*). Blender’s own simulated input is not a person’s: its Escape stopped no render (*Frames 15*), and its pointer brought up no tooltip in the path-field test (*Dependencies 17*).

**Where files were written — local disk against a folder over a network.** Local disk everywhere, with one exception: a folder mounted over the network by a rented machine from itself, with both renders still on that one machine (*Frames 14*). **That exception is the reason this dimension matters.** Two Blenders sharing that folder collided four, five or six times in thirty repeats, against none in thirty on the same machine's own disk, in all four versions (*Frames 14*). 5.3 wrote to the Mac's own disks alone, one Blender at a time (*Frames 14*). **Never reached:** two separate computers sharing one folder, which is the arrangement the render-farm recipe is written for (*Frames 14*, *Writing 11*, *Writing 14*, *The machine 7*); and any network folder at all in several topics, which have no file server to point at (*File output nodes 5*, *Dependencies 7*, *Dependencies 20*).

**Whether a screen or a display server was there.** All three states were reached — a machine with a real screen, a machine whose display was taken away, and machines with no display server at all — and in what Blender says and does the last two cannot be told apart (*The machine 1*). 5.3 reached the first alone: it was never run on a machine without a screen (*Settings 6*, *The machine 1*). Several rented machines have also had a display server, some as rented and some with one installed in software, and Blender's window has been opened and photographed on them (*Frames 15*, *File output nodes 16*, *File output nodes 17*, *Settings 19*, *Writing 13*, *What a render demands 7*). **A software-drawn display is no workstation's screen**, and every comparison that used one says so: what a viewport draws, and how fast, represents nothing (*Settings 19*, *Frames 15*). **And no run anywhere in this research records which device an EEVEE render used** — on a software-drawn machine or on any other, 5.3's Mac included (*What actually ran 1*). Every version's manual says EEVEE only ever renders on the graphics card and cannot fall back to the processor; the nearest thing to a test is that on a machine with no display server, with the card's graphics library hidden on purpose, every version answered that it was drawing with a software renderer and still finished, wrote its frame and exited 0, more slowly. **Neither record establishes what EEVEE's own sampling ran on**, so nothing here may be read as EEVEE rendering without a card, and nothing may be read as it needing one (*The machine 2*, *The machine 7*, *Frames 15*, *Settings 6*). **Never reached:** a Mac with no display server (*Settings 6*); a screen physically unplugged (*Add-ons 3*); and any **card** question asked at a window with more than one card behind it (*Reports 6*).

**Whether the machine had graphics hardware at all.** **Reached twice, and not on Linux:** both Windows machines that produced results had **no graphics card of any kind**. Blender rendered on one of them, in 4.5, **on its processor** (*Frames 15*); nothing rendered on the other (*Dependencies 20*). One limit on the first: it was driven over a remote-desktop screen, and what drew that screen is nowhere written down beyond a basic display adapter reporting no memory of its own. **Never reached:** a Linux or macOS machine with no graphics hardware (*Settings 6*, *Add-ons 3*). **And the whole card half of any Windows question cannot be run on the Windows machines this project can reach**, because they have none — that is recorded as unable to be run rather than as work waiting (*What a render demands 7*).

**Video output as well as stills.** Reached in five topics: 104 real encodes across container-and-codec pairings, in all four versions on both machines, and twenty-six of them in 5.3 on the Mac (*Images 4*); a lossless video taken past the point where Blender closes one file and opens a second, on Linux in 4.5 to 5.2 and on the Mac in 5.3 (*Frames 5*); a video render named for a four-thousand-frame range (*Writing 11*); a video render stopped part way and read back by a media tool, which got out the frames written so far — a tool installed on a rented Linux machine in 4.5 to 5.2, and one already on the Mac in 5.3 (*Writing 14*); and a video run stopped on purpose, which printed nothing about the video or its closing, in 5.3 too (*Frames 15*). **Never reached:** sound in a video render — no scene in any video round carried any (*Frames 12*). **And partly reached:** what a whole run prints when the output is a video is not recorded in the question that sets out a run from its first line to its last, which names no video run at all (*Reports 8*); what is on record is that a video run stopped on purpose printed nothing whatever about the video or its closing (*Frames 15*).

**How long a run was.** ***Mostly not said.*** Few comparisons record how long a run took. The longest on record are the Autosplit video runs, thirty to sixty-seven minutes each (*Frames 12*); after them a four-minute limit that killed a render with hours of work estimated as remaining (*Settings 15*), and a sample count begun with nine to fourteen hours estimated (*Settings 18*). The longest single frames on record are two first frames on the Mac's own graphics in 5.3: one took 184 seconds before it was saved where the next two took under a second each, with nothing printed about the wait (*Frames 10*), and another spent almost four minutes loading render kernels before its first sample (*What a render demands 7*). Beyond those two, the longest is about **ninety seconds**, reached two ways: under EEVEE on a rented machine whose display is drawn in software — with nothing recording which device that render ran on (*Frames 15*, *The machine 2*) — and on the Creator's Mac at Cycles' own default of 4,096 samples, in 4.5 and again in 5.3 (*Settings 16*). Beside them a thirty-seven-second frame on that rented machine, a thirty-second frame on Windows, and a twenty-one-second frame on the Mac. Ordinary renders behind these findings ended in seconds (*The machine 7*). **Never reached:** a render lasting a day or more (*Frames 12*, *Settings 15*, *Settings 18*).

**Whether the machine was quiet.** **Exactly one rented machine was ever checked to be uncontended rather than assumed to be** — the Austrian machine behind *What a render demands 7*, whose whole processor and whole card were confirmed free before anything ran, and whose times are given as three repeats each. Every other machine in this research is a share of somebody else's hardware. **And a machine this project owns is not automatically a quiet machine: no timing taken on the Creator's Mac on 2026-08-30 is clean**, another agent having been running Blender on it throughout (*What a render demands 7*, *Settings 18*, *Settings 19*, *Writing 14*, *Reports 14*). Earlier rounds say the same of their own days (*What a render demands 1*, *Settings 16*), and so does every 5.3 timing: the Mac was running other work then too (*What a render demands 7*, *Settings 16*).

**Whether the machine knew its own size.** **Most rented machines misreport it** — the ordinary readings show the host's processors and memory rather than the machine's own ceiling, and the over-report has run to ten gigabytes and more. One machine's ordinary readings were checked and found honest (*Frames 15*); one very nearly so (*Reports 14*). **And one machine behind a comparison cannot be sized at all**: the machine behind *Dependencies 20*'s Linux half has no recorded instance, kernel, driver, card memory, processor model or quota, and its twelve cores and 31 GB are ordinary readings with nothing to check them against. Nothing in that question rests on how big it was, and nothing may.

**How big a `.blend` was.** ***Not said*** as a condition anywhere. Two file sizes are on record: a test scene of 434,663 bytes saved without compression, from a question that warns a compressed file's exact byte count means nothing (*Reading a file 1*), and a 5.3 test file of 600,713 bytes whose size a plain save kept (*Dependencies 20*). **No comparison gives the size of the 125.8-million-triangle file, or of any other heavy scene** (*What a render demands 7*, *Reports 6*) — and nobody may put a number to them here, since a heavy scene can be built by a small file. **No test set a heavy file’s byte size as a condition** (*Reading a file 1*, *Dependencies 20*, *What a render demands 7*, *Reports 6*).

**How closely two runs of the same thing agree.** ***This is the floor under every claim that two things differ, and it is not one number.*** Measured at length once: **a hundred and twenty pairs on the Creator's Mac**, across four versions, three scenes and five repeats each, and thirty more in 5.3 on the same Mac (*What a render demands 7*).

- **Those processor pairs were identical to the pixel.** All sixty in 4.5 to 5.2 and all fifteen in 5.3, on three scenes on this Mac. That settles those renders alone. In 5.3 two processor renders using existing texture-cache files differed, so a processor comparison needs a repeat of its own (*What a render demands 7*, *What a render demands 2*).
- **On a card the floor moves.** Fifty-eight of the sixty pairs sat between about three and six ten-millionths of a channel value. The other two — **about one card pair in thirty, one in each of two versions** — came back at 5.3 × 10⁻⁵ and **0.0182**, the second some forty thousand times the rest. In 5.3 one card pair in fifteen came back far from the rest, at 0.0098. **Nothing explains any of the three** (*What a render demands 7*).
- **That long measurement is macOS and Apple's own graphics only.** The Linux control was one pair per row, between 4.17 × 10⁻⁷ and 9.54 × 10⁻⁷, on a machine that no longer exists — so the NVIDIA rows rest on a floor measured once rather than at length (*What a render demands 7*, *What a render demands 2*).
- **A count of differing channels is not always reproducible either.** One denoising comparison's channel count moved over two, three or four neighbouring values across forty-eight rounds in 4.5, 5.0 and 5.1, and not at all in 5.2 or in 5.3's twelve rounds, while its control stayed at zero throughout. The earlier wobble came with a rebuilt scene; six repeats of one saved scene in 5.0 were steady, and 5.3’s twelve rounds used one saved file. So this is no version difference in repeatability, and the last digit is not a number to build on (*Settings 19*).

**Consequences a reader should carry.** A difference of a few ten-millionths on a card is noise. A difference of hundredths on a card is usually real, but **how clear of the floor it is depends on the scene**: on the small and mesh-heavy scenes the measured differences run hundreds to tens of thousands of times above the worst same-method figure those scenes produced, while on the curve-heavy scene they are only a factor of six to eight above it in 4.5 to 5.2, and only one and a half to two in 5.3, and that comparison says outright that a reader should lean less hard on those rows, in 5.3 most of all (*What a render demands 7*). A control of exactly zero is achievable and several rounds got one, which is what makes any of these comparisons mean anything (*Settings 19*, *What a render demands 7*).

## What needs equipment this project has never had

- **A desktop edition of Windows** — every Windows machine this project has read reported a Server edition, so nothing here speaks for the Windows an artist runs (*Dependencies 20*). **An ordinary Windows machine is not on this list**: one can be rented, and every topic's Windows silence is untried rather than out of reach (*Dependencies 6*, *Writing 14*, *Builds 10*, *Reports 14*, *Settings 18*, *What a render demands 3*).
- **A Windows machine with a graphics card** — both whose hardware was read had none of any kind, so no card question can be put to that route at all, however long it stays open (*What a render demands 7*, *Frames 15*, *Dependencies 20*).
- **Two computers sharing one folder** — whether a claim on a frame holds across machines, and what the render-farm recipe actually produces. A rented machine mounted a network share from itself; that does not establish the same for a container. What is missing is the second computer and a folder both can reach (*Frames 14*, *Writing 14*, *The machine 7*).
- **An AMD or an Intel graphics card** — whether the figures Blender draws for them are enforced, what a file's graphics-card setting means on one, and what denoisers such a machine offers (*The machine 2*, *Settings 9*, *Settings 19*).
- **Two cards joined by NVLink** — what the memory-sharing setting does when cards really are linked, and whether its box is drawn or greyed (*The machine 5*, *What a render demands 7*).
- **A card near the floor the manuals name** — what a 5.x Blender does with a card below the raised compute-capability minimum (*What a render demands 7*). For 5.3 the driver floor is open as well: its manual and its own Cycles file give different NVIDIA driver figures, and no NVIDIA card ran 5.3 (*The machine 2*, *What a render demands 7*).
- **A Blender built without one of the optional picture formats** — what a render does when the file asks for a format its Blender cannot write. Every official build tested carries all of them, the 5.3.0 Beta on the Mac included, so the case was never produced; a Blender that came with a rented image rather than from Blender's own download server was never asked anything at all, and its machine is gone; and how the 5.3.0 Beta came onto the Mac is not recorded. The comparison's own reading is **never asked** rather than cannot exist (*Images 4*, *Builds 7*, *Builds 10*).
- **A *published* third-party render engine** — whether an add-on switched on at launch is seen when the engine argument is read, which no run has reached (*Settings 13*). **What a file naming a missing third-party engine does is not on this list**: one was built for the purpose, installed and then taken away, in all four versions on two machines, and the file gave the same result in 5.3 on the Mac (*Add-ons 7*).
- **A Mac with no display server, and a Linux or macOS machine with no graphics hardware at all** — whether every engine really renders with no screen for reasons of the screen rather than of the card (*Settings 6*, *Add-ons 3*).
- **A Wayland session** — every Linux machine with a display server this project has had ran an X server, so what the launch argument that removes a window's frame changes on Wayland is untested (*The machine 1*).
- **A machine below Blender's stated floor**, and a Mac running an old macOS or an Intel processor (*The machine 1*, *The machine 2*).
- **A media player other than the four used** — QuickTime on the Mac (*Frames 5*), Blender's own movie reader (*Frames 12*, *Frames 14*), the reader installed on one rented Linux machine, and the reader already on the Mac that read 5.3's files (*Writing 14*).

**Four kinds of equipment were reached.** A machine with a screen **and** more than one card was had (*Frames 13*, *Frames 14*). A machine checked to be uncontended was had (*What a render demands 7*). A media player on the machine that renders was had (*Writing 14*). And a machine deliberately **bare** was had — one that arrived with `tar` and `gzip` and none of the tools or libraries the question was about (*Builds 10*). **That last one is an instrument rather than a convenience**: a repeat of those tests on a machine that already had those tools would prove nothing, and anyone re-running them must rent bare or not at all.

## Where one machine, or one round, carries the whole answer

- **The whole of 5.3 is one machine and one build** — the 5.3.0 Beta on the Creator's Mac. Where 5.3 came out differently from 5.2's record, the same test was usually run that day on the Mac in Blender 5.2.2, and that control helps tell whether the difference is 5.3’s; where 5.2.2 did what 5.3 did, no difference between those versions is established. The setup must match too. A 5.3 result set beside a 4.5 to 5.2 result that only a rented machine gave does not establish a difference between versions, and nothing says the finished 5.3 behaves as the beta did (equipment section; *Reports 7*, *The machine 7*, *Builds 3*).
- **Two whole questions were run on the Creator's Mac and on no other computer in 4.5 to 5.2, and in both the gap is narrower than the sitting.** *Settings 17* — the launch-instruction names — and *The compositor 9* — the engine and count a scene pulled in through the compositor renders with. **Nothing was rented for either.** But four other questions set three of those six names at launch on four different rented Linux machines and got the values they asked for, so what rests on the Mac alone is five narrower things: **4.5's swallowing of a misspelled name on Cycles' settings**, the unknown-switch result, the relative output path with no file loaded, the count of launch sub-commands, and the on-screen label of each setting (*Settings 17*). The two names nobody has set on Linux are EEVEE's and Workbench's, and in 5.3 none of the six was set there. And *The compositor 9*'s split between 4.5 and 5.0 to 5.2 — whether a view layer's sample override is honoured under EEVEE — **is settled again on a second operating system and a second make of card** by *Settings 16*, in all four versions; 5.3 sides with 5.0 to 5.2 on the Mac alone (*The compositor 9*, *Settings 16*).
- **One version's failure was a fact about one card and one driver.** Cycles tracing on the card under OptiX failed outright in Blender 5.2.1 on **one RTX 3060 on driver 550.120**, and worked in the other three versions on the same machine (*Settings 19*). **That is not a statement about Blender 5.2 anywhere**, and whether it happens on any other machine is untested; 5.3 had no NVIDIA card to try it on. It is kept because a render farm choosing a driver would want to know it can happen — and because it is the clearest case in this research of a machine, not Blender, deciding an answer.
- **One result needed two denoisers on one machine**, so it ran on the rented Linux machine alone; the Mac offers one denoiser and could not have posed the question at all, so it is untested in 5.3 (*Settings 19*).
- **One strict ordering result rests on separate instruments and they are separate claims.** Blender was asked and the disk was watched on both machines, and in 5.3 on the Mac; every file call was traced on Linux alone, in 4.5 to 5.2. **The trace settles Linux and says nothing whatever about macOS.** In 5.3 a third instrument, a folder check run thousands of times a second, settled on the Mac the one ordering the watcher could not; 5.3 was never traced (*Writing 14*).
- **The one Windows render is one test, one version, one Server edition, one machine with no card, and a frame half as long again as the Mac's.** Its own comparison says: never set these figures beside the Mac's or the Linux machine's (*Frames 15*). **The other Windows results are a different machine again** — six tests that rendered nothing and read text out of files (*Dependencies 20*) — so the two Windows halves of this research share an edition and not a computer.

## Where a claim of "never" rests on runs that could not have shown otherwise

- **Tiles.** **Tiled progress has been captured**, at 2049 by 2049 in 4.5 to 5.2 and in 5.3 on the Mac (*Reports 7*). What every *untiled-shape* line on record came from is a picture too small to be split (*Reports 3*). **The tested boundary crosses one tile’s worth of area, reached twice and separately**, and again in 5.3 on the Mac (*Reports 7*, *Writing 11*) — but **that is not the arithmetic a picture is cut up with**: every tile count on record is the number of whole tiles across times the number down, each side rounded up on its own, which the area rule contradicts at three of the counts. The two rules agree at every threshold anybody has reached, so what crosses it — the width or the area — is untested, in 5.3 too, and no size tried crossed one without crossing the other (*Reports 7*, *Writing 11*). *Writing 11* adds the condition either would be read without: the tile setting decides it too, so a very large picture with a large enough tile is not tiled at all, and a small one with a small enough tile is.
- **"The two compositor devices make no difference."** The small comparison used 320 by 240, four trees and one Linux machine; 5.3 repeated it on the Mac with the compositor set to processor or graphics, coming within one step of 255 (*The compositor 1*). **Size and content limit that answer.** At 9000 pixels wide one earlier version wrote black with the graphics setting. In 5.3, with that setting, only 8192 pixels along the longer side were composited, across both tall and wide pictures; the file kept its full size. The same-Mac 5.2.2 control showed the same at 9000 by 400. Whether the 5.3 graphics setting really used the card is not established (*The compositor 1*, *The compositor 6*). Other sizes, trees and file formats gave non-identical pictures, including a 5.3 Denoise node whose device difference was larger than the small four-tree comparison’s (*The compositor 1*, *What a render demands 2*). **Three things join here.** Failures were seen with the graphics setting; that is the compositor’s starting value in 5.2 and 5.3; and it belongs to no engine, so Cycles’ device argument does not reach it (*Settings 9*, *Settings 3*, *The compositor 1*, *The compositor 6*).
- **"Eleven settings leave the picture untouched."** All eleven were compared on one 640 by 480 scene, at 64 samples, in Cycles, **on the processor** — 4.5 to 5.2 on two machines, 5.3 on the Mac's processor alone (*What a render demands 2*). Two of the eleven are the tile settings, tried at 64 and at 8192 on that same small picture. **That run was tiled**: 640 by 480 against a tile of 64 is eighty tiles under the per-side rule and seventy-five under the area rule, so it crosses either way, and the run it was measured against was one tile. A Cycles frame split into tiles and the same frame unsplit gave the same 1,228,800 channel values, in all four versions on both machines and in 5.3 on the Mac. **The split is arithmetic against a measured rule and not a sighting** — that run kept no console output and watched no folder (*Reports 7*, *Writing 11*). **The processor repeats behind three measured scenes matched to the pixel**, over sixty pairs and fifteen more in 5.3 (*What a render demands 7*). That supports those scenes, not every processor render; 5.3 repeats using existing texture-cache files differed (*What a render demands 2*). The bound on the scene, the size and the sample count is untouched, and the controls show that the comparison could see *a* change, not that each of the eleven had something in that scene to bite on.
- **"A stop leaves nothing part-written."** True of stops sent at no particular moment (*Frames 3*, *File output nodes 13*, *Frames 15*). A stop aimed at the instant the file appears leaves the last image short every time, for two of the three ways of stopping, in 4.5 to 5.2 (*Frames 13*) — and *File output nodes 13* withdraws its own "nothing at all" on that ground. In 5.3 the stop was aimed instead at the middle of a write made long on purpose, on the Mac, and the same two ways left the file short (*Frames 13*). **A stop aimed at a write has never been given inside Blender's own window**, in any version (*Frames 13*, *Frames 15*). **The empty placeholder is a separate question.** Whether an ordinary stop leaves it in 4.5 to 5.2 is not settled: the tested results disagree. In 5.3 on the Mac a terminate or kill left it and an interrupt removed it (*Writing 11*, *Frames 3*). **A process killed is not a machine lost.** One kernel reset on the Ada machine lost even the contents of a frame already reported saved, in all four versions; no such reset was tried in 5.3 (*Frames 13*).
- **"Two Blenders sharing a folder do not collide."** Twelve runs on one Linux machine never collided, while the same test on the Mac collided in three versions out of four (*Frames 4*); the same arrangement run thirty times per version collided on the Mac in one version, never on that Linux machine's own disk, and four, five or six times in thirty over a network folder (*Frames 14*); a third topic's two-Blender test started its second Blender five seconds late, so no collision was possible in it at all (*Dependencies 17*); and a fourth kept only the last fifteen lines of each copy's log, so it could not have shown a collision either way (*The machine 7*). And 5.3 never ran two Blenders at once (*Frames 14*). **A fifth clean run exists and is not one of these** — one render writing its frames in order with no overlap — and it must not be added to the list, because it is not a two-render result (*Writing 14*).
- **Every two-Blender result anywhere was measured on a small picture** — 64 by 64 up to 1920 by 1080 (*The machine 7*, *Frames 14*, *Reading a file 2*, *Add-ons 12*, *Builds 6*, *Writing 11*). Cycles opens no tile file below one tile's worth of pixels, and a frame's own file is part-written for only about a fifth of a second at 1920 by 1080 (*Writing 11*). **No two-Blender run watched a tile file or a long frame write.** The small-picture runs do not settle either case, and 5.3 ran no two Blenders at once (*Frames 14*, *Writing 11*, *Reading a file 2*).
- **"Standard error stays empty."** Well founded, and still not a rule. A control run with nothing wrong stands in front of every silence, on two machines in all four versions and on the Mac in 5.3 (*Reports 13*), and a whole run's error stream was fed to a reader and gave nought bytes (*Reports 14*). **The exception stands**: on a Linux machine with a screen running, a windowless 5.2 put display-library lines there on a completely successful render, and 5.3 never ran on such a machine (*Reports 2*); other questions fill it for other reasons, among them a missing Alembic cache in 5.3, whose library writes there in its own words (*Reports 7*, *Reports 10*, *Reports 12*, *Reports 13*).
- **Silences found by searching for words.** Several "Blender says nothing" findings are a search of the output for a fixed list of words, and a line worded differently would have been missed: the card-memory silence, searched for four phrasings (*Reports 6*), the silent fall-back to the processor at factory settings, searched for three (*The machine 4*), and the silent fall-back to system memory when a card fills, searched for four (*Reports 1*). **The missing-texture silence in 4.5 is not one of these**, though it was searched the same way: the whole of that run's output was also compared against a control run with the texture in place and came out the control's with one ordinary stage line added, which would have caught any wording (*Reports 13*, *Reports 1*).
- **"A File Output node's failure never moves the exit status."** Every run behind it, 5.3's included, put the scene's own output on a disk with room to spare on purpose, so the two writes have never failed together (*File output nodes 15*).
- **"A missing dependency never changes the exit status."** Rests on missing-input cases only, across more kinds in 5.3 on the Mac than in any earlier version; whether it holds beyond them is not settled (*Dependencies 12*). A zero ending also hides other refusals: bad or empty frame arguments, a missing option value, a reversed animation range, saving switched off, a picture too small to render, and refused video settings (*Reports 12*, *Reports 7*, *Images 4*). **The no-camera case is narrower**: in 5.3 on the Mac, a command-line still of the tested Cycles scene, on the processor at 160 by 120 and 16 samples, with its Camera object deleted, wrote nothing and ended at zero. The same test in 5.2.2 ended in failure. A script-called render of that scene still failed in 5.3 (*Reports 1*, *Reports 12*). This does not settle Workbench: its two tested 5.3 files kept the Camera object, either unassigned or assigned but hidden, and both wrote a frame (*Settings 6*). **Saving off leaves no output in 5.2 and 5.3, but whether 5.2 renders before ending is not settled**; 5.3 refused before rendering on the Mac (*Reports 12*).
- **"Rendering one scene says nothing about the other scene's missing file."** In 4.5 the control run was silent as well, because 4.5 never names a missing image at all — so 4.5's silence in that test carries nothing. The topic says so outright and answers the question another way, by watching the machine instead of asking Blender (*Dependencies 14*, *Dependencies 12*). In 5.3, on the Mac, the control did name the other scene's missing file, so there the silence carries something (*Dependencies 14*).
- **"Two of the seven places Windows leaves alone are not results."** Seven recorded places keep their forward slashes on a Windows save; **two of them are `//lib.blend`, which has no separator in it to change**, so five real places are left alone and two could not have come out any other way. The comparison says so itself (*Dependencies 20*).
- **Every Windows result rests on a machine whose edition nobody chose.** The two questions' Windows results come off **two different machines**, both created from the same image entry named for a desktop edition, and **both booted Windows Server 2025 Standard on five independent readings each**. A third machine, from the only image in that account whose text says *desktop*, booted Windows Server 2016 and was abandoned unused because it has no SSH server and cannot be given one. **The image name does not predict the edition, so nothing about a desktop edition follows from any of it** (*Dependencies 20*, *Frames 15*).
- **"No click could be shown to land."** That was a fact about a panel, not about clicks. Six attempts to click the status bar's cancel button failed because Blender's own Quick Setup panel, drawn inside the main window and shown by no window list, takes the first click made anywhere; with one click spent first, the button lit in all four versions. **The reason was read off Blender 5.1's photographs alone** — for 4.5, 5.0 and 5.2 what stands is the narrower statement that the first click did not arrive and a second did (*Frames 15*).
- **"An unwritable output folder fails."** On a rented machine reached as the root user it does not: root walks through the folder’s permissions, and the run gives zero and writes its picture. **Root’s power had to be dropped for the single command before that test meant anything**, and on one machine that route did not work at all and an ordinary account had to be made (*Reports 12*, *Reports 1*, *Writing 5*, *Writing 12*). In 5.3 the earlier folder-permission setup was not repeated; a volume mounted read-only stood in and failed for that different reason (*Writing 5*, *Writing 12*). A write into an existing system-owned folder was also refused on the Mac in 5.3; that is another setup, not the altered-permission test (*The machine 7*).
- **"Nothing is left in the temporary folder."** A large Cycles render with the tile size forced small left nothing there (*Writing 7*), while *Writing 11* finds the tile buffer file in that same folder. Both stand, and *Writing 14* explains why without settling it: Cycles' tile cache is **one file per frame**, written in the temporary folder during that frame's render and removed no later than the reading in which that frame's own output file appears. **The strict ordering of that removal is a Linux result in 4.5 to 5.2**, where the Mac's watcher cannot separate the two events; in 5.3 a faster instrument showed it on the Mac (*Writing 14*). And the temporary folder is not the whole disk: in 5.3 a render on the Mac's own graphics left compiled render kernels in a cache folder under the user's home (*Writing 7*).
- **"Nothing on the render path waits for a person."** The card-render-with-no-screen half is one operating system and machines that no longer exist; the Mac has no card of that kind and cannot speak to it (*The machine 7*). **5.3 was never tested with the Creator’s session logged out, or under screen locking, display sleep or machine sleep.** Its no-window unsaved-change tests did not even reach a state Blender counted as unsaved; whether the earlier tests reached that state is not settled (*The machine 7*). **Runs that stay running after a refusal are a different case**: the unknown-argument example in 5.0 to 5.3, a missing launch-time engine in 4.5 on Linux, and 4.5 asked for a window with no display (*Reports 12*, *Settings 6*). The runs were stopped by time limits; whether they would ever end by themselves is not settled. None shows a wait for a person.
- **The card-memory messages the manual names, and one of them has been produced.** When **another program** held almost all of one card's memory, Blender failed loudly and exited 1, naming the card being out of memory — run in 4.5 and 5.2 only, on one machine, with 5.0, 5.1 and 5.3 never asked and the machine gone (*Reports 2*). What was **not** produced is the same message from a render whose **own** scene overfilled the card: there the fall-back was silent, the spill stayed well inside that machine’s memory, and running that out as well was never attempted — **nor has the fall-back ever been run behind a smaller machine** (*Reports 1*, *Reports 6*, *The machine 4*). **And on the Creator's Mac that case cannot be set up even in principle**, because processor and graphics share one pool there, so a scene big enough to fill the graphics memory fills the machine's (*Reports 6*). The documented EEVEE shadow-buffer failure has been produced nowhere at all, and needs a card whose memory can be exhausted on its own (*Reports 1*). None of the card-memory failures was reached in 5.3, which never ran on a card of its own (*Reports 1*).
- **"A version accepts the older engine name."** Rests on one run, on one machine, on a 64 by 64 scene, and whether that run really rendered in that engine was never captured (*Settings 13*, *What actually ran 2*). *The compositor 9* records the same rename from its own runs and does not close that gap. In 5.3 the old name was refused from a script on the Mac; it was not tried on the command line (*Reports 3*, *Settings 13*).
- **"A changed file is read again at render time."** Rests on one small frame with 16,384 colour values compared; a re-read changing nothing visible in that frame would have looked identical (*Node setups 1*).
- **"Installing an add-on disturbs nothing."** The successful tests used eight-frame pictures no larger than 640 by 480 and one small add-on at a time. The two-Blender tests in 4.5 to 5.2 began renders from the command line, so their comparison of printed output used the route that prints most (*Add-ons 12*). **Installing into a running Blender’s own folder was never tried**: every install went into a user repository in a separate settings folder. Copying over the running Blender’s folder is the write that killed a render on Linux (*Add-ons 12*, *Builds 6*). In 5.3 only an install inside the rendering Blender was tried, on the Mac. With a window, an install from a frame handler failed in both 5.2.2 and 5.3; one from the main loop succeeded in 5.3 with the interface lock read back on or off. The earlier successful frame-handler runs do not settle this disagreement, because how they started the render is not established. An install from a second Blender was not tried in 5.3 (*Add-ons 12*).
- **"A Blender copied over a running render writes no crash report."** Two runs of the same test, same version, same machine, disagree; the comparison keeps both and chooses neither. Copying over a running 5.3 was never tried, no second copy of 5.3 being startable (*Builds 6*).

### And where a negative was tested rather than merely noticed

These are the opposite case, and a reader should be able to tell one from the other. Each of these silences was looked for by an instrument shown able to speak first, so each is worth what it says:

- A reader on the far end of a pipe was **proved able to show both patterns** — held-back output and prompt output — before anything was concluded about when a line arrives, and proved again in 5.3 on the Mac (*Reports 14*).
- The write was made slow on purpose, at 144 megabytes a frame, and the folder read every two milliseconds, so that **an overlap of a few milliseconds could not have been missed**; sixty-three readings fell inside a single write, and in 5.3 on the Mac 88 to 177 (*Writing 14*).
- A deliberately wrong assignment was made in the same run as the silent correction, and was refused loudly — **so the silence over a corrected number is a silence and not a channel that never speaks** (*Settings 18*). The same shape stands behind the silent correction of a resolution percentage, where a run made to fail on purpose came back at the chosen status in every single run, so the ordinary 0 means the correction really is not signalled (*Settings 15*).
- Whether rendering one scene reads another scene's files was settled by watching the machine rather than by asking Blender, two ways at once — every file the program opened, and the memory a swapped-in larger picture would have cost — **and both controls fired**, which is what makes the negative worth anything after the same question's earlier control had come back empty in 4.5 (*Dependencies 14*). In 5.3 the Mac's own two readings stood in — Blender's control, which fired, and the disk's access times, checked first on a text file; 5.3 was not traced (*Dependencies 14*).
- An ordinary render with nothing missing was run first, on the same machines in the same shape, and on the Mac in 5.3, so **an empty error stream means something** (*Reports 13*).
- Every zero-ending run was made into a folder emptied immediately beforehand, so **"nothing was written" is about that folder and not about a stale file** (*Reports 12*).
- Picture comparisons carried **controls of exactly zero** — two identical runs compared channel by channel — before any "no effect" was claimed (*Settings 19*), and sixty processor pairs on three scenes, with fifteen more in 5.3, matched to the pixel (*What a render demands 7*). That is no promise for every processor render: two using existing texture-cache files differed in 5.3 (*What a render demands 2*). **Not every round's control was a clean zero**: one settling-switch round's repeats came back either identical or apart in 15 of 65,536 values by a hair, and its "no effect" claims stand on that narrower ground (*What a render demands 6*).
- A ratio that held steady across scene sizes **was an available answer and did not happen** (*What a render demands 7*).
- Rendering the brought-in scene on its own was run first, so **an empty folder in any later test can only be that test's own doing** (*File output nodes 16*).

## What bears on nothing here

**A ceiling depends on how far any topic went.** A question may change nothing about Blender and raise a ceiling; another may change a great deal and leave every ceiling alone. That is why this document rests on all fifteen comparisons and the equipment section rather than on a list of questions said to bear on nothing.

The negatives above keep their test limits beside them: what could have shown another answer, what could not, and what was never tried. Each ceiling is worth only as much as those limits.
