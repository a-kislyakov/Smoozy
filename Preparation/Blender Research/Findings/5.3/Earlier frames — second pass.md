## 1. What in a scene can make its look on one frame depend on the frames worked out before it? Name every kind your sources describe.

**Label: documented for the explicit cases below; inferred where marked.** I read “depends on” as dependence on evaluation history, rather than simply having animation or reading an image sequence. The sources do not give a closed, exhaustive classification.

- **Cloth. Documented.** “the cloth simulator may not be able to compute or show you an accurate mesh shape for that frame, if it has not previously computed the shape for the previous frame(s).” Manual, [Cloth introduction](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/cloth/introduction.html), “Do Not Jump Ahead”.
- **Soft bodies, including deforming curves, surfaces and lattices. Inferred.** “Soft body simulation works for all objects that have vertices or control points (meshes, curves, surfaces, and lattices).” Also: “You can interact in real-time with the simulation, e.g. by moving collision objects or shaking a soft body object.” Manual, [Soft Body introduction](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/soft_body/introduction.html). Its [Cache](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/soft_body/settings/cache.html) says: “Soft Body physics simulations use a unified system for caching and baking.” Step: an evolving simulation with intermediate cached states is a candidate for history dependence; these words do not establish its exact out-of-order behavior.
- **Simulated emitter particles: Newtonian, Boids, and particle Fluid/SPH. Inferred for inclusion of each subtype; documented cache-history condition.** “When an animation is played, each physics system writes each frame to the cache. Note that for the cache to fill up, one has to start the playback before or on the frame that the simulation starts.” Manual, [Particle Cache](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/particles/emitter/cache.html). [Boids](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/particles/emitter/physics/boids.html): “Boids particle systems are controlled by a limited artificial intelligence”. [Particle Fluid](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/particles/emitter/physics/fluid.html): “Fluid particles are similar to Newtonian ones but this time particles are influenced by internal forces like pressure, surface tension, viscosity, springs, etc.” Step: these are simulation modes of the emitter system covered by its cache warning. This does not prove every particle setting requires preroll. No Physics and Keyed are not independently established as history-dependent by this evidence.
- **Hair dynamics. Inferred.** “Hair particles can have dynamic properties using physics. To enable hair physics, click the checkbox beside Hair Dynamics.” Manual, [Hair Dynamics](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/particles/hair/dynamics.html). The API's [ParticleSystem](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.ParticleSystem.html) and [PointCache](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.PointCache.html) expose particle-system caching. Step: dynamic simulated hair belongs among simulation candidates; the quoted manual sentence alone is not a cold-frame guarantee.
- **Rigid-body worlds. Inferred.** “The Cache subpanel specifies the frame range in which the simulation is active.” Manual, [Rigid Body World](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/rigid_body/world.html). [Rigid Body introduction](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/rigid_body/introduction.html): “The rigid body simulation can be used to simulate the motion of solid objects.” Step: motion simulation with cached frame states is a history candidate; a direct statement of cold-render output was not found.
- **Fluid domains, gas and liquid, including their derived noise/mesh/particle outputs and guides. Inferred.** “The Cache panel is used to Bake the fluid simulation and stores the outcome of a simulation so it does not need to be recalculated.” Manual, [Fluid Cache](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/fluid/type/domain/cache.html). Its Replay mode says: “The cache will be baked as the simulation is being played in the viewport.” The [Fluid Effector](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/fluid/type/effector.html) describes “Values in the velocity field from a previous frame or guiding object”. Step: the simulation can carry earlier velocity/state forward. The output layers are not separate start-frame owners merely because they have separate bake buttons.
- **Dynamic Paint canvas surfaces: paint/wetness, evolving weight, incremental displacement, and waves. Documented for the described cumulative/evolving effects; inferred as a general history class.** [Canvas](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/physics/dynamic_paint/canvas.html): “A new displace is added cumulatively on top of an existing displace.” “This surface type produces simulated wave motion.” “Used to make the surface smoothly return to its original state during a defined Time period.” “Paint slowly spreads to surrounding points eventually filling all connected areas.” Both Vertex and Image Sequences formats are described. Step: cumulative displacement, spreading/drying/dissolving and wave evolution can carry earlier state. A static intersection-only displacement is not proved history-dependent just by being Dynamic Paint.
- **Geometry Nodes Simulation Zones. Documented.** “Simulation zones allow the result of one frame to influence the next one.” Manual, [Simulation Zone](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/modeling/geometry_nodes/simulation/simulation_zone.html). This includes custom simulations, not just named physical solvers.
- **Armature iTaSC in Simulation mode. Documented.** “The Simulation mode is the stateful mode of the solver: it estimates the target’s velocity, operates in a ‘true time’ context, ignores rotation from keyframes (except via a joint rotation constraint) and builds up a state cache automatically.” Manual, [Inverse Kinematics](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/animation/armatures/posing/bone_constraints/inverse_kinematics/introduction.html), “Simulation”. Animation mode is explicitly “stateless”.
- **Ocean foam accumulated during baking. Documented, specifically bake history.** “When simulating live, the ocean simulator retrieves data for that current frame only.” “With baking, it is possible to approximate that behavior, by accumulating foam from previous frames, leaving it remaining on the surface.” Manual, [Ocean Modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/modeling/modifiers/physics/ocean.html), “Foam Fade”. This does not establish live ocean displacement as requiring preroll.
- **Custom Python behavior, including frame-change handlers and state-carrying driver functions. Inferred.** API, [Application Handlers](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.app.handlers.html): “Handlers are functions that Blender calls when certain events occur, such as loading a file, changing frame or rendering.” It explicitly provides an example of “a mesh that is altered from a handler”. Step: a callback that changes existing data cumulatively can preserve earlier evaluation effects. The supplied example itself calculates position from the current frame and does not demonstrate accumulation. Driver-specific evidence is supplied in answer 7.
- **Temporal render/viewport history, with a different scope from timeline simulation. Documented reuse; timeline-preroll requirement not established.** Manual, [EEVEE Raytracing](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/render/eevee/render_settings/raytracing.html): “Accumulate samples by re-projecting the last ray tracing results.” Release notes, [Cycles](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.3/cycles/): “On NVIDIA GPUs, a new DLSS denoiser is available for the viewport. It provides a cleaner image faster through upscaling and reusing samples from previous frames.” These describe image history; they do not say that a final still render requires earlier timeline frames.

Follow-up searches covered animation, physics, compositor, render and scripting for `previous`, `preceding`, `state-full`, `state cache`, `history`, `incremental`, `frame_change`, `persistent`, `side.effect`. **Not covered.** An exhaustive proof for every possible scene, every add-on, or every arbitrary Python program; precise cold-frame pixels; proof that each member of a simulation family is always stateful. The inferred entries deliberately identify the step beyond the documentation.

**Outside the interface.** The handler API explicitly includes rendering. The physics and simulation descriptions do not generally guarantee the same cold-frame behavior in background mode. DLSS evidence is explicitly viewport-only. EEVEE's temporal description does not specify timeline-history behavior for background final rendering.
**Operating system.** These passages give no general OS exception or universal guarantee. DLSS is limited to supported NVIDIA hardware/builds; that is not a statement of support on every OS.

**Label: documented for properties and printed defaults; inferred for the effective Geometry Nodes choice; not in the sources for the unresolved initialization rules.** “When nobody has set it” is ambiguous: the API's printed property default and a newly created object's initialized value need not be established by the same evidence. ## 3. For each kind, can a script run with no window open find that first frame before anything is rendered? Say how certain your sources make it.

**Label: inferred for combining background scripting with the documented property paths; documented for the individual API fields; not in the sources for a universal effective-start guarantee.** Yes for reading the stored settings of the ordinary cache owners. No documented single answer covers every kind, initialization rule or program.

The route is data access, rather than clicking a panel or requiring a render first. API [Quickstart](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/info_quickstart.html): “Accessing data from the currently loaded blend-file is done with the module bpy.data.” API [Tips and Tricks](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/info_tips_and_tricks.html), “Use Blender without its User Interface”, supplies `blender myscene.blend --background --python myscript.py`. Manual [Command Line Arguments](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/5.3/advanced/command_line/arguments.html): “Run in background (often used for UI-less rendering).” `--python`: “Run the given Python script file.” These are documented as of **2026-11-08**. **Inference:** a script can load the file and inspect exposed data before requesting a render. None of these statements proves that loading a file causes no evaluation at all.

| Kind | What the script can inspect before a render | Certainty and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cloth | Walk `bpy.data.objects`, `object.modifiers`; a Cloth modifier's `point_cache.frame_start` | Stored start is exposed. Exact initialized/default and cold-render behavior are separate questions. |
| Soft body | A SoftBody modifier's `point_cache.frame_start` | Same. Mesh-only scanning would miss documented curve/surface/lattice soft bodies. |
| Emitter particles | `object.particle_systems`; each system's `point_cache.frame_start`, `settings.frame_start` and physics settings | Stored cache and emission starts are exposed. The API does not give an effective-start resolver for every mode. |
| Hair dynamics | System's `use_hair_dynamics`, `point_cache`, and its `cloth.point_cache` | Both stored cache starts are readable. Which determines the effective initialization boundary is not settled by these sources. |
| Rigid bodies | Walk `bpy.data.scenes`; `scene.rigidbody_world`, if present, then `point_cache.frame_start` | Scene-level start exposed. Individual rigid bodies do not each supply their own independent world start. |
| Fluid | Fluid modifier's `domain_settings`, if present, then `cache_frame_start`; also `cache_frame_offset` when interpreting loaded cache time | Stored bake start/offset exposed. This does not calculate all upstream guide/flow dependencies. |
| Dynamic Paint | DynamicPaint modifier → `canvas_settings.canvas_surfaces`; read **every** surface's `frame_start`, not just an active surface | Per-surface start exposed. A canvas can hold independent surfaces. |
| Geometry Nodes | Geometry Nodes modifier's `bakes`; distinguish Simulation Output nodes from ordinary Bake nodes using each entry's `node`; read the custom-range toggle/start and relevant scene settings | Strong API support for potential bake enumeration, including nesting. Effective start follows the inferred override hierarchy in answer 2. Missing linked data can make `node` unavailable. No guarantee that a potential zone actually affects output. |
| iTaSC Simulation | `object.pose.ik_solver`, `object.pose.ik_param`, and the iTaSC `mode`/`reiteration_method` | Can identify configured stateful mode. No documented numeric start property or cache-history start reader found. |
| Ocean bake foam | Ocean modifier's `frame_start`, with its foam/bake settings | Bake start exposed. This is not a start for live-ocean history. |
| Custom handlers/drivers | Handler lists and driver expression/namespace are inspectable to the documented extent | No common start field; no documented automatic interpretation of arbitrary callback/function behavior. |
| EEVEE temporal / Cycles DLSS | Render/viewport settings identify the configured feature | No documented first timeline frame to read. Viewport DLSS is not a background final-render preroll requirement in the cited sources. |

Manual Scripting & Security explicitly documents `--enable-autoexec`/`--disable-autoexec` for background rendering. A scanner and renderer using different script policies may inspect/evaluate different behavior. This does not prevent ordinary data reads.
**Operating system.** No universal OS distinction for these semantic gaps is documented. External code/data/hardware availability can differ; the sources do not give an OS-by-OS first-frame detectability guarantee.

## 8. Can a script ask, for a whole file, whether anything in it depends on earlier frames, or from which frame the earliest of them begins?

**Label: not in the sources for a universal whole-file query; inferred for a bounded scanner of documented settings.** I found no documented function/property that answers either question for every kind. This is a finding about these supplied sources, not a claim that no custom analysis is possible.

A script can enumerate the loaded file's data and assemble a list of known candidates and readable starts. API [BlendData](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.BlendData.html) describes itself as “Main data structure representing a .blend file and all its data-blocks”. Its `all_ids` is a “Read-only list of all IDs listed in Blender data-base. Warning: Order is not guaranteed and should be considered an internal implementation detail”. It also exposes `objects`, `scenes`, `node_groups` and `libraries`. API [Data Access](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.data.html) provides examples iterating `bpy.data.objects` and reading `bpy.data.scenes.keys()`. These support enumeration, not history classification.

**Inferred bounded method.** Enumerate all loaded owners, identify the kinds in answer 1, read the fields in answers 2–3, follow the nesting/references in answer 6, and keep unresolved cases from answer 7 explicitly unresolved. For recognized owners whose effective starts are established in a common time coordinate, a script can take the minimum of those starts. This computes the earliest start **among those recognized owners**. It does not establish the earliest sufficient frame for the whole file when there is arbitrary code, missing data, iTaSC cache initialization, scene-strip retiming, or ambiguous scene contexts. Nor does finding a potential simulation prove that it influences the selected render.

“Whole file” also has two readings. All stored data includes unused groups and scenes; the dependencies of one rendered output are a smaller, evaluated selection. An inventory of the first can over-report the second. The supplied sources do not offer a universal semantic query for either reading.

**Nearby API candidates checked and ruled out.** Read **2026-11-08**:

- [Scene](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.Scene.html), `simulation_frame_start`: “Frame at which simulations start (in [-inf, inf], default 1)”. The accompanying `use_custom_simulation_range` restricts the feature to “simulation nodes that don’t override the frame range themselves”. This is a configurable scene setting, not a computed minimum over every file simulation or legacy cache.
- [BlendData](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.BlendData.html), `user_map()`: “Returns a mapping of all ID data-blocks in current bpy.data to a set of all data-blocks using them.” This maps users, not temporal requirements. `file_path_map()` maps IDs to paths, not original simulation starts.
- [Depsgraph](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.Depsgraph.html), `ids`: “All evaluated data-blocks”; `objects`: “Evaluated objects in the dependency graph”; `object_instances`: “All object instances to display or render”. `updates` is “Updates to data-blocks”. `id_type_updated()` returns “True if any data-block with this type was added, updated or removed”. These are evaluated contents/update information, not a history-dependence predicate or an earliest-frame report. `debug_stats()` says “Report the number of elements in the Dependency Graph”, not the requested temporal minimum.
- [DepsgraphUpdate](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.DepsgraphUpdate.html): “Information about ID that was updated”; its flags refer to geometry, shading and transformation updates. A changing object is not thereby established as requiring prior evaluation.
- [Scene](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.Scene.html), `frame_set()`: “Set scene frame updating all objects and view layers immediately”. It sets/evaluates a frame. It does not promise to discover or run all earlier frames required by every kind.
- [NodesModifier](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.NodesModifier.html), `bakes`: “All potential bakes, as defined by the assigned Geometry Nodes”. Useful for one family, not all-file history. [NodesModifierBake](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.NodesModifierBake.html) explicitly permits missing node references.
- [Ptcache operators](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.ops.ptcache.html), `bake_all()`: “Bake all physics simulations in the current scene”. This is a mutation scoped to a scene, not an all-file detection or minimum-start query. It does not replace the distinct fluid/node documentation.
- [Driver](https://docs.blender.org/api/5.3/bpy.types.Driver.html), `is_simple_expression`: “The scripted expression can be evaluated without using the full Python interpreter”. It says nothing about every program's historical inputs or an earliest frame.
- Release notes [Python API](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.3/python_api/) describe an all-IDs ordering change: “The property's order is also now documented as internal implementation detail (i.e. scripts should not rely or make assumptions on it).” This is enumeration support, not a temporal query. [Geometry Nodes release notes](https://developer.blender.org/docs/release_notes/5.3/geometry_nodes/) describe new grid nodes “with future custom fluid simulations in mind”; they do not supply a whole-file detector.

## Checks a person can settle by hand

These are proposed checks, not results. Use Blender 5.3 and record its exact build, OS, render engine, whether the run has a window, and every printed value. Use separate freshly reopened copies for cold and sequential comparisons; a fresh session with a saved bake is a different condition from an unbaked scene.

1. **Initialized start defaults.** In a factory file, add Cloth and Soft Body to separate meshes, an emitter particle system, a Hair particle system with Hair Dynamics, a Rigid Body World, a fluid domain, and a Dynamic Paint canvas. Before playback, inspect each relevant Cache/Settings panel. In the Python Console print each `point_cache.frame_start`, particle `settings.frame_start`, both hair cache starts, domain `cache_frame_start`, and every canvas surface `frame_start`. Record API metadata defaults separately from these actual initialized values. This settles the 0-versus-1 ambiguity for that build/setup.
2. **Particle and hair cache authority.** Use one emitter with different emission and cache starts, and one hair system whose two exposed cache starts differ if the interface/API permits it. Record the values before and after editing their UI Start controls. Reopen and compare simulation onset under Play Every Frame. Note whether one value changes the other, is overridden, or cannot be edited independently. Do not infer authority merely from matching defaults.
3. **Geometry Nodes range precedence and nesting.** Make a Simulation Zone that moves a point by a fixed amount each step. Place it inside a nested node group. Set scene playback start 1, custom scene simulation start 10, and per-node custom start 20 in three separate variants with the relevant toggles enabled/disabled. Print every modifier bake entry's node, ID, start and override toggle **before playback**. Repeat with the nested group used twice. Record when the point begins moving, which entries already exist, and how each use is represented.
4. **Cold versus sequential physics output.** For each of Cloth, Soft Body, Newtonian particles, Boids, SPH particles, dynamic hair, rigid bodies, fluid, Dynamic Paint Paint/Waves/incremental Displace/evolving Weight, and Geometry Nodes, make a small visibly evolving unbaked example. In one reopened copy render frame 30 directly. In another, use Play Every Frame from its documented/configured start through 30, then render 30. Save both images and compare positions/shapes/surface values. For fluid test Replay separately from Modular/All. Report the exact difference instead of naming an assumed rest-state fallback.
5. **Background counterpart.** Save one small example from check 4. Run a foreground terminal invocation of Blender with `--background`, loading the file and running a script that first prints its settings, then calls `scene.frame_set(f)` for each integer from the start through 30, then renders 30. Compare with a separate direct-frame background render and the UI result. Record script execution policy. This tests the unproven equivalence between UI playback and scripted stepping without changing the research's results retroactively.
6. **iTaSC initialization.** Build a two-bone IK chain with a moving target, select iTaSC Simulation, and inspect all displayed solver controls. For Never, Initial and Always, compare a fresh jump to frame 30 against playback from frame 1 through 30. Record the initial pose and later target tracking, and check whether any Python-readable cache-start number exists in the live RNA beyond the documented fields. Also record the actual newly selected mode/reiteration values to resolve the manual/API default disagreement.
7. **Indirect sources and governing scene.** Put the simple zone from check 3 in a collection instance, a linked collection, a recursive background scene, a scene selected by a compositor Render Layers node in a non-active scene effect, and a Scene strip inside a Meta strip. Give source and output scenes distinct playback/simulation starts. Print the references and source range fields before playback. Compare cold/sequential outputs and identify which scene's enabled range is used in each case. For Scene strips record source-to-output timing and retiming rather than assuming equal frame numbers.
8. **Cross-simulation start sufficiency.** Let a rigid body collide with cloth, with the rigid world starting before the cloth cache. Compare cloth rendered after stepping only from the cloth start with a fresh run stepping from the rigid-world start. Keep all settings fixed. This can show whether a local start alone is sufficient for that particular coupled example.
9. **Ocean bake-history scope.** Enable visible ocean foam. Compare live rendering of frame 30 after a direct jump and after stepping from frame 1. Then bake foam from frame 1 through 30 and reopen the file before rendering 30. Record which differences come from baking and whether earlier session evaluation changes live output.
10. **Program-state detectability.** In a disposable test file, register one frame-change callback that increases an existing object's position on each call and another that sets position solely from `scene.frame_current`. Print handler lists, driver expressions and registered text before rendering; compare direct frame 30 with stepping 1–30. This demonstrates the difference between finding a callback and identifying its history semantics; it does not prove a universal analyzer impossible.
11. **Temporal image history.** On supported hardware, enable EEVEE Temporal Accumulation and, separately, Cycles viewport DLSS. Compare the first displayed viewport image after a reset with several subsequent updates, first with a fixed timeline frame and then during playback. Separately compare fresh final still renders. Record whether the visible reuse belongs to viewport updates, timeline history, or final-render samples; do not transfer one conclusion to the others.
