Welcome. This is the front door to the Smoozy v2 project. Whether you are a human or an AI agent, read this file first.

## What this project is

Smoozy turns a rented bare-metal GPU server into a personal Blender render farm — automating the setup, file upload, rendering, and download, so a 3D artist never has to touch a terminal.

## The three folders

- **Preparation/** — all planning documents. No code.
- **App/** — the application code, produced during the Build stage.
- **Audits/** — the audit trails (audits, verdicts, decisions records), which keep growing for the life of the project.

Rule: **planning documents live in Preparation, code lives in App, audit trails live in Audits.**

Inside those folders, the app is split into **modules** — self-contained parts (like File Browser or Rendering, plus shared plumbing they all use). A module's plans are spread across the stage folders in Preparation (its spec, its design, its architecture); its code sits in App, its trail in Audits. This is what makes Smoozy maintainable: you add, change, or remove one module without disturbing the rest. Not everything is module-shaped, though — each stage can also hold a `Whole app` document for what spans the whole product.

The planned app is built as one pass through the stages, and a later addition is a new module sent through those same stages. But the order is a strong default, not a cage: when a later stage exposes a problem in an earlier one — Design revealing a hole in the Specs, Build revealing a flawed Architecture — go back, fix the earlier document, record why, and re-check whatever depended on it. **Protect the product, not the process.**

**One direction is now closed, and only one.** From 2026-08-23, **Design conforms to Architecture rather than the other way round** — [Rule 10](Rules.md). Where a design and an architecture answer disagree, the design changes or the thing is dropped; it does not go back up as a hole and it does not reopen an answered row. Everything above still holds for every other pair of stages.

That is a *hole* — the plan was already broken. It is not the same as a *want*, which is a good idea arriving out of nowhere while the work is underway. The two feel identical in the moment and are handled completely differently, so the difference is worth knowing cold: Rule 7 sets out both paths.

## The six stages

The project moves through six stages, in order:

1. **Groundwork** — names, rules, folders, conventions.
2. **Specs** — plain English: *what* the app does. Not one word about how.
3. **Blender Research** — the true facts about Blender needed for this app. Blender's official documentation is the preferred source, but a fact may also come from the Creator or a controlled test — always labelled with which (see Rule 2). Comes after Specs, because Specs tell us what to research.
4. **Design** — what the app looks like and how it feels to use: the screens, the layout, the visual language, the finer interactions. Comes after Blender Research (so it can expose Blender's real options) and before Architecture.
5. **Architecture** — *how* the app does what the Specs describe. Comes after Design, because the technical structure must be built to serve the intended experience, not the other way round.
6. **Build** — making the actual app. Starts with a plan (what to construct, in what order), then the construction itself.

A stage is not ticked complete until its **seams** have been checked — the places where its documents have to agree with each other. A document can be sound on its own and still contradict its neighbour, and a contradiction carried into the next stage gets copied into everything built on it. **Scenarios** — one person doing one piece of real work, played through the documents from start to finish — are checked at the same point, where the stage has any. [Auditing](Auditing.md) sets out how each is checked, and which stages can have scenarios at all; which of them are worth a round, and when, is the Creator's call.

## When the Creator asks for a job, you are the one doing it

Six of this project's jobs are started by asking for them, in whatever words come naturally. *(Four until 2026-08-31, when the Build stage opened and added its own; five until 2026-09-06, when the Creator asked for releases to work the same way.)* **"Let's research Blender", or "let's carry on with the File output nodes", or anything close to either, makes you the researcher-runner for a topic — starting a new one or picking up one already under way.** **"Let's audit the Servers spec", or anything close to it, makes you the runner for that round.** **"Let's carry on with architecture", or anything close to it, makes you the architecture runner.** **"Let's do design", or anything close to it, makes you the design agent. And "let's build", or anything close to it, makes you the build runner. And **"let's build a new version", or anything close, makes you the release runner** — a different job from the one before it, and the difference is that a release goes out to the world.** Three come with a filled-out brief — [Researcher Runner](Templates/Researcher%20Runner.md), [Runner](Templates/Runner.md) and [Release Runner](Templates/Release%20Runner.md) — and the brief is for you. **The release one is deliberately the shortest brief in this project, because everything a release does has to already work; a release is not the moment to be deciding anything.** The other three have no brief. Architecture's instructions are the [Architecture method](../Architecture/Method.md), so read that and start where it says, with its board. **Build's are the [Build method](../Build/Method.md) and [the Plan](../Build/Plan.md), and its board is `Audits/Build/Progress.md`** — read the board first, then the method, then the plan, and start where the board says. Design's are the [Design method](../Design/Method.md) and its board — `Audits/Design/Progress.md` — which says where the stage is, what the next action is, and what is settled that must not be re-opened. Read the board first and start there; the Creator should never have to brief you.

**A fifth job was added on 2026-08-24, and it is the one Design is now on.** *"Let's audit design"*, or *"carry on with the design audits"*, or anything close, makes you the runner for the **mockup seams** — each Design document read against the part of the drawing that draws it, twelve rounds of them. That job has a brief and it is [Design Mockup Seam Runner](Templates/Design%20Mockup%20Seam%20Runner.md); open it and follow it as your own instructions. **It is the one job in this project that runs to the end without stopping for the Creator**, on their ruling of that date, so do not build in a gate and do not report between rounds. The board still says which rounds are closed.

So open it and follow the block inside as your own instructions, exactly as if it had been pasted into a fresh chat. Do not hand the brief back for the Creator to paste somewhere else, and do not answer with what the job would involve. All of them start by working out what to do first — which topic, which Subject, which row, which screen, which build step — so begin there and say your pick.

If two jobs could fit what was asked, ask which. Ask it as the person already doing the work, not as a question about whether to start.

**None of this ever makes you a researcher.** That role is a fresh chat holding a brief, a question sheet and `Blender Sources/` and nothing else, and an agent that has read any part of this project can never take it — the guesses in these documents would come back as findings and nobody downstream could tell the difference. The researcher-runner writes the questions, briefs a researcher, and files what comes back. It answers nothing itself, and this binds hardest once it has read the register and knows what the project hopes the answers are.
