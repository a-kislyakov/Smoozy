# Sources — Queue & History

*Where the outside-world facts in the [Queue & History spec](../Queue%20&%20History.md) came from. The spec carries each fact's label in place; this file carries the citation behind it, and what that citation does not cover.*

Every Blender entry below points at the comparison document for its topic, by question number — `Preparation/Blender Research/Comparison/Frames.md`, `.../Reports.md`, `.../Images.md`, `.../Reading a file.md`, `.../Settings.md`, `.../The machine.md` and `.../What actually ran.md`. That is the whole citation: the comparison says what every version does, question by question, and has been checked against the findings behind it. Read 2026-08-06, and every entry read against its comparison again on 2026-08-12. Two pointers into the research's own audit trail are left standing — at the repair of the record behind the render-start entry, and at the one sample-count record that does not fit — each because what it names is held in no comparison.

**One standing limit on all of them.** Smoozy renders on rented Linux machines. Several answers below rest on the Creator's Mac alone, and several on one rented Linux machine alone; each says which. A Mac-only result does not settle what a rented machine does, and it is marked rather than smoothed over.

## Still open

- **A queue put on a rented machine keeps going with nobody connected, and comes back after a reboot** — that what Smoozy puts on the machine goes on rendering with no connection to it, starts again by itself after a reboot or a crash, and reads back the line of work it was holding. Nobody in this project established this: no source was consulted, and it did not come from the Creator. Label when this entry was raised: **unresolved** — agent-raised on 2026-08-03; see the block below for where it stands now. **What breaks if it is wrong:** the whole of *When the machine goes down* — the surviving queue, the automatic re-run, and the giving-up rule that follows a second interruption — together with the promise that nothing in the queue depends on the user being connected or on Smoozy being open. That promise is the module's foundation rather than one of its features, so a negative answer is a hole in the spec rather than a narrowing of it. It is not a Blender question, and no Blender research touches it. Parked for Architecture in [Open Threads](../../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), with the other rented-machine assumptions. **The question is asked as *can a rented machine do this at all*, and not as *can it do this with no room left on the disk*** — which is the condition under which writing anything down is most likely to fail, and the condition a machine whose disk filled during a render is sitting in. Whatever Smoozy puts on the machine has to keep its line of work somewhere. If that line cannot be read back after the disk fills, the re-run after an interruption and the giving-up rule that follows a second one go with it.

  **ANSWERED 2026-08-18, on rented machines of both kinds — and the second question this entry insists on came back no.** Label: **settled at Architecture**. Nobody's documentation says this; the project established it itself, on two rented machines of different kinds, and the record is `Preparation/Architecture/The last two boxes — the hook that cannot be read back, the full disk, and the unit that kills a render.md` §1 and §15.1, carried as Fact 1 in `Preparation/Architecture/Facts.md`. What Smoozy leaves on a machine outlives the connection that started it, holds and advances the line with nobody connected, hands that line back on a fresh connection, and comes back by itself after a reboot — **and after a crash as well, which is more than this entry asked for**: the machine was made to fall over at the deepest level, and what came back still held the job that was held, the job that was running at its second attempt, and the place in the line each had reached. **The disk-full form is no, and it is the heaviest negative of that work.** With no room left every write is refused loudly and truthfully — nothing is quietly accepted and nothing moves behind the refusal — and every read still answers, free space included. But what Smoozy leaves there cannot *start* on a full disk, because its first act is to write something down. **On one of the two kinds it picks itself up the moment room returns; on the other it stays down until it is started again** — and that difference is made by a setting Smoozy's own setup writes onto the machine rather than by anything about the machine, which the maker's own manual for that setting confirms (read 2026-08-20; the exact reference is in the architecture record). **What this does not settle:** the act that gets such a machine going again, which belongs to the setting-up side of Smoozy rather than to this module and is parked in [Open Threads](../../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md). And one loss is named rather than smoothed over: while it is down nothing is written about that machine at all, its own death included, so what the user meets is Smoozy saying it could not check that machine — never a guessed line. *(Agent-raised 2026-08-03; parked for Architecture; answered there.)*
- **A machine can be read for the note it keeps against a job** — that on a later connection Smoozy can read back what the machine recorded about a job: that it finished, that it failed and why, that it was interrupted and re-run and from which frame, that it was interrupted twice and given up, and the times the render started and ended. Nobody in this project established it: no source was consulted, and it did not come from the Creator. Label when this entry was raised: **unresolved** — agent-raised on 2026-08-08; see the block below for where it stands now. Not a Blender question — it is the same ability as reading a machine for the work sitting on it. **What breaks if it is wrong:** every ending Smoozy learns on a connection rather than seeing for itself, which is every job that ended while the user was away; the reason a job failed, which is kept on the machine and read from there; the catch-up notice, which would have nothing to name; the times a history entry shows, since a job's start and end are read off the same note; and the half-hour idle wait counted from the moment a machine actually went quiet, which needs the end time. This is the ordinary case rather than an edge, because the user is not watching. **Already parked** for Architecture in [Open Threads](../../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), as whether a machine can be read for the work left sitting on it — the same ability, and nothing new is parked for it. **The question is asked as *can a rented machine do this at all*, and not as *can it do this with no room left on the disk*** — which is the condition under which writing anything down is most likely to fail, and the one a job killed by a full disk ends in. The user's whole account of what happened comes off that note, so if it could not be written they are left with a job carrying **no ending Smoozy ever learned** — the description this spec reserves for a machine that was destroyed, not one sitting connected in front of them.

  **ANSWERED IN FULL 2026-08-18, on rented machines of both kinds, including the disk-full form.** Label: **settled at Architecture**. Established by the project itself on two rented machines rather than read anywhere — recorded at `Preparation/Architecture/The last two boxes — the hook that cannot be read back, the full disk, and the unit that kills a render.md` §15.1, carried as Fact 40 in `Preparation/Architecture/Facts.md`. Over the connection, on both machines, the note answered for each job: how many attempts it had had; how it ended, with the ending's own detail; whether it had been re-run and from which frame; its label; and its times — **and where a job had not ended it said so plainly rather than inventing an ending**. The line of waiting and running work answered in the same breath, job by job. **The disk-full form — the one this entry says is the one that matters — came back yes for reading:** with the disk at a genuine zero every read still answered normally and truthfully, before the refused writes and again after them, and no read reported success from nothing. **What it does not settle, and it is a real limit the spec now carries:** writing. While the disk is completely full the note cannot be written to at all, so a job that ends in that window leaves the note still showing no ending until there is room again — the note waits rather than guessing, and catches up once a write can go through. *(Agent-raised 2026-08-08; parked for Architecture; answered there.)*
- **The machine's own copy of a history entry can be removed over a connection** — that Smoozy can reach onto a connected machine and take off what it holds about a job the user has deleted. The neighbouring ability, *reading* that note, is the entry above; taking something off it is a separate thing, and nobody in this project established it: no source was consulted, and it did not come from the Creator. Label when this entry was raised: **unresolved** — agent-raised on 2026-08-09; see the block below for where it stands now. Not a Blender question — it is about driving a rented machine. **What breaks if it is wrong:** the promise that deleting reaches both copies, and with it the guarantee that a deleted entry does not reappear on a later connection. Parked for Architecture in [Open Threads](../../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), with the other rented-machine assumptions.

  **ANSWERED IN PART 2026-08-18, on rented machines of both kinds — the record goes and the bytes stay.** Label: **settled at Architecture for what the machine afterwards reports, and settled but not yet observed for the rest**. Established by the project itself on two rented machines — recorded at `Preparation/Architecture/The last two boxes — the hook that cannot be read back, the full disk, and the unit that kills a render.md` §15.2, carried as Fact 41 in `Preparation/Architecture/Facts.md`. **What went**, on both machines independently, with no other job disturbed: the machine's own history entry for the job, the note it kept against it, the mark that its outputs had been collected, and its place in the line. **What did not go:** the words captured while it rendered, the frame it had rendered, and the material the run was set up from — all still on the disk under the deleted job's own name, fully readable, while the machine reported nothing about them. The frame staying is right and always was, since the frames are the user's. The rest staying is the shortfall, and Smoozy's answer to it — naming every part the removal owes before it takes the first one off, and carrying what it could not take as a debt against that machine until a later connection pays it — **is written down and has not yet been watched through to the end on a real machine** (one removal per machine, not repeated). **What breaks if it under-reaches:** record material left sitting on a machine the user pays for after they deleted the job, with the machine saying nothing about it — which is exactly the shape that was observed. **What holds either way:** the half on the user's own computer, unconditionally — the entry is gone, it is never filled back in from a machine, and the debt is recorded and retried. *(Agent-raised 2026-08-09; parked for Architecture; answered in part there.)*
- **A job's label can be carried in the name of its output folder** — that whatever a user types as a label can become part of a folder name on a rented machine. No source was consulted. Label: **unresolved**. Not a Blender question — it is about what a machine's own file naming takes. **What breaks if it is wrong:** the folder naming itself, and with it the guarantee that no job ever writes over another, since that guarantee rests on the folder's name being made from the job's identity and its label. Already parked for Architecture in [Open Threads](../../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), which asks what happens to a label holding characters a folder name cannot take, and to two jobs given the same label. Nothing new is parked for it.
- **A rented machine bills by the hour** — that a rented machine is charged for in whole hours whether it is rendering or not. No source was consulted, and it did not come from the Creator. **ANSWERED 2026-08-21, and it went the way this entry predicted.** Label: **documented**. Vast.ai, *Billing*, `https://docs.vast.ai/guides/reference/billing`, read 2026-08-20: *"You are charged the base active rental cost for every second your instance is in the active/connected state."*, *"You are charged the storage cost … for every single second your instance exists and is online (for all states other than offline)."*, and *"There are separate prices and charges for: Active rental (GPU) (in $/hr), Storage costs (in $/GB/hr), Bandwidth costs (in $/TB)."* **So the rate is quoted per hour and the charge accrues per second** — an hourly rate on a listing was read as hourly billing. **This settles Vast.ai and nothing else** — the project has also rented from Sesterce and Hyperstack and neither was read, so what is refuted is the **blanket** form, *a rented machine bills by the hour* as a fact about rentals in general. **What this entry foresaw, word for word, is what happened:** *"What breaks if it is wrong: nothing about the feature. … What goes wrong is the stated reason."* The half-hour idle message stands — a machine doing nothing costs money at any granularity — and the spec's sentence has been corrected. *(Agent-raised 2026-08-03; parked for Architecture; closed there.)*
- **A whole job's total can be worked out before the job runs.** **The method is settled and the fact underneath it is not.** On 2026-08-13 the Creator ruled how both progress bars are counted: a renderlet's size is its tiles multiplied by its samples, and a job's total is that sum over every frame-and-view-layer pairing it will render, honouring whichever sample count is in force. Label for the method: **Creator-stated**. **What it still needs** is knowing which view layers are active on each frame before the job starts. **Reading a file 3** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Reading%20a%20file.md) — settles that a file can be read for whether each view layer renders at a named frame, including where that has been keyframed, in all four versions on both machines. **Not covered:** a switch driven rather than keyframed, and a script inside the file changing it as each frame comes round; neither was tested in any version. Label for that half: **unresolved**. **What breaks if it comes back badly:** the whole-job total is wrong for a file of either kind, in a way the user sees — a bar that runs past its end or stalls short of it. **It is not weakened into silence:** the spec now says outright that a total is a plan rather than a promise, which it has to be anyway, since a frame with adaptive sampling on can finish well short of its sample count (**Reports 11**). The [Render Settings spec](../Render%20Settings.md) owns the driven-and-scripted gap, and it is parked in [Open Threads](../../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md) for **Architecture**, which builds the read — deliberately not sent back to Blender Research, whose stage is closed. *(Creator-ruled 2026-08-13.)*
- **Whether the diffuse colour pass shows that a scene's textures loaded.** Label: **unresolved** — Images 2 says only the finished picture was ever looked at for the substituted colour, and no separate pass was, in any version or on either machine. The [Glossary](../../Groundwork/Glossary.md) states the claim as Creator-stated and still owns it. **What breaks if it is wrong:** only the preview's second choice. The first choice carries the promise on its own, so a negative answer removes a fallback rather than a feature.

  **It is worth less than it was, though the question is unchanged.** **Images 9**, answered on 2026-08-09, settles that even the *finished picture* only shows a missing texture that was feeding colour — a missing bump, displacement or normal map leaves no mark that reads as a failure, and a missing transparency map hands out the fully solid end, which is what a measured value implies rather than something any test rendered — though two of them change the picture a great deal. So the thing this rung exists to do is narrower than the rung was written for, whatever the answer here turns out to be. The rung stays: a picture of what the surfaces are coloured with is still worth more than the last utility pass a job happened to write.
## Answered by the Blender research

- **A render cannot be told to stop once the frame it is working on is finished.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 11, in every version, on the Creator's Mac and on a rented Linux machine. No version offers such a control, inside Blender's own window or outside it, and nothing is written for the frame the render was on, by any of the three ways of stopping, **where the stop lands while that frame is being rendered**. Does not cover a stop aimed at the moment the frame's own file appears: Frames 11 hands that to **Frames 13**, and the entry below on what each way of ending leaves of the last image carries it. **This is the answer the spec was waiting on, and it came back no.** The *stop after the frame it is on* action has therefore been removed, which is what the earlier entry here said would happen: there is nothing to weaken it to, since a stop that cannot wait for the frame is the ordinary stop the spec already offers. The [Telegram spec](../Telegram.md) and the [Rendering spec](../Rendering.md) each carry the same ability under the same label and are not this document's to change. Does not cover **how long** a stop takes to take effect: Frames 11 measures that on the Creator's Mac, and says plainly that its own Linux figures are not offered as a measurement because that machine was shared with other work — a Linux machine with nothing else running has never been asked — so nothing here rests on it.
- **An interrupted render cannot be picked up from where it stopped.** Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — Frames 1, 2 and 3, the same in every version, on the Creator's Mac and on a rented Linux machine. **The Creator stated this on 2026-07-21 and the research agrees with them**, so nothing was overturned. Does not cover a machine losing power outright — see the next entry.
- **What a machine losing power outright leaves behind.** Label: **unresolved** — Frames 1 records a real power cut as tried on no computer and answered in no version. It could not be run on the Creator's Mac, which they declined, and the power of a rented machine cannot be cut over the network. **The nearest reachable case has been run, and it is not a power cut**: a reset at the kernel, in all four versions on one rented Linux machine, left no frame's contents at all — including a frame reported saved seconds earlier — and which directory entries survived at all differed between two runs of the same test. Label for that: **observed in a test** — **Frames 13**, which Frames 1 points at for it. **What breaks if the power cut itself comes back badly:** the re-run's skipping, which reads a file's bare presence. A power cut is the case the automatic re-run exists for. It is no longer the only way a re-run can meet a file that is present and not whole — **Frames 12** puts a short file at the finished frame's own name after a polite close or an outright kill, and the entry below records that — but it is still the one nobody has answered. It goes on the list of things a hand test could settle, and needs a machine whose power can be cut.
- **A re-run can be asked for only the frames that are not there.** Label: **documented** that the ability exists, **observed in a test** for how it decides — Frames 4, the same in every version, on the Creator's Mac and on a rented Linux machine. The decision is bare existence of a file at the place the frame would be written, and it is made frame by frame as the render reaches it. **The Creator stated this on 2026-07-21 and the research agrees with them.** **Corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired: this said a folder written to over a network was tried on neither machine, and it was tried.** *Frames 4* now points at another question that ran it — **and it changed the answer.** What the new answer is was not in the reading this correction was given, so **the false sentence is withdrawn and no replacement is invented**; somebody has to go and read that question. **What breaks meanwhile:** nothing Smoozy builds — no promise here rests on a network folder, and the re-run's decision is unchanged on an ordinary disk — but anybody reasoning about a network folder from this file must not take this entry's silence for an absence of evidence.
- **A file existing is not proof that the frame finished writing.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 6, the same in every version, on both machines. Two separate reasons, both recorded there. This confirms what the spec had been carrying as an open worry since round 1, and it is why the re-run cannot simply trust what it finds.
- **Two renders writing into one folder can take the same frame.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 4. **Corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired.** This said the comparison keeps two records that disagree and chooses neither — the Mac seeing the same frame rendered twice in three of the four versions, and **twelve runs on a rented Linux machine never colliding**. **The Linux half is no longer the record.** Another question ran the same arrangement harder and **found collisions on Linux, including in the version that had not collided before.** So the two records do not disagree after all: it is a race, on both machines, and the earlier Linux run simply did not lose it. **The conclusion below is unchanged and is now better supported, not weaker.** One collision is enough to show that an empty file put down first reserves nothing. Does not cover any arrangement Smoozy actually has, since a machine renders one job at a time; it is recorded because it is what would break if that changed.
- **An ended render leaves no file at all for the frame it was on.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 3, 6 and 10, in every version, on both machines, by every way of ending a render that was tried, **where the stop lands while that frame is being rendered**. Does not cover the power-cut case above, nor a stop aimed at the moment the frame's own file appears, which all three of those questions hand to **Frames 13**.
- **What an animation written as one video file leaves behind.** Label: **documented** for the warning, **observed in a test** for what the file actually holds — Frames 5, the same in every version, on both machines. Ended in an orderly way it holds the frames finished before the stop and opens; killed outright it holds nothing. **This is the question the spec parked and could not answer, and it is now answered.** Does not cover what any media player makes of the leftover file: Frames 5 settles that for one player on macOS alone.
- **Skipping what is already there has no effect on a video output.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 4, in every version, on both machines. This is what makes a video job's re-run a full re-render.
- **A long video render is split across more than one file, and a file already closed survives an interruption.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 5, in every version. **It rests on one rented Linux machine only**: Frames 5 says the Creator's Mac never reached the size at which the split happens, so nothing about it is established for macOS. That is the right way round for Smoozy, which renders on rented Linux machines, but it is one machine and it has since been destroyed. Does not cover how much of the second, unfinished file survives — Frames 5 keeps two runs that disagree on it and chooses neither.
- **Blender can be given a scattered set of frames rather than a plain run.** Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — Frames 7 and 8, the same in every version, on both machines. The spec leans on this only for the existence of the ability; the exact words are Architecture's. Does not cover what happens with nothing selected in one particular case Frames 7 names, which bears on nothing here.
- **What Blender reports while it renders.** Label: **observed in a test** — Reports 3 and 5, in every version, on the Creator's Mac and on a rented Linux machine, which agreed. It says which frame it is on and how far through that frame it has got in samples; it gives no figure covering a whole animation; its estimate of the time left covers the frame being rendered and nothing wider, is not on every line, and is absent altogether under EEVEE; and its report can be read as it arrives rather than in one lump at the end. **Three parts of the Creator's 2026-07-24 statement did not survive.** *Tiles* are nowhere in **those two questions**, whose progress lines all came from pictures too small to be split; **Reports 7** answered them later and the entry below carries it. *That the estimate is often well off* is not established anywhere. And *that Blender reports its own memory use on the graphics card* is contradicted — the entry below.
- **Blender's running report is not written the same way in every version.** Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — Reports 2, 3, 4 and 5, with the change falling at version 5.0 and Blender's own release notes for that version warning that render farms reading this output may need changing. **One consequence lands on the spec rather than on Architecture:** from 5.0 the progress line no longer carries the view layer beside the frame, so what an entry can name from that line is the frame alone unless it is 4.5. Does not cover how the reading is done, which is Architecture's.
- **Blender reports nothing marked as the graphics card's memory.** Label: **observed in a test** for the window readout, **not established** for what a machine with several cards prints. **One limit found by the cross-sweep on 2026-08-11, and it narrows the claim.** The runs behind this on the machines with several cards kept **one line of each render** — Blender's closing `Finished` line — and threw everything else away before anyone read it (**Reports 6** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Reports.md), which states that the closing `Finished` line was the only line kept from the several-card runs and that what the rest of them printed is not known). So a memory figure naming a card could have been printed on some other line and gone unread, and the topic's own wording — one figure, never several, no card named beside a memory number — cannot stand on that evidence. What survives is that no line the project kept carries such a figure. The spec has been reworded to say that and no more. Reports 6, in every version, on all four machines the research reached, with one card and with four. Its one such readout lives in Blender's own window, is off until switched on, and Reports 6 says outright that what its first figure counts is not established. **This contradicts something the Creator stated**, and under the research-wins rule the research stands. **The clash, written out rather than overwritten:** on 2026-07-24 the Creator stated that Blender reports its own memory use on the graphics card, and that a rendering entry should show that figure beside the whole card's, marked as Blender's. The research finds there is no such figure to show. The second figure has been removed from the spec; the headline, which reads the whole card from the machine, is untouched and was never Blender's. **What this costs:** nothing the spec promised the user, since the headline was always the one chosen to predict the failure. What it removes is the comparison between the two, which can no longer be offered.
- **A full graphics card ends a job in one of two ways, and which one turns on who filled the card.** Label: **observed in a test** — **Reports 2 and 6**. **(1)** A card filled by **another program** stops the render outright, says so plainly and ends as a failure — the loud case Blender's own documentation names, run in Blender 4.5 and 5.2 on the four-card machine and **not established for 5.0 or 5.1**. **(2)** A render whose **own scene** outgrows the card fills it, spills into the machine's memory, writes its picture and reports success about ten times slower — all four versions, and reached **only** on the four-card machine, whose cards hold sixteen gigabytes against nearly two hundred of its own. **Not covered:** what a render that outgrows the card does on a machine with far less memory to spill into, which is the ordinary rented box — **Reports 6** says plainly that the out-of-memory message Blender's own manual names "was not reached" from a render's own scene, because that machine had 197 GB to spill into. Also not covered: the card readout with more than one card, any card that is neither Apple's nor NVIDIA's, and Windows.

- **A render taken away by the machine, which is not a full-card result and must not be filed as one.** Label: **observed in a test** — recorded at `Preparation/Architecture/The rented machine.md`, not in any comparison. One frame of thirty thousand by thirty thousand, in Blender 4.5 and 5.2 on the 4090 machine, was **killed by the machine itself** during denoising having taken sixty-five gigabytes of **ordinary** memory. That record says in as many words: "**Neither run reached the card's limit**" and "a frame large enough to fill the card exhausts the machine's 79 GB first". So nothing spilled off a full card; the frame was simply too big for the machine. It is cited here because it is what one rented machine of ordinary size did when a render asked for too much **during denoising**, and because Blender said nothing at all about that run — no message, no error, no file. Four further attempts on the same machine to fill the card from textures rather than frame size all rendered normally at exit 0, the card never holding more than 1,819 MB. **The neighbouring case — the machine's own memory running out while a frame is being sampled — is a comparison's, and is the entry below. The two are different moments and neither stands in for the other.**

- **What a render does when the machine's own memory runs out while it is sampling.** Label: **observed in a test** — **Reports 10** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Reports.md), run twice through in all four versions. **It differs by version.** Blender 4.5 and 5.0 are killed by the machine, with standard error empty and nothing printed about memory at all; Blender 5.1 and 5.2 refuse the allocation, put Blender's own words on standard error, and abort. In all four nothing is written, not even part of a picture, no crash file appears, and the last thing on the ordinary output is a sampling line — so anything watching that one stream learns nothing in either case. **What it bears on:** *When a render fails* keeps Blender's own words and nothing tidier, and in two of the four versions this ending leaves no words at all to keep. **Not covered:** macOS and Windows. Reports 10 was run on one rented Linux machine alone, the Creator's Mac not being a machine whose memory is filled on purpose.
- **No version of Blender gives a failure in a form a program can sort or act on.** Label: **observed in a test** — Reports 2, in every version, with a window and without. What there is to keep is Blender's own words. Does not cover which words, which differ by version and by machine — Reports 1 and 2 record several places where the wording follows the computer rather than Blender.
- **The finished picture is one named pass, and the name is the same in every version.** Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — Images 1, on the Creator's Mac and on a rented Linux machine. Blender calls it Combined. Does not cover which file it comes to out of everything a job wrote, which is [What a job writes](../What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s. Note for anyone reading the passes by name: Images 1 records that two other passes were renamed at version 5.0, so the diffuse colour pass does not carry one name across the versions the way Combined does.
- **No pass anywhere reports whether a texture loaded, and an empty image slot is the one case the engines differ on.** Label: **observed in a test** — Images 2, in every version, on both machines, and on every graphics card the research could reach. There is no pass to read that says a texture failed, which is why the preview leans on the picture itself; and where an image slot was left empty rather than pointing at a file that has gone, Cycles gives magenta, EEVEE black and Workbench plain grey. **What a failed texture does to the picture is no longer this spec's claim.** [What a job writes](../What%20a%20job%20writes.md) owns that fact and the three limits on it, and the citation behind it — including the untested case, a texture driving something other than colour — is in [that document's sources file](What%20a%20job%20writes.sources.md). The preview's promise here reaches no further than that.
- **A frame with several view layers written as an ordinary image gives one picture, holding the first view layer alone.** Label: **observed in a test** — Frames 9, in every version, on both machines. This is what narrows the preview's open question to the case of a format that holds several layers inside one file. Does not cover a render started from inside Blender's own window, which Frames 9 says was never tried, and which Smoozy never does.
- **A render can produce a beauty pass and utility passes, and they are different pictures.** Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — Images 1. The names live in the [Glossary](../../Groundwork/Glossary.md), and [What a job writes](../What%20a%20job%20writes.md) leans on this fact harder than this spec does — it uses it to find a job's main output, not only to pick a picture. This entry was Creator-stated (2026-07-24) and the research agrees with it.

- **Whether Blender reports how far through a frame it has got in tiles, and at what size the splitting starts.** Label: **observed in a test** — **Reports 7**, see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Reports.md), in all four versions on both machines. The progress line carries `Rendered N/M Tiles` beside the sample count once the frame is split, and the threshold is one tile's worth of area at the default tile of 2048 pixels square. The same line stands behind what **Settings 15** summarises as a picture "cut into 53,284 tiles" — see [that comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Settings.md).

  **Why Reports 3 missed it, which is worth keeping.** That topic's runs were all of pictures too small to be split — one tile — so no tile figure could appear, and its answer reads as though Blender never reports one. The figure was captured by a different topic asking a different question, and nobody joined the two up. **A finding of *never* drawn from one set of test conditions is only ever a finding about those conditions.** Raised by the Creator on 2026-08-11 from their own use of Blender, and confirmed against the hand tests.

  **A second witness, and Reports 7 holds it.** The same line came from a quite different run: 30000 by 30000 pixels, Cycles on an NVIDIA card, **Blender 5.2**, with the frame-splitting setting deliberately switched **off** — `Rendered 0/225 Tiles, Sample 1/1`. Both runs print the field in the same shape and the same place. So the line is not an artefact of the resolution-percentage trick — Reports 7 reached the threshold by rendering one frame at six sizes, with no percentage trick anywhere — and **in 5.2 it does not depend on splitting being asked for**. **That second run is one run, on one machine now destroyed, on one day, and it did not finish**, and 4.5, 5.0 and 5.1 were never run with the setting off for what they print, so nothing here says the same of them. All of it is in the same [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Reports.md), under question 7.

  **Not covered:** whether it is the picture's **area** or its **width** that crosses the threshold; and, where both figures are present, which of tiles and samples measures progress through the frame. **Both reasons given here were false and are corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired.** *Every size tried grew both sides at once* — one run went 2048 by 2048 to 2048 by 2049, growing one side only. *Every tiled run on record was single-sample* — **thirteen tiled lines are on record in full**, and the sample number restarts inside each tile against the scene's own undivided maximum. **Both questions are still open, and the first has a better reason to be:** five tile counts are recorded — 2, 4, 1024, 225 and 53,284 — and the obvious area arithmetic gives 977, 215 and 53,081 for three of them, so **nothing may work the count out; it is read from what Blender prints.** **What breaks if either comes back awkwardly:** one item on a rendering entry. Samples carry the same information for the same purpose.

## Added when the second and third rounds of Blender Research were carried in (2026-08-10)

- **How far the frame preview can be said to show that a job's textures loaded.** Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on both machines). **Images 9** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Images.md). **Only for a texture that was feeding colour.** The stand-in Blender uses for a picture it cannot find is handed out at the lookup itself, before anything downstream knows what the value will be used for, so it reaches a bump map or a mask as a number rather than as a visible colour. A missing bump map gives no bump — indistinguishable from nothing connected. A missing displacement map shrinks the object evenly with no relief. A missing normal map leaves the whole surface shaded as though tilted. A missing transparency map hands the fully solid end to whatever reads it, which is what a measured value implies rather than something any test rendered. In the compositor the substitute is black and in a geometry node setup zero, neither of them the visible colour, and a volume whose data file is gone draws nothing at all. **Two of these change the picture a great deal** — the displacement one moves the object's outline by hundreds of pixels — so what is missing is a mark that reads as a failure, not a mark. Every one finishes at a successful ending with the frame written and nothing on the error stream. **So this module's promise narrows**: the preview says the render happened and shows a missing texture that was feeding colour, and it does not say a job's textures loaded. The fuller statement and its limits are [What a job writes](../What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s. **Not covered:** Windows; a missing picture reached through an older kind of texture data-block; a render actually made with the value driving roughness or metallic; graphics cards of other makes.

- **Whether a finished job can say which parts of the machine did the work.** **Corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired: *a stopped render never reaches it* was reasoned from the timings of a run that finished, and no stopped render was ever run for it.** One has been now. **Two of the four versions print the whole closing report after a stop, one prints a cancelled line and no report, and one prints nothing at any level** — and in the runs that printed, the frame's own sampling had finished inside the wait, so **what a frame genuinely cut short prints is recorded nowhere.** **And the version that prints it publishes its own list of faults calling that report fake, with wrong numbers**, borne out by a run whose report claimed 600 samples for a frame the line above it called 599. **So a line found on a job that did not finish is evidence of nothing, and Smoozy reads it only from a job that finished.**

   Label: **observed; how you ask differs by version**. **What actually ran 1** for the line and the route to it, and **What actually ran 6** for when it can be learned — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/What%20actually%20ran.md). Yes, and only once the job is over: the line naming the device is a **closing report**, printed after the last sample and before the frame is written, so a running entry cannot show it and a job stopped part-way never produces it — **established on the Linux machine alone**, while the route to the line was run on both. Every device-naming result in that question is Cycles' own path-tracing report; nothing establishes that an EEVEE or Workbench job names anything. Blender 4.5 reaches the line by one route and the newer three by another. The settings read back afterwards **do not answer it and can be shown to name a card while the processor did the work**, in every version on both machines, so nothing may be guessed from them. **Not covered:** whether a card running out of memory announces the move into the machine's own memory; Windows; a card of another make; a machine with two cards.

- **What each way of ending a render leaves of the last image.** Label: **observed; three sets of runs disagree and none is preferred**. **Frames 12** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Frames.md). **This overturned what this file's re-run section leaned on.** It had read that every way of ending a render leaves nothing at all for the frame in flight, so the frames a re-run finds are the frames that finished. In the largest set — each of three signals sent eighty times in all, ten in each version on each machine, within about a thousandth of a second of the image file appearing — **an interrupt left the last image whole every time, and a polite request to close and an outright kill each left it short every time**, every short file on Linux being exactly zero bytes. Two smaller earlier sets found them mostly whole. All three stand, none has been chosen between, and **the disagreement is between the runs and not between the versions** — in the largest of the three sets all four behaved identically, while the smallest does split by version, which is part of why none has been preferred. **Frames 11 also says the frame in flight is always discarded by every means of stopping, with nothing written for it whole or partial — but only where the stop lands while that frame is being rendered**, which is not the moment the short files above came from: both Frames 11 and Frames 12 say in as many words that a stop aimed at the instant the frame's own file appears is a separate case, and hand it to Frames 13. So a zero-byte file at exactly the finished frame's own name is a real thing a re-run can meet, and it is *present*, which is what the already-there test reads. **Not covered:** what pressing Esc inside Blender's own window leaves, and Windows.

- **What an interruption leaves of a video output, and what its size says about progress.** Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on both machines; the splitting on a rented Linux machine only). **Frames 12**. The file is created within moments of the render starting at a token size and **its reported size does not move while the render runs**, reaching its real size only when the file is closed — at the end, or at a split. So watching it says nothing at all about how far the job has got. This rests on **one container and one codec** and on one scene at one frame rate, and a size that does not move is not the same as nothing being written: what waits for the close is the size the file reports. **How many frames the reader finds in an interrupted file is not a fixed number** and the comparison says plainly it should not be relied on. **Not covered:** the splitting and the second-run replacement on macOS, what pressing Esc inside Blender's own window leaves, and Windows. An **interrupt** leaves a file holding the frames that were finished and fires Blender's own cancelled-render notification; a **polite request to close** and an **outright kill** each leave the token size with nothing in it, and the polite one fires nothing at all. A file already split away survives whole. A second attempt replaces what was left rather than adding to it. And **one render's own output cannot be a video and a set of images at once**, which closes a premise two versions' earlier readings had rested on with nothing behind it.

- **Whether a render survives the connection it was started from being lost.** Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on both machines). **The machine 7** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/The%20machine.md). It does: the connection was cut, the shell killed, and a hang-up sent to the whole group of programs the render belonged to, and in every case the render carried on and frames kept appearing. So none of *When the machine goes down* fires for a lost connection — there is nothing to re-run. **Not covered:** what a whole machine going to sleep does, and Windows.

- **What a render does when a cache or a bake the file records is absent.** Owned and cited in full by the [Dependency Upload & Remap sources](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.sources.md). Listed here because it is another shape of the same thing this module's entry marks exist for: the render works the simulation out again, says nothing, finishes at success, and gives a picture that is not the baked one, so a job can come home looking perfectly ordinary and be wrong. *(Corrected 2026-10-02, in the owning file: at the desk it was often not worked out again — a geometry-nodes simulation never baked stays on its first frame, a physics cache marked baked with its files missing leaves the object unmoving, and a missing Bake node folder leaves the baked object out. The harm is the same: silent, a success, and wrong.)*

## Added from the cross-sweep of the Blender Research (2026-08-11)

The sweep read the fifteen checked comparisons against each other — a check nobody had made, since every earlier check reads one topic against itself. The three of its findings that land here have since been taken into the comparisons, so the entries below cite them there, by topic and question number, as every other entry in this file does. Two more amend the entries above rather than adding new ones.

- **How the render was started decides whether Blender prints any progress at all.** Label: **observed in a test** — **Reports 9** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Reports.md). Two ways of starting one render were compared, on both machines and in all four versions. **The fact's home is the [Rendering spec](../Rendering.md)**, which owns how a render is started; it is cited here because everything this module says a rendering entry shows rests on it. A render started the reporting way prints its full progress in all four versions; started the other way, Blender 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 print **no progress line at all** — no samples, no tiles, no stages — while still printing the line naming the file they saved. Blender 4.5 prints its full progress either way. The record repaired itself, which makes it stronger: the first runs lost their lines to a buffer, were run again with the buffer emptied line by line, and a second control shows the newer three still print their saved line under that route, so it is an absence rather than a lost log — that repair is recorded in the research's own verification trail, **which nothing downstream may open** — pointer removed 2026-09-01 — and no comparison carries it. **So this half of the entry rests on a record this file may not send a reader to.** Nothing decided here moves: the absence itself is *Reports 9*'s and is in the comparison. **What is lost is the ability to check the repair**, and that is stated rather than papered over. **What breaks if Smoozy starts renders the other way:** every live reading on a rendering entry, on three of the four versions — the frame in hand, the counts, the two bars, Blender's estimate and its running commentary. Nothing in this document decides how a render is started, and nothing here should be read as promising it.
- **The sample count says whether a frame finished as asked.** Label: **observed in a test** — **Reports 11**. A frame that reaches its total ends at the total; a frame that stops short prints one line reading zero of the total immediately before it ends. Three shapes of stop are on record and all three print it: a frame that settled early with adaptive sampling on, a frame cut by a time limit **with adaptive sampling explicitly switched off**, and a frame cancelled by an interrupt — the middle one being what shows the zero is not a quirk of one sampling setting. A frame that reaches its total shows no such line. **What this costs:** anything reading the last count to judge progress reads zero at the moment a shortened frame ends, so the largest count seen is the figure to keep, not the latest. **One record does not fit and is named rather than smoothed over:** a citation-check record lists an early-settling run's sample lines with no trailing zero, and reads like a list of the distinct values seen rather than every line in order — nothing says which, and it has not been settled. **The pointer to it was removed 2026-09-01**: it went into the research's own audit trail, which nothing downstream may open.

  **And the whole entry is bounded 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired: it holds on a picture small enough not to be cut into tiles, and Smoozy's pictures are not.** *Reports 11*'s runs were 480 by 360 and 320 by 240. **Above the threshold a frame that finished perfectly well can end on a line reading zero** — at 2049 by 2049 a run that exited cleanly and wrote its picture ended on `Rendered 3/4 Tiles, Sample 0/16`. **So *the largest count seen is the figure to keep* is a rule about small pictures**, and nothing may read a trailing zero as a sign of trouble at the sizes this app renders at. **And separately: a full count is not evidence a picture exists** — one recorded run printed the full count, then `Finished`, then `Cancelled`, ended at a failure status and left no file.
- **What Blender 4.5 prints about a missing picture, which is not nothing.** Label: **observed in a test** — **Images 2** — see the [comparison](../../Blender%20Research/Comparison/Images.md); the Cycles half is under **Images 9** as well. **The fact's home is [What a job writes](../What%20a%20job%20writes.md)**, which owns what a job's printing says about a missing picture. Cited here only because this document's preview promise sits next to it: under **Cycles**, 4.5 prints nothing that separates a missing picture from one that is there — the line naming the file is printed either way, in every version, so it marks nothing — while under **EEVEE**, 4.5 does print one line, and prints it only when the picture is missing, though it does not say which picture. That was run on one rented Linux machine through EEVEE alone. **What changed here:** one clause, from *there is nothing to read that says a texture failed* to *there is no pass to read that says a texture failed*, which is what the research supports and all this document ever needed. No promise moved.
