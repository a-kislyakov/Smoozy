# Queue & History

*What the Queue & History module does, in plain terms: how submitted render jobs line up and take their turn on the user's servers, what happens when work is interrupted, and the record of jobs that have finished. Not one word about how it's built.*

## Where Queue & History fits

Queue & History owns two things: the **queue** of render jobs waiting and running on the user's machines, and the **history** of the jobs that have rendered.

**The queue lives on the machines themselves, not inside Smoozy.** That single fact shapes most of what follows. Because the queue sits on the machine, it keeps working when Smoozy is closed, when the laptop is shut, and when the connection drops — the machine renders whether anyone is watching or not. It also means Smoozy does not *hold* the queue; it reads it from a machine it is connected to and shows the user what is there. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-20).

Where the queue lives is Smoozy's own decision. That a rented machine permits it is not: what Smoozy puts there has to keep going with nobody connected, start again by itself after a reboot or a crash, and read back the line it was holding. **That has since been established, on rented machines of both kinds.** What Smoozy leaves on a machine keeps going after the connection ends, holds and advances the line with nobody connected, comes back by itself after a reboot, and hands the line back when Smoozy connects again. One limit is on the record and belongs here: **where the machine's disk fills completely the arrangement goes down, and on one of the two kinds of machine it does not come back merely because room is freed** — it stays down until it is started again, while on the other kind it picks itself up the moment there is room. That difference is made by a setting Smoozy's own setup writes onto the machine rather than by anything the rental does or does not allow — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). Label: **settled at Architecture** (2026-08-23).

**History is kept in both places.** Each machine keeps its own, and Smoozy keeps its own record on the user's computer — because a machine rented for one project is destroyed at the end of it, and the record of what was rendered is worth more than the machine. See [History](#history).

**Connections.** The [module map](Whole%20app.md) owns how Queue & History is wired to the rest of the app. That covers what it takes from **Rendering**. It covers what it tells **Download** — that a job has started running, and that a job is over and which ending it was, plus whatever happened while Smoozy was away. It covers that the queue is one of the places the user reaches Download's auto-download toggle. It covers what **Servers** can ask of it when a server is removed, and what **Servers** leans on it for when warning about one. And it covers its dependence on a connection from **Servers** for everything it reads from a machine or acts on there — its own record of finished jobs being the exception, which needs no connection at all. It also opens **File Browser** at a folder, which is what *show outputs* does, and is told by that module when the user has gone ahead and disturbed a file a live job needs. **Telegram** carries what an entry says to a phone and brings back the answer to a job waiting on the user, adding nothing of its own.

**One thing it leans on rather than owns.** This module gives every job a folder and shows a picture out of it, but what a job actually writes into that folder — and which of it matters — is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md), because four modules promise into it and none of them owns it.

## What a job is

A job is one render, submitted once: a `.blend` file on a particular machine, one scene inside that file, the settings it was submitted with, the machine it runs on, and an identity of its own. Submitting is **Rendering**'s action; what happens to the job from that moment until it is over is this module's.

**A job is always exactly one scene.** A `.blend` can hold several scenes, and no job ever spans them. Asking for every scene in a file does not make one job covering all of them — it submits **one job per scene**, each carrying its own scene's settings, shown together as a **group**. How the user chooses one scene or all of them belongs to **Render Settings**; what reaches the queue is single-scene jobs either way. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24).

A job's settings are fixed when it is submitted. Everything about a job that can still be changed afterwards is listed under [Acting on a job](#acting-on-a-job) — its place in the line, whether it runs at all, and whether its outputs come back on their own. Its render settings are not among them: to render the same file differently, the user submits it again.

**Every job has an identity no other job shares**, fixed when it is submitted. What that identity is made of is Architecture's; Specs needs only that two jobs can never collide, however quickly one follows another. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-22).

**A job's outputs are written into a folder of its own, named for that identity.** The job's folder sits above the artist's own folders and holds all of them: everything the job writes lands inside it, and the artist's own arrangement of subfolders survives unchanged. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-22). **And it sits where the file's own outputs point**, beside the artist's work on the server, falling back to Smoozy's own area there only where that place belongs to another computer or cannot be used — [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) settles which folder that is. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-04), replacing the 2026-07-22 rule's placing of the folder in Smoozy's own area. *(Corrected later on 2026-10-04: the fallback is the folder the user chose for that server, where they chose one, and Smoozy's own area otherwise — [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md).)*

Keeping that true takes work, because a `.blend` can be set up to write files anywhere on the machine, in any number, through its compositor. How Smoozy holds them all under this folder while keeping the arrangement the artist built is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md), which owns everything about what a job puts on disk. This module owns the folder; that document owns what goes into it.

Three things follow, and each is leaned on elsewhere:

- **No job ever writes over another.** The same file submitted twice with the same settings produces two folders, not one folder rendered over the top of itself.
- **A re-run writes where its first attempt did**, because it is the same job with the same identity. That is what makes [re-rendering only the frames that are not there](#when-the-machine-goes-down) work at all: the frames the first attempt wrote are exactly the ones the re-run finds already present.
- **A finished job's outputs stay findable.** Long after the render is over, the folder still says which job made it — which is what lets the user ask for one particular job's outputs out of everything on the machine. Bringing them down is **Download**'s.

**The user can give a job a label, and it is optional.** The label is carried alongside the identity, so a job reads as something the user recognises rather than only as a machine-made identifier — in the queue, in history, and in the name of its output folder. It is set when the job is submitted and fixed from then on, like the settings: the folder on the machine is named at that moment, and a label that could change afterwards would leave Smoozy and the machine disagreeing about it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-22). Whether every label a user might type can become part of a folder name on the machine is a separate matter, and it is now settled: the folder's name carries the job's identity and a **bounded** rendering of the label, and **a part of a label that cannot go into a name is substituted rather than the job being refused** — the label itself is kept whole on the entry, which is what this document shows. So an awkward label costs a folder name that is not quite what was typed, and never a job that will not run. The rule lives in `Preparation/Architecture/Job files and outputs.md` 2.2 and is not restated here. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-18) for the ruling that put it there.

## The queue is one list made of each machine's own line

The user sees **a single queue** covering the servers Smoozy has reached. That is one list, in one place, so the whole farm reads at a glance. It is not a list of everything the user's machines are doing: a queue is read off a machine while connected, so a server Smoozy has not reached puts nothing into that list — see [What Smoozy knows when it is not connected](#what-smoozy-knows-when-it-is-not-connected).

Underneath, **each job belongs to the machine its file is on**. The `.blend` was uploaded to that machine and its heavy assets were sent there; the job can only render where its file already is. So each machine keeps its own line of work, and the single list is those lines shown together. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-21).

Two things follow:

- **A job never moves to another machine.** A free machine does not take work waiting on a busy one. If the user wants a job on a different machine, the file goes to that machine and the job is submitted there.
- **Order only means anything within one machine's line.** Moving a job up puts it ahead of the other jobs on *its* machine, and does nothing to any other machine's work.

How one list shows work belonging to several machines, and how a job's machine is made obvious, is Design's.

## Taking turns

Each machine renders **one job at a time**, and that job gets **whatever GPUs the machine has** — all of them, rather than the machine's GPUs being split between several renders. A machine with four GPUs puts all four on the one render, instead of running four jobs at once with a GPU each. Where a machine has no GPU, or the render does not use one, nothing about this changes: it is still one job at a time, with the whole machine on it. Label: **Creator-stated** — one job at a time is recorded in the [Product brief](Product%20brief.md) (date unrecorded); the whole machine going to that one job, 2026-07-21.

Different machines work in parallel, each on its own job. Three connected machines means three jobs rendering at once, one apiece.

When a job ends — finished, failed, stopped, or given up — that machine starts the next job in its line straight away. The machine does not wait for Smoozy to tell it to. Nothing in the queue depends on the user being connected, or on Smoozy being open.

**A new job joins the end of its machine's line.** It goes behind everything already waiting there. Nothing jumps the line on its own. Moving a job up is the user's own act, and is one of the things they can do to a waiting job.

**Separately, a machine can hold work Smoozy is not tracking**, and the machine's own order is what decides what runs next. Such work sits ahead of everything Smoozy shows, so the first job in the list need not be the next to start — see [Work Smoozy is not tracking](#work-smoozy-is-not-tracking).

## A job waiting on the user

Almost every waiting job is waiting for a machine. Two kinds wait for a person instead.

**The first is the first-frame check.** Where the user asked to see a job's first frame before the rest of it runs, the whole range, its first frame included, is submitted as a job that does not start until they say yes *(the rest of the range until 2026-10-02 — Creator-stated; see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md))*. The submitting is **Rendering**'s (see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md)), the answer usually comes from a phone (see the [Telegram spec](Telegram.md)), and what such a job does while it waits is this module's.

**It does not hold the machine up.** It is held outside its machine's line while it waits, so it takes no place there and is not reordered. Whatever else is queued on that machine runs while the user decides. A machine only stands still on account of this job if it was the only thing that machine had.

**The answer can be given from the queue as readily as from a phone.** The job sits in the queue marked as needing the user's yes, and both answers are offered there. That keeps this a feature of the product rather than a feature of the bot.

**Where both routes carry an answer, the first one to arrive is the one that counts.** A second answer arriving after the first has already been acted on is ignored. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-14).

**Saying yes puts the job in line in the ordinary way** — behind whatever is waiting on that machine at the moment the answer comes, by the rule above. It does not go to the front on the strength of having waited, and where nothing is waiting it starts straight away.

**Saying no leaves nothing behind.** The job never rendered, so it leaves the queue with no trace, exactly as a cancelled job does and for the same reason. Cancelling it and saying no are one act, not two.

**It waits with no time limit.** Nothing expires it and nothing decides for the user. They were told it is waiting, the machine is theirs, and a job dropped on a timer would spend their money on nothing or throw away work they meant to have. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-03).

**Where the first frame never arrives, the waiting job is dropped.** However the first-frame job ends without producing a frame — it fails, the user stops it, it is given up after a second interruption, or it is cancelled before it ever runs — there is nothing to look at, so the job waiting on that look leaves the queue and Smoozy says why: the first frame did not come back, so the rest was never started. It leaves no trace, exactly as saying no does and for the same reason. This is the check doing its job rather than a failure of its own: the user ends up with nothing running and nothing waiting, which is where saying no leaves them too. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-03).

**The second kind waits at the moment its render would start.** Reading a job's file as the render begins can turn up something the user has to decide on before any rendering is paid for, and where it does, the job is held there rather than carried on with. Which reads do that is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md), which owns them. Both answers are offered on the job's entry, and the queue is where they are given — the same as for the first-frame check.

**It stands aside too.** A job held at the start of its render does not hold the machine up either. It waits outside its machine's line, the machine starts the next job there, and the held job rejoins the line in the ordinary way when the user answers — behind whatever is waiting on that machine at that moment. Otherwise a machine left to work through the night would stand still from the first held job onwards, with nobody there to answer.

**Either way, the entry says it is waiting on an answer.** That is one of the few things a waiting entry shows beyond the job's setup; [What an entry shows](#what-an-entry-shows) lists them.

## A job waiting for the driver

*Added 2026-10-11, the Creator's ruling that a job needing the newer driver is held back and the user is told (`Audits/Build/Step 10c/The Creator's rulings on OptiX and the driver, 2026-10-11.md`, its last section). No auditor has seen it.*

**Where a server's graphics driver is too old for OptiX in the Blender a job uses, and Smoozy can replace that driver, a Cycles job that renders on the cards is held back rather than started.** Replacing the driver restarts the server, so it waits until nothing else there is rendering or waiting to render (see the [Server Setup spec](Server%20Setup.md)). A job started before then would meet the old driver and fail. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-11).

**It stands aside, like a job waiting on the user.** It takes no place in its server's line and cannot be moved up, and the other jobs there run. The driver's replacement does not count it as work to wait for, since it is the job the replacement is for.

**Then the driver is replaced, the server restarts, and the job joins the line in the ordinary way** — behind whatever is waiting on that server at that moment — and runs. Several such jobs keep the order they were submitted in.

**Smoozy replaces the driver, so this happens only while Smoozy is connected to that server.** With Smoozy closed the job goes on waiting, and nothing expires it.

**Its entry says it is waiting for the driver update.** Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-11). There is nothing to answer, so no answers are offered. The user can cancel it, and that leaves nothing behind, exactly as cancelling any job that has not started.

**Where the replacement does not reach a new enough driver, the job is let go anyway.** It then does what any job does on a Blender whose driver is too old for it: it cannot use OptiX, or from 5.3 the cards at all under Cycles, and it fails. Nothing else is tried in its place. Its entry says the driver was too old for OptiX in its Blender, or from 5.3 for Cycles on the cards, and could not be updated, and that stays on the entry into history, so the failure is not left unexplained. *Words changed 2026-10-28 (was *and Smoozy couldn't update it*): Creator-stated, the final words sweep. No auditor has seen it.*

**Nothing is held where Smoozy cannot replace the driver.** There the server has said so from the first connection and each Blender the driver is too old for carries its mark (see the [Server Setup spec](Server%20Setup.md)), and a job sent anyway fails as it always did.

**Telegram sends no message about it**, as it sends none about any job waiting its turn: nothing here waits on the user. Where the bot's running status names the job, it says what the entry says. How the job ends is told like any other job's.

## When a machine runs out of work

A rented machine costs money whether it is rendering or not, and Smoozy is the only thing that knows a machine's line is empty. Label: **documented** (2026-08-21) — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). *(This used to say a machine bills **by the hour**, labelled unresolved. It is wrong for the one provider this project actually rents from, which charges by the second at an hourly rate, and the reason is corrected rather than left standing. The message is unchanged: a machine doing nothing costs money at any granularity.)* So a connected machine with nothing rendering and nothing that can start is **idle**, and after **half an hour** of that, Smoozy says so — through the same notifications every other away-from-Smoozy message uses (see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md)). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-27).

**Nothing that can start** takes in a machine whose only work is a job waiting on the user. Such a job cannot start until they answer, so the machine stands still exactly as it would with an empty line, and the same message is what tells them (see [A job waiting on the user](#a-job-waiting-on-the-user)). *So does a job waiting for the driver update, which cannot start until the driver is replaced (see [A job waiting for the driver](#a-job-waiting-for-the-driver)). Added 2026-10-11, the Creator's ruling that a job needing the newer driver is held back.*

Half an hour rather than the moment the line runs dry, because that moment is already covered by the last job's own notification, and the two together would say one thing twice. What the user needs telling is that the machine is *still* doing nothing long after they stopped thinking about it — a machine with nothing to do is still being charged for.

**It speaks once per idle stretch.** If the machine takes on work and falls quiet again, the wait starts over.

Smoozy knows this only while it is connected, so an idle machine Smoozy is not connected to goes unremarked. That is the limit every notification carries, and it is not worked around here.

**The half hour is counted from the moment the machine actually went quiet, not from the moment Smoozy could see it again** — so a connection that finds a machine which finished its last job an hour ago finds it long overdue, and says so rather than starting the wait over.

What Smoozy says is bounded by what it can do. It never stops, powers down, or gives up a rented machine — a whole-app boundary owned by the [module map](Whole%20app.md) — so this tells the user that a machine is being charged for while it does nothing, and leaves the decision to them. That makes the wording matter more than most: it is a statement, never a prompt to act. How it reads is Design's.

## Acting on a job

The user can:

- **Cancel a job waiting its turn.** It leaves the queue without ever rendering — and leaves nothing behind. A cancelled job does **not** reach history: history is a record of work that happened, not of work that was planned. Afterwards there is nothing anywhere in Smoozy showing that job was ever submitted.
- **Stop a job that is rendering.** The render ends where it is, the machine moves on to the next job in that machine's line, and the stopped job goes into history marked as stopped. Ending the render is **Rendering**'s work, asked for from here (see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md)). **The frame being rendered is lost**, unless it is the gentle stop and it lands once that frame's own file has begun to be written — see [When the machine goes down](#when-the-machine-goes-down), which sets out what each way of ending leaves and which one **Rendering** uses. Nothing is written for a frame the stop catches part-way through, however the render is ended, and there is no way to ask a render to finish that frame first and stop after it. So a stop on a long frame throws away however much of it had been rendered — on a forty-minute frame, forty minutes of paid machine time. The user is told that, and told what survives the stop as well; **Rendering** owns that promise and states both halves — see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md). Where it is said, and in what words, is Design's. Label: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).
- **Reorder the jobs waiting on one machine.** A job can be moved to any position in its machine's line, not only swapped with the job next to it. A job already rendering cannot be reordered — it is no longer waiting.
- **Switch auto-download on or off**, for a job that is waiting *or* one already rendering. The toggle itself belongs to **Download**; the queue is one of the places the user reaches it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-21).
- **Bring the job's outputs down.** A download of what the job has written can be started from its entry, in history and in the queue alike — the whole of the job's outputs at once, rather than files picked out one at a time in the file browser. It is offered whatever the job's ending was, including a job that failed, was stopped or was given up, since whatever those wrote is still sitting on the machine. The download itself belongs to **Download**; the entry is one of the places the user reaches it, exactly as the auto-download toggle above is (see the [Download spec](Download.md)).
- **Answer a job that is waiting on them.** Where a job is held back pending the user's yes, both answers are offered on its entry — see [A job waiting on the user](#a-job-waiting-on-the-user).
- **Act on a whole group at once.** Where an all-scenes submission produced a group of scene-jobs, the user can cancel, stop or **reorder** the batch in one go rather than one job at a time. Cancelling and stopping follow the same order as stop and clear — the group's waiting jobs go first, then its running one is stopped — for the reason set out there (see [Stopping and clearing a server's jobs](#stopping-and-clearing-a-servers-jobs)). Reordering moves the group as one block to a chosen position in its machine's line, keeping the order the scene-jobs already have among themselves. A scene-job of that group already rendering stays where it is, because a job rendering cannot be reordered; the rest move without it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24); reordering added on 2026-08-12.
- **Show a job's outputs.** This opens the file browser on **that machine**, at the job's **main output** — the folder holding the work that matters, with the rest of the job a step or two away. It shows the files where they were rendered, never whatever copies happen to have come home; bringing them down is a separate act and **Download**'s. What the main output is and how it is found is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md); the browsing is **File Browser**'s. It is offered on a job that is still rendering as well as a finished one, because outputs exist long before a job is over — and on a job that has written nothing yet, or one whose file was never read so its main output is not known, both of which open the job's own folder rather than leaving a control that does nothing. Once the file has been read — at **Pre-load Settings**, or when the job starts — the entry knows its main output, and *show outputs* opens that from then on. Where the job's machine is not the one the user is browsing, the browser moves to that machine, which matters because it is also where an upload would then land — see the [File Browser spec](File%20Browser.md). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28).
- **Show a job's downloaded files on the user's own computer.** This opens the local folder that job's outputs landed in. It is the other side of *show outputs* above: that one opens the machine and shows the files where they were rendered, this one shows what is actually on the user's computer. It needs no connection and no machine, since the files are already there, so it still works after the machine is gone. It covers anything of that job that came home **as a whole job, however it was asked for** — an auto-download arrangement, or a whole-job download the user started by hand from a queue or history entry. Both land in that job's own folder, so both are the same thing to open; where that folder is, and the rule that puts them there, are **Download**'s — see the [Download spec](Download.md). **There is a second exception beside the one below, and it is Download's own.** A user who changes where downloads land part-way through a job leaves that job with two landing places, and so does a whole-job download the user sends by hand to a folder of its own; Download says the entry shows both, and permits the second act explicitly. So this control opens the one place where there is one, and where there are two it opens them both rather than picking. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-25). It is offered whatever the job's ending was, including the part of a job that failed, was stopped or was given up before it finished. Where nothing of the job came home, Smoozy says so plainly rather than leaving a control that does nothing. **And where the only thing that came home was picked out file by file in the file browser, Smoozy says that instead**: those files went wherever the user sent each of them rather than into the job's folder, so there is no one place to open, and saying so is better than opening somewhere that is not it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-07).

**A scene-job is an ordinary job.** Belonging to a group takes nothing away from it: it can be reordered, cancelled or stopped on its own, exactly like a job submitted by itself. So moving a single scene-job still pulls it out of its group, and a group is not guaranteed to stay together in a machine's line — but that is now the user's own choice rather than the only thing available, since moving the whole group is offered beside it. A group whose jobs have been scattered that way is gathered back together when it is moved as one. Where a group matters as a whole, it is treated as one: a finished batch produces a single notification when all of it is done, which the [module map](Whole%20app.md) owns.

All of these need a connection to the machine, since that is where the queue lives — all but two. **Switching auto-download on or off does not.** The arrangement is Smoozy's own, held on the user's computer rather than on the machine (see the [Download spec](Download.md)), so it can be changed with nothing connected; only bringing back what has already been written waits for a connection. **Showing a job's downloaded files does not either**, for the same reason: they are already on the user's computer. On a server the user is not connected to, its jobs can be looked at from what Smoozy last saw, and not otherwise acted on — see [What Smoozy knows when it is not connected](#what-smoozy-knows-when-it-is-not-connected).

Whether cancelling and stopping ask for confirmation, and how reordering is done, is Design's.

## What an entry shows

Every job the user can see is shown as an **entry** — waiting its turn, rendering, or in history. What an entry tells the user is settled here. How it is packed together on screen is Design's.

**Every entry carries the job's setup.** Which server, which `.blend`, which scene, which Blender, the frame range it was submitted with, the output resolution — both in pixels and as the percentage the settings carry — and any settings the user overrode at submission. None of it changes, because a job's settings are fixed at submission, so it reads the same while the job waits, while it renders, and long after it is over. A **waiting** job shows this and almost nothing more, since there is nothing live to say yet. Four things are the exceptions: a job waiting on the user's answer, which says so; a job held at the start of its render, which carries the choice as well (both under [A job waiting on the user](#a-job-waiting-on-the-user)); a job whose files have been disturbed, below; and whether anything had been checked for the file's missing assets when the job was submitted, also below. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24). *A fifth, added 2026-10-11 on the Creator's ruling of that date: a job held back for the driver update, which says so (see [A job waiting for the driver](#a-job-waiting-for-the-driver)). Where that update did not reach a new enough driver, the entry says so too, and that one stays into history.*

**Two parts of that setup need the file read, and a job can be submitted without a read.** Nothing forces the user to run Pre-load Settings first (see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md)), and without a read there is usually no scene name and no picture size — the scene's full size in pixels sits inside the file, and so does the percentage unless the user typed one over it. The entry says that rather than showing a blank or a guess. It says the job was submitted with no scene named, so it renders the file's active scene (see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md)); it carries a percentage only where the user typed one; and it states no size in pixels at all. *Words changed 2026-10-28 (was *renders whichever scene the file was saved showing*): Creator-stated, the final words sweep. No auditor has seen it.*

**One path is the exception, and it is the common one for a repeat.** A job **submitted again from a history entry** carries the old job's scene name across without any read at all — the name is Smoozy's own record of what that job ran, not something read out of a file — so such an entry names its scene even where nothing has been pre-loaded. [Render Settings](Render%20Settings.md) owns that carrying-across; what this document owes is not to say the opposite. The picture size is not carried the same way, because it is worked out from the file's own full size and that still needs a read. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-10) as the correction; the carrying-across itself was settled on 2026-08-08. All of it fills in when the render reads the file at the start of the job — the same moment the six statements from [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) arrive on the entry, below.

**Which Blender is part of that setup, and the entry keeps it.** A job is submitted to render with one of the Blenders installed on its machine, picked on the settings page — see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md), which owns the pick. That choice is fixed at submission like the rest of the setup, and the entry records it: the version, which every installed build can be asked for and answers — see the [Server Setup spec](Server%20Setup.md), which owns that fact. It needs no read of the file, because it is a fact about the machine and the user's own answer rather than anything inside the `.blend`. Two things lean on the entry keeping it. **Server Setup** reads it before removing a Blender a live job needs (see the [Server Setup spec](Server%20Setup.md)). And a job submitted again carries it with the rest of its setup, so the version the old job ran with survives the machine — what is done with that on the settings page is **Render Settings**', and this module keeps the record rather than any warning about it.

**A waiting job whose Blender was taken off the machine is dropped, and it leaves no trace.** Server Setup warns before removing a Blender a live job needs and the user may go ahead anyway (see the [Server Setup spec](Server%20Setup.md)), which leaves a job in the line with nothing to render with. A job already rendering is unaffected and finishes. The waiting one leaves the queue, and Smoozy says why: the Blender it was submitted with is not on the machine any more. **It leaves no trace**, exactly as a job dropped for want of a first frame does and for the same reason — it never started rendering, so there is nothing to keep and no entry to make. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-23).

**An adopted job's entry shows only what could be read off the machine.** Work Smoozy has taken over was not submitted from here, so nothing recorded its setup — the entry carries whatever the machine can be read for and no more. How much that is depends on the same unconfirmed ability to read a machine that the offer itself rests on (see [Work Smoozy is not tracking](#work-smoozy-is-not-tracking)).

**A finished entry keeps that setup, alongside how the job ended.** That is deliberate rather than incidental: after a failure the next attempt is nearly always the same render with something changed, and the user should not have to find the file again and re-enter everything from memory. What they change is not this module's to say — see [Open Threads](../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), where what actually helps after the usual failure is an open question. Submitting again is **Rendering**'s action; what this module owes it is an entry that still says what the job was.

**An entry that ended short is also where the user carries the render on from, and that is a second action rather than a version of the first.** Where a job stopped, failed or was given up part-way, **Rendering** offers to carry on from where it stopped — a new job covering the frames the old one never reached. It is offered on any entry that ended short, whatever the ending was. The action, and everything about what it sends, is **Rendering**'s — see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md). What this module owes it is two things. The first is the figure it already keeps: **the last frame Smoozy can vouch for as finished** (see [History](#history)), which is what the new job starts after. Where an entry has no such figure — no ending Smoozy ever learned — there is nothing to start after and the action is not offered, and Smoozy says why rather than leaving a control that does nothing. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**A job submitted with Simplify on says so on its entry.** Where the user takes **Rendering**'s offer to render a failed job again with Simplify — see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md), which owns the offer and what it exposes — the frames that come back do not match the file the artist saved. Nothing else on the entry would explain why, and the entry outlives the moment the user chose it. So the mark sits on the new job's entry, in the queue and in history alike, and it says Simplify was on rather than listing what each setting was set to — the settings themselves are part of the setup the entry already keeps. It stays into history like the other marks. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12). How it reads is Design's.

**The second is that the new job says on its entry which job it carries on from.** It is an ordinary job in every other way — its own identity, its own folder, its own place in the line — and that is exactly why the entry has to say so: without it the user is looking at two entries of the same file over the same file's range, with nothing on either to show that one picks up where the other left off. The mark is on the new job's entry, in the queue and in history alike, and it names the earlier job. The earlier entry is not changed by being carried on from, and can be carried on from more than once. How the mark reads, and how it links to the earlier entry, are Design's. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**A rendering job adds what is happening now.** All of it is live while the render runs. Two things outlast it: the job's times, and what Blender said while it worked (see [History](#history)):

- **The frame being rendered.** Every version of Blender says which frame it is on while it works. Naming the **view layer** on that same line — which together would name one renderlet (see the [Glossary](../Groundwork/Glossary.md)) — is something 4.5's progress line does and the newer three's do not. So the entry names the renderlet where it can and the frame alone where it cannot. Label: **observed in a test**.
- **How far through that frame Blender has got, in samples and — where the frame is split — in tiles.** Blender counts samples within the frame it is on, in every version, and gives no figure covering the whole job. **The count also says whether the frame finished as asked — but only on a small picture, and Smoozy's pictures are not small. Corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired.** On a picture small enough not to be cut into tiles, a frame that runs to its full count ends at the total, and, up to Blender 5.2, a frame that stops short — settling early, cut by a time limit, or stopped by hand — prints one line reading zero of the total just before it ends. **That was measured at 480 by 360 and 320 by 240.** Above the size where Blender starts cutting the frame into tiles the count restarts inside each tile, and **a frame that finished perfectly well can end on a line reading zero**: at 2049 by 2049 a render that finished and wrote its picture ended on `Rendered 3/4 Tiles, Sample 0/16`. **So a trailing zero is not on its own a sign that anything went wrong**, and nothing may read it as one at the sizes Smoozy actually renders at. **Blender 5.3 prints no closing zero. Added 2026-11-02; no auditor has seen it.** A frame that settles early or meets a time limit goes from its last count to a line saying `Finished`, one stopped by hand goes to `Cancelled`, and a split frame ends on its full count, `Sample 16/16`. Label: **observed in a test**, on the beta on the Creator's Mac alone — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**And a full count is not proof a picture exists either.** One recorded run printed the full count, then `Finished`, then `Cancelled`, ended at a failure status, and left no file at all. **Whether a frame is really there is answered by looking on disk, never by reading the line.** **Blender also reports tiles, on the same line, once a frame is large enough to be cut into them**: `Rendered 780/53284 Tiles, Sample 1/1`, seen in all four versions on both machines, and the same line came again from a render made on the cards with the frame-splitting setting switched off — so it is not tied to one way of producing it. A small frame is one tile and no tile figure appears, which is why a first reading of the research concluded there was none. **The size at which the splitting starts has been measured**: one tile's worth of area, the tile at its default size of 2048 pixels square, so a frame of 2048 by 2048 prints no tile figure and one of 2049 by 2049 prints four. The same boundary held in all four versions on both machines. What is still open is whether it is the picture's area or its width that crosses. **The reason given for that used to be that every size tried grew both sides at once, and that was not true — corrected 2026-09-01**: one run went from 2048 by 2048 to 2048 by 2049, growing one side only. **The question is still open, for a better reason: the number of tiles does not follow the obvious arithmetic.** Five tile counts are on record — 2, 4, 1024, 225 and 53,284 — and working them out from the picture's area gives 977, 215 and 53,081 for three of them. **So nothing here may work out how many tiles a picture will be cut into**; the counts are read from what Blender prints, and only the boundary itself is settled. Label: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).
- **How long the render has been going**, counted from the moment it started rendering rather than from when it was submitted.
- **Two progress bars** — one for the renderlet in hand, one for the whole job. Both are counted the same way, below.
- **Blender's own estimate of the time left** — below.
- **The render's own running commentary**, as Blender writes it. Blender does report while it runs with no window open, and its report can be read as it arrives rather than arriving in one lump at the end. Label: **observed in a test**. It is the one live reading that is not thrown away when the render ends — see [What the machine keeps about a job](#what-the-machine-keeps-about-a-job).

**All of that rests on how the render was started.** Whether Blender prints any progress at all turns on it, and in three of the four versions one of the ways of starting a render prints none. The fact, and which way Smoozy uses, are [Rendering](Rendering.md)'s. Where nothing is printed, nothing above can be read. Label: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

That this is what a rendering entry shows is Smoozy's own choice. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24). The Blender facts the items lean on carry their own labels above — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**What Blender says while it renders is not written the same way in every version.** Version 4.5 writes plain lines and never announces a frame before rendering it; 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 write timestamped, sorted lines and announce every frame. Anything Smoozy reads a running render from has to cope with both, and how it does is Architecture's. Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**Progress is counted in samples, and both bars count the same thing.** Blender renders every view layer on a frame whose *Use for Rendering* is ticked, so a frame yields nothing, one renderlet, or many. A renderlet's size is plain arithmetic: its tiles multiplied by its samples. A renderlet of four tiles in a file set to 500 samples is 2000 units of work. Where a frame is small enough not to be split it is one tile, and the sum is simply its samples. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

- **The first bar is the renderlet in hand** — how much of that arithmetic is done. Not the frame: a frame with three active view layers is three renderlets, and this bar fills and finishes three times over it.
- **The second bar is the whole job** — the same sum over every frame-and-view-layer pairing the job will render, shown as a percentage.

**Nothing here tries to be clever about tiles.** One tile can hold most of the picture and another almost nothing, so a bar counting tiles evenly will move unevenly against the eye. That is accepted. A simple count is honest about what it is measuring and cheap to be right about; weighting it would be a guess dressed as precision. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-13).

**The sum honours whichever sample count is actually in force.** A view layer can carry its own count, and a count handed in at submission may use the layer's, cap it, or replace it — which of the three is a scene setting, and the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md) owns it. The total follows that rule rather than multiplying one number by everything.

**A total is a plan, not a promise, and the bars are written to survive it being wrong.** Two things make it so. A frame with adaptive sampling on stops when it is clean, so a renderlet costed at 500 samples can genuinely finish at 200 — a job can therefore end with its bar short of the end, and that is a correct reading rather than a fault. And working the total out at all needs to know which view layers are active on each frame before the job runs: that is answered for a switch set by a keyframe and **unresolved** for one driven or changed by a script inside the file, which was tested in no version. See the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md), which owns that gap, and [Open Threads](../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md), which carries it to Architecture. Where the total cannot be trusted, the bar says what it is showing rather than showing a percentage of a number nobody can stand behind.

Because a job is one scene, the count never spans scenes; a group of scene-jobs is several counts, not one.

**A re-run picks its bar up where it left off.** A job interrupted at 90% and re-run shows 90%, not nothing — the work already done is still done, and the count carries the truth about the whole job. This rests on Smoozy being able to trust which renderlets are genuinely finished, and a file existing is **not** proof that it finished writing — see [When the machine goes down](#when-the-machine-goes-down), and [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) for what a re-run does about it. Where that cannot be trusted, the bar falls back to showing only the work still to do.

**Smoozy does not promise when a job will finish.** On a machine billed by the hour, a confident wrong answer is worse than none. So it shows **Blender's own estimate for the renderlet in hand**, marked as Blender's and as rough, and nothing more; the sense of how the whole job is going comes from progress and elapsed time together. Nothing here promises a countdown to the end of a job — Architecture may later offer one only if it firms up as the job runs and is always presented as an estimate. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24).

Blender does give that estimate, in every version, and it covers the frame being rendered and nothing wider — no version offers one for a whole animation. Two limits go with it: it is not on every line Blender writes, so it is a figure that comes and goes; and a render using EEVEE gives no estimate at all, so a job on that engine reads without one rather than waiting for it. Label: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**GPU memory is read from the machine, not from Blender.** What the entry shows is **the whole card's usage against its capacity**. The card's **capacity** is a fact about the machine and belongs to the server list, which **Servers** owns; the **live usage** belongs here. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24) — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**A full card ends a job in two quite different ways, and which one it is turns on who filled the card.** That is why the reading has to cover the whole card rather than the render alone, and why it is worth showing at all.

- **Another program holds the card's memory.** Blender stops, says so plainly, writes nothing and ends as a failure. This is the loud one, and it is the case Blender's own documentation names. Label: **observed in a test**, in Blender 4.5 and 5.2 on one machine.
- **The render's own scene outgrows the card.** Nothing stops: the card fills, the rest comes out of the machine's memory, the picture is written and the job reports success — having taken around ten times as long. On a machine billed by the hour that is the expensive ending, and it is the one that looks like everything went well. Label: **observed in a test**, in all four versions on one machine.

See [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**The second was reached on one machine only, and that machine was unusually large** — cards of sixteen gigabytes against nearly two hundred of the machine's own. Whether a render that outgrows the card behaves the same way on a rented box with far less memory to spill into is **not established**, in any version. Blender's own documentation names an out-of-memory error that no run in this project ever reached from a render's own scene, because that machine always had somewhere to spill.

**A separate thing kills a render on a rented machine and is not about the card at all.** A single frame large enough to want tens of gigabytes was taken away by the machine itself, during denoising, having reached sixty-five gigabytes of the machine's own memory — **the card's limit was never approached**. No message from Blender, no error, no file, nothing in its output: a job that simply vanishes. Label: **observed in a test**, in Blender 4.5 and 5.2 on one rented machine — recorded at `Preparation/Architecture/The rented machine.md`.

**The machine's own memory running out while a frame is being sampled is settled in all four versions, and they do not all end the same way.** Blender 4.5 and 5.0 are killed by the machine, with the error stream left empty and nothing said about memory anywhere. Blender 5.1 and 5.2 refuse the memory, say so on the error stream, and stop. In all four nothing is written, not even part of a picture, and anything reading only Blender's ordinary output sees a sampling line and then nothing more. Label: **observed in a test**, on one rented Linux machine — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). Both endings are named here because a rented machine of ordinary size can meet either, and because nothing on the entry would explain them.

**Those four are 4.5 to 5.2, and nothing was run in 5.3. Added 2026-11-02; no auditor has seen it.** Whatever 5.3 does, Smoozy records how the render ended and reads no cause into it. A 5.3 render taken past a rented machine's memory would settle it.

**There is no second figure from Blender, because none has been found.** Nothing Blender printed in any run on record is marked as the graphics card's memory. It has one such readout, and it lives inside Blender's own window, is switched off until somebody turns it on, and what its first number counts has never been established — none of which Smoozy can reach, since it never opens Blender's window. **The printed half is narrower than it reads**: the runs on machines with several cards kept one line of each render and threw the rest away, so a figure naming a card could have been printed on another line and gone unread. Label: **observed in a test** for the window readout, **not established** for what a machine with several cards prints — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**A finished entry says which parts of the machine did the work, and a running one cannot.** [Rendering](Rendering.md) asks every render for the line that names them, and that line is a **closing report** — printed after the last sample and before the frame is written — so it exists only once the job is over. A running entry therefore says nothing about it. **A stopped job was believed never to produce it, and that was wrong — corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired.** A stopped render does print the report in two of the four Blender versions, prints a cancelled line and no report in a third, and prints nothing in the fourth — **and the version that prints it says outright, in its own published list of faults, that the figures in it are wrong.** So a line found on a job that did not finish is not evidence of anything. **Smoozy reads it only from a job that finished**, and for every other ending — including a stopped one that printed a whole report — the entry says the job's devices are not known. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions; the route to the line was run on both machines, its timing on one) — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**Where it can be had, the entry states it plainly and where it cannot, it says it is not known.** This is the answer to the question a rented machine raises hardest — the user rented it for its cards, and a job that quietly used the processor cost them ten times over. It is worth having even though it arrives too late to act on, because the next job can be sent differently. Smoozy does not guess it from the settings: those can be shown to name a card while the processor did the work. Label: **the decision is Smoozy's**, taken with [Rendering](Rendering.md)'s half of it. Where it sits on the entry is Design's.

**How much room is left on the machine's disk is read from the machine too, and a rendering job shows it beside the card.** The disk is what a job's outputs fill, and unlike a full card, a disk with nothing left ends the job rather than making it slow — so the number is worth watching from the first frame rather than found afterwards. What a machine's free space is, and the reading behind it, are the [File Browser spec](File%20Browser.md)'s; this module states only that a rendering entry carries it, and reaches no further than that reading does. How it is packed onto the entry is Design's.

**A job that will produce nothing says so.** There are two ways that happens, and the entry says so for either. A scene with no view layer active anywhere in its frame range renders nothing at all (see the [Glossary](../Groundwork/Glossary.md)). And a file can be set up so that nothing it renders is written down — see [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md). Either way the job still gets an entry, marked as producing nothing, rather than quietly rendering nothing and looking like a failure. Smoozy can only say this once the file has been read — which happens when the user runs **Pre-load Settings**, and otherwise when the job starts and the render loads the file anyway.

**An entry says when a file the job needs has been disturbed.** The file browser warns before the user deletes, moves, renames or overwrites a file a running or waiting job depends on, and the user can go ahead anyway — it is their machine (see the [File Browser spec](File%20Browser.md)). Where they do, that module says so here, and the job's entry carries it: the job is marked as having had a file disturbed, and says which file it was. The mark changes nothing about the job — it still runs, and it may well render perfectly — but where it does fail, the entry already says the likeliest reason instead of leaving an unexplained failure hours after the warning was read and forgotten. It stays on the entry into history. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-30).

**An entry also says whether anything had been checked for the file's missing assets.** Submitting is where the user is warned that nobody has looked for what the file is missing, and that warning is read once and then gone (see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md)). The entry records what was true at that moment, on the same terms as the disturbed-file mark above: it changes nothing about the job — it still runs, and it may well render perfectly — but where the render comes back wrong, the entry already says a likely reason instead of leaving it to a memory of a warning clicked past hours earlier. It stays on the entry into history. Nothing is checked or re-checked to put it there; it is a note of what was known when the job was submitted.

**An entry also carries what the job is writing and where.** Which folders the job's outputs are landing in, and a warning where two of the file's outputs collide, both appear here for a job whose file was not read before it was submitted — what the user would have seen at the settings, arriving at the start of the render instead. Some of it does more than tell them: where that read turns up something they have to decide on, the job is held and the entry carries the two answers as well as the statement, so the choice is made before any rendering is paid for rather than read about afterwards (see [A job waiting on the user](#a-job-waiting-on-the-user)). What it says is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s; that the entry is where it lands is this module's. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28).

## A job's frame preview

**An entry carries a picture of what the job has rendered.** It answers one question — did it work, are the textures there — and it is **not an image viewer**. The pictures are deliberately rough; to look at a frame properly the user downloads it and opens it in their own program. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-24).

**It says the render happened, and it shows a missing texture that was feeding colour. It is not what the colour is judged by.** That is a smaller promise than it first looks, and deliberately so: a job's important pictures may be written in a format that has no single look on screen, so Smoozy may be choosing how to show it and may not match what the artist sees in Blender. [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) sets that out. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28), **narrowed by the research on 2026-08-09**. The textures half is much narrower than it once read, and how far it reaches is set out under *How far the preview says the textures loaded* below.

**Which picture is shown.** Not simply the most recent image, which may be a utility pass — and most utility passes say nothing about whether the textures loaded. The preview looks for the **beauty pass** first and the **diffuse colour pass** second; which file that comes to, out of everything a job wrote, is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28).

**The beauty pass has a name of its own, and it is the same name in every version of Blender.** Blender calls it Combined, and describes it as the render before anything is done to it, so it can be picked out by that name rather than guessed at. Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**How far the preview says the textures loaded, which is less far than this section once claimed.** No pass reports it, in any version — there is no pass to read that says a texture failed. Label: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). What answers the question is the picture the preview already shows, and **it answers it only for a texture that was feeding colour**: such a texture marks the finished picture plainly, and a user who knows what to look for sees it at a glance.

**A texture feeding anything else leaves no mark a viewer could read as a failure.** A missing bump map gives no bump; a missing displacement map shrinks the object; a missing normal map tilts the shading everywhere; and a missing transparency map hands the fully solid end to whatever reads it, which is what a measured value implies rather than something any test rendered. Two of those change the picture a great deal — what none of them leaves is a mark that **reads as a failure**, so every one comes back looking entirely plausible with one property silently wrong, at a successful ending with the frame written. **What that mark is and every limit on it are [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s**, and this promise reaches exactly as far as that document's and no further.

**So the wording of the promise narrows here too.** The preview says the render happened, and it shows a missing texture that was feeding colour. It does not say a job's textures loaded, and it must not be read as saying so. Label: **observed** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

One neighbouring case is this module's, and the engines part company on it: an image slot left **empty** rather than pointing at a file that has gone. Cycles gives magenta there, EEVEE gives black and Workbench gives plain grey, so an empty slot reads as a fault only under Cycles. Label: **observed in a test**.

**The diffuse colour pass is the weaker of the two choices, and it is now weaker still.** It is the second choice because it is meant to show whether the textures loaded, and research did not establish that: only the beauty pass was ever looked at for what a failed texture leaves behind, and no separate pass was. Label: **unresolved** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). What has since been established is that even the beauty pass only shows a texture that was feeding colour, so the thing this rung exists to do is narrower than the rung was written for. It stays where it is: a picture of what the surfaces are coloured with is still worth more than the last utility pass a job happened to write.

**The promise gives way rather than breaking.** Where neither of those passes can be picked out — a job whose file was never read, or a case research leaves unsettled — the preview falls back to the latest image. Which of a job's files can be read at all is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s, along with the rest of the choosing. That last-resort picture may well be a utility pass, so it says only that the job wrote something: it cannot confirm that the render came out right or that the textures loaded.

**Where the job has written no image at all, there is no picture to show**, and the entry says so rather than leaving an empty space — see [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md). So the promise is that the user is never left without a picture of a job that wrote one, not that every job has one — **and, added 2026-08-22, that promise now runs for as long as the picture is kept rather than for ever; the ruling below is what shortened it.** An entry whose picture has been cleared says so in the same way, rather than showing a blank where a picture used to be. That is this section's existing rule applied to the new case and not a new promise; the wording is Design's, as it already is for a job that wrote no image.

The full-resolution frames stay on the machine and reach the user's computer only through **Download**, at full quality, when asked. The preview works from small copies held on the user's computer, so moving through a job's finished frames never drags a full render across the connection. Those copies are throwaway on the machine, where they can always be made again; how they are made is Architecture's. **When the ones on the user's own computer are cleared out is not** — it is the ruling below, and the copy there cannot be made again once the machine is gone. *(This used to say the copies are throwaway and can always be made again, and that when they are cleared out is Architecture's. Both halves are true of the machine's copies only.)*

**A preview and a download are different things.** A preview is small, local, disposable, and for glancing at. A download is full, local, permanent, and for keeping. Nothing about the preview brings a rendered file home.

**Previews already held can be looked at with nothing connected.** Frames rendered while the user was away need a connection before they can be previewed, catching up on the next connection the way an auto-download does.

**A finished job keeps its preview**, so a history entry shows the picture too — and that is the picture a finished-job notification carries, which the [module map](Whole%20app.md) owns. **It keeps it for a while rather than for ever**, which is the next paragraph. *(This used to say only that a finished job keeps its preview, with nothing about how long. The ruling below is what changed it.)*

**Previews are cleared once they get old, and the entry is left without a picture.** Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-22). Preview pictures pile up on the user's own computer — one for every finished frame of every job — and until this ruling nothing ever removed them except deleting the job's history entry, which takes the whole record with it. The Creator was shown the three ways out — leave them piling up with no limit at all, put a limit on how much room they may take, or clear them once they are old — and chose to **clear them once they are old**.

**What that costs the user, plainly.** A picture that has been cleared does not come back. The machine that made it is usually long gone by then, and even where it is not, Smoozy does not go and fetch it again. So an old job in history shows what it always showed — what was rendered, with what settings, how it ended, when, how long it took, how much got done, and what Blender said — with **no picture**. Recent jobs, which is what the preview is for, are unaffected. Nothing else about the entry changes, and the rendered files themselves are untouched, on the machine and on the user's own computer alike.

**A preview is kept for thirty days and is cleared after that.** The thirty days run from the moment the picture lands on the user's own computer, by that computer's own clock. Label: **ruled 2026-09-02 under the Creator's standing delegation** — the Creator ruled on 2026-08-22 that previews are cleared by age and did not set the period, and on 2026-09-02 handed every decision that could be handed over. The figure follows the promise the paragraph above makes. ## When a render fails

A render can fail while the machine stays perfectly healthy.

The job is **marked failed and the machine starts the next one** straight away, whether or not Smoozy is watching. There is no retry: Smoozy cannot tell a failure worth retrying from one that is not, and guessing wrong spends the user's rented hours. The failed job goes into history marked as failed, and the queue keeps working.

The reason it failed is kept on the machine with the job, so it is there to be read when the user next connects — see [What the machine keeps about a job](#what-the-machine-keeps-about-a-job). What is kept is Blender's own words and nothing tidier: no version of Blender hands out a failure in a form a program could sort, group or act on. That is why the whole-app rule below has Smoozy pass the words through rather than diagnose the render — there is nothing else to pass. Label: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**A render that ends at a successful status having written none of what the job owed is a failed render, in every version.** Blender can end cleanly with nothing on disk: in 5.3 a scene with no camera does. Label: **observed in a test**, the 5.3.0 Beta on the Creator's Mac — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). So where a job owed files and none of them is there when the render ends, the job is marked failed like any other, the machine starts the next one, and Blender's own words are there to be read. **The count decides, never what Blender printed**, so this covers anything else that ends the same way. A job that wrote some of what it owed is not touched: it stays *finished*, with the count coming up short beside it, **and it never reads *All frames rendered.***: the entry and the Telegram message say how many files were written of how many. *(Added 2026-11-02.)* Nor is a job that owed nothing ([What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)). **Smoozy does not look for a camera before the render**: that is past [Rule 8](../Groundwork/Rules.md)'s line, and telling the truth about the ending is what is owed. Label: **ruled 2026-11-02 by the build runner, under the Creator's standing delegation**. It fixes a hole: *All frames rendered.* over nothing was false.

**A render can also fail having said nothing about why, and that is an ending like any other.** *(Narrowed 2026-10-11, the Creator's eighth notes, note 6: this is the memory ending only. An empty error stream is not, on its own, a render that said nothing — Blender 5 writes its errors to the ordinary stream, `Desktop app and delivery` M13.)* The machine's own memory running out kills Blender 4.5 and 5.0 outright: **the error stream is left empty and no crash file is written, while the ordinary stream runs on to a sampling line and then stops** — so there are words, and not one of them mentions memory. Blender 5.1 and 5.2 say so and stop — the four-version split is set out under [What an entry shows](#what-an-entry-shows). So on half the supported versions there is no complaint to pass through, and this is the failure that has happened most often on this project's own machines. **The ending is decided by the render having ended, not by what it printed.** A job that stops with nothing to read is failed, not still rendering, and the entry moves to failed the same as any other. Without that rule a rendering entry would sit there reading as live work for as long as the user left it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12), resting on the observed split above.

**And the entry says plainly that the render ended without complaining.** An empty panel where the machine's complaints usually sit is exactly what a broken Smoozy would look like, and the user has no way to tell the two apart. So Smoozy states the silence rather than showing a blank: the render ended, and nothing came on its error stream. *(Narrowed 2026-10-11, the Creator's eighth notes, note 6: it said the render complained about nothing and there is nothing further to read. Blender 5 writes its errors to the ordinary stream, so outside the memory ending an empty error stream can sit beside a failure Blender did report, and Smoozy claims only what is true of the stream it read.)* **What the user does see beside it is the ordinary output, ending mid-render at a sampling line** — which says nothing about why, and must not be presented as if it did. It does not guess at a cause, and it does not offer the memory explanation as a diagnosis — that is one of the things that produces this ending, not the only one. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12). The wording of it belongs with *how a failure reaches the user* in [Whole app](Whole%20app.md), which owns the case of a failure with no words behind it.

This is deliberately different from a machine that goes down, below: a crash says nothing about whether the job was sound, while a failure says quite a lot.

How the failure reaches the user is settled by the whole-app concern *how a failure reaches the user* — it appears on the job's entry, stays until the user is done with it, is a plain sentence with the machine's own words behind it, and says what to do only where Smoozy knows the cause, which for a render that died is the one case that concern carves out (see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md)). In what words, and in how much room, is Design's.

## When the machine goes down

A rented machine can reboot or crash with work on it. When it comes back:

**The queue survives.** The jobs that were waiting are still waiting, in the order they were in. This is the whole point of keeping the queue on the machine — and it rests on the unestablished claim about what a rented machine permits, set out under [Where Queue & History fits](#where-queue--history-fits). If a job Smoozy expected to resume neither starts again nor ends up with any recorded ending, that is a hole in this plan to close once that claim is settled — not something to design around now.

**The interrupted render does not survive, but its settings do.** A render cannot be picked up from the point the machine died — that work is gone. There is no partial frame kept anywhere, and no record of how far into a frame the render had got. What Smoozy still has is the job: which file, which settings, which frames were asked for. Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**The machine re-runs it, without asking.** It starts the interrupted job again with the settings it was submitted with, then carries on down the queue. Nothing waits on the user, and nothing waits on Smoozy being open. This is not Smoozy acting on its own initiative — it is finishing work the user already asked for, which sits inside the explicit-connect model the [Servers spec](Servers.md) sets out rather than being an exception to it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-21).

**A re-run covers only the frames that are not there.** Finished frames were written out before the machine died and are still sitting there, so the re-run leaves them alone. A render can be told to skip a frame that is already on disk, and it can also be handed a scattered set of frames rather than a plain run — so a re-run can either walk the whole range passing over what is there, or be given the missing frames outright. Which of the two, and in what words, is Architecture's. Label: **documented**, and **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). **Where the file holds a simulation never baked, the re-run steps through the scene from its start first**, so frames it starts part-way get the same simulation as the first attempt's — see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-02).

**What counts as already there is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)**, because a job can write into several folders at once and a frame can be present in one and missing from another. A job that wrote nothing before it died simply runs again.

Three things about the skipping are settled and each one costs the plan something. Label for all three: **observed in a test** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

- **The test is bare existence.** A file at the place the frame would be written is enough, whatever is in it — an empty file counts as a finished frame.
- **A file existing is not proof the frame finished writing.** A frame's file can be caught part-written, and a render can also put an empty file in place before it starts on that frame. So the two together mean a re-run can skip a frame that was never finished, and Smoozy cannot tell from the file alone.
- **The check is made frame by frame as the render reaches it**, not once at the start. So two renders started against the same folder both look correct and can still take the same frame: an empty file put down first is a claim, not a lock, and the same frame being rendered twice has been seen. Nothing in this spec puts two renders on one folder, since a machine renders one job at a time — but this is what goes wrong if that ever changes, or where a machine holds work Smoozy is not tracking that writes to the same place.

**What this section once leaned on has not survived, and the replacement is narrower.** It used to read that every way of ending a render leaves nothing at all for the frame in flight, so the frames a re-run finds are the frames that finished. **That holds for one of the three ways of ending a render and not for the other two.** An interrupt — the gentle one, and the one [Rendering](Rendering.md) now uses — has never left a short image, in any run by anybody, in any version, on either machine. A polite request to close and an outright kill each left the last image short every single time in the largest set of runs, and on Linux every one of those short files was **exactly zero bytes**. Two smaller earlier sets found them mostly whole; all three stand and none has been chosen between, and the disagreement is between the runs rather than between the versions. Label: **observed; the runs disagree and none is preferred** — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**So a zero-byte file at exactly the finished frame's own name is a real thing a re-run can meet**, and it is *present*, which is what the already-there test reads. What stands between that and a frame nobody ever renders is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s rule that the last frame Smoozy asked for is re-rendered anyway — a rule that used to be described there as a safety net for an untested case and is now the ordinary case. **A write that ran out of room is a separate matter again** and is also that document's.

**The one case nobody has answered is still a machine losing power outright**, where the operating system may not have written a file out yet, and it is the case a re-run after a crash is most likely to meet. A real power cut has been tried on no computer and in no version. **The nearest thing to it has been tried**: a machine reset at the kernel with nothing written out left **no frame's contents at all**, including a frame Blender had reported saving seconds earlier, in all four versions on one rented Linux machine. Label: **unresolved** for the power cut, **observed in a test** for the reset — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). Where Smoozy cannot trust what it finds, the honest fallback is to render the frames again rather than to trust a file it cannot vouch for; which frames that covers is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s.

**That reset was not tried in 5.3. Added 2026-11-02; no auditor has seen it.** The fallback above holds regardless. Resetting a rented machine during a 5.3 render would settle it.

**And one thing that reads like an interruption is not one.** A connection dropping mid-render does not end the render: it carried on and kept writing frames, in all four versions, both when the connection was cut and when the whole group of programs it belonged to was sent a hang-up. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on both machines) — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). So none of this section fires for a lost connection — there is nothing to re-run, and the job is simply carrying on unwatched. What the user sees meanwhile is [Servers](Servers.md)'.

**An animation written as one video file gets none of this.** Skipping what is already there has no effect on a video output, so a re-run renders the whole range again, however far the first attempt had got. What the interrupted attempt leaves behind depends on how it ended, and the three endings are not alike: **an interrupt** leaves a file the reader could find frames in — how many is not a fixed number and should not be relied on; **a polite request to close and an outright kill** each leave the file there with nothing in it at all, whatever the render had got through. So the same choice that protects the last image protects the video, and it is [Rendering](Rendering.md)'s. Either way the file is named for the whole range that was asked for, not for what it holds — and **its size on disk does not move while the render runs**, reaching its real size only when the file is closed, so **watching it says nothing whatever about how far the job has got**. That rests on one container and one codec, and a size that does not move is not the same as nothing being written: what waits for the close is the size the file reports. A second attempt replaces what was left rather than adding to it. Label: **observed in a test** — the size and the three endings in all four versions on both machines; the replacing second run, and the fact that an interrupt fires Blender's own cancelled-render notification while a polite close fires nothing, on a rented Linux machine alone. **No version's documentation answers any of this.** See [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**Whether 5.3's stopped videos play anywhere but Blender's own reader is not established. Added 2026-11-02; no auditor has seen it.** That reader opened them, and that only an interrupt leaves frames in the file held in 5.3 too. Opening one in a video player would settle it. Label: **observed in a test**, on the beta on the Creator's Mac alone.

**The entry says so before any of that matters.** [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) settles that a job whose output is a single video file is named as one before it is submitted, and where the file was not read first the queue entry carries it instead — along with what an interruption costs such a job, which is the whole range rather than the frames since the last one.

**A long enough video render can be written as more than one file, and the finished ones survive.** Where the artist has Blender's own splitting switched on, the render closes the file it was writing when it passes a size limit and opens another, and a file already closed off is whole and readable after an interruption. Without that setting there is one file and nothing is closed off early. That condition is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s, which owns it. So where that setting is on, a long video job interrupted late has not lost everything — but it has lost everything since the last file was closed, and the re-run still renders the whole range either way. Label: **observed in a test**, on a rented Linux machine only — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md).

**It re-runs once, not forever.** If the same job is interrupted a second time, the machine does not start it again. The job goes into history marked as **abandoned**, the machine moves on to the next job in the line, and the user is told. *Words changed 2026-10-28 (was *given up*): Creator-stated, the final words sweep. No auditor has seen it.* The frames it did write stay in its own folder on the machine's disk, to download or browse like any other job's. To try the render again, the user submits it again from **Rendering** — and that is a new job with its own identity and its own folder (see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md)), so it renders the whole range from the start rather than picking the abandoned job up where it stopped. Where what the user wants is the rest of the range rather than the whole of it, carrying on from where it stopped is the other action on that entry, and it is a new job too — see [What an entry shows](#what-an-entry-shows). The ordinary case — a machine rebooting once — needs nothing from the user, and keeps the work that can safely be recognised as finished; the frames the re-run does again still use paid machine time. The bad case, a scene heavy enough to take the machine down every time it is loaded, would otherwise loop all night on a machine the user is paying for and produce nothing. ## What the machine keeps about a job

Everything above happens with nobody watching, so the record of it cannot depend on Smoozy being open. The machine keeps a note against each job of what has happened to it: that it finished; that it failed, and why; that it was interrupted and re-run, and from which frame; that it was interrupted twice and given up. It keeps the job's **times** in the same note — when it started rendering, and when it ended — since those cannot be worked out afterwards either. Smoozy reads that note when it connects. That is where an ordinary finish and its times are read, where the reason for a failure is read, and where the notice below gets what it says.

**It keeps what Blender said while it rendered, whatever the ending was.** Not only after a failure: a job that finished is kept the same way, and the entry carries the words into history alongside the times. All of it is kept, in Blender's own words and nothing tidier, and the entry is where the user reaches it. That costs almost nothing to hold and answers the question a picture cannot — the run said something at the time and nobody was there to read it, and once the render is over there is no second copy. Smoozy does not read those words, sort them, or draw any conclusion from them: it keeps them and shows them, and what they mean is the artist's to judge. How much of them an entry puts on screen at once, and how the rest is reached, is Design's.

**The note can be read.** It is kept on the machine itself, written by the piece of Smoozy running there, and each fact is written in the same act that makes it true — so the note and the machine never disagree, **with one window, and it is the full disk**: while the machine's disk is completely full nothing can be written down at all, so a job that ends in that window leaves the note still showing no ending until there is room again. The note waits rather than guessing — it never invents an ending — and it catches up as soon as a write can go through. Where a render failed, the note points at what Blender said rather than trying to name the cause. Label: **settled at Architecture** (2026-08-23).

Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-11, the Creator's ninth notes, note 3: *"pretty much useless as it is now … replace it with something a lot less invasive … Maybe just a counter of finished jobs is enough, but think it through."*). *(Changed 2026-10-12. ## What Smoozy knows when it is not connected

Smoozy keeps **no lasting copy** of a server's queue. It reads the machine while connected and shows what it read; what it read is held only until the app closes.

So for a server the user is not connected to, Smoozy holds only **what it last saw while connected**. Close Smoozy and reopen it, and it knows nothing at all about a disconnected server's jobs until it connects again. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-21).

So on a fresh launch the single queue is **empty**. Nothing is connected yet, so there is no machine to read and nothing to show — not because there is no work, but because Smoozy has not looked. Connecting a server fills in that machine's line, and the list grows as more are connected.

That is an honest position rather than a limitation to work around. A remembered queue goes stale the moment the connection ends — jobs finish, the next one starts, a render fails — and Smoozy would have no way of knowing how wrong it had become. It is better to say "connect and find out" than to show a confident list that is hours out of date.

**History does not work that way.** A job that is over cannot change, so the reason for keeping no lasting copy of a queue does not reach it. Smoozy keeps its own record of finished jobs on the user's computer, and it is not a stale copy of anything: it starts as Smoozy's own note, written when the job was submitted, so a job Smoozy submitted is never lost by the app closing — see [History](#history). What is dropped is only what Smoozy read off a machine.

Two consequences reach other parts of the app:

- Anything the user is shown about a disconnected server's **queue** is **last-known**, and is presented that way, never as the current state.

  **A job that was rendering when Smoozy last looked needs more than a general mark.** Its entry carries the readings that make a render look alive — the frame in hand, how far through it, the two bars, the time it has been going — so an entry that only says "last-known" still reads as something happening now. Such an entry says plainly that it **is not updating**, and when it was last seen. What it must never do is read like a live render, because the job may have finished, failed or stopped hours ago and Smoozy has no way of knowing which. How that is marked is Design's.
- The [Servers spec](Servers.md) warns, when removing a disconnected server, that jobs may be left on its machine. That warning is built on this last-known record, so it needs a remembered job to warn about. Where Smoozy holds none, no warning appears, whatever the reason there is none — the app was closed and reopened, the server has not been connected to since, or nothing was on the machine the last time Smoozy looked. Smoozy does not reach out to the machine to find out, and it does not guess.

## Work Smoozy is not tracking

Because the queue outlives Smoozy's knowledge of it, a machine can hold work that Smoozy has no record of. Two paths get there, both set out in the [Servers spec](Servers.md): a server removed under *leave it be*, whose jobs keep running on a machine Smoozy has forgotten; and a server whose host or port was edited while jobs were running, leaving that work behind on the old machine.

Servers promises that **any** connection finding untracked queued or running work says so and offers to take it back over. This module is what that offer means.

The offer is **one per machine, all or nothing**. A machine holding three untracked jobs produces one offer covering all three, not three offers. That matches how the work got there: all of it was left behind by the same event.

- **Accepting** puts the work into the queue as though it had been submitted from here. It takes its machine's line in the order the machine already has it, and from then on it can be seen, reordered and stopped, and it lands in history when it is over. Whether an adopted job can also be re-run after an interruption depends on how much of it can be read off the machine — the same unconfirmed ability marked below.
- **Declining** leaves the work alone. It keeps rendering or waiting in the machine's line, and Smoozy neither shows it nor acts on it.

**Declined work still occupies the machine.** The machine's own order decides what runs next, so a job submitted afterwards goes behind whatever is already there, tracked or not — it can sit at the top of the queue and not start. Smoozy says that the machine is busy with work the user chose not to track: the fact of it, not the job itself, so the wait has a stated cause.

**Declining is not final.** The next connection that finds that work still there offers again. A running render is costing the user money whether Smoozy is tracking it or not, so a single "no" should not make it invisible for good. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-21).

Reading a machine for the work sitting on it is a promise this spec makes and Architecture has to deliver. If it turns out a machine cannot be read that way, that comes back here as a fault in the plan — and it breaks the Servers promise too, not just this one. Where a particular machine cannot be read, Smoozy says it could not check rather than connecting silently: do what can be done, and say what can't (see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md)).

**A machine can be read this way.** One request on a connection brings back everything the machine is holding — what is running and what is waiting — and it reaches work Smoozy never tracked as readily as work it did. Label: **settled at Architecture** (2026-08-23).

## Stopping and clearing a server's jobs

When the user removes a connected server that has jobs on it, [Servers](Servers.md) offers to **stop and clear** them first. Which removals get that offer is Servers' to settle, and it settles it on the machine having jobs queued or running; a removal with nothing on the machine is a plain removal and this section never comes into it. The request, when it is made, lands here, and it means two separate things:

- **Clear** the jobs waiting behind the running one. They leave the queue without ever rendering.
- **Stop** the job that is rendering. The render ends where it is, exactly as if the user had stopped it by hand.

**In that order.** The waiting jobs are cleared first, then the running one is stopped — because stopping the running job makes the machine start the next waiting one straight away, so clearing first leaves nothing for it to start. The other way round, the user asks for an emptied machine and gets a fresh render instead.

**Where the waiting jobs cannot be cleared, the running render is not stopped.** Stopping it would set the machine off on one of the jobs that could not be cleared — the very outcome the order exists to prevent. Smoozy says what failed and leaves the choice to the user instead of going ahead.

**What is left behind.** A job that was rendering **goes into history, marked as stopped**, exactly as when the user stops one by hand (see [Acting on a job](#acting-on-a-job)). Jobs that were only waiting leave **no trace**, exactly as a cancelled job does and for the same reason.

Smoozy can stop and clear only a machine it is connected to; Servers already settles that, and never reaches out to a disconnected server to try.

Servers also settles what happens when this only partly works — the waiting jobs go and the render cannot be stopped, or the machine drops out of reach mid-way. It reports what worked and asks the user what to do next. This module's part is simply that each half is a separate action with a separate outcome, which is what makes a partial result possible to describe.

## History

**A job appears in the history list once it has ended**, or once its server is gone (below). While it waits or renders it is a queue entry and nothing else, and the queue entry says what is happening now (see [What an entry shows](#what-an-entry-shows)). When the job ends it leaves the queue, and its history entry shows with whatever ending Smoozy learns — finished, failed, stopped, or abandoned. *Words changed 2026-10-28 (was *given up*): Creator-stated, the final words sweep. This paragraph read: "A job appears in history the moment it starts rendering, not when it ends. Until then it is a queue entry and nothing else. From the start of the render until the job is over it is in both lists at once: the queue entry says what is happening now, and the history entry holds the job with no ending yet." **Only what the list shows changed**: the record below is still written at submission and still takes the render's start, so nothing about what history keeps moved.)*

**Smoozy keeps its own record of finished jobs, on the user's computer, and that record is what history shows.** This is not a cached copy of a machine's state, and the rule against keeping one does not reach it: a job that is over cannot change, so there is nothing to go stale. It is Smoozy's own note of what the user asked for and what came back — the same kind of thing as an auto-download arrangement (see the [Download spec](Download.md)). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-27).

**Smoozy writes that note when the job is submitted, not when it ends.** That is about the record rather than about what the user sees: writing it at submission is what lets the record outlive the machine, so a job Smoozy never heard the end of is still a job Smoozy wrote down. The note becomes the history entry the user sees at the moment above, and takes on whatever ending Smoozy learns afterwards.

**A job that never started rendering leaves no trace at all** — cancelled or declined. There is no entry, and nothing anywhere in Smoozy shows the job was ever submitted. That is the rule a cancelled job already follows, and it is why history holds the jobs that rendered rather than every job that was ever submitted. **A job left on a machine that is gone is the one exception**, because there Smoozy cannot tell whether it started at all — see below.

Each machine still keeps its own history, exactly as it keeps its queue. The two are not rivals: where both hold the same job, the machine is where Smoozy reads it from, and Smoozy's record is what survives the machine.

**History is one list**, across every job Smoozy has a record of — whatever machine ran it, and whether or not that machine still exists. It is there on a fresh launch, before anything is connected, and is empty only when the user has never rendered or has cleared it. Connecting to a machine fills in anything Smoozy does not already hold, such as jobs it has taken over from that machine (see [Work Smoozy is not tracking](#work-smoozy-is-not-tracking)). Filling in never brings back an entry the user deleted, whatever the machine still holds — the deletion rule below is the stronger one, and how that is kept is Architecture's. This is the one place Queue & History does not follow the queue's shape: the queue is empty until a machine is reached, and history is not.

**Smoozy learns how a job ended only while it is connected.** It sees the ending itself, or reads it off the machine's note on the next connection (see [What the machine keeps about a job](#what-the-machine-keeps-about-a-job)). So a machine destroyed before the user connects again takes those endings with it, and the jobs it was running end up in the record with **no ending Smoozy ever learned**. Those entries are kept and shown as such rather than dropped: a job the user submitted and never heard the end of is worth showing, and Smoozy does not guess at how it went.

**A job Smoozy submitted and never heard had started is kept too, once its server is gone, and shown as a job Smoozy never heard whether it started.** A job that rendered for hours while nobody was connected and a job that only waited look exactly the same from the user's computer, and the machine that could tell them apart is gone. Dropping both would lose work the user sent; so both are kept, and the entry says only what Smoozy knows. The cost is accepted: a job that in fact never ran can appear too, and the user deletes it like any other entry. Smoozy never decides on its own that a machine is gone (see the [Whole app spec](Whole%20app.md)), so this happens only when the user removes the server, or when Smoozy connects and finds nothing of its own left on the machine. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-09-18).

The everyday version of the same thing: a job that was rendering when Smoozy closed is already written down, so it is in history with its ending on the next connection that learns it — or in history with no ending Smoozy ever learned, where the machine never comes back.

**History survives both the removal and the machine.** Removing a server costs no history, and neither does destroying the machine: the record is on the user's own computer, so what was rendered, with what settings, and when it finished all stay. That matters because destroying the machine is the ordinary ending, not a rare one — a machine is rented for a piece of work and thrown away when the work is done, and the user still needs to know what that work cost the last time they did it. The rendered files are a separate matter: whatever the user brought down to their own computer is theirs, and untouched by any of this — by the removal, by the machine going, and by the user deleting from history.

**An entry keeps the server's name as it stood at submission**, and removing that server does not take it. The name is part of the setup the entry carries, and the setup outlives both the removal and the machine, so an entry never falls back to a blank or to something only Smoozy would recognise. That matters when the user is looking for one job among several machines with near-identical names.

**An entry outliving its machine keeps its setup but loses its reach.** The record says what was rendered, with what settings, and how it ended, and that stands on its own. Anything reaching back to the machine does not: bringing a job's outputs down needs that machine, its server, and a connection (see the [Download spec](Download.md)), and so does opening the file browser at its outputs. Smoozy says so plainly instead of offering an action that cannot work.

**Submitting the job again is the exception, and it is the common case.** Everything a history entry holds is Smoozy's own record on the user's computer rather than anything read off a machine, so the setup outlives the machine even where the reach does not. The user can point that setup at a `.blend` on **another** connected machine, which makes submitting again useful after the ordinary ending of a job rather than only in the rare case where the machine is still rented. **Rendering** owns the action, what has to be found again on the new machine, and what cannot carry across — see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md). What this module owes it is an entry that still says what the job was, and this narrowing of the rule above. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-30).

**Nothing expires, with one exception, and the exception is the frame preview.** History is kept until the user clears it. They can delete individual entries, wipe a machine's history entirely, clear the whole of history at once — the third of those added 2026-08-24, label: **Creator-stated** — or clear at once the history of every machine no longer in Smoozy, which reaches no machine, since Smoozy no longer has those machines to reach — the fourth added 2026-10-24, label: **Creator-stated**, the eighteenth notes, note 13: *"History should have one more button — delete jobs from servers that are no longer in Smoozy."* No auditor has seen it — and Smoozy sets no limit and drops nothing on its own — it is the user's record, so when it stops being worth keeping is their call. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-21). **The exception, added 2026-08-22:** an entry's preview picture is cleared once it is old, and the entry itself stays exactly as it was, minus the picture — see [A job's frame preview](#a-jobs-frame-preview). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-22). *(This paragraph used to say nothing expires with no exception at all. The entries do not expire; the pictures do.)*

**Deleting reaches both copies.** Deleting an entry takes it out of Smoozy's record, and off the machine as well when that machine is connected. When it is not connected, the entry still goes from the record, and it does not come back: a later connection to that machine never restores what the user deleted. Wiping a machine's history works the same way. The alternative — an entry the user deleted reappearing the next time they connect — would make deleting meaningless. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-27).

**Smoozy can reach onto a connected machine and take the machine's own copy off**, and what goes is written down exactly: the machine's record of the job, what it captured while rendering, and the material the run was set up from. **The rendered frames are never touched**, because those are the user's. **One part of that reach has been watched happening and the rest has not, and the difference is stated rather than smoothed over:** on both kinds of rented machine, everything the machine would afterwards *tell* Smoozy about the deleted job went — its record, its note, the mark that its outputs had been collected, its place in the line — with no other job disturbed; but the words captured while it rendered and the material the run was set up from stayed on the disk under the deleted job's own name, readable, while the machine reported nothing about them. Smoozy's answer is to name every part the removal owes before it takes the first one off, and to carry whatever it could not take as a debt against that machine, paid on a later connection rather than forgotten — and that has not yet been watched through to the end on a real machine. See [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). Label: **settled at Architecture for what the machine afterwards reports, and settled but not yet observed for the rest** (2026-08-23).

Deleting history destroys the record, not the renders: the frames on the machine's disk are files, untouched by anything here.

**Smoozy names what goes with an entry before it deletes it.** More goes than a row on screen, and none of the rest is obvious from looking at it. The record goes for good, and no later connection brings it back. **Submitting that job again** goes with it — the entry is the only thing in Smoozy holding the setup that action starts from, and the action itself is **Rendering**'s (see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md)). So do the entry's two ways back to the files: *show outputs* on the machine, and *show a job's downloaded files* on the user's own computer. What is untouched is named as well, and both are stated above — the frames on the machine's disk, and whatever of the job already came home. Whether any of this is a box, and in what words, is Design's.

**What a history entry holds** is the setup every entry carries, how the job ended, how much of the job was made where that ending was not a clean finish, the job's times, whether it was interrupted and re-run and from which frame, what Blender said while it rendered, and the job's frame preview **for as long as that picture is kept** — see [What an entry shows](#what-an-entry-shows), [What the machine keeps about a job](#what-the-machine-keeps-about-a-job) and [A job's frame preview](#a-jobs-frame-preview). *(The preview used to be listed here with no qualifier; everything else in this list is kept for the life of the entry.)*

**The times are kept.** An entry records when the job was submitted, when it started rendering, when it ended, and how long it rendered — taken from what Smoozy saw itself, or read off the machine's note. That is what answers the question history exists for: what a piece of work cost the last time it was done. An entry with **no ending Smoozy ever learned** has no ending time and no total, and says so.

**For a job that did not finish, how long it rendered runs from the render starting to the moment it ended** — the moment it failed, was stopped, or was given up. It is what the attempt cost, which is the question above, rather than a measure of how much of the work got done.

**How much of the work got done is kept as well.** For every ending short of a clean finish — stopped, failed and given up alike — the entry says **the last frame Smoozy can vouch for as finished**, and says that it cannot vouch for the frame the job was on. It does not go further and assert that nothing was written for that frame: what a job leaves on disk for the frame it was working on depends on how it ended, and is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s. Without this figure a stopped job reads as the range it was submitted with and nothing else, and the only other place those frames can be counted is the machine, which history exists to outlive. It is stated in **frames** rather than in renderlets, because whether a whole job's renderlets can be counted at all is not established and this must not lean on it — see [Sources](Sources/Queue%20&%20History.sources.md). An entry with **no ending Smoozy ever learned** has no such figure either, and says so, exactly as it does for the times. How it is shown is Design's.

**What the figure counts is *already there*, and a file of zero bytes is not.** [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) owns that rule: a frame counts only when every file it owes is present and none of them is empty. That matters most where a machine went down, which can empty a frame Blender had reported saved. **The figure kept on the entry is what was true when the job ended**, and *carry on from where this stopped* reads the machine again before it uses it — see the [Rendering spec](Rendering.md), which owns that action. Where that reading cannot be had, this figure is what the page falls back to, and says so.

**A history entry offers *show outputs* too**, on the same terms as a queue entry — it opens the file browser at the job's main output (see [Acting on a job](#acting-on-a-job)). Like every other action reaching back to a machine, it needs that machine and a connection, so an entry whose machine is gone offers it no more than it offers a download.

## What Queue & History does not do

- It does not submit jobs — that is **Rendering**'s action; this module takes them from there.
- It does not decide what a job renders. Settings come from **Render Settings** and are fixed at submission.
- It does not bring rendered outputs back to the user's machine — that is **Download**'s. This module tells Download when a job starts running and when it is over, saying which of the endings it was, and hands over what happened while Smoozy was away; a job that was re-run is still one job, told once. The frame preview an entry shows is not a download and never stands in for one.
- It does not retry a failed render — see [When a render fails](#when-a-render-fails).
- It does not connect to a machine. Everything it reads from a machine, and every action on one, needs a connection from **Servers**; its own record of finished jobs is the one thing it can show without one.
- It does not move a job between machines, and does not balance work across them.
- It does not touch the rendered files on the machine's disk, including when history is cleared.
- It does not decide what a job writes or where. It owns the job's folder; [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) owns what lands in it, which of it is the main output, and what the preview reads.
- It does not browse a machine's files. *Show outputs* opens **File Browser** at a folder and stops there.
