- 2026-07-29 — Claude Opus 5 (Claude Code) — created the Access spec from decisions settled with the Creator in one sitting. What they ruled: the trial clock starts when the user asks for it rather than at first launch, so the days are not spent renting a machine; an expired trial locks the app but always leaves the ways out open, since a machine may be rented by the hour with work on it; a licence is bought once and is the user's forever, covering two computers, with a third able to release one of them; the key is bought on the website and arrives by email, and the seller is not named in the spec because it is expected to change; and the licence is re-checked from time to time, with nothing said under three days without a successful check, a plain notice at three, and the lock at seven. The short window is the Creator's, on the reasoning that Smoozy is no use without a connection anyway; the three days are the exception argued for and accepted, so an outage at the other end does not lock out somebody who paid. A trial is checked on the same terms as a licence, and a licence reported dead locks at once. The tamper promise is written as the outcome only — ordinary tricks fail, and nothing claims to be unbreakable — with the mechanism labelled an assumption and left to Architecture
