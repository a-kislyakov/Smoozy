# Rendering

*What the Rendering module does, in plain terms: submitting a render job to a machine, what Smoozy puts in place at the moment a render starts, ending a render that is running, and submitting a job again from history. Not one word about how it's built.*

## Where Rendering fits

Rendering is the moment between settling a render and watching it. It is deliberately thin: **Render Settings** and **Dependency Upload & Remap** come before it, and **Queue & History** takes over after it. What is left is small and nothing else owns it.

Four things:

- **Submitting** — turning what the user settled into a job on a machine.
- **How a render is started, and what is put in place at that moment** — which of the two routes into Blender is used, the redirect that brings every output under the job's folder, the machine's GPUs, the file finding its own dependencies, and rendering every job as an animation.
- **Ending a render that is running**, when the user or **Servers** asks for it. There is one way to end it and it throws away the frame in hand, because Blender offers no other.
- **Submitting a job again** from a history entry, and **carrying on from where a job stopped** — two actions on the same entry, the second covering only the frames the first job never reached.

**Connections.** The [module map](Whole%20app.md) owns how Rendering is wired to the rest of the app. It needs a Blender from **Server Setup**, the settled settings from **Render Settings**, the render-ready file from **Dependency Upload & Remap**, and a connection from **Servers**. It hands submitted jobs to **Queue & History**, and takes back from that module the setup a finished job ran with when the user submits that job again — and, for carrying on from where a job stopped, the last frame that module can vouch for as finished. It hands that module back the earlier job a carried-on job continues, which the entry then marks. Two modules ask it to end a running render: **Queue & History**, when the user stops a job, and **Servers**, when a server is removed. **Telegram** asks for the same things from a phone — ending a render, and submitting a failed job again — and asks with **Queue & History** for the first-frame check. Submitting is also where **Download**'s auto-download is normally switched on, and where the choice of how much a job reports to a phone is reached. It opens **File Browser** on a machine when a job is submitted again and its `.blend` has to be found.

**One thing it leans on rather than owns.** What a job actually writes, and where, is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md). This module carries out the redirect that document describes; it decides none of it.

## Submitting a job

**Submitting is the user's act of putting a job on the machine.** It is the only way a job comes into being. It takes the settled settings from **Render Settings** and produces a job on the machine, with settings that are fixed from that moment. What a job is, and everything that happens to it afterwards, is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s.

**The machine is never chosen here.** A job renders where its file already is, so the machine is settled by which server the `.blend` sits on — see [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md).

**Submitting starts no transfer.** The `.blend` and everything it needs are on the machine before the job is submitted, and putting them there is the user's own act — uploading through **File Browser**, and sending a file's missing assets through **Dependency Upload & Remap**. Nothing about submitting sends a file up, and no submitted job is waiting for its file to arrive. So a user preparing several jobs to run through an unattended night uploads all of their files first, and submits afterwards. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-07). **So while the dependency check is sending this file's missing assets to this server, and until it has answered for a linked `.blend` that landed, *Render* waits and says why**, because a job made then would finish without them and look as if it had worked. *(Added 2026-11-03, the build runner's ruling, out of the second Sesterce pass. No auditor has seen it.)*

**A submitted job joins the end of its machine's line**, behind whatever is already there, including work Smoozy is not tracking. That is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s rule, and moving a job up afterwards is the user's to do and that module's to settle.

**Asking for every scene submits several jobs in one act.** A job is always exactly one scene, so all-scenes means one job per scene, shown together as a group — [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) owns that rule, and this module is what carries it out. What that means when only part of it arrives is set out below.

**A job is pointed at its scene when the render is started.** A render started from outside Blender can be told which one scene of the file to render. Told nothing, it renders whichever scene was showing when the file was saved. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**A name that matches no scene does not fail.** Blender says it cannot find that scene, then renders the one it would have rendered anyway and finishes as though nothing were wrong. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions). So a mistaken name does not come back as a failed job. It comes back as a paid render of the wrong scene, and the promise that a job is exactly one named scene rests on Smoozy getting the name right rather than on being told when it is not. One thing behind that is untested: the files tried were saved by a Blender with no window, and an artist's file is saved from the interface.

**Several things are settled at the moment of submitting, and not one of them is this module's to define.** The user can give the job a **label**, which belongs to [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md). They can switch **auto-download** on, which settles more than the switch: where the files come to, and whether the arrangement is narrowed to particular formats, are chosen at that same moment, and all three belong to [Download](Download.md). They choose **how much the job reports to their phone**, which belongs to [Telegram](Telegram.md). And they can ask for the **first-frame check**, which is below because it changes what submitting produces. Submitting is where all of them are reached. Auto-download can also be switched later on a job already submitted, which is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s.

## The first-frame check

**The user can ask to see a job's first frame before the rest of it runs.** Why that is worth having — a wrong camera or textures that did not load, found after one frame instead of one paid night — belongs to [Telegram](Telegram.md), which is where the frame is usually looked at and the answer usually given. What it costs at the moment of submitting is this module's, because it is not one job.

**It submits two jobs, not one.** The first frame of the range goes as its own job, and **the whole range, its first frame included,** goes as a second job that does not start until the user says yes. A render cannot be held part-way and picked up where it left off — carrying on means a fresh render of the frames that are left — so a single job held mid-way is not available to us. See [Telegram](Telegram.md) for that fact and its label. The cost is that the second job loads the file, starts Blender afresh and renders the first frame a second time, which is small against the hours it protects. *Corrected 2026-10-02, Creator-stated: the second job used to start at the frame after the first. The whole range stays in one job's folder. The simulation limits below still apply.*

**Both are ordinary jobs.** ~~Each has its own identity and its own folder, so neither writes over the other,~~ Each has its own identity, **and both write into one folder, the whole range's**, and each shows and behaves like any other job — [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s rules apply to both unchanged. What that module adds for this case is a job that waits on a person rather than on a machine, and what happens when the answer comes either way.

**The check's files are marked, so neither job writes over the other.** Every file the check writes carries `check_` at the start of its name — *check_racket_0001.png* — and the whole range writes its files exactly as it would have without the check. So the user ends with two copies of the first frame side by side, one of them plainly the check's, and one folder for the job on the server and, where frames download as they render, one on their computer. A check whose only output is a video, written as a picture (below), is marked the same way. Removing the check — deleting it, clearing History, or cancelling the range after it — never takes a file of the whole range's. Label for the decision: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-29, the twenty-first notes, note 6): *"the first frame output should get some kind of a prefix or suffix automatically and stay in that folder, but then when that same first frame gets rendered again if the user approves should simply get saved as it would have initially … That way we get rid of those extra folders."* **The mark is a recommendation awaiting the Creator's choice**: a mark at the start of the name was chosen over one at the end because it keeps every file of the check together in a folder sorted by name, it does not sit inside the frame sequence a compositing program reads, and where the frame's digits fall when text follows them is a fact about Blender that a mark at the start never needs. *Added 2026-10-29, a want entering at Specs under Rule 7. Until then each job had its own folder, which put the check and the range in two folders on the server and two on the computer. No auditor has seen it.*

**A job that writes only a video has its first frame written as a picture.** One frame of a video is a one-frame video, which no preview can show and no phone can judge, so for the check alone Smoozy renders that frame as a still picture, with everything else the job's own — the same scene, camera, size, engine and samples. The second job writes its video exactly as the file asks. A job that writes pictures as well as a video is left as it is, because its pictures already give the check something to show. Label for the decision: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-27, the nineteenth notes, notes 3 and 9: *"the first-frame check on a video-only job doesn't work and is useless now"*; the Creator chose *"Render it as a picture"*). That a scene set to write a video can be switched to a picture for one render, keeping its size and its transparency, and writes one picture named by its frame: Label: **observed in a test** (2026-10-27, Blender 4.5.15 and 5.2.3) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). *Added 2026-10-27, a want entering at Specs under Rule 7. No auditor has seen it.*

**The user's yes is not a submission.** The second job is already on the machine from the first act; the answer only releases it. So nothing here is asked of Rendering when the user says yes, and nothing needs Smoozy to be running for it — see [A job waiting on the user](Queue%20&%20History.md#a-job-waiting-on-the-user). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-30).

**Every scene gets its own check.** All-scenes already submits one job per scene, so asking for the check on top of it doubles that — a first frame and a waiting job for every scene in the file, each needing its own answer. That is accepted rather than avoided. A job is always exactly one scene, and each scene has its own camera, so one answer covering the whole group would pass a wrong camera in a later scene straight through, which is the failure the check exists to catch. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-03).

**Submitting needs a connection and a Blender on the machine.** Without either, no job can be sent — see [When a submission cannot happen](#when-a-submission-cannot-happen).

## A file whose missing pieces were never looked for

Nothing forces the user to check a `.blend` for the assets it is missing before submitting it. That check is its own action and belongs to **Dependency Upload & Remap**.

**So Smoozy warns, before the job goes, that the file's assets may not be on the machine.** The warning appears while nothing shows that this file has been checked, and stops once something does. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28).

The warning exists because of what users bring with them. Other render farms require a file to be packed — everything it needs bundled into the `.blend` before it is sent anywhere — and a user who expects that will assume Smoozy has it covered too. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). Smoozy deliberately does not work that way: the assets go up once and stay up, which is the whole point of **Dependency Upload & Remap**. The cost of that choice is that the file's pieces have to actually be up there, and nothing about the file on screen shows whether they are.

**The warning offers three ways on, and blocks none of them.** The user can go and check the assets, which takes them to the check in **Dependency Upload & Remap** and keeps the settings they have already made, so nothing has to be entered again. They can submit anyway: Smoozy does not check the file itself — checking is the other module's action — and a user who knows their assets are already up there should not be stopped. Or they can **submit with the first-frame check**, which is the answer in between: the job goes without anyone reading the file, and one frame comes back for the user to look at before the rest of the range runs, so assets that did not load cost a frame rather than a night. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**The third way adds nothing to the product and only moves where it is offered.** The first-frame check is already one of the things settled at the moment of submitting (see [The first-frame check](#the-first-frame-check)), and none of it changes here — the same two jobs, the same waiting job, the same answer. What this does is put it in front of the user at the one moment Smoozy already suspects the file's pieces may be missing, instead of leaving them to remember it exists. It is not offered where the user has already asked for the check, since it is then the same thing they have.

**The warning can also be switched off for good.** It is one of the dismissible ones, and the whole-app control in Settings brings it back with the rest — see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md). The two reasons it can be absent are different things: the user switched it off across the whole app, or Smoozy has something showing this file was checked. Restoring the switched-off warning brings back the first and not the second — a file Smoozy believes was checked still gets no warning.

**What counts as showing that a file has been checked** is settled by the module that owns the check — see the [Dependency Upload & Remap spec](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md). In short: a check that leaves nothing unfound stops the warning, and one that left things unfound replaces it with a specific warning saying how many pieces could not be placed. Three things hold either way: it does not matter whether the user acted on what the check found; it is remembered rather than lasting only the sitting; and it returns when the file is replaced, because a check describes the file it read.

**So the warning covers three different cases, and it says which one it is.** Either nobody has ever checked this file on this machine; or a check was run and the file has been replaced since — the last of the three above; or a check was run, and it was run with a different Blender than this job will use. Smoozy can already tell those apart, so it says which. The replaced case says plainly that it is the new upload that put the warning back, and the version case says plainly that the check stands and that it does not cover the Blender this job will render with — a check reads with the Blender the job will render with, so one made with a different version describes a file the render never reads (see the [Dependency Upload & Remap spec](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md)). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-19) for the third case. Left identical, they read as the one sentence the user has clicked past twenty times, and the twenty-first is the one that matters — and a warning that says nobody has checked when somebody has is worse than identical, not better. This changes what is said and nothing that is looked at: no file is read, nothing is blocked, and all three stay dismissible and switchable off. The wording is Design's.

## When only part of a submission arrives

Asking for every scene in a file can mean a dozen jobs. The connection can drop in the middle of that.

**Whatever reached the machine stays.** Five jobs of twelve are five real jobs, queued on a machine and ready to render, and undoing them to make the act tidy would throw away work the user asked for. Smoozy says which scenes were submitted and which were not. The group is whatever arrived. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28).

This is the whole-app rule *do what can be done, and say what can't* — one of the [concerns that span the whole product](Whole%20app.md#concerns-that-span-the-whole-product).

**A single job is all or nothing.** It either reaches the machine and joins the line, or it does not exist at all. That is a promise this spec makes and Architecture has to deliver; a plan that cannot deliver it is a flawed plan rather than something to work around here.

**Smoozy may not know which of them went.** A connection can drop after the machine took a job and before Smoozy heard back, so a scene has three possible outcomes rather than two: submitted, not submitted, or not yet known. Where the connection comes back, Smoozy reads the machine before it names which scenes went, so what it says rests on what is actually there rather than on what it last heard. Where the connection does not come back, it names the scenes it could not account for as exactly that.

**Submitting the rest is the user's next act, not Smoozy's.** Nothing retries on its own and nothing is held in reserve waiting for the connection to come back. Smoozy offers **submit the remaining scenes** as one action, so the user neither sends all twelve again — paying to render the five that already arrived — nor sends the missing ones one at a time.

**The scenes sent afterwards are their own group.** One act makes one group, so a submission broken by a dropped connection ends up as two groups rather than one, each acting and speaking on its own.

## How a render is started

**There are two ways to set a render going from outside Blender, and they are not interchangeable.** One names the frames to render on the command line. The other hands Blender an instruction to render and lets that do it. Both produce the picture. What differs is what the render says while it runs, and what names it will accept on the way in.

**Only one of them reports progress.** A render started by naming the frames on the command line prints its progress in all four versions. A render started by handing Blender an instruction to render prints **no progress line at all** in Blender 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 — no samples, no tiles, no stages — while still printing the line naming each file it saved. Blender 4.5 prints its full progress either way. All of that is a Blender with no window, which is what a rented machine runs; with a window open, no route prints any progress in any version. Label: **observed; it differs by version** (both machines, all four versions) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**So whatever starts a render has to use the route that reports.** Everything this project knows about what a running render says was learned on the command-line route. On the other route there is nothing to show while a render runs, on three of the four versions. What that takes is Architecture's; that it is required is stated here, because this is the module that starts the render.

**One promise this module already makes sits on the same route.** Smoozy asks every render for the line naming what did the work — below, under the machine's GPUs. In Blender 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 that line comes on the ordinary output, which is the output the other route prints none of. So the two are only known to fit together on the route that reports, and nothing establishes that the line survives the other one. **Nothing turns on that, and it is not the Creator's to settle.** Architecture has since fixed every render to the route that reports — it is the one constraint the rest of that plan sits inside — and it is on that route that the line naming what did the work is asked for. The other route is never used here, so however it would have behaved changes nothing. Label: **settled at Architecture**; the marker this paragraph carried is cleared on 2026-09-02.

**A name is not accepted alike by both routes either.** The older name for EEVEE is accepted by Blender 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 when it is named on the command line, and refused outright by all three when it is set by an instruction handed to Blender. Blender 4.5 is the mirror image, taking only its own name by either route. The name each version knows as its own works both ways. Label: **observed** — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). So where anything Smoozy sends names an engine, the name to use is the one the chosen version knows, whichever route sends it — and that cannot be relaxed on the strength of the older name working on the command line.

## What is put in place when a render starts

Six things belong to the moment a render is set going, rather than to the moment it was submitted. *(Four until 2026-10-02, when the stepping of a simulation never baked was added; five until 2026-10-08, when the file's own scripts were.)*

### The redirect

**Every place the job would write is brought under the job's folder.** The rules — which destinations are bent where, what survives of the artist's own arrangement of folders, and what is promised — are all settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md). This module carries them out and decides none of them.

**It happens when the render starts, not when the job is submitted.** Working out the redirect needs the file read, because the rule looks at every destination the job writes into at once, and a job can be submitted without the file ever having been read (see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md)). That read is the render itself loading the file on the machine, not Pre-load Settings — so it needs no connection and nothing from Smoozy. It is the first moment the redirect can be worked out at all.

**The two halves of that fit together.** What the machine is given at submission is the instruction. The machine carries it out when the render starts, with nobody connected and nothing for Smoozy to do at that moment.

The job's **folder** is a separate thing and is settled earlier: [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) names it at submission, for the job's identity and label. So the folder is decided when the job is submitted, and what the job writes is redirected into it when the render starts.

**An output path can be imposed on a render without writing it into a file, and this module is where that lands.** The place a render writes can be set at the moment the render is started, and the `.blend` on disk is left byte for byte as it was. A file output node's place can be reached the same way, by a different means. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on macOS and on Linux) — owned by [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md), and see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). So the redirect never needs the artist's file written to, and never needs a file Smoozy has written the paths into. What it takes to arrange is still Architecture's; that it can be arranged at all is now settled.

### The machine's GPUs

**When a job is set to use the cards, Smoozy makes every card on the machine available to it.** A job set to use the processor does not use them. The promise that a rendering job gets all of a machine's GPUs is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s; this module is what carries it. What it takes to keep is Architecture's, exactly as with the redirect above, and it is stated here because this is the module whose promise breaks without it.

**A machine's cards are not necessarily used just because a file asks for them.** Three settings have to line up: one card technology chosen for the whole of Blender, each card's own switch, and the scene's own choice between the processor and the cards. The scene's choice starts at the processor, and a rented Linux machine starts with no technology chosen at all. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**What it takes to keep the promise is now known, and it is one instruction.** A render started from outside can be given the card technology, and that overrides both the machine's saved settings and the file's own choice. It takes every card of that technology, whatever the machine's saved switches say. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions). It cannot pick out one card of several — that needs the machine's saved settings — and this module never wants to, since the promise is every card. That it is done at the moment a job runs rather than when the machine is set up is Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-27), and it is why the promise sits here rather than in **Server Setup**.

**Every card means Cycles.** EEVEE renders on one card, and no version supports more than one for it, so this promise is a Cycles promise. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions). Which card EEVEE picks on a machine with several is not established.

This was worth stating plainly because of what it costs when it fails: a machine rented for its cards renders without them, quietly, at full price, and the user only finds out when the render takes far longer than it should.

**And that is exactly what happens.** Ask for the cards on a machine where none can be used, and Blender does not refuse. It renders on the processor, writes the image, finishes as though nothing were wrong, and says nothing about the missing card. The same silence covers the state a rented Linux machine arrives in: every card switched on, no technology chosen, a scene asking for the cards, and the render goes to the processor without a word. What decides which of the two happens is not whether the machine could serve the technology but where the technology came from: left to the machine's own saved settings, a technology with no usable card behind it produces the quiet failure above, while the same technology **named when the render is started** refuses loudly and stops — as does a name that means nothing. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions), on an Apple-silicon Mac and on rented Linux machines with NVIDIA cards.

**So nothing a finished render leaves behind tells Smoozy which it used.** The render succeeds either way. That closes off the obvious way of noticing — waiting for a failure — and leaves the promise above resting on Smoozy getting the setup right rather than on being told when it did not.

**Two other ways of knowing do exist, and the research has since sharpened both.** Blender can be asked which cards it can see, from outside, with no window, on a machine with no screen at all. And a render can be made to name the part of the computer that did the work — but only as a **closing report**, printed after the last sample and before the frame is written, so it says nothing while the render runs. **A render stopped part-way was believed never to reach it, and that was wrong — corrected 2026-09-01, after the research was repaired**: two of the four versions print the whole report after a stop, one prints a cancelled line instead, one prints nothing, and the version that prints it publishes its own note saying the figures in it are wrong. **So the line is trusted only from a job that finished.** **How you ask differs by version**: Blender 4.5 offers one route and puts the line on the stream reserved for complaints, while 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2 answer to an ordinary logging switch on the ordinary output. A render asked nothing names no device in any version, and the settings read back afterwards **do not answer it and can be shown to name a card while the processor did the work**. Label: **observed; it differs by version** (the route was run on both machines, the closing-report timing on one) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). **Two limits go with it.** Every one of those results is Cycles' own report, so nothing establishes that an EEVEE or Workbench job names anything at all. And in Blender 4.5 the only route is a Cycles debugging switch writing to the stream Blender already uses for its complaints, so what else it puts there is not established.

**So what Smoozy does about a quiet processor render is settled, in two parts, and the first turns the silence into a refusal.**

**Smoozy names the card technology when it starts a render that is meant to use the cards.** It has to name one anyway — a rented machine arrives with every card switched on and no technology chosen, and a scene asking for the cards then goes to the processor without a word. What the research adds is what happens when the technology is named and there is no usable card behind it: **the render stops at once, writes no image and ends at a failing status**, saying it found no device of the kind asked for. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on a rented Linux machine whose card was working) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). That is the loud failure this section said did not exist, and it is reached by naming the thing rather than leaving it to a default. So a machine that cannot give what was asked for refuses the job instead of rendering it on the processor for six paid hours. **What was tested is a technology with no hardware of that kind behind it, not a broken driver** — those are different conditions, and whether a broken driver produces the same refusal was never run that way. Label: **the decision is Smoozy's**, taken on the strength of that finding.

**And Smoozy asks every render for the line that names what did the work, so the finished job can say it.** It is the only thing anywhere that answers the question. In Blender 5.0 and later it costs an ordinary logging switch; in 4.5 it costs a Cycles debugging switch writing to the stream Blender uses for complaints, and what else that route puts there is not established. What the finished entry then says is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s. Label: **the decision is Smoozy's**. Two things it cannot become: a warning before the render, because nothing available beforehand tells what the render will actually use; and a live readout, because the line is a closing report. Where the reading cannot be had — **a job that did not finish, whether or not something was printed** — or output nobody kept, the entry says the job's devices are not known rather than guessing. *(Corrected 2026-09-01: this used to say *a job stopped part-way*, meaning one that prints nothing. Some stopped jobs do print, and what they print cannot be trusted, so the rule is about how the job ended rather than about whether a line appeared.)* **Which route starts the render bears on this**, and the two are only known to fit together on one of them — see [How a render is started](#how-a-render-is-started).

**Neither part second-guesses the artist.** Where the file asks for the processor, Smoozy passes that through unchanged and names no technology, exactly as the paragraph below says.

**And Smoozy names a technology only for a Cycles job.** A file set to GPU under Cycles and then switched to EEVEE names none, and never fails for a library Cycles alone would use. Label: **the decision is Smoozy's**, the build runner's ruling of 2026-11-02.

**Four kinds of machine are still untested**, and each is a machine Smoozy cannot yet say what it does with: cards joined by NVLink, cards of two different makes together, cards of unequal speed, and — for EEVEE — which one card is picked when there are several. See [The machine](../Blender%20Research/Comparison/The%20machine.md). A card that fails part-way through a render is untested too. None of them unsettles the instruction above; they are cases beside it.

**Where the file asks for the processor, nothing here overrides it.** That choice is the file's and the user's, carried on the settings page — see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md). Smoozy makes the cards available; it does not decide what the render uses.

### The file finding its own dependencies

**The `.blend` a render is built from carries no remap of its own**, because [Dependency Upload & Remap](Dependency%20Upload%20&%20Remap.md) reads that file and never writes to it. What its references should point at on the machine is held by that module, and is put into effect **when the render starts** — the same moment as the redirect above, on the same file.

That module owns what the remap says and where it is kept. This module owns nothing about finding assets or sending them up; what it owns is that the remap is in force by the time the render loads the file. **How it is put in force is now settled, and the shape of the answer is worth knowing here**: nothing can run before the file is loaded, so the load happens first against what the file itself records, and the remap is then applied — with a reload where the kind needs one — all of it before the render is told to begin. The outcome the promise asks for is unchanged; what moved is that it is reached just after the load rather than just before it. The redirect's answer above does not serve it: that one is about where a render writes, and this is about what the file reads. Label: **settled at Architecture** (2026-08-23).

### Every job renders as an animation

Even a job covering a single frame. Settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md), which owns the reason; this module is where it is applied.

**The difference the rule was made for arises outside Blender's interface too, so the rule does what it was meant to do.** Started from outside, a single-frame render and an animation do not write the same names: the single frame leaves the frame number out and the animation puts it in. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on macOS and on Linux) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). A file output node adds its own version of the same split: in 4.5 it writes the frame number every time, and from 5.0 only on an animation. Label: **observed**. Rendering every job as an animation removes both.

### The file's own scripts run

**Every render lets the file's own scripts run — its Python drivers and the scripts stored in it.** Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-08). The Creator: *"yes, Smoozy should definitely make every Blender on it enable this."* Left alone, Blender does not run them: it starts with them switched off in every version and skips them with a printed line, so a file built to use them renders without them. Label: **documented** and **observed** — the research behind the [Rendering architecture](../Architecture/Rendering.md) L9 (*The machine 7*). So every render Smoozy starts switches them on, and nothing turns that off. **Smoozy's readings of the file do the same** — *Read the file* and the dependency check's reading — so what Smoozy reads from a file is what it renders. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-10-08). The Creator: *"Yes to running scripts when reading."*

**This is about the render.** Whether *Read the file* and the dependency check run the file's scripts too is not settled; it is parked in [Open Threads](../Groundwork/Open%20Threads.md).

### All six have to survive Smoozy not being there

A machine re-runs an interrupted job by itself, with nobody connected and Smoozy possibly closed — [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) settles that. So none of the six above can be something Smoozy applies by hand each time a render begins. Whatever the machine was given at submission has to carry them. Otherwise a re-run writes to the wrong places, may not use the cards, may not find the file's dependencies, may not write what it wrote the first time, may render a simulation never baked standing still, and may run without the file's own scripts. What that takes is Architecture's; that it is required is stated here, because this is the module whose promises break without it.

## Ending a render that is running

**Ending and being interrupted are different things.** Ending is Smoozy's act, made on a connection. An interruption is the machine's own, with no connection and no Smoozy, and the machine re-runs the job afterwards by itself. This section is about ending.

**Rendering ends the running render when it is asked to.** Two things ask: the user stopping a job from the queue, and **Servers** on a removal where the user chose to stop and clear. Both are settled where they are asked from — see [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) and the [Servers spec](Servers.md).

**A render running on a rented machine can be reached over a connection and told to end**, and this was tried on machines of both kinds against a real render rather than a stand-in: the stop reaches Blender itself and nothing standing in front of it. **It is not instant** — the stop took seconds to take effect rather than landing the moment it was asked for — so anything waiting on a stop waits with a limit of its own. See [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). Label: **settled at Architecture** (2026-08-23).

**The render ends where it is.** Frames already written stay on the machine's disk, untouched. A stop that lands at no particular moment leaves those frames and nothing else; a stop that lands inside a frame's own write leaves something at that frame's name, which is below. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on macOS and on Linux) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). What becomes of the job afterwards — history, marked as stopped, and the machine starting the next job in its line — is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s.

**There is one way to end a render, and it costs the frame in hand.** Smoozy does not offer to let the current frame finish first. Blender has no such stop: no version of the four has one, inside its window or outside it, and the obvious way of arranging it from a script was tried and did not stop the render at all. The frame the render was on is thrown away. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on macOS and on Linux) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**How the stop is sent decides what it leaves behind, and Smoozy sends the one that leaves whole frames.** There are three ways to end a render from outside and they are not alike. Aimed at the moment a file is being written, an **interrupt** — the gentlest — left the picture whole in all eighty of its runs, while a **polite request to close** and an **outright kill** each left it short in all eighty of theirs — ten of each signal in each version on each machine — every short file on Linux being exactly zero bytes. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on macOS and on Linux) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**Earlier runs that found nothing short do not disagree with that.** What decides whether a short file is left is when the stop lands relative to the write — not which signal was sent, not the picture size, and not the version. Those earlier runs sent eight stops at no particular moment, against a write that lasts about a thousandth of a second, so landing inside one was never likely. Label: **observed** — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). The same reading settles what looked like a second clash: that the frame in hand is thrown away with nothing written for it is what a stop sent at no particular moment leaves, and the short files come from a stop that lands inside the write.

**A stop aimed at the scene's own picture write comes out the same way, and that is now tested.** Aimed at the instant the frame's own file appears, an **interrupt** finished the write and left the frame whole, while a **polite request to close** and an **outright kill** each left a short file at the frame's own path. Blender writes a frame straight to its final name and the file grows in place, so there is no temporary name for a stop to be caught at instead. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions, on macOS and on Linux) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**Two limits go with it, and one matters to the re-run.** How many bytes a stop leaves is a race, with no reading by version or by machine, and a part-written file can still open and report the full picture size — so nothing may treat a file opening, or its size, as proof that a frame finished. And every stop on record was sent from outside; what a hand inside Blender's own window leaves at that moment is not established.

**So Smoozy ends a render with the interrupt, and never with the kill.** Label: **the decision is Smoozy's**, taken on the strength of that finding. It matters beyond tidiness: a short file sitting at exactly the name the finished frame would carry is *present*, and presence is what the re-run's *already there* test reads (see [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)), so an abruptly killed job could leave a frame that is never rendered. The same choice decides what is left of a job whose output is one video file — an interrupt leaves the frames that were finished, and the other two leave a file with nothing in it at all.

**Where the interrupt does not take, Smoozy says the render could not be stopped rather than escalating quietly.** Reaching for the kill would trade a stop the user asked for against a frame they may lose without being told, and the whole-app rule on failures already covers saying so plainly (see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md)). Label: **the decision is Smoozy's**.

**A dropped connection is not a stop, and does not end a render.** A render outlived the connection it was started from, in all four versions — the connection cut on one machine, the shell killed on the other, and then a hang-up sent to the whole group of programs it belonged to. Each time the render carried on and frames kept appearing. Label: **observed** (the same in all four versions; one treatment per machine rather than both on each) — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). So losing a connection mid-render costs the user nothing of the render itself, and what Smoozy shows them meanwhile is [Servers](Servers.md)' and [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s.

So the user is told what a stop costs rather than offered a gentler one, and a stop may throw away forty minutes of paid machine time. This module once offered *end after the frame it is on*, resting on an assumption that Blender might allow it. The Creator ruled on 2026-08-06 that the offer is dropped rather than reached some other way, and it is gone from [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) and the [Telegram spec](Telegram.md) as well. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-06).

**What the user is told covers both halves of a stop, not only the cost.** The frame in hand is lost, and the frames already finished stay on the machine and can be brought down like any other job's outputs — see [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md). Told only the loss, a user may take the whole attempt to have gone with it and never go looking for the frames that are sitting there. Where it is said, and in what words, is Design's.

**Ending a render needs a connection**, like everything else that acts on a machine. Where there is no connection the stop cannot be sent, and how the user is told follows the whole-app rule *how a failure reaches the user* (see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md)).

**Nothing is retried.** A render that fails is not started again, and that is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s rule, not a decision taken here. The only automatic re-run in Smoozy is the machine's own, after an interruption, which needs nothing from this module at the time, because what the machine was given at submission already carries it.

**A job that ends short delivers what it wrote.** Stopped, failed or given up, the frames already written stay on the machine and can be brought down. Smoozy tells the user which frames were rendered and that the rest were not. This is the whole-app rule *do what can be done, and say what can't*: the job is not thrown away because it did not finish, and the user is not left to wonder what happened to the missing frames. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-14).

## Submitting again from history

A history entry keeps the whole setup its job ran with — file, scene, range, resolution, overrides — precisely so the user does not have to find the file and enter everything again ([Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)). Acting on that is this module's.

**Submitting again opens the settings page, filled in with what that job ran with.** It does not send the job off again on the spot. The user lands where they would have been, with everything already entered, changes whatever they want to change, and submits. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-28).

That is the shape because sending an identical job straight back to the machine can do only one of two things: repeat the same failure, or spend the user's money on a result they already have.

**What to change is not Smoozy's to suggest, with one exception, and the exception is named.** Smoozy shows the reason the machine gave and does not diagnose the render — the whole-app rule in [Whole app](Whole%20app.md). The one thing it does offer after a failure is **Simplify**, below. Everything else on the page is the user's.

### Simplify, offered on a job that failed

**Where the entry is a failed one, submitting again offers to render with Simplify on.** It is Blender's own name for the feature and the name the page uses, because it is the name an artist already knows — see Rule 9 in the [Rules](../Groundwork/Rules.md). Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**It is off unless the user turns it on.** Smoozy never applies it by itself and never submits a job with it on that the user did not ask for. Simplify changes the picture, so switching it on quietly would hand somebody a frame that does not match their file with nothing to explain it.

**It is offered on any failed job, not on a diagnosis.** Smoozy usually cannot say why a job failed: two of the four supported versions are killed by the machine running out of memory and never say so — their error stream is empty and their ordinary output simply stops at a sampling line (see [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)). So the offer does not claim to know what went wrong. It says what Simplify does — it cuts how much of a scene is loaded — and leaves the judgment to the artist. Offering it only where Smoozy could name the cause would mean never offering it in the case it is most for.

**Three settings are exposed, all from Simplify's render half.** Max Subdivision, Child Particles and Texture Limit. Every one exists in Blender 4.5, 5.0, 5.1 and 5.2, so the offer reads the same whichever Blender the new job renders with. Label: **documented and observed** — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**Two things in that group are deliberately left out.** Blender 5.2 carries a Texture Resolution setting the other three versions have not got; offering it would mean a control that appears and disappears with the Blender chosen, for one setting. And Simplify's two culling settings are not offered at all: they drop whole objects out of the frame, which is a far larger change to somebody's picture than a coarser mesh or a smaller texture. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**It reaches every engine, which is not what its own documentation suggests.** Blender documents Simplify on a Cycles page, and the research found its render half changing the picture under Cycles, EEVEE and Workbench alike, in all four versions on both machines. Label: **observed** — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md).

**One of the three is proved at the moment a render starts and two are not, and that is stated rather than smoothed over.** Switching Simplify on and setting Max Subdivision from outside, as a render begins, was watched changing the picture in all four versions on both machines. Texture Limit was written into a file and read back at the value set, in all four versions on both machines, but no test ever imposed it as a render started and looked at what came out; Child Particles was read in every version's documentation and neither imposed nor read back. They sit on the same switch and the same lever as Max Subdivision, so they are promised here on that ground. Label: **observed** for the switch and Max Subdivision, **inferred** for the other two — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). **The Creator ruled on 2026-08-12 that this is promised as it stands rather than held for another test**, so if either misbehaves it will be found at Build rather than on paper.

**A job rendered with Simplify on is marked as one.** The frames it produces do not match the file the artist saved, and nothing else on the entry would say why. The mark belongs to [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md), which owns what an entry shows.

**It is not on the ordinary settings page.** Smoozy is a render farm and the file is the artist's to prepare; offering to cut their scene down every time they submit is not this app's job. It appears where a job has already failed and the user is deciding what to change. Why the whole group is off the settings page is the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md)'s, which owns that decision.

**It is offered on a failed job and on no other entry, and this sentence used to say the opposite.** Until 2026-08-25 the same section both headed itself *offered on a job that failed* and said further down that it was offered on any history entry. The narrow reading is the one that holds, because it is the one the rest of this section is written for: the offer exists where a job has already failed and the user is deciding what to change, and Simplify makes a picture worse, so putting it in front of somebody whose render came out right is offering to spoil it. Submitting again with the settings filled in is still offered on every entry — that is above, and it is untouched. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-25).

**What comes back is a new job.** It gets its own identity and its own folder, so it can never write over what the earlier attempt left behind — that follows from [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md), and it is what makes submitting again safe.

**Which means finishing a part-done range this way leaves two folders, and Smoozy says so.** Where the earlier job ended short — stopped, failed or given up — its frames stay where they are, and the job submitted to cover the rest writes into a folder of its own. So one sequence ends up in two halves that sit apart, on the machine and, where the outputs come home, on the user's computer as well, and putting them together is the user's own work. That is said plainly when the job is submitted again, rather than left to be found afterwards. Smoozy states the cost and does nothing about it: it changes no setting, fills in no range, and suggests nothing. Where it is said, and in what words, is Design's.

**The label and the auto-download switch are not carried over.** Both are offered fresh, like every other choice made at the moment of submitting. What comes back is a new job, and neither of them is part of the setup the history entry keeps.

**It needs a connected machine, but not the original one.** Where the machine that ran the job is still there, the job goes back to it. Where it is gone — which is the ordinary ending, since a machine is rented for a piece of work and destroyed when the work is done — the user can point the same setup at a `.blend` on **another** connected machine. Everything a history entry holds sits on the user's own computer rather than on the machine ([Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)), so the setup outlives the machine even though nothing that reaches back to it does. Without this, submitting again would work only in the rare case. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-07-30).

**Moving it to another machine changes three things.** The `.blend` has to be found on the new machine — Smoozy opens the file browser there so the user points at it, since a file on a destroyed machine has no location to carry over. The Blender version is whatever that machine has, which may not be the one the job ran with. And nothing else the old machine held comes with it — the assets the job read, and any add-ons it needed, are on the machine they were put on, so the new machine has to be supplied the same way any other new machine is.

**The Blender the old job used travels with the setup.** A history entry records which Blender the job was submitted to render with, so that version is not lost with the machine — see the [Queue & History spec](Queue%20&%20History.md), which owns what an entry keeps. What the new machine has is its own affair, and which of its Blenders renders the new job is settled on the settings page. **Render Settings** owns that pick and what a version difference costs — see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md). This module keeps no version warning of its own.

**Which machine the browser opens on.** Where the machine the job ran on is still connected, it is that one. Where it is gone, the browser opens on the machine the user is already browsing, and they move to another connected one from inside it if that is not where the file is — one machine is browsed at a time (see the [File Browser spec](File%20Browser.md)). Smoozy picks no machine for the user: as with any other job, the machine follows from where the `.blend` turns out to be. Label: **settled at Design** (2026-08-23).

**On a machine that never ran the job, the `.blend` is usually not there at all**, and Smoozy says so rather than opening on a folder that means nothing to the user. It says the file has to be found on this machine, and that where it sat on the old one carries over nowhere. It does not claim the file is or is not up there — it has not looked, and hunting through the user's own files for them is not something this app does.

**Sending the file up is the user's own act, from the browser that just opened.** That browser is the ordinary file browser, so an upload works there as it does anywhere else and lands where the user is browsing — see the [File Browser spec](File%20Browser.md). Smoozy starts no transfer of its own here, exactly as stated above for any other submission.

**The setup waits while they go.** Sending a project up and checking it for the pieces it is missing can take hours, and the settings page holds what it is carrying while the user is elsewhere in the app — the setup out of a history entry sits where typed values sit, and what the user typed is not lost while they look elsewhere (see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md)). Closing Smoozy takes it, and even then nothing is lost: the history entry still holds the setup, and submitting again from it is offered as often as the user likes. **One part of it is narrower: the frame range it loads belongs to that one job.** Once the job is submitted, or the page is opened on another file, the file's page reads the range it read before, and says no more that it came from an entry. A range the user typed stays, and looking elsewhere in the app with the page in its strip leaves everything as it was. *(Added 2026-11-03, the second 5.3 live pass:* Resume — 30 frames *set 11–40, and the next page for that file still held 11–40 on a file whose own range is 1–1. The build runner's ruling. No auditor has seen it.)*

**So the order is the user's.** They can send the file up from the browser Smoozy opened and come back to the page still waiting, or leave the page entirely, put the file and everything it needs on the machine first, and submit again from the same entry afterwards. Both reach the same place, and an entry is not spent by being used once.

**What stands behind that third change is thinner than the others, and it is stated here rather than left to be assumed.** The settings page opens with the old setup filled in, so the user sees what they are about to submit; and at the submission they are told what has not been checked — this module's warning that nobody has looked for the file's missing assets, and **Render Settings**' statement of what a file that was never read cannot tell them, a missing engine among it (see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md)). That is the whole of it. Nothing in this flow reads the file for its add-ons, and a history entry does not carry which the old job used — so an add-on missing from the new machine is named as unchecked rather than caught, and the user decides.

**Where the machine is there but the `.blend` has moved**, Smoozy says so and opens the file browser on that machine so the user can point at the file — rather than leaving them to hunt for it, which is the work this feature exists to save. That entry point is the same one the other-machine case uses; the [File Browser spec](File%20Browser.md) carries its side of it.

**It can be reached from a phone**, on the message saying a job failed — see [Telegram](Telegram.md). It is this same action rather than a new one, and only the machine the message came from is in question, since a machine that is gone is not sending messages.

**But a phone has no settings page, and that is what has to be settled.** Everything above turns on the user changing something before the job goes again, which is why the page opens rather than the job being sent. A phone offers one tap. Sending an identical job from a tap is the outcome this section exists to prevent, so a tap cannot simply stand in for the page.

The research it waited on has landed, and it supplies raw material rather than the answer. What can make a render fail is now known for all four versions. Label: **observed** — see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md). Reading that list, the causes fall into two kinds: the file's own doing, which sending the identical job again repeats exactly, and the machine's, which sending it again may clear. Which of the two a tap may act on is settled here.

**The tap sends the same job again, unchanged, and it is only offered where the failure was the machine's.** The machine gone, the connection lost, the render killed by something outside the file — sending the job again may clear any of those, so the message offers the tap and the tap sends exactly what ran before.

**Where the failure was the file's own, the message does not offer the tap at all.** An add-on that is not on the machine, an engine the machine cannot run, a setting the file carries that the render will not accept — sending the same file again buys a second identical failure and a second bill. The message says instead that the job needs a change before it can go again, which is the settings page's work and not a phone's. Where Smoozy cannot tell which of the two kinds a failure was, the tap is not offered.

So the tap and the page are not the same act, and this module no longer claims they are: the page is where a job is changed, and the tap only ever repeats a job that had nothing wrong with it. Label: **ruled 2026-09-02**, under the standing delegation of every decision the Creator can hand over. The marker this section carried is cleared with it.

## Carrying on from where a job stopped

**A second action sits beside submitting again, on any history entry whose job ended short.** Stopped, failed or given up, the frames the job did write are on the machine and the rest of the range was never rendered. *Resume* opens the settings page filled in with that job's setup, exactly as submitting again does, with **one difference: the range starts at the frame after the last one Smoozy can vouch for as finished.** *Words changed 2026-10-28 (was *Carry on from where this stopped*): Creator-stated, the final words sweep. No auditor has seen it.* [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) keeps that figure on the entry and owns it. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**It is a different action from submitting again, and that is what keeps the promise above intact.** Submitting again fills the page in with what the job actually ran with, and changing the range under it would break the one thing that feature promises. This does not touch it: it is asked for by name, it says plainly that it has moved the range, and submitting again is still there beside it doing exactly what it always did. The user who wants the whole range renders the whole range.

**The figure is read from the machine when the action is taken, not remembered from when the job ended.** A machine that went down can leave a frame empty that Blender had reported saved seconds earlier — label: **observed** on one rented Linux machine, macOS untested; see [Sources](Sources/Rendering.sources.md) — so a figure carried over from the job's own ending can name a frame whose file is no longer there, and the range would start after a hole nobody would ever fill. What the reading counts is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s *already there*, which does not count a file of zero bytes. Label: **Creator-stated** (2026-08-12).

**Where the reading cannot be had, the action still works and says what it is standing on.** This already needs a connected machine, and the section below covers a submission with no connection at all. What is new is a machine that answers but whose outputs cannot be read — a folder gone, a login no longer allowed to read it. Then the page opens on the figure [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md) kept, and says plainly that it could not check the machine and this is what the entry recorded. Smoozy does not refuse the action over it, and does not present a remembered figure as a checked one.

**The range is still theirs to change, and that is what carries the rest.** The page opens with a range worked out rather than a range remembered, and everything on it can be typed over like any other value — Smoozy has set a starting point, not made a decision. **That matters more here than it looks.** A file that is short but not empty still counts as finished, so a machine that went down can leave a hole this figure walks past; [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md) states that limit and does not hide it. The user who knows their server went down drags the start back themselves, which is why the figure is shown with what it rests on rather than quietly used.

**It is a new job, with all that follows.** Its own identity, its own folder, the label and the auto-download switch offered fresh, and nothing written over what the earlier attempt left. So the two-folders cost stated above is the ordinary outcome of this action rather than an accident of it, and it is said the same way, at the same moment.

**Its entry says which job it carries on from.** Without that the user has two entries against the same file with nothing to show that one picks up where the other left off. The mark is [Queue & History](Queue%20&%20History.md)'s — see that spec, which owns what an entry shows.

**It is not offered where there is nothing to start after.** A job that finished no frame at all, and an entry with no ending Smoozy ever learned, both leave nothing to carry on from — the first because carrying on would be the whole range, which is submitting again, and the second because Smoozy cannot say what was reached. In both, Smoozy says so rather than leaving a control that does nothing.

**Everything else is the section above, unchanged.** It needs a connected machine and not the original one; where the `.blend` has to be found, the file browser opens on the machine and the user points at it; the Blender the old job used travels with the setup and which one renders is **Render Settings**'; and the setup waits on the page while the user goes and puts the file where it needs to be.

**What the page does with that setup** is the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md)'s, and it states it: the settings mean what they always mean, and the job's overrides simply sit where entered ones would.

## When a submission cannot happen

**A machine with no Blender takes no jobs**, because there is nothing on it to render with. Label: **Creator-stated**. **Render Settings** already withholds submitting and says why, pointing the user at **Server Setup** before they ever reach the submit — see the [Render Settings spec](Render%20Settings.md).

**Where the connection is gone at the moment of submitting, no job exists.** Smoozy does not hold it to send later, and does not pretend it went. How the user is told is the whole-app rule *how a failure reaches the user*: it appears on the thing that failed, stays until they are done with it, and is a plain sentence with the machine's own words behind it (see [Whole app](Whole%20app.md)).

**A job that will write no files is not one of these cases — it is allowed to run.** Smoozy warns and lets the user go ahead, with the option to stop; it is settled in [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md), which owns it. Where the file was read before submitting, the warning comes then; where it was not, the job is held at the start of its render for the user to choose. This module carries that out and decides none of it.

## What Rendering does not do

- It does not choose the file, and it does not read it to fill settings in. Choosing is **File Browser**'s; that reading is **Render Settings**' Pre-load Settings. The render this module starts loads the file on the machine, which is how the redirect is worked out.
- It does not decide any of the settings, or change them. It submits what **Render Settings** hands over, and after that nobody can change them.
- It does not own the queue, the order of it, or anything that happens to a job once submitted. That is **Queue & History**'s, including cancelling, stopping's aftermath, reordering, failure, and the re-run after an interruption.
- It does not decide what a job writes, where it lands, which folder matters, or how many files there will be. That is [What a job writes](What%20a%20job%20writes.md)'s. This module carries out the redirect and nothing else about it.
- It does not check a `.blend` for missing assets, find them, or upload them — that is **Dependency Upload & Remap**'s. It only warns that nobody has looked.
- It does not install Blender or anything else on a machine. That is **Server Setup**'s; this module picks nothing and installs nothing.
- It does not connect to a machine. Every act here needs a connection from **Servers**.
- It does not bring anything home. Auto-download is switched on here and is **Download**'s.
- It does not retry a failed render, and it does not re-run an interrupted one — the machine does that by itself.
